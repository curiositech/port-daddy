% Figure IV.new --- the Myerson-Satterthwaite wedge: efficient trades the
% mechanism itself refuses. ch6-15 (register: must) and ADDENDUM 4 (breaks
% the visual-less run at sec:ms).
%
% Reader question: how much real, mutually-beneficial trade does budget
% balance and incentive compatibility actually cost the market?
% Claim: on the chapter's own (v,c) square, the double auction refuses a
% 10-cr efficient trade at the worked point (Bob v=70, Alice c=60) because
% v-c=10<M/4=22.5; integrated over the whole square, that wedge is
% 7/32 (approx 21.9%) of all draws, independent of M.
% Evidence: Thm. ms (Myerson-Satterthwaite); Chatterjee-Samuelson linear
% equilibrium, trade iff v-c>=M/4; worked point (70,60); wedge probability
% a(2M-a)/(2M^2) at a=M/4 = 7/32.
% Must distinguish: the "trade happens" corner (v-c>=M/4) from the
% "efficient but refused" wedge (0<=v-c<M/4, real surplus left on the
% table) from the genuinely-no-surplus region (v<c).
% Grammar chosen: regime plot in (v,c) space, the wedge filled between two
% lines (S11 fillbetween), the worked point dropped to both axes (atlas
% first choice). Grammar rejected: the probability alone in prose (already
% stated) -- it hides that the wedge is a fixed-width band running the
% whole diagonal, not a corner case.
% Five-second test: a reader can find (70,60) inside the shaded wedge and
% see it sits on the trade side of the diagonal but the wrong side of the
% refusal line.

\caption{\textbf{The double auction refuses more than one efficient trade in
five.} Above the diagonal $v=c$ every draw has a real surplus; the mechanism
only trades it when $v-c\ge M/4=22.5$ (gold line). The band between the two
lines is efficient but refused, $7/32\approx21.9\%$ of the square, the same
fraction at every $M$. At the chapter's own worked point, Bob values the
rental at $70$ and Alice's cost is $60$: a $10$-cr surplus sits on the
table, but $10<22.5$, so no trade happens. [internal; algebra shown]}
