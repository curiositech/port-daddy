% The deviator's net payoff from one defection, against the discount factor, for
% the three-round graduated trigger and for grim trigger. Both curves are the
% chapter's own closed form (d-c) - (c-p)*delta*(1-delta^k)/(1-delta) at the
% chapter's stage-game payoffs (c,d,p) = (3,4,1): k=3 gives 1 - 2*delta*(1 +
% delta + delta^2), k -> infinity gives 1 - 2*delta/(1-delta). The marked root
% delta* = 0.3425 is proofs/economics/delta-threshold.expected.txt (Z3: unique
% real root of 2x^3+2x^2+2x-1 in (0,1), delta* = 0.3425080314) [verified]; the
% grim bound 1/3 is the closed form stated in the same section; the marked point
% -3.878 at delta = 0.9 is the chapter's own worked arithmetic (1 - 2*2.439)
% [verified].

\caption{Net payoff to a player who defects once, against the discount factor,
under the two punishment regimes of this section. Both curves cross zero within
$0.009$ of each other --- $\delta^{\star}=0.3425$ for the three-round graduated
trigger, $1/3$ for grim trigger --- so bounding the punishment at three rounds
costs almost nothing in the threshold. Away from the threshold the two regimes
are not close at all: grim trigger's punishment grows without bound and leaves
the frame by $\delta=0.72$, while the graduated trigger's stays finite, reaching
the chapter's worked value of $-3.878$ at $\delta=0.9$. The diamond at
$\delta=0.30$ is the one discount factor the model checker has actually been
run at --- the session printed below --- and the curve's sign there, defecting
still pays, is exactly what that run reports. Both curves are the section's own
closed form at $(c,d,p)=(3,4,1)$, not a simulation
[verified, \texttt{proofs/economics/delta-threshold.z3} and
\texttt{claim\_signaling\_delta30.run.log}].}
\label{fig:bc-delta-threshold}
\end{figure}
