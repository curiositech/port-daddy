% Where the succession price binds. The boundary is the theorem's own closed
% form D* = eta*K/(1 - eta*K) (Thm. succession-price); the plane is the pair the
% criterion is stated over: eta*K on x, the mortality-to-succession ratio xi/eta
% on y. Both marked points are the chapter's worked instance (lambda=1, c=4
% servers at mu_g=1.2, A=0.2, w=1, eta=0.25, W_pool=0.8362, K=1.0362), so
% eta*K = 0.2591 for both: the boundary value D* = 0.3496 and the script's own
% canary at xi/eta = 2.0, where even mu_s = 1e8 loses to the pool
% [verified, skills/harbor-results/scripts/b8_specialization.py].

\caption{The succession price as a boundary in the plane the criterion is
actually stated over. Below the curve a high enough skill premium can still
justify renting one sole specialist; above it the pool wins at \emph{every}
finite premium, including one the buyer cannot reach. At the chapter's worked
instance $\eta K=0.2591$, so the boundary sits at $D^{\star}=0.3496$ --- and the
script's own canary, at $\xi/\eta=2.0$, sits nearly six times higher up that
same vertical read, which is why an infinitely skilled specialist still loses
there. The curve steepens without limit as $\eta K\to1$; past $\eta K=1$ the
criterion does not apply rather than being satisfied
[verified, \texttt{b8\_specialization.py}].}
