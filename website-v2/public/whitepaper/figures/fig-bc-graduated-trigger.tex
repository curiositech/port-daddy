% New figure for Sec. claim-signaling-ic: what the graduated trigger costs in
% patience. The chapter states delta*_{k=3} ~ 0.342 against the grim-trigger
% bound 1/3 and says the three-round window "adds barely 0.0092" -- but never
% shows how fast that premium falls, which is the whole reason the graduated
% strategy is affordable. A k-round graduated trigger sustains truthful claim
% signalling iff (d-c) < (c-p) * sum_{i=1..k} delta^i; with the chapter's stage
% game d-c = 1 and c-p = 2 this is 2*sum delta^i > 1, whose root is delta*(k).
% Data: whitepaper/figures/data/graduated-trigger-threshold.csv, emitted by
% whitepaper/figures/data/make-figure-data.py (deterministic, no seed).
% delta*(3) = 0.3425 reproduces the chapter's 0.342 [verified].

\vspace{2mm}
\caption{\textbf{A forgiving punishment window is almost free, and the whole
price is paid in the first two rounds.} The smallest patience at which
truthful file-claim signalling survives a $k$-round
graduated trigger, at the chapter's stage game ($d-c=1$, $c-p=2$), against the
grim-trigger floor $\delta>1/3$ that a never-forgiving strategy needs. One
round of punishment demands $\delta>0.500$; two rounds already fall to $0.366$;
the three-round window the chapter adopts costs $0.3425$, a premium of
$0.0092$ over grim, and by five rounds the premium falls under $0.001$. This is why the
production strategy can afford to forgive: crashes and deliberate defection are
indistinguishable to an observer, and buying that tolerance costs almost no
patience. The curve is the root of $2(\delta+\cdots+\delta^k)=1$ and holds only
for this stage game; a different payoff spread moves every point.

\texttt{figures/data/make-figure-data.py}, deterministic]}
