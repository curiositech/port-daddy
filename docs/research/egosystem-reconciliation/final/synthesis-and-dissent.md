# Synthesis and retained dissent

Status: solo editorial analysis, September 8, 2026. This is not a transcript of
independent agents or a finding of consensus. The operator's halt suspended the
original independent-paper and fresh-review phases. The independently supplied
[Field Guide review](../research/field-guide-case.md) is the only external
review input attributed as such here.

## The choices that survived review

The governing order is: explicit operator halt and disclosure boundaries;
attributable decision authority; claim-specific evidence and temporal scope;
reversible, bounded inquiry; then throughput and convenience. This order is a
design decision, not a claim that all stakeholders rank values identically.
An unresolved value dispute goes to the named decider within their jurisdiction.

The core choice is an institutional event projection alongside actor assertions,
not a merged agent memory. The projection answers what was authorized, when it
applies and which commitments survive. It cannot establish what everybody
privately believes or whether an authorized decision is factually correct.

## Six lenses, one author

The following steel-mans were written sequentially by the same author. Each
preserves three useful claims, a proposed improvement and a connection to
another lens. None is counted as an independent vote.

### Privacy and jurisdiction

Three things this lens gets right: a claim can reveal a secret even without its
source; a new audience is a new disclosure decision; and a role assignment is
not blanket access to everything relevant to that role. Therefore retrieval
must filter before ranking, derived claims retain provenance-bound disclosure
limits, and aggregate conflict counts cannot reveal restricted cases.

Improvement: make the audience intersection and retention limit explicit on
each derivative, with an authorized declassification path instead of assuming
every synthesis inherits a union of permissions. Connection: distributed
projections must enforce the same boundary on replay and deletion, not only
on the interactive query path.

### Formal methods and argumentation

Three things this lens gets right: incompatible sentences are not necessarily
incompatible propositions; a valid implication is only as useful as its
premises and scope; and an attack on an argument is different from a rebuttal
of its conclusion. A detector should return a bounded proof path or an
explicitly unverified allegation, not a universal contradiction score.

Improvement: normalize modality, quantifiers, jurisdiction and valid time
before applying a verifier to a declared decidable fragment. Connection:
human-value tradeoffs remain outside that fragment even when their operational
consequences can be checked formally.

### Distributed systems and temporal state

Three things this lens gets right: event order differs from effective time;
replicated availability does not confer authority; and an idempotent decision
record does not make an external side effect exactly-once. The project needs
deterministic as-of queries and explicit supersession, not a last summary that
silently overwrites prior exceptions.

Improvement: separate the authorized decision, attempted effect and verified
effect receipt, retaining an uncertain state after ambiguous publication.
Connection: the cost ledger must also retain reservations across ambiguity
instead of funding a duplicate attempt.

### Governance and human values

Three things this lens gets right: consensus cannot substitute for jurisdiction;
people can disagree coherently about acceptable costs; and appeal must survive
the departure of the person who wrote the initial decision. A Synthesis Steward
accounts for findings but does not acquire every outcome owner's authority.

Improvement: preserve the strongest rejected alternative with its reopening
condition, and record a bounded appeal route. Connection: the interface should
show who can decide each outcome, not just who wrote the most persuasive text.

### Practitioner experience and attention

Three things this lens gets right: alert volume is not benefit; a reader needs
the consequence of a change before the entire argument history; and authority
can be misrepresented by a confident button label. The useful entry point is
“what changes if we do this?” with concise evidence and dissent nearby.

Improvement: group related allegations into one case, show uncertainty, and
make deeper provenance available on demand. Connection: grouped alerts must
retain independently actionable findings and cannot average away a blocking
privacy or authority issue.

### Economics and adversarial reliability

Three things this lens gets right: a per-agent budget is not an aggregate cap;
retries, reviewers and publication can incur additional costs; and unavailable
telemetry is not zero spend. An absorbing operator halt dominates all plans.

Improvement: reserve aggregate worst-case costs before admitting paid work,
count descendants and retries, and require an explicit authority-bearing
release for renewed spending. Connection: an uncertain effect is a reason to
reconcile its receipt, not to spawn an alternative worker.

## Retained dissent and reopening conditions

These are unresolved design objections, not attributed stakeholder statements.

1. **Institutional ceremony may cost more than it saves.** A typed decision
   history could burden small projects where a well-maintained ADR is enough.
   Reopen if H1 shows no material temporal-state benefit at matched retrieval,
   or if adjudication time exceeds the preregistered attention budget. Keep
   manual structured packets as the viable baseline.
2. **Consequence graphs may overstate causality.** Dependencies are often
   conditional, and an extracted edge may sound more certain than its source.
   Reopen automated expansion if H2 produces excessive false conflicts or
   explanations whose premises cannot be verified. Retain manual edges and
   visibly conditional paths.
3. **Separation can reduce correction as well as herding.** Withholding useful
   context may duplicate mistakes. H3 must measure correct amendment and total
   cost, not simply vocabulary diversity. A shared-context workflow remains
   a legitimate alternative if it performs better.
4. **Provenance can become a surveillance system.** Durable traces can expose
   sensitive work, and witnessed perception is not general ground truth.
   Reopen collection defaults if H4's benefit requires unnecessary retention
   or if redaction defeats the claimed evidence contract. Prefer minimal
   scoped references and user-visible retention.
5. **Steward concentration may become a bottleneck.** An accountable editor is
   easier to audit but may suppress alternatives or accumulate decision power.
   Audit rejected material findings and appeals; split jurisdictions when
   latency or authority concentration crosses a predefined threshold.

The final editorial decision is to ship a reviewable, offline design package
locally, not a running reconciliation service. Further agent work, publication
and production integration require their own explicit authority. Technical
readiness never releases the operator's halt.
