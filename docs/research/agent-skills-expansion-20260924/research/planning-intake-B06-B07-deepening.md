## Evidence map and limits

- Rochet & Tirole, [“Platform Competition in Two-Sided Markets” (2003), full paper](https://www.ugr.es/~scarbo/ROCHETIR.pdf), especially §§2.1–2.2 and Proposition 1: in their model, a platform chooses a price structure across buyer/seller sides; optimal allocation depends on side-specific demand elasticities (and the setup/competition assumptions). It does **not** imply “subsidize whichever supply side is scarce,” much less the skill’s proposed automatic supply-then-demand flip. The skill’s six-side taxonomy is local; the cited paper analyzes two sides. Armstrong’s [“Competition in Two-Sided Markets” (2006)](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/4324/) distinguishes monopoly, single-homing and competitive-bottleneck/multihoming cases; use its model only after naming the local sides and homing/externality assumptions.
- Grossman & Hart, [“The Costs and Benefits of Ownership” (1986), Harvard author/repository record](https://hart.scholars.harvard.edu/publications/costs-and-benefits-ownership-theory-vertical-and-lateral-integration): residual rights of control matter when specific rights cannot be fully contracted; ownership is analyzed through how integration changes parties’ incentives to make noncontractible investments. This does not prescribe renter runtime controls, owner reputation penalties, or a bond as the bridge.
- Akerlof, [“The Market for ‘Lemons’” (1970), full paper](https://cies.org.pe/wp-content/uploads/2016/07/akerlof_1970.pdf), §§I–II: adverse selection can lower average quality and shrink trade given asymmetric quality information and the paper’s model conditions. It does not establish that skill quality is hidden in every license market, or that usage metering/clawback/portfolio proofs are proven remedies.
- Chiu, Zhang & van der Schaar, [arXiv:2512.04988v2 (2026), full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.04988), §§2–4, 5, Appendix B.2–B.3 and C.5.3–C.5.4: a stylized finite-horizon gig-market simulation with worker agents, jobs/clients and a platform’s scoring/matching rules; skills and reputation are agent state/signals, not a distinct market “side.” Matching uses job-side rankings and worker preferences via capacity-constrained job-proposing Gale–Shapley; client score combines task-specific reputation and normalized bid; paid/benchmark outcomes update discounted Beta-style reputation. Authors explicitly state functional forms are tractability choices, not uniquely correct, and the work is a simulation, not empirical deployment proof. A later v2 title is “When AI Agents Compete for Jobs: Strategic Capabilities and Economic Dynamics of AI Labour Markets”; cite version and do not retain the skill’s assertion that this paper explicitly models three sides (agents/employers/reputation system). It does not say reputation is a separate side. The paper’s discussion reports simulated findings; its self-improvement findings do not justify the skill’s prescriptive “cap reputation’s marginal return” fix.
- Smith, [“The Contract Net Protocol” (1980), full primary paper](https://cse-robotics.engr.tamu.edu/dshell/cs631/papers/smith80contract.pdf), §§II–VI: asynchronous nodes, manager/contractor roles, task announcement with task-dependent content, bids, manager award, monitoring/results; managers and contractors evaluate from their own perspectives, with local mutual selection. The paper permits a contractor to partition a task and subcontract. This is a resource-allocation/task-sharing protocol, not an incentive-compatibility theorem and not “choose highest confidence.”
- FIPA, [Contract Net Interaction Protocol SC00029H (2002) primary text mirror](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=e560bbf29d1af433792fb5419845db1ab29bf7fc&repid=rep1&type=pdf), main interaction diagram and §1.1: initiator sends `cfp`; participants may reply `refuse`, `not-understood`, or `propose` before deadline; initiator sends `accept-proposal` to selected proposal(s) and `reject-proposal` to others; accepted participant reports `failure`, `inform-done`, or `inform-result`. §1.1 explicitly leaves cancellation, asynchrony, abnormal termination, and nested interactions unaddressed. Base CNP does not prescribe a confidence metric or winner rule. A modified/reissued CFP belongs to the separate [Iterated Contract Net SC00030](https://citeseerx.ist.psu.edu/document?doi=549b7fcda2d0b05ced04776ae38ba4f835921f6a&repid=rep1&type=pdf) family, not evidence for RCP-3’s bounded “scope unclear” behavior. (Official `fipa.org` endpoints timed out; actual primary spec body was read in the mirror.)
- HTN is a useful boundary only: Erol, Hendler & Nau, [“Semantics for Hierarchical Task-Network Planning” (1994)](https://www.cs.umd.edu/~nau/papers/erol1994semantics.pdf), formalizes task-network syntax/semantics and planning methods. None of the two decomposer skills cites or implements HTN semantics: a prose DAG/workplan is not an HTN plan unless primitive tasks, method applicability, preconditions/effects and solution semantics are specified.
- B07 prior baseline [orchestration-B07.md](orchestration-B07.md) and B06 prior [planning-B06.md](planning-B06.md) were used as context, not as external validation. No numeric threshold in these five bundles is supported by a calibration study or benchmark found here.

## Reproducible local methods and worked hand-checks

### Market design: what the local procedure can establish

The skill’s useful procedure is to list parties, outside options, information asymmetries, money/control/evidence/reputation flows and settlement outcomes, then ask whether a claimed “side” adds a distinct participation constraint and a cross-side externality. Keep this as a design worksheet; “incentive constraint” is not the formal side-counting rule in the cited papers. Price structure becomes an empirical/model question only after sides, elasticities, multihoming, platform competition, and transaction/membership charges are stated.

Hand-check: list requester R, operator O, asset owner W, skill licensor L, and intermediary P. Draw separate edges: R pays O for delivered work; O pays W for a rented worker and L for a license; P charges O a transaction fee; R’s demand may increase W/L participation and W/L availability may increase R’s participation. This list alone proves **five parties, not five sides** and does not prove three-sided economics. If W is also R and licenses are bundled without separate participation, collapse those roles for the relevant decision. If O is an employee of P with no independent outside option, count P+O’s joint constraint for that model. Then test cross-side participation effect with a stated counterfactual: e.g. holding total price fixed, moving a fee from R to W changes volume only if the response differs by side and the platform cannot bargain away the externality. Do not apply Rochet–Tirole’s two-sided formula directly to this five-role list.

Preserve the float-plan/escrow arithmetic only as a **first-party proposed settlement**, not as theory or proof: in the sample, 8 + 2 + 1 + 0.5 = 11.5 credits held; 3% of bounty 8 is 0.24, so net operator bounty is 7.76; transfers 7.76 + 2 + 1 + 0.5 + 0.24 = 11.5. This checks arithmetic, not conservation across funding, disputes, reversals, partial completion, oracle error, collusion, or identity loss. Skill’s `oracle(success|partial|sabotage|dispute)` needs explicit authority, appeal, and reversal transitions before it can claim every side’s worst case is bounded. “Hosted trust,” identity continuity, rent control rights, fee clawback, metering, subsidy flip, and reputation debits remain product design hypotheses; source each against first-party contracts and measure their effects.

### `task-decomposer`: preserve the decomposition questions, replace keyword routing and arbitrary gates

Its current sequence asks about domain, granularity, dependencies, vagueness, and complexity. This is a useful local prompt scaffold, but keyword-first routing (`research`, `build`, etc.) misclassifies mixed asks; “one agent one call,” three vague paths, 30% parallelizable, three skills, and a $5 cap are unsupported defaults. No cited method validates the phase templates. Use outcome/acceptance evidence, constraints, authority, unknowns, and irreversible actions first; then make deliverables or decisions into nodes, record the concrete reason for each hard dependency, and keep unresolved choices as named evidence-gathering/decision nodes. A shared input alone does not prove independence or parallel safety.

Hand-check: “ship a URL shortener” yields `contract` (observable endpoint/response requirements), `choose-storage` (decision and evidence), `implement` (depends on contract + storage decision), `test` (depends on implementation + acceptance cases), `approve-release` (authority gate), `deploy` (depends on passing test receipt + approval), `verify` (deploy receipt + observed endpoint result). API requirements and storage investigation may run in parallel only if the storage decision does not redefine the API contract; deployment may not cross the approval edge. If no test receipt exists, the `deploy` node remains blocked. The skill’s first example incorrectly says `api-spec` and `data-model` are parallelizable despite also declaring `api-spec → data-model`; retain only after correcting this internal contradiction. The HTN source is a contrast, not validation of this workflow: no method/precondition/effect semantics are supplied.

### `windags-decomposer`: first-party prediction pipeline; thresholds are not calibrated method

Preserve its stated 3-pass flow: (1) extract tasks/uncertainty, (2) map candidates to known skills/domain treatment, (3) build nodes/dependency edges and derive waves; keep the output’s task IDs, reasoned edge provenance, confidence/commitment, unknowns, and readiness checks. The skill’s `>=.8`, `.5–.8`, `.2–.5`, `<.2`, `.05` tie, >15 nodes, depth >4, >80% committed, cascade >6, and 20% schema missing rules are first-party heuristic/policy thresholds with no opened calibration data. Keep only when clearly labeled versioned policy and allow an abstain/research path. “Pass 2 deterministic/no LLM” and schema guarantees are local implementation claims needing code+version verification; do not promise them from the skill text. A DAG is a planning representation, not an executed schedule or proof of independence.

Hand-check, use typed edges and show topological layers: A `inspect current auth` and B `read threat model` are independent evidence tasks; C `choose auth policy` depends on A,B; D `implement` depends on C; E `test` depends on D. With unlimited parallel slots, layers are `{A,B}`, `{C}`, `{D}`, `{E}`. A and B could share a codebase but are only parallel-safe if they are read-only or have isolated outputs. If B instead needs A’s discovered endpoint list, add A→B and the layers become `{A}`, `{B}`, `{C}`, `{D}`, `{E}`. This checks edge/layer consistency; it does not establish the numeric confidence cutoffs, `parallelizable` field’s runtime behavior, agent count, makespan, or the skill’s sample wave correctness. The sample’s “resolve Node3” is not a runnable node until split into a decision/research node with defined input/output and dependencies.

### `wave-by-wave-parley`: valid checkpoint idea; RCP-3 is a distinct, unvalidated local protocol

Preserve a checkpoint after all nodes in wave N return and before any side effect in N+1: join completed outputs to explicit dependency IDs, update risk entries with evidence and version, decide proceed/revise/hold/escalate, validate graph/contracts, then launch only if gates pass. Keep failure recovery separate. The actual pseudocode currently checks uncertain **upcoming** nodes but checks risk IDs against the whole completed-output map rather than the just-completed wave (despite comments); risk reassessment is called with all completed outputs. Use the set intersection `risk.affected_nodes ∩ justCompletedNodeIds`, and require matching evidence provenance. It also says COMMITTED is irrevocable while its stated purpose is to reconsider assumptions from new evidence. Make a new revision with explicit supersession/reopen rationale rather than silently demoting prior state. Do not proceed automatically when a high risk remains under `ACCEPT_WITH_MONITORING`; monitoring is not a waiver. Failure, partial output, timeout, missing dependency, or untrusted output need explicit non-success branches.

The worked security example is illustrative: a scan result with two package versions is insufficient evidence to declare “no breaking changes” without compatibility checks/source. Its result says 40 CVEs/high risk may still proceed under `ACCEPT_WITH_MONITORING`; that contradicts the skill’s stated safety gate. Don’t treat an LLM confidence/reassessment as calibrated probability absent an outcome/validation set.

RCP-3’s five local actions (broadcast, CFP, proposal/refusal, award/reject, prune) can be retained as a **custom protocol sketch**, but correct its attribution: FIPA performative names do not make RCP-3 FIPA CNP conformant; highest self-reported `confidence_estimate` is neither Smith’s nor FIPA’s mandated bid semantics and is not truthfully elicited by either source. A second CFP after scope change is closer to Iterated CNP; `cancel` is outside the cited base CNP diagram and the spec says cancellation is unaddressed. RCP’s prune mutates downstream `input_contract` to remove a missing predecessor—unsafe unless successors are revalidated or also pruned. The claimed `2k` LLM-call budget also conflicts with steps 2–4 requiring bid evaluation plus coordinator selection; count actual invocations in an implementation, do not promise it from prose.

Hand-check: after wave 1, dependency node `scan` returns “package X has a major-version API break, evidence hash h1”; upcoming `patch-X` is TENTATIVE and depends on `scan`; `publish` depends on `patch-X` and an explicit approval. Checkpoint revision 4 records risk `R7=high`, evidence h1, decision `hold`, required action `inspect call sites`. No promote/prune or publish occurs. New node `inspect-call-sites` is added as revision 5, depending on `scan`; only its validated output can resolve R7. If output fails/missing, remain held; if evidence proves the compatibility break is isolated and tests cover it, the human/policy gate decides whether to revise the patch and then the approval edge still blocks publish. This is an auditable state-transition example, not an empirical claim about risk scores.

### `work-intake-node-shaping`: deterministic consistency audit plus manual evidence trace

The seven labels and “Single Operator Action” rule come from the first-party control-plane synthesis cited by `references/seven-archetypes.md`, not a universal topology taxonomy. Preserve its order: capture one WorkIntent; rate coupling, context pressure, skill boundary, review independence, budget and operator burden with reasons; select exactly one local archetype; for every reachable legacy verb trace the call site to prove it writes no separate node/session/transcript; then audit the JSON. The seven-archetype mapping itself is judgment-only: the script does not score/validate the six signals, the schema accepts arbitrary signal strings, and an asserted boolean `writesIndependentState:false` is not call-site proof. It audits the supplied assertion.

Script result is exactly hand-checkable: selected archetypes must be cardinality 1 and among seven; unknown archetype, 0/multiple, or any listed route with `writesIndependentState:true` adds critical 30-point findings; unknown legacy verb adds medium 10; score is `max(0,100−sum(weights))`; pass iff no critical and score≥75. Thus a lone unknown legacy verb can still pass at 90—contrary to “fail closed on any critical finding” only if unknown alias is supposed to be critical. Decide and document whether it is intentionally reviewable or a hard failure. The script detects only five canonical verbs and flags other names as medium; traces of actual state writes remain external evidence.

Hand-check: sample input has one valid `node` and `{verb:"spawn", writesIndependentState:false}`; 0 findings → score 100/pass. Bad example has two selected entries, one unknown archetype, one true legacy-write route (three criticals, 90 weighted points), plus unknown `summon` (10), total 100 → score 0/fail. This reproduces the checked expected output. Do not infer from passing JSON that the actual spawn code shares the WorkPlan path. Require call chain and persisted-state evidence in a real product audit.
