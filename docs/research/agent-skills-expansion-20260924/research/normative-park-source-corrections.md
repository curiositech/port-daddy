## Normative BDI publication identity and source boundary

Three related works must be cited separately; title and DOI in the current `normative-bdi-agents/_book_identity.json` do not match. The DOI `10.1007/978-3-319-21548-8_7` is **not** “A Normative Extension for the BDI Agent Model.” Springer’s [book page](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-319-21548-8) lists the 2015 chapter as Mihnea Tufiș and Jean-Gabriel Ganascia, “Grafting Norms onto the BDI Agent Model,” *A Construction Manual for Robots’ Ethical Systems*, pp. 119–133; the author-uploaded [ResearchGate full text](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/286854510_Grafting_Norms_onto_the_BDI_Agent_Model) exposes the body, and was inspected at §§7.2–7.7 (pp. 123–133). It explicitly says the consistency definition is their own interpretation, calls norm acquisition and coherence checks limitations/future work, and describes the implementation’s received norms as external ACL input. This is a proposed architecture plus a scenario and prototype, not an empirical demonstration of ethical correctness.

The earlier **2012** workshop paper is Tufiș and Ganascia, “Normative rational agents – A BDI approach,” in the *Proceedings of the 1st Workshop on Rights and Duties of Autonomous Agents (RDA2 2012)*, printed pp. 37–43 (proceedings contents list the contribution at pp. 37–43; PDF text begins printed p. 37). The official [CEUR workshop record](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-885/) and [full proceedings PDF](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-885/RDA2-2012-Proceedings.pdf) were opened; the paper body at printed pp. 37–43 was inspected, including §§2–4, consistency definitions, BDI loop, norm recognition/instantiation/internalization, example, implementation, and future-work discussion. The author/hosted [standalone PDF](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-885/paper6.pdf) is also indexed. Its abstract expressly calls evaluation difficult and uses a science-fiction scenario for intuition.

The **2014** work is Tufiș and Ganascia, “A Normative Extension for the BDI Agent Model,” in *Mobile Service Robotics: Proceedings of the 17th International Conference on Climbing and Walking Robots and the Support Technologies for Mobile Machines* (CLAWAR 2014), pp. 691–702 per the authors’ institutional [LIP6 conference publication record](https://www.lip6.fr/production/publications-type.php?LANG=en&annee=2014&id=-1&type_pub=COL); DOI `10.1142/9789814623353_0080` appears on the indexed publication record. Publisher full body was not accessed in this pass. Do not call 2012 or 2015 the 2014 proceedings item, and do not assume line-by-line identity among iterations. The 2012 and 2015 bodies are available and similar, but source each claim to the version actually read.

**What to preserve from the primary method.** The source distinguishes norm recognition from adherence. The abstract norm base (ANB) stores detected in-force norms but “has not yet adhered” to them; the norm instance base (NIB) stores instantiated active norms, and the proposal later internalizes consistent obligations/prohibitions by changing the desire set. The 2012 version simplifies acquisition to norms received by messages from a trusted normative authority. The 2015 implementation likewise says it receives external ACL messages and flags norm acquisition/coherence as future work. So ANB is neither a verified truth store nor a certificate of issuer authority. Keep source/provenance, validity interval, activation/expiration, scope, and the separate local deliberation step. A local “adoption” or desire update is not authorization to execute an external effect, nor a decision about legal/ethical validity.

**Consistency definition: exact meaning and correction.** In 2012 §3.2.1, Definition 9, and the matching 2015 §7.3.2, Definition 8, plan/NIB consistency is described using plan effects, active prohibition effects (with permissions as exceptions), and obligation effects versus negated plan effects. The PDF equation is image/character-encoding degraded, so cite the prose and cautiously transcribe the intended set relation; do not silently present a guessed typeset formula as a verbatim equation. This predicate concerns whether one plan’s declared effects conflict with normative effects. It is not a general plan-plan overlap test.

The source’s obligation classification is quantified over plan instantiations satisfying an obligation `o`:

- strong inconsistency: every satisfying plan is prohibited or makes the agent inconsistent with NIB;
- strong consistency: every satisfying plan is permitted and keeps consistency;
- weak consistency: at least one satisfying plan is permitted and consistent.

As written, `strong consistency = ∀ p ∈ P_o: safe(p)` and `weak consistency = ∃ p ∈ P_o: safe(p)` overlap whenever `P_o` is nonempty and every plan is safe. Further, the universal predicate is vacuously true when `P_o = ∅`, while the existential is false; this can mislabel “no capable plan” as strong consistency. With an incomplete plan search, observing only safe plans cannot justify the universal. Repair the *operational classification*, labeling it a disambiguating completion rather than falsely attributing this refinement to the paper:

1. First distinguish `P_o=∅` (no known satisfying plan) from consistency; return `unknown/no-capability` if search is incomplete, `inconsistent` only if plan library/search is declared complete and has no satisfying plan.
2. If plan enumeration is incomplete, return `undetermined` unless a concrete counterexample plan is already found.
3. Given a complete nonempty `P_o`, strong consistency means all plans safe; weak consistency means at least one safe and at least one unsafe; strong inconsistency means none safe.
4. Define `safe(p)` with an explicit effect model and NIB rule. Mark unknown/missing side effects as unknown, not safe.

**Positive/negative hand checks.** Existing intentions `i: done` and new plan `p: done` share the same positive effect. Under the source’s intended “plan effects do not negate current intention-plan effects” relation, identical positive effects do not conflict. If the new plan produces `¬done`, it conflicts. By contrast, if an active prohibition forbids `send(data)`, a plan with positive effect `send(data)` is inconsistent unless a genuinely applicable permission exception exists; a different plan also producing `send(data)` does not become inconsistent merely because both plans share that effect. This is why pairwise `effects(p_i) ∩ effects(p_j)=∅` is wrong as a generic intention conflict rule: conflict concerns complementary/forbidden effects, not equality of harmless effects. Model positive and negative literals separately, and specify whether effects are add/delete, state predicates, or actions before using set intersection.

The draft consistency reference should remove its “strong consistency → immediate adoption” instruction. Even a complete, strongly consistent result says only that the modeled candidate plans meet the model’s NIB predicate; source validity, authority, scope, hard constraints, capability reality, and external effect permission remain distinct checks. The current Mermaid branch taking “compatible” directly to adoption is unsafe/inconsistent with its own prose: route first through provenance/scope/policy and then to local deliberation; route every effect to a separate effect-admission authority.

**Worst-consequence ordering and maximal subsets.** The 2012 §2.3 definitions and 2015 §7.2.4 / §§7.3–7.4 use a belief-set consequence relation and a *partial* comparative relation that explicitly permits incomparable outcomes. It is not a scalar `rank()`, an expected utility, or a total “minimax” order. For each selected maximal non-conflicting subset, trace derivable consequences under stated beliefs; compare only where the source’s partial order establishes comparison. If the worst consequence of one alternative is incomparable with another, the source does not provide a universal tie-break or authorize pretending there is one. Preserve alternatives and ask for additional evidence or an explicit applicable tie policy. Consequence sets are only as complete as the causal rules in beliefs; the 2015 authors themselves leave coherence/norm acquisition as future work.

A simple hand-check fixture: choices `plan_A -> {injury, cost}` and `plan_B -> {delay, cost}`; beliefs say `injury` worse than `cost`, `delay` worse than `cost`, but do not compare injury and delay. The method should expose incomparability rather than choose one via arbitrary numeric ranks. A separate fully ordered fixture can demonstrate a choice, but must label ordering as an input assumption.

**Diagrams.** (1) Keep distinct swimlanes `authority/provenance → ANB (recognized) → activation/binding → NIB candidate → consistency/search result → local deliberation → BDI desire/intention`, and a separate effect-authorization lane. (2) Truth table/state diagram for complete nonempty plan search: `all safe / mixed / none safe`, plus explicit `empty` and `incomplete` states returning no classification. Show positive duplicate effects and contradictory effects as different edges.

## Park et al. 2023 generative agents: methods versus overclaim

**Primary source and access.** Park, O’Brien, Cai, Morris, Liang & Bernstein, [“Generative Agents: Interactive Simulacra of Human Behavior,” arXiv:2304.03442v2 (2023-08-06)](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.03442v2), full HTML opened 2026-09-24. I read §§3–8 and Appendix/method passages on memory, reflection, planning/reacting, evaluation, emergent behavior and failure. The published paper record is UIST ’23, DOI [10.1145/3586183.3606763](https://doi.org/10.1145/3586183.3606763). Version-pinned arXiv body is used below; avoid latest-unpinned `/abs/` as if same version.

**Exact retrieval method and constants.** §4.1 describes memory objects with text, creation timestamp and most recent access time. Recency is exponential decay in *sandbox game hours since last retrieval*, factor `0.995`; importance is an LLM-generated integer 1–10 at memory creation; relevance is cosine similarity between LLM-generated embeddings for memory description and query memory. They min-max normalize recency/relevance/importance to [0,1], sum them with each alpha equal to 1, and include top-ranked memories that fit the context window. Those are paper implementation settings, not universal human-memory constants. Since the live port-daddy repo requires hybrid lexical+dense retrieval, exact immutable embedding-space identity, and authority filtering before ranking, describe cosine-only retrieval as Park’s source design and do not adopt it as current product retrieval guidance.

§4.2 triggers reflection when summed importance of recent events exceeds 150 in that implementation; agents reflected about 2–3 times per simulation day. For reflection-question generation the prompt sees the 100 most recent records; generated questions retrieve supporting records, and the model is asked to output insights with record citations. These are simulation settings; fixture-defined local values should be labeled as such. The references currently present these values as timeless constants or claim reflections “enable generalization” universally; scope to this experiment and mark evidence citations as lineage pointers, not validation that an inferred statement is true.

§4.3 plan construction is top-down: broad daily plans, recursive decomposition for near-future detail, then moment-to-moment action selection and reactions can regenerate a plan from current time. Preserve that mechanism. Avoid “mirrors how humans actually think” claims. The paper studies a Smallville simulated world, not a cognitive model validated against people’s planning processes.

**Evaluation and correct ablation claim.** The paper has (a) controlled believability interviews after a two-game-day simulation, with 100 human raters in a within-subject design comparing four agent architectures plus a human-authored condition; and (b) an end-to-end 25-agent, two-full-game-day interaction run measuring information spread, relationship formation, and coordination. The paper’s Valentine's party is Isabella Rodriguez’s; the scenario runs over the two simulated days (not a three-day paper experiment). The source describes Isabella as a café owner/community-oriented seed character, not an “artist” as the draft’s example asserts. Paper results say 12 other agents heard about the party by simulation end; five of 12 invited agents attended (check the paper’s specific metric wording before repeating). Replace draft’s artist/three-day/date narrative with either the paper’s actual two-day scenario or a separately named, clearly labeled local fixture that does not borrow source character identity while inventing events.

The controlled test ranks full architecture above ablations for *believability* in this setup. The full architecture’s reported TrueSkill μ=29.89, no-reflection μ=26.88, no-reflection/no-planning μ=25.64, and fully ablated μ=21.21 are not proof that reflection is necessary for generalization or human-like reasoning. The experiment does not isolate every retrieval factor: ablations remove access to observations, plans, reflections in combinations; it does not show recency-only or importance-only systems fail, nor establish the necessity of equal factor weights. It measures perceived believability of sampled interview responses on accumulated memory; raters could see memory stream. The paper explicitly warns that ablated agents shared the full architecture’s accrued memories for comparability, so this is a controlled response comparison, not a rerun of diverging life trajectories. Use the experiment’s outcome and limits together.
