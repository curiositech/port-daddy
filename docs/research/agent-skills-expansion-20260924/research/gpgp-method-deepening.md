## Corrected method model

GPGP is an extensible family of scheduling-coordination mechanisms over TÆMS task structures, not a central planner that chooses agents’ actions. The coordination module observes each agent’s subjective task view, communicates selected structure/results, proposes and tracks social commitments, invokes the existing local scheduler, and chooses among the schedules/alternatives that scheduler returns. The report’s local-scheduler contract is intentionally weak: it consumes subjective task structure plus local and non-local commitments, and returns schedules, estimated utility, violated commitments and possible alternatives. Stronger guarantees require stronger scheduler properties. Even a satisfier can report a violation when it does not exhaustively search (TR §§2–2.2, 3.1).

The report’s initial five mechanisms are: (1) update non-local viewpoints; (2) communicate results; (3) handle **simple method redundancy**; (4) handle hard predecessor relationships; and (5) handle soft predecessor relationships. It says the five are independently parameterizable, but a common substrate must arbitrate their constraints; incompatible constraints or a heuristic scheduler can leave unsatisfied commitments. The later load-balancing/meta-level communication analysis is an additional mechanism discussed separately, not one of those initial five (TR §§3.1–3.6, 4.5).

| Another agent needs a result | **M2 results:** minimal sends results needed to satisfy commitments; TG adds final task-group results; all sends all results. Commitment-related result goes to its recipient; extra TG/all result traffic is broadcast (TR §3.3). | Distinguish task-structure/viewpoint exchange (M1) from result exchange (M2); cost and audience differ. |
| Same executable method can produce the same result for multiple agents | **M3 simple redundancy:** simultaneous candidate commitments are compared; a deterministic shared tie-break (implementation: smallest unique random method ID) keeps one, other agents retract; the chosen executor sends the result (TR §3.4). | Not general alternative-task allocation or load balancing; report explicitly says it does not load-balance. Reliable channels and agents following the same choice rule are assumed for omitting a second retraction message. |

### Quality accumulation and redundancy correction

The skill and older reference material incorrectly describe the simple-redundancy parent as using `min`; the report says the opposite. TR §3.4: a task with **MAX** quality accumulation (one possible OR semantics) can need only one subtask when no other relationships intervene. Its simple redundancy mechanism is for exact same-method/same-result duplication. A **MIN** parent is an AND-like composition in which the bottleneck/each required subtask matters; a single successful duplicate must not be treated as satisfying every required child.

Hand-check: two identical methods `mA` and `mB` each yield quality 0.8; `QAF_MAX(mA,mB)=max(0.8,0.8)=0.8`, so if methods are truly equivalent and no cross-effects apply, execute one and communicate its result. For `QAF_MIN`, `min(0.8,0)=0` when the other required child is absent; a single method does not satisfy the parent. This fixture demonstrates the distinction only; it is not a universal redundancy algorithm. In a complex MAX hierarchy, the authors explicitly defer general multi-agent redundancy avoidance to other work.

### Mechanism selection is about relationships, not invented “power” bands

The W skill’s primary decision tree wrongly equates hinders with a GPGP deconfliction protocol, uses arbitrary “power factor” thresholds, and conflates redundancy with any agents achieving a common goal. The report’s direct crosswalk is narrower: enables → hard predecessor mechanism; positive facilitates → soft predecessor mechanism; exact duplicate method under relevant MAX structure → simple redundancy; hinders → not handled by the described five, possible future mechanism; private related structure/result need → select M1/M2 policies. The relationship detector itself may be domain-specific, while the representation and response mechanisms are domain-independent.

For facilitation, the report describes two effects: executing predecessor before successor can reduce successor duration by an effect related to `φd` and increase its maximum quality by an effect related to `φq`. Do not convert “power” into a generic scalar ranking or invent cutoffs. A constructed fixture matching the paper’s described example: let a successor method, absent help, take 10 time units and allow quality 100; a predecessor’s facilitation is modeled in this fixture as duration 5 and maximum quality 150 after predecessor completion. If successor deadline is 9, scheduling the predecessor first makes the higher-quality route feasible; if the predecessor itself is late or costs more than the gained value, a local scheduler may prefer the baseline. These numbers are fixture choices matching the paper’s example of half the time / 1.5× quality, not a universal φ formula.

For hinders, model the shared resource/negative effect explicitly and either use a separately specified application scheduler/protocol or mark the gap. Do not cite the report’s five mechanisms as evidence for a hinders solution. A valid independent design needs its own conflict/resource semantics, safety boundary, and tests.

## Views, commitments, negotiation and replanning

Each agent’s beliefs are subjective and can be incomplete or inconsistent with the objective task graph. M1’s `detect-coordination-relationships` can discover that a private task is related to an otherwise unknown task and share the relationship plus task/context; it is domain-specific work with a modeled cost (TR §3.2). An agreement between views proves only shared exchanged data, not common knowledge of the global graph.

Commitments constrain a local scheduler and are **social/directed**. The paper distinguishes `C(Do(T,q))` (achieve at least quality `q`) from `C(DL(T,q,tdl))` (achieve quality `q` by deadline `tdl`); it states that sending a commitment also implies communicating its result to the recipient (TR §2.2). Its Figure 2 example adjusts a local deadline for communication delay: a locally proposed completion by 7 is represented to the recipient with a later deadline 8 under a one-time-unit delay. This is an explicit assumption/example, not a universal transport bound.

Negotiability is a categorical **high / medium / low heuristic** for how hard it would be to reschedule if a commitment is broken (TR §§3.1, 3.4–3.6), not a continuous 0–1 criticality score, promise-strength, permission, or priority. The schedule substrate first compares estimated utility and commitments; when utility ties, it prefers the schedule with more negotiable commitments; after selecting a schedule it replaces violated commitments with scheduler-proposed alternatives and informs other recipients (TR §3.1). Preserve the ability to produce a lower-quality/later alternative or `NIL` when no feasible alternative exists. Do not equate “renegotiable” with silently mutable: the other party must receive the changed commitment.

**Constructed hard-dependency fixture:** agent A has a predecessor method that can produce quality 40 by local time 7; agent B has a successor requiring that quality first, and message delay is assumed 1. A queries its local scheduler for an earliest feasible commitment and communicates `C(DL(predecessor,40,8))` to B. B may schedule the successor after 8. If A’s local scheduler later returns a higher-utility schedule that violates this commitment, the coordination substrate compares alternatives; it may send a revised deadline/quality or no alternative, and B must see the update. This fixture assumes known delay, compatible clocks and cooperative/truthful agents; it is not a guarantee of execution.

## Result/view policies, cost and quiescence

**Constructed policy fixture:** A knows local task `x`; B knows task `y`, and only B knows the `facilitates(y,x)` edge. With M1 `none`, A never learns that relation. M1 `some` runs a detector and shares only a discovered cross-agent relation plus relevant task/context. After execution, M2 `minimal` sends only results owed under commitments; `TG` also sends final group result; `all` broadcasts all results. The choice should be based on what recipients need to schedule and the communication cost, not one universal “share if value >10%” rule.

The report counts coordination overhead from (a) communication and information-gathering action executions, (b) calls to the local scheduler, and (c) the mechanisms’ own algorithmic computation. It relates these to task/method count, relationship density, overlap and commitments (TR §4.1 and Table 2). Extracted text exposed the categories and Table 2 headers, but not legible symbolic coefficients; do not repeat inherited asymptotic formulas or numeric costs as verified from this report. The useful method is to baseline these dimensions per mechanism and measure them alongside quality, method executions, deadline misses and termination time.

**Constructed quiescence fixture:** A is idle but has an outstanding commitment to B and expects B’s result; A is not locally quiescent. Once A has no enabled work, no outstanding commitments, and no expected communications, it may locally stop processing that task group under the report’s substrate rule (TR §3.1). This is a local state condition, not proof every agent globally terminated, network messages cannot arrive later, or a failed peer is dead. A distributed system must define how pending deliveries/late messages and the observation horizon affect a global termination claim.

Quiescence is tied to idleness, no expected communications, and no outstanding commitments; it is not “no recent messages.” Avoid borrowing consensus-style global termination guarantees that the report does not supply. The local view/result/commitment accounting enables the paper’s local termination rule under its implementation assumptions.

## Evaluation evidence and limits

The report’s approach was implemented in the TÆMS simulation testbed with the Design-to-Time local scheduler; experiments use randomly generated abstract episodes, not production services or LLM agents (TR §§1, 2.1, 4.1). In the 300 paired comparisons in §4.2, the balanced GPGP configuration had all mechanisms on with partial non-local views and committed/TG results; “simple” had mechanisms off, no non-local view and broadcast all results; “parallel” was a centralized reference with complete global view and heuristic parallelization of an optimal single-agent schedule. Episodes were kept artificially small to make the centralized reference tractable; the reference omitted the cost of obtaining its global view and allowed no dynamic task-structure change. It is therefore an upper-bound comparison, not an apples-to-apples deployable alternative.

Reported aggregate results are conditional: centralized parallel achieved higher total quality in 57% of pairs, tied 36%, and distributed GPGP was better in 7%; GPGP averaged 85% of centralized quality. Balanced beat the “simple” configuration in total quality in 21% of pairs vs simple’s 8%, with 71% tied; it improved method execution time in 72% of pairs, while deadline misses were the same in 96%. Termination differences within two time units counted as “same” because balanced had fixed 2-unit startup cost. These values must be accompanied by the setup and criteria above (TR §4.2, Tables 3–4); they do not prove GPGP always improves quality or deadlines.

The soft-mechanism comparison used 234 generated episodes with four agents, paired with and without mechanism 5; 102 non-tied blocks entered the reported Wilcoxon signed-rank analysis (TR §4.3). The family-member comparison varied MIN-vs-MAX QAF and enables probability over five levels, with no time pressure and four-agent/five-task-group setup (TR §4.4). The resulting selection lesson is environmental interaction: coordination configurations can differ by task-structure distribution; the result is not an agent-count or power threshold. The experiment’s significance statement is author-reported; exact extracted statistic text was garbled, so no precise p/Z value is repeated here.

## Relation to current W references and held stubs

All seven W reference topics remain useful, but the former long versions contain invented quantitative thresholds, misattributed guarantees, and uncited cross-domain extrapolation. The B04 13-line stubs correctly block unsupported assertions but lost nearly all source method detail. Restore the methods above, then preserve these distinctions:

- `commitments-as-social-contracts`: restore formal `Do`/deadline forms, local/non-local state, result implication, alternative/violation path, and high/medium/low scheduling heuristic. Avoid claims of enforceable sanctions, generic accountability, or GPGP mechanism verification.
- `coordination-as-constraint-posting-not-control`: retain the local-scheduler/coordination-module interface and utility-vs-commitment schedule selection. Do not claim GPGP schedules or commands other agents.
- `no-universal-coordination-mechanism`: support “family, not one best configuration” with the five mechanisms and §4 evidence; do not assert all mechanisms independently help or are mutually conflict-free. The common substrate can expose conflicting constraints; local satisfice may report violation without proving inconsistency.
- `overhead-as-first-class-design-concern`: restore three measurable categories and relation to episode/task structure. Mark Table 2 coefficients inaccessible from current extraction; remove old O(…) equations unless separately sourced and checked.
- `subjective-views-and-partial-information`: restore exact M1 none/some/all policy and domain-specific relation detector. Do not imply subjective views converge to a complete global model.
- `task-decomposition-through-relationship-types`: restore task-group/task/method hierarchy and QAF semantics; correct redundancy MAX/OR vs MIN/AND. Include enables/facilitates mechanisms and mark hinders extension as future work.
- `termination-and-quiescence-in-distributed-coordination`: restore the report’s local stop predicate only, and clearly separate local termination from global distributed termination/delivery guarantees.
