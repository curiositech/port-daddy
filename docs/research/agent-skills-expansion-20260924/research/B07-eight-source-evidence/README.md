## Additional opened sources

- Capability ranker: Bruch, Gai & Ingber, [“An Analysis of Fusion Functions for Hybrid Retrieval”](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.11934), full body sections Introduction, experimental comparisons, and Discussion inspected. They report parameter sensitivity in RRF and their tested convex fusion outperformed RRF on their in-/out-of-domain datasets. This is contrary to universal “RRF is best” readings; results are benchmark- and method-specific.
- Cycle analysis: Vasiliauskaite, Evans & Expert, [“Cycle analysis of Directed Acyclic Graphs”](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10154887/1/1-s2.0-S0378437122001340-main.pdf), full 22-page paper opened; Sections 2.1–2.5 and stability results inspected, including the exact F_dir/F_undir mapping, source/sink/neutral definition, generalized cycle taxonomy and Table 1, edge-wedge contraction, undirected cycle-space/MCB definition, De Pina fundamental basis choice, TR effect and repeated-run stability study. Their method maps an undirected MCB plus DAG metadata to directed cycle images, and experiments use named generated graph families (N=300/500, 10 runs or 20 realizations depending on experiment). They report MCB itself can be non-unique/run-dependent; reduction may stabilize measured descriptors for tested models. This supports retaining the technique with its assumptions and scope visible, not stating cycle-basis uniqueness or universal functional semantics.
- Cycle and dependency implementation references: [NetworkX 3.7 DAG reference](https://networkx.org/documentation/stable/reference/algorithms/dag.html), full short reference page inspected; it warns most routines require a DAG and often do not check acyclicity, and lists topological sorts/generations, antichains, reachability and transitive reduction.


## Confidence method body inspected

- Gneiting & Raftery (2007) full 20-page author-hosted PDF: Section 1 distinguishes calibration (joint property of forecast and outcomes) from sharpness; Section 2 states propriety/strict propriety; Section 3 includes the quadratic/Brier score and its expected-score/divergence interpretation; later sections contain scoring-rule applications/case study. The hand calculation in the companion uses binary Brier loss `p²` when `y=0` and `(1-p)²` when `y=1`, equivalently `(p-y)²` up to the paper's score-vs-loss sign convention.
- Dimitriadis, Gneiting & Jordan (2020) full body: Section 1 defines calibration as matched predicted and observed conditional event frequencies; Sections 3–4 describe PAV/isotonic CORP reliability estimation and resampling/asymptotic uncertainty; Section 5 gives score decomposition. This supports an evaluation procedure, not a sample-size threshold.
- Prospective probability forecasts remain possible when calibration is unavailable; their status must say uncalibrated. Proper-score evaluation happens after the specified event resolves.
