## Steelman (category theory's strongest framing of this layer)

Read charitably, L3 is the one rung of the stack that earns the manifesto's olog/functor rhetoric **literally rather than decoratively** — and it does so on the single concept a category theorist would pick out as load-bearing: **conservation as a preserved invariant under a structure-preserving map.**

The strong reading is this. Each harbor is an object carrying internal structure — a finite category whose objects are `{wallet, escrow, commons}` and whose morphisms are the legal money-flows (debit, escrow, slash, settle, refund). The conservation law `wallet + escrow + commons = supply` is not a side-fact; it is a **commuting-diagram constraint** in exactly Spivak's sense — every path of settlement morphisms from a starting balance to a terminal state composes to the same total. The four terminal settlement states (success / partial / sabotage / dispute) are four morphisms out of the escrow object that must all commute with the conservation functional. This is precisely the olog discipline the `OLOG-LIBRARY-PROGRAM.md` demands: arrows are functional (each settlement has exactly one outcome), and the diagram commutes (no path leaks value).

Federation is then the genuinely categorical move. The dossier's §4 invariant — **"conservation composes upward... federation adds only an in-escrow accounting term for in-flight bonds"** — is, stated in my field's terms, the claim that there exists a **functor F: Harbor → Federation** that is *conservative* (reflects/preserves the conservation invariant), and that the cross-harbor transfer is a morphism in the target category whose existence does not break the source-category invariant. The honesty clause "any L3 design that needs a second ledger has broken the contract" is the correct functorial intuition: a second ledger would mean the supposed functor is not preserving the monoidal/accounting structure, i.e., it is not a functor at all but an unrelated diagram glued on. The "bounded escrow, extraction ≤ φ" theorem (federated-harbor) is the right shape too: it bounds how far the cross-boundary morphism can deviate from the identity-on-value, which is exactly what you want from a lax/approximate structure-preserving map when an exact one (trustless HTLC) is proven impossible.

And the three-sided market is, charitably, **three different decorations of one underlying category**: labor, capital-rental, and IP-licensing are three functors *into* the same bond-ledger category, all factoring through the single `FloatPlan → escrow` morphism. That is real shared structure, not analogy — it is why "one float-plan escrow → global conservation without a second ledger" is a *theorem-shaped* claim and not a slogan. The Grossman–Hart residual-control-right mapping (jail = the un-contractible residual) and the Akerlof lemons → metered+clawback mapping are the kind of cross-domain functor the `OLOG-LIBRARY-PROGRAM`'s "Verification Chains (K~20)" and "State Machines with Rollback (K~25)" equivalence classes are built to exploit. So at its best, L3 is the layer where the manifesto's central bet — *find the preserved invariant, transport the solution* — actually has a preserved invariant (conservation) and an actual transport (the federation functor). That is the strongest version, and it is genuinely strong.

## Gaps & inconsistencies (prioritized)

**1. (Fatal to the headline claim) "Conservation composes upward" is asserted as functoriality but is exactly what the open problems admit is unproven — and the dossier ships it as the layer's single consistency invariant.** A functor must preserve composition: `F(g∘f) = F(g)∘F(f)`. The §4 invariant claims the federation map preserves conservation. But §3 Open Problem #3 ("trustless cross-harbor settlement... **conjectured impossible**") and #9 ("**cross-harbor unit-of-account**... multi-currency settlement breaks the clean invariant") are precisely the statement that **F is not a functor in the multi-currency / non-fungible case** — the diagram does *not* commute once harbors price bonds in heterogeneous units. You cannot simultaneously (a) make "conservation composes upward" the load-bearing consistency invariant of the whole layer and (b) list "does conservation survive cross-harbor at all" as an open problem. In categorical terms: the dossier proves conservation is preserved *within one unit of account* (a functor on the subcategory of single-currency harbors) and then quietly extends the claim to the full category where it is, by its own admission, at best a **lax functor** (preserved only up to the bounded-escrow slack φ) and possibly not structure-preserving at all. The honest formulation is: *single-currency federation is a conservative functor (proven); multi-currency federation is at most lax and the coherence conditions are unproven.* The dossier states the strong version in §4 and the weak version in §3 without reconciling them. **This is the central inconsistency and it sits on the layer's only named invariant.**

**2. The three-sided "shape" is asserted to be one structure but the dossier never exhibits the structure-preserving map — so by the manifesto's own validation rule it's a partial functor at best, and that's never scored.** The `OLOG-LIBRARY-PROGRAM` is explicit (line 127): "If some arrows map and others don't, you have a **partial functor**... it tells you which parts of the structure are shared and where the domains diverge." The dossier claims all three sides "ride one float-plan escrow." Fine — but a category theorist demands the **type-by-type and arrow-by-arrow mapping table** (the program's Exercise 3 deliverable) showing that labor/rental/licensing map onto the *same* objects and morphisms. They demonstrably do **not** map cleanly: the **reputation-bearing entity differs per side** (Gap #1: principal for labor, asset-owner for rental, code-content-hash for licensing — Gap #10). Three different "what does reputation key on" objects means three different functors with three different source objects, i.e. *not* one structure with three decorations but three partial functors into the ledger that **disagree on the identity object**. The manifesto's own framework would force you to publish `coverage` and `fidelity` numbers here and admit the fidelity is <1. The dossier instead uses the prose "a three-sided market by design" — which is exactly the "decorative analogy" the review brief warns against. The shared structure is real *only at the conservation layer*; at the identity/reputation layer the three sides are not isomorphic and the dossier never says so.

**3. Reputation is claimed to be "multi-dimensional" (accuracy/aesthetics/efficiency) with no aggregation rule — categorically, that is a functor into a product object with no specified universal property, which is incoherent.** Gap #2 and Open Problem #7 correctly flag that vector reputation has no combination rule. In category terms: if reputation is a map `R: Outcomes → Accuracy × Aesthetics × Efficiency`, then **the product structure is doing real work** — a product comes with projections and a universal property (any other cone factors uniquely through it). The dossier wants the projections (three separate neutral judges) but rejects the product's universal property (any single buyer-readable score), then notices in §3 that without it you either re-introduce a scalar (collapsing the product) or let buyers Goodhart the most-weighted axis (the product has no canonical map to ℝ). This is not a missing feature; it is a **category error in the reputation model itself**: you cannot have "separate axes judged by separate experts" *and* "a profile a federated buyer reads" unless you specify the cone. The honest state table rates this VISION, which is correct — but the §1.C *framing* presents multi-dimensionality as a designed primitive when it is an unspecified product.

**4. Reputation revocation (Gap #4 / Open Problem #8) breaks the append-only ledger's categorical model and the dossier reaches for Sagas without noticing the type mismatch.** The bond ledger's conservation is provable *because* it is append-only — it is a free monoid on settlement events, and the invariant is a monoid homomorphism to (ℤ, +) that is trivially preserved by concatenation. Reputation-claim revocation requires *retracting* a settled outcome. The dossier cites Garcia-Molina & Salem Sagas (compensating transactions) as the fix. But Sagas compensate **financial/effectful** transactions where a compensating entry restores the *same* invariant (money returns). A fraudulent *reputation* outcome has no conserved quantity to restore — reputation is not a monoid under a conserved total; a tombstone does not "give back" the trust spent during the propagation window. So the Sagas functor that works beautifully for the bond ledger **does not transport** to the reputation ledger, because the target lacks the conserved structure the source relied on. This is a textbook case of the manifesto's own failure mode: assuming a functor exists because two things look like ledgers, when the structure-preserving map only exists for one of them. The dossier files this as an open problem, which is honest, but it should explicitly state *why* the Sagas analogy fails (no conserved quantity), not list Sagas in the prior-art table as if it solves it.

**5. Consistency-with-the-stack: L3 keys everything on non-forgeable identity (ADR-0040, DESIGNED), but the L0 conservation proof and the L3 reputation functor have different domains, and the dossier elides the gap.** The conservation invariant is proven over **bonds**, which are keyed on whatever escrows the float plan. The reputation functor must key on the **principal** (Gap #1's own correct observation: Sybil bond-farming defeats agent-keyed reputation). But the BUILT conservation property test (`bonds-conservation-property.test.js`) conserves over an abstract `supply` with no principal structure at all. So the "spine" the dossier names — *non-forgeable identity → outcome ledger → reputation* — is a chain of three objects where **only the last arrow's source is even defined**, and the conservation proof everyone leans on lives one level below the identity it supposedly composes with. The dossier's own honest-state table (rows 2 BUILT vs. 8 DESIGNED vs. 1 the principal-as-economic-entity VISION) makes this visible if you cross-read it, but the §4 "conservation composes upward" sentence papers over the fact that *the thing it composes through (identity) is the one object in the chain that does not yet exist.*

**6. Minor but real: the dossier never engages the actual categorical tools the canon ships.** `OLOG-LIBRARY-PROGRAM.md` §5 names CQL (categoricaldata.net) and Catlab.jl as the verification path, and the manifesto cites Spivak (2012) by name. Federation is a textbook **sheaf-theoretic / colimit** problem (local sections = per-harbor ledgers, global section = federated state, the gluing condition = cross-harbor conservation). The dossier proves cross-harbor conservation in TLA+ and ProVerif (operational/cryptographic tools) but never reaches for the compositional tool the manifesto itself endorses. An expert would note that "does conservation glue across harbors" is *literally* the question of whether the assignment `harbor ↦ its ledger` forms a **sheaf** (or fails to, becoming only a presheaf with a non-trivial obstruction in H¹ — which is exactly where equivocation/double-spend lives). Open Problems #3, #4, #9 are all the *same* gluing-obstruction question in disguise, and the dossier never unifies them because it isn't using the categorical lens it claims as a foundation.

## What an expert would add

- **State the federation map as a (lax) functor explicitly, with its coherence conditions.** Define the source category (per-harbor ledgers + settlement morphisms), the target (federated state), and prove `F` preserves composition of settlements. Where it's only lax (multi-currency, non-fungible bonds), write the **coherence 2-cells** and bound them by φ. This converts "conservation composes upward" from a slogan into the layer's actual central theorem and immediately exposes which open problems are coherence-failures vs. genuine impossibilities.

Cite Spivak & Kent's *Ologs* (2012) and the applied-category-theory sheaf literature (Fong & Spivak, *Seven Sketches in Compositionality*, 2019; Robinson's sheaf-theoretic data fusion). The double-spend/equivocation race (#4) is the **non-vanishing of the first cohomology of the gluing**; that's the precise statement, and it tells you exactly what a defense must kill.

- **Score the three-sided "functor" honestly.** Produce the Exercise-3 mapping table for labor/rental/licensing → bond-ledger, publish `coverage`/`fidelity`, and admit it's a *partial* functor that agrees on the conservation object and *diverges on the identity object*. - **Fix the reputation product or drop "multi-dimensional."** Either specify the cone (the buyer-supplied weight vector *is* the universal map ℝ³→ℝ, and the routing seed already has the preference vector — so use it and own the Goodhart consequence), or commit to a single quality dimension plus orthogonal adversarial QA. Categorically you cannot have the product without choosing how it maps out.

This turns Gap #4 from "missing mechanism" into "structural impossibility result," which is more honest and more useful.

- **Engage Resnick & Zeckhauser (2006) and Tadelis (2016) on the *direction* of the reputation signal, not just its price.** The dossier cites the 8% eBay premium but the deeper result (reputation systems measure *seller* behavior precisely because buyers can't be trusted to report) bears directly on Gap #3 (judge-capture) and Gap #12 (wash-trading). The relevant categorical object is the **two-sided feedback graph and its automorphisms** — collusion is an automorphism that fixes the conserved total while permuting reputation. That framing makes the wash-trade defense (cost-per-settled-outcome > marginal reputation value) a statement about *breaking a symmetry*, which is sharper than the listing-fee hand-wave.

Until they land, the layer is making a functor-strength claim on lax-functor evidence — exactly the grand claim a category theorist is obligated to default to skepticism on.
