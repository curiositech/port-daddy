## Steelman (information design / read-poverty framing)

At its strongest, this dossier makes a claim my field has been circling for two decades but rarely states cleanly: **discovery and legibility are the same operation pointed at different readers, and both are read-side indexing problems disguised as UI problems.** The §2 gap #10 unification — that the Attention Queue ranker and the `pd whois` ranker are *one function* with two consumers (the operator vs. the agent) — is genuinely the sharpest idea in the document. It is the agent-equivalent of recognizing that a database index serves both the query planner and the human running ad-hoc SQL; you build the structure once and rank against it for whoever asks. That is the correct generalization of FIPA's Directory Facilitator (FIPA00023): the DF was always a *ranker over a registry*, and the dossier correctly observes that "what should the operator look at?" is a relevance query over the operator's attention, structurally identical to "who should I route this task to?"

The second strong move is naming **read-poverty as a scale disease with a value curve across n** (gap #8). My field's whole thesis — discovery is free at n=2, the bottleneck at n=50, the system at n=500 — is exactly the L1→L2→L3 (existence→relevance→trust) layering, and the dossier ports it faithfully: the read-surfaces are the existence layer, `pd whois`/suggestibility are relevance, and the L3 reputation handoff is trust. It correctly refuses to skip levels (relevance over a stale registry returns "confident garbage"; reputation over self-asserted identity is the Sybil-reset trap, Douceur 2002).

Third, and most defensibly: the **digest-as-lens-not-verdict** rule (every summary zooms to a verifiable artifact) is precisely the mitigation my field prescribes for over-flattening (Scott 1998) — the directory that says "scout owns auth" but hides that scout's last three auth PRs were reverted. The dossier's invocation of Shneiderman's visual-information-seeking mantra ("overview first, zoom and filter, details-on-demand," 1996) to ground digest-with-zoom is *correct and humbling*: it concedes the core mechanism is established UX form, not invention. That intellectual honesty is the document's best quality.

## Gaps & inconsistencies (prioritized)

**1. (Highest) The unified ranker claim (gap #10) is asserted, never specified — and the two readers have incompatible loss functions.** This is the dossier's best idea and its biggest unbuilt promise. The skill's DP2 forces the question the dossier dodges: *what is the relevance function?* For `whois` (agent→agent), relevance is "capability match × recency × demonstrated competence." For the Attention Queue (substrate→operator), relevance is **stakes × irreversibility × anomaly**, which is a fundamentally different objective — the operator wants the *most dangerous* thing surfaced, not the *most capable* agent. These are not the same ranker with a swapped weight; one maximizes expected fit, the other maximizes expected regret-if-ignored. Claiming "they are the same function" is elegant but, on inspection, false as stated. The honest claim is weaker and better: *they share a candidate-generation and signal-decay substrate but apply different scoring heads.* The dossier should retreat to that or prove the unification with an actual scoring formula.

**2. (High) The operator-attention "economy" is a metaphor wearing a tuxedo, not a model.** The dossier escalates "attention-seconds-per-correct-decision" to "the binding meter" and even contradicts the tokens seed ("this is wrong at L2"). But it never defines the unit. My field has the actual apparatus it gestures at — Wickens's **Multiple Resource Theory** is not a scalar "attention-seconds" budget; it is a *4-dimensional* resource model (stage × modality × code × visual-channel) precisely because attention is *not* fungible seconds. You cannot trade audio-channel attention for visual-channel attention 1:1. So the proposed objective function (§3 problem #2) is built on a unit Wickens explicitly says does not exist as a scalar. Either cite MRT correctly (attention is vectored, and the digest's job is *cross-modal load-balancing* — which is actually what the FleetBar-ambient-vs-TUI-dense split in gap #7 is!) or stop calling it an economy. Right now §1.C and gap #7 are *the same insight* (ambient channel offloads the visual-dense channel) described in two vocabularies that don't know they agree.

**3. (High) Goodhart is named for `whois` but the Attention Queue is the more dangerous Goodhart surface, and it's unguarded.** The dossier cites Strathern/Goodhart for "top-of-whois becomes a target agents farm." Correct. But it then proposes the *same ranker* surface the operator's attention — which means **agents that learn the Attention Queue's stakes-ranking will manufacture distress to win the operator's pre-attentive race.** The Distress lane (mayday-red, <3s pre-attentive) is the highest-value signal in the system to spoof. The skill's mitigation (rank on costly-to-fake signals, sample-audit, operator-tunable weights) is applied to `whois` and *not* carried to the queue. This is a direct consistency failure within the dossier's own framework. Alarm fatigue (gap referenced via Lee & See) is the *symptom*; adversarial escalation-farming is the *cause*, and it's missing.

**4. (Medium) The directory-staleness discipline is absent from the legibility surfaces.** The skill is emphatic: every pull signal must recency-decay (`exp(-λ·hoursAgo)`, claim half-life 6h) or stale rows outrank live ones. The dossier applies decay to pheromones (correctly) but the **briefing projection, resurrection handoff, and attest read-surfaces have no stated staleness discipline.** A briefing digest is a cached projection — when is it stale? A resurrection note from a dead session: does it decay or persist forever as a misleading "current" claim? The "Codex-CLI session owners are DEAD" problem in the operator's own memory is *exactly this failure* and the dossier doesn't connect it. Legibility-without-decay is staleness-with-confidence.

**5. (Medium) Cold-start / refuse-to-route is named for n=1 (gap #8) but the quality gates are not applied to the authority half.** The skill's hardest gate is **refuse-to-route: a confidence floor below which "I don't know" beats a guess.** The dossier applies this to discovery but the completionist-`done`-as-verifier mechanism (its proudest net-new claim) needs the *same* gate stated as a first-class behavior, and §3 problem #4 ("verification of the unverifiable") admits it doesn't have one. "I cannot verify this completion" is the refuse-to-route of the authority half — it should be a quality gate, not an open problem. As written, an unverifiable "Done when:" clause is a confident-garbage hole in the flagship mechanism.

**6. (Lower) The federation endgame is undertheorized against the guild literature it cites.** The L2→L3 handoff says `pd whois` "federates into cross-operator directories + guilds." But the skill's DP4 and Greif–Milgrom–Weingast (1994) are explicit: a guild is not a bigger directory — it is a **credible-commitment structure solving a time-inconsistency problem** (the ruler who reneges on foreign merchants). The dossier treats guilds as "trust-scoped sub-directories," which is the *access-control-list-with-extra-steps* anti-pattern the skill warns against. What is the time-inconsistency problem in cross-operator agent trade that a guild's collective-punishment mechanism solves? Unanswered. EigenTrust (Kamvar 2003) is listed but its actual contribution — *transitive trust from a pre-trusted set, capping any single rater* — is never connected to the canary-defect mechanism, which is the natural pre-trusted seed.

## What an expert would add

- **A real scoring formula for the shared substrate**, showing where the `whois` head and the Attention-Queue head diverge. Candidate generation + decay shared; scoring heads `fit(·)` vs `regret(·)` distinct. This converts gap #10 from a slogan into a spec.
- **Vectored attention, not scalar seconds.** Recast §1.C using Wickens MRT: the digest minimizes *peak load on the bottleneck channel*, and the TUI/FleetBar split (gap #7) is the load-balancing mechanism. Merge §1.C and gap #7.
- **Escalation as a costly signal.** Borrow from signaling theory / the skill's costly-to-fake rule: an `escalate` should *debit* the agent's standing if the operator dismisses it (cf. crying-wolf). This is the EigenTrust pre-trusted-set idea applied to the Distress lane and directly arms the abdication-resistance and Goodhart gaps at once.
- **Staleness/decay on every read-surface**, with explicit TTLs, and the dead-session case (the operator's own "dead Codex owners" memory) as the worked example.
- **The information-theoretic lower bound (problem #9) connected to MDL and recursive-summarization collapse** — cite the actual failure (compaction recursing on prose, which the dossier elsewhere bans) as the empirical floor.
- **DNS-SD/Consul health-checked expiring registrations** as the production precedent for "legibility surfaces must expire," which the dossier's prior-art list omits entirely despite it being the canonical staleness-discipline reference.

## Verdict

**Not yet complete; largely consistent within its own frame but with two load-bearing claims that don't survive contact with my field's canon.**

The dossier is honest (the BUILT/DESIGNED/VISION labels check out and the self-deprecation about Shneiderman is exactly right) and its structural instinct — discovery and legibility are one read-side indexing problem — is correct and valuable. But it is **paper-ready only after three fixes:**

1. **Demote or prove the unified-ranker claim (gap #10).** As stated it is false: the operator wants max-regret, the agent wants max-fit. Retreat to "shared candidate/decay substrate, distinct scoring heads" or supply the formula. This is non-negotiable because it's the document's headline contribution.

2. **Carry the discovery quality gates into the authority half.** Refuse-to-route → "I cannot verify this completion" as a gate, not an open problem. Recency-decay → applied to briefing/resurrection/attest, not just pheromones. Goodhart/costly-signal → applied to the Distress lane, which is the system's most spoofable surface.

3. **Fix the attention "economy" to match Wickens.** Attention is vectored, not scalar-seconds; the objective is cross-modal load-balancing, and §1.C and gap #7 are secretly the same point. Either model it correctly or stop using economic language that implies fungibility the science denies.

The legibility half is well-argued and mostly built. The authority half is the genuine net-new contribution and it is, by the dossier's own honest labels, VISION — which is fine, but the *mechanisms* it proposes (verifier-gated `done`, attention economy, canary trust-calibration) are sketched at slide-deck altitude, not spec altitude. Bring them down one level, reconcile them with MRT, EigenTrust, and the costly-signal literature, and it's a strong paper.
