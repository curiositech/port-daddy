## Point-by-point responses

### Critique A — Political theory (legibility)

**A1. "Consent is asserted, never built; this is tacit consent, which Hume demolished. Where is consent located, scoped, revoked?"**
**CONCEDE — this is the single most important fix, and they are right that the word was doing all the work.** Fix: add a first-class **consent grant primitive** — `grant(scope, stakes_ceiling, reversibility_class, ttl, revoked_by)` stored as its own legible, attestable row in L0 (alongside claims/commitments). The daemon's authority to act unprompted is *defined by the active grants*, not by the operator merely running the tool. An act outside any grant's scope **must** route to HiTL (it is, by construction, unauthorized). This makes "the operator consents to cede authority" a checkable predicate, not a vibe. I'll cite Locke (*Second Treatise* §§95–122) on express-vs-tacit and Pateman 1979 on consent theory's collapse into the hypothetical, and explicitly mark our design as *express, scoped, revocable* consent precisely to escape Hume's critique.

**A2. "You misread Hobbes on the inalienable right of resistance; the escalation predicate is all daemon→operator, no operator→daemon 'I withdraw authority, stop now' as a *right*."**
**CONCEDE.** Fix: specify an **inalienable operator-override** as a reserved right, not a feature — un-suppressable by any autonomy level (ADR-0046 Phase 6 included): a single, always-available `pd halt`/kill-and-revoke that (a) revokes all active grants, (b) preempts the merge-queue, (c) cannot be gated, rate-limited, or hidden by the ranker. This is the computational analog of Hobbes's right of self-preservation (*Leviathan* ch. 14, 21). Pair it with **Hirschman 1970 (*Exit, Voice, Loyalty*)**: we have rich *voice* (escalate/spray/queue) and zero *exit*; the override + grant-revocation is the exit machinery that keeps voice honest. Good catch — this is also a real safety gap, not just a canon nit.

**A3. "Regress: the legibility apparatus is itself an exercise of authority — who makes the *ranker* legible? A queue that buries an escalation governs opaquely."**
**CONCEDE.** Fix: the ranker's *omissions* become first-class legible objects — a **"what the queue did not show you, and why"** counter-surface (suppressed-items log with the score and the suppressing rule, zoomable like any other event). The ranker's weights and the suppression decisions are attestable. This closes the regress one level: the most powerful organ (what-you-see-now) is made the *most* auditable, consistent with the legible-sovereign rule rather than an exception to it.

**A4. "Goodhart is quarantined to whois; the gravest risk is canary-catch-rate itself — Campbell's Law on the *governor*."**
**CONCEDE.** This is the same hole the HCI reviewer (B3) and info-design reviewer (C3) hit from other angles, so I'm fixing it once, structurally. Fix: (a) explicitly state Campbell's Law 1979 against my own central instrument; (b) make canaries *non-memorizable* — synthesized per-session from real defect distributions, not a fixed library, so a "looks-like-a-canary" heuristic is orthogonal to nothing real; (c) retreat to the weaker, honest claim — catch-rate is a **noisy, gameable, time-confounded** trust *signal*, never a clean invariant, and it triggers *investigation* (raise sampling, rotate, ask) rather than an automatic friction knob. I'll say plainly where it is net-positive and where it self-corrupts.

**A5. "Mētis is named but there's no place to *store* it; zoom-to-artifact is more legibility, not tacit knowledge. The avatar's automation atrophies the very mētis it can't capture."**
**CONCEDE — and this is the deep synthesis I left on the table.** Fix: stop claiming digest-with-zoom "preserves mētis." It preserves the *route to ground truth*; mētis (the operator's "this agent is about to do something dumb," the cursed-corner knowledge) is by definition uncodifiable and the abdication gradient *destroys* it. Reframe the skill-retention tax explicitly as **mētis-preservation, not annoyance-management**, and concede honestly that some of the operator's most valuable knowledge is exactly what L2 cannot make legible — which is *why Scott and Bainbridge are the same warning*. Add **Hayek 1945 (*Use of Knowledge in Society*)** as the economics half: the binding constraint is decentralized, non-aggregable knowledge in one head — the operator-attention "economy" is a Hayekian knowledge-allocation problem, not only a Wickens resource problem.

**A6. "Role assignment is unresolved: is the operator the multitude, the sovereign, or Hobbes's *author*? The metaphor breaks differently per answer."**
**CONCEDE.** Fix: one explicit paragraph. The mapping is: **the agents are the multitude** covenanting *among themselves* (the coordination protocol = the covenant) to the **daemon-as-sovereign-actor** (Arbiter/guard = the sword); the **operator is the *author*** in Hobbes's author/actor distinction (*Leviathan* ch. 16) — the one who authorizes the actor and bears ultimate responsibility, *above* the sovereign-actor. This is why the consent grant (A1) and the inalienable override (A2) are coherent: the author can scope and revoke the actor's authorization. The operator is not the sovereign; the daemon is, and the operator authors it. I'll state Scott's *Two Cheers for Anarchism* 2012 as the prescriptive complement to *Seeing Like a State*'s diagnosis, since this document is a prescription.

### Critique B — HCI / automation & human factors

**B1. (CRITICAL) "Forced-zoom/canary is a vigilance task; the vigilance decrement (Mackworth 1948; Warm/Parasuraman/Matthews 2008) predicts catch-rate degrades in 20–35 min for reasons unrelated to swarm danger — your safety signal is confounded."**
**CONCEDE — unqualified.** This is a genuine completionist failure (alarm/vigilance lit absent from a sampling mechanism). Fix: (a) cite Mackworth and Warm/Parasuraman/Matthews; (b) make catch-rate **time-on-task-corrected** — it is meaningless without a vigilance-decrement baseline; (c) accept and *state* the real tension my objective hates: defeating the decrement requires a canary rate high enough to sustain attention, which *costs attention-seconds* — a citable trade-off, not a free lunch; (d) the countermeasure for the decrement is *not* "rotate zooms" but **rotate operators / force breaks / cap session-on-task**, which I'll add. The honest consequence: a low catch-rate is *ambiguous* between "swarm got dangerous" and "operator is 35 min in," and the system must disambiguate by the clock before it touches friction.

**B2. (CRITICAL) "You cite Lee & See then implement the scalar global trust model they argue against; trust is multi-dimensional (performance/process/purpose) and needs resolution + specificity."**
**CONCEDE.** Embarrassing and correct — I cited the paper and built the thing it warns against. Fix: trust calibration becomes **function-specific and history-dependent** — track approve-without-zoom and catch-rate *per action-class* (refactor / migration / dependency-bump / infra) so the operator who correctly trusts refactors and distrusts migrations is not flattened into a middling global number. Forced-zoom friction is set *per class*, not by one global knob. This is Lee & See's *resolution* (distinguish reliable from unreliable contexts) and *specificity* (differentiate across functions and time) implemented rather than merely cited.

**B3. (MAJOR) "The objective has no decision-quality / error-cost model — it's Goodhart-bait. The binding question is d′/criterion (Green & Swets 1966), not throughput. Minimizing seconds-per-correct-decision rewards lowering the criterion to wave things through."**
**CONCEDE — and this re-spines the whole attention-economy claim.** Fix: replace "attention-seconds-per-correct-decision" as the headline with a **Signal Detection Theory** objective: the operator is choosing a criterion β trading **misses against false alarms under an asymmetric cost matrix** (miss = rubber-stamping a destructive-on-stale-premise merge ≫ false alarm = zooming a safe diff). The objective is *minimize expected cost = C_miss·P(miss) + C_fa·P(fa) + λ·attention-seconds*, with attention as a regularizer, not the maximand. The forced-zoom rate p(r,s,v) is then *derived* as the criterion that minimizes expected cost given the cost matrix — which also gives §3.1 a principled answer instead of an open knob. This is the most important single fix in the HCI critique and I take it wholesale.

**B4. (MAJOR) "SA is cited but not operationalized; digest-with-zoom is Level-1/2 (perceive/comprehend), almost nothing for Level-3 projection — and a present-only digest is SA-destroying no matter how good the zoom."**
**CONCEDE.** Fix: promote **projection (Endsley Level 3)** to a first-class digest mode — a "what the swarm is about to do / state in N minutes" panel (next merges, pending lands, grants about to expire), distinct from both the present-snapshot and the backward replay/time-axis (which I correctly had as *forensic* — the reviewer is right that forward projection is the deeper omission). Add the **freeze-probe (SAGAT-style)** measurement as the apparatus for "how much zoom is enough?" (§2.5/§3): pause the swarm, ask the operator to predict the next three merges, score accuracy. That converts an open problem into an experiment.

**B5. (MAJOR) "<3s pre-attentive claim conflates two mechanisms; Feature Integration Theory (Treisman & Gelade 1980) says pop-out degrades toward serial search as a dense TUI's distractor heterogeneity rises — your busy multiplexer defeats its own mayday-red."**
**CONCEDE on the mechanism; partially REBUT the number's home.** Concede: (a) the "<3s" is unsourced and sits in no-man's-land between pre-attentive (~200–500ms, single feature, low clutter) and deliberate search (many seconds); I'll drop the bare number and cite Treisman & Gelade; (b) a dense conversation-multiplexer is exactly the cluttered field where pop-out fails. **The fix is the reviewer's own corollary, which I adopt:** promote the **ambient channel (FleetBar — a separate, single-feature, zero-clutter field)** to the *primary* alarm path, where pop-out actually works, and demote the in-TUI Distress lane to a *secondary/confirmatory* surface with an explicit **clutter budget** for the multiplexer. Mild rebut only on framing: this isn't a contradiction in the dossier so much as an unforced ranking error — I had both channels (§2 gap #7) but wrongly made the dense one primary. Reversing the priority resolves it.

**B6. (MAJOR) "You built an alarm system with no alarm-management literature; the escalation predicate is 'unspecified,' so the Distress lane's false-alarm rate is uncontrolled and will be habituated into uselessness (EEMUA 191 / ISA-18.2; Cvach 2012; 85–99% of clinical alarms non-actionable)."**
