# Product beat: is there a product here?

**Decision:** BUILD and test a narrow single-run governance wedge. **HOLD** the broad Drydock/Anchor platform until demand, usability, containment, cancellation, willingness to pay, and operational burden are measured.

This beat uses the same exact repository anchor and sealed packet as the engineering review. The packet digest reproduced; no runtime was invoked.

## Reciprocal-review correction

`provider-neutral` is initially a contract hypothesis, not demonstrated
runtime portability. The first mode is **no-settlement**, not “zero-money”:
there are no bonds, bounties, or payment effects, while subscription allowance,
model/API usage, compute, time, and operator attention remain scarce. Product
discovery may begin during S0, but a study run before its named evidence gate
returns `INCOMPLETE`, not support.

## Strongest product case

Drydock is strongest as a provider-neutral governance layer that lets an accountable operator delegate one coding change to any agent while bounding workspace, credentials, effects, capacity, retries, and successor behavior, then producing independently inspectable evidence for review, interruption, and recovery.

That is a product-shaped sentence. “A constitutional economy for autonomous swarms” is a research program, not a beachhead.

## Evidence for the problem

- DORA's 2025 report drew on nearly 5,000 practitioners: AI use was widespread and perceived productivity high, but trust remained limited and adoption still correlated negatively with delivery stability. The report emphasizes platform quality and fast feedback rather than AI alone: <https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/ai-machine-learning/announcing-the-2025-dora-report>.
- Stack Overflow's 2025 survey found more respondents distrusted AI accuracy than trusted it, while accuracy, security, and privacy were major concerns. It also found agents were not yet mainstream and existing observability tools dominated agent monitoring: <https://survey.stackoverflow.co/2025/ai>.
- Anthropic reports high approval rates and approval fatigue; its own containment work reduced prompts by moving routine freedom inside harder filesystem/network boundaries: <https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/how-we-contain-claude>.
- GitHub already exposes enterprise policies, recent sessions, audit events, third-party agent controls, and MCP governance: <https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/concepts/enterprise/agent-management>.
- NIST's Generative AI Profile frames governance, provenance, testing, and incident disclosure across the lifecycle: <https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf>.

These sources establish a trust-and-stability problem. They do **not** establish demand or willingness to pay for Port Daddy's answer.

## The first product

One accountable engineer or platform owner can:

1. choose a repository and a bounded coding job;
2. inspect an exact proposal showing body, workspace, tools, network, credentials, attempts, descendants, time, and capacity;
3. assent to exactly what was shown without that assent executing anything;
4. let independent policy either deny or admit one sandboxed run;
5. watch work and evidence without turning projections into authority;
6. request Stop and see delivery, fence, process, lease, effects, and teardown converge separately;
7. review the diff, tests, unresolved claims, and evidence before choosing ordinary Git disposition.

The first profile is no-settlement, one agent, one repo, one bounded task, and one controlled environment. It integrates with existing PR, CI, audit, identity, and SIEM systems while exposing usage spend separately.

## Initial personas and jobs

### Economic buyer and risk co-owner

VP Engineering, CTO, or Developer Platform leader working with security. Job: permit useful coding-agent adoption without accepting unknown local processes, silent effects, uncontrolled retries, or unauditable decisions.

### Platform governor

Platform/security engineer. Job: define reusable policies, prove that boundaries are actually enforced, export receipts into existing systems, and investigate ambiguity without widening authority.

### Accountable delegator

Senior engineer or maintainer. Job: describe the work once, understand the proposed authority, follow progress, intervene, and receive a reviewable change without babysitting every shell command.

### Evidence consumer

Reviewer, incident responder, auditor, or compliance partner. Job: move from a summary to exact code, command, test, policy, effect, or witness evidence in at most two actions.

### Anti-persona

A developer who wants an unrestricted agent and accepts native provider controls does not need this first product. Do not hide that tradeoff.

## Product hypotheses

| ID | Hypothesis | Earliest evidence gate | Test | Kill condition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H1 | Teams with blocked agent rollouts share a cross-provider governance gap. | Now · interviews only | 15 problem interviews across platform/security/engineering. | Native provider controls satisfy the job without material gaps. |
| H2 | One governed run reduces review or incident time enough to justify friction. | S1–S3 deterministic scenarios | Compare matched bounded tasks with and without the evidence spine. | Review time, errors, or operator load do not improve. |
| H3 | Operators can understand and independently operate exact assent, denial, Stop, ambiguity, and resurrection. | S6 static, then novel transfer scenario | Separate teach-back and operation across keyboard, screen reader, 200% zoom, and forced colors. | Any unsafe action or critical authority misconception remains after repair and a fresh scenario. |
| H4 | A design partner will install and security-review the layer. | S7 · explicit halt lift | Two non-founder design partners complete architecture and threat review. | Privileged deployment burden exceeds perceived value. |
| H5 | A durable budget exists. | Post-S7 | Paid pilot followed by expansion/renewal conversation. | Praise without payment, ownership, or production adoption. |

## What is deliberately excluded from the wedge

- swarm orchestration and multi-agent economies;
- bounties, bonds, slashing, reputation, agent pay, and remote custody;
- a replacement IDE, Git host, CI service, SIEM, or general observability platform;
- universal provider-allowance forecasting;
- claims of safe autonomy, hard stop, replay, or complete mediation before runtime proof.

Those remain founding research and possible expansion paths. Keeping them out of V1 protects them from being implemented as brittle theater.

## Differentiation test

Sandboxing, logs, enterprise controls, approvals, and MCP policy are becoming expected native features. The candidate differentiation is narrower and harder:

- provider-neutral policy over heterogeneous bodies;
- identity and authority continuity across bodies without credential transfer;
- pre-effect, one-use evidence joins;
- truthful ambiguity instead of silent retry;
- causal, typed receipts that preserve disagreement and export to existing telemetry.

If buyers say native controls cover this, the market thesis is falsified. The correct response is not to broaden the product until it sounds unique.

## Product language

Lead with: **one governed coding run, bounded access, no hidden work, verifiable intervention, review-ready evidence.**

Do not lead with internal nouns such as bodies, harbors, hypertrees, Float Plans, or constitutional settlement. Those concepts can remain exact inside the system while the user sees the job they are trying to finish.
