# Mega-volume figure-quality ledger

## Purpose

This is the acceptance ledger for every analytical figure in the seven-volume
*Port Daddy Coordination Papers*. It is a figure does not pass merely because
LaTeX compiles. It passes only when a printed colour page makes its one claimed
relationship immediately legible.

This ledger governs technical figures only. It must **not** crop, recolour,
retouch, relabel, or otherwise alter supplied title, chapter, cover, or jacket
art.

## Global acceptance contract

Every figure card must state one reader question and select its mark before its
metaphor:

| Claimed relation | Required form |
| --- | --- |
| Ordered decision or causal sequence | Decision path, causal chain, or swimlane |
| State or legal transition | State machine |
| Roles and scoped authority | Small directed institutional route |
| One durable source, several reader-specific views | Source ledger plus aligned output rail |
| Two objectives over one substrate | Shared substrate split into two lanes |
| Threshold, error, rate, or distribution | Proper plot with axes and external annotation band |
| Hierarchy, lineage, or provenance | Tree, layered ledger, or provenance chain |
| Dense many-to-many relation | Matrix or reduced path view, never a hairball |

### Hard failures

- Any maritime, pirate, ship, anchor, map, voyage, crew, parchment, old-book,
  nautical, or scenic imagery in a technical figure.
- A generated illustration carrying semantic labels, arrows, values, or axes.
- A paragraph broken into rounded boxes in place of an analytical relationship.
- A label touching a border, arrow, curve, plot field, or another label.
- Homogeneous nodes whose structure only restates set membership.
- Colour used as the sole distinction, raw saturated primary-blue treatment, or
  a palette unrelated to analytical roles.
- A one-off figure that ignores the common serif type, ink/warm-paper base,
  restrained teal/cobalt/amber accents, and print-scale spacing system.

### Required evidence for each replacement

1. Source-owned TikZ/PGFPlots/SVG/Mermaid source and a short card naming the
   visual question.
2. Standalone colour preview at its final column or page width.
3. Colour page render from the parent paper PDF, inspected at normal reading
   size; no grayscale derivative is required.
4. A result of the three five-second checks: focal entity, relation direction
   or scale, and what changes/is selected.
5. Exact source path, paper PDF page, and a statement of which obsolete figure
   was removed.

### Placement and reader-orientation checks

- A figure that supplies evidence for a theorem, comparison, or mechanism must
  appear on the claim page or the immediately following page. An appendix-only
  figure does not count as visual support for a body claim.
- A volume longer than a normal single-sitting read needs a compact front-matter
  Reader's Map: the major layers, one load-bearing result per layer, and a
  route for a formal reader, systems reader, and governance reader. The map is
  a navigational figure, not decorative front matter.
- Where a diagram teaches a protocol primitive, pair it with the first prose
  introduction of that primitive; captions and late cross-references are not a
  substitute for argument proximity.

## Reference standard distilled from `legible-volume1-audit`

Use this as a print-layout contract, not an invitation to copy its subject
matter:

- **One question, one visual grammar.** A comparison is two balanced panels; a
  threshold is an axis-bound plot; a protocol is a short, ordered sequence.
  Never combine all three because the source prose mentions all three.
- **Grid before decoration.** Panel widths, baselines, actor columns, time
  ticks, and annotation bands must align before any colour or rounded frame is
  introduced. Captions carry the conclusion; the plot carries the evidence.
- **A disciplined semantic palette.** Warm paper and dark ink are the default.
  Teal marks the working/verified route, amber marks a boundary or cost, and
  cobalt is reserved for one genuinely comparative or counterfactual trace;
  it never becomes the default outline of every component.
- **Labels live in reserved space.** Put legend, formula, stage strip, and
  explanation in dedicated exterior bands. Nothing may sit on top of a curve,
  arrow bend, terminal, or another label.
- **The page is the test.** A figure that works as a crop but forces the prose,
  caption, or next heading into a cramped page does not meet this standard.

Keep:

- Figure 6's phase-boundary plot: one common scale, a shaded decision region,
  a distinct dashed counterfactual, and an explanation panel outside the axes.
- Figure 8's abdication plot: direct values on a common scale, a stage rail
  below the plot, and intervention markers that have ample clearance.
- Figure 3's side-by-side comparison: symmetric panels, a shared baseline,
  and the conclusion carried by caption rather than duplicated prose.

Do **not** copy:

- Figure 2's crowded node-link grammar when a queue or timeline is the actual
  relation. The necessary page-level discipline is valuable; its early graph
  is not the reusable visual form.

## Audit protocol

For every card, inspect the source and its colour PDF page manually. Mark only
one of these outcomes:

- **PASS:** the printed page answers the reader question in five seconds.
- **REPAIR:** the selected visual form is correct but a print-layout defect
  remains. Record the exact collision, off-grid element, or unreadable label.
- **REPLACE:** the visual form itself does not encode the claim. State the
  replacement form before writing TikZ.
- **REMOVE:** no visual relation remains once duplicated prose is deleted.

No figure may be marked PASS from source review, compile success, or an isolated
crop alone.
