\noindent\textbf{Premise.} Two of the three reviews are, at heart, product documents, and they agree on the shape: rebrand to a Unix-daemon identity, lead with an enterprise compliance wedge, and grow into a clearinghouse for machine-to-machine trust. The rigor review is a science document, but it hands the product two gifts it does not know it needs: a defensible security story (channel-level control, not taint hand-waving) and a sequencing discipline (secure the work before pricing it). This document fuses them into one roadmap, and --- crucially --- marks the claims you may make from each phase, because the fastest way to lose an enterprise deal is to overclaim and get caught.

\section{Positioning and the naming question}
\begin{verdict}\textbf{On the rebrand.} Adopt \textsc{agentsd} as the daemon, \texttt{agentsctl} as the operator CLI/TUI, \texttt{agentsdaemon.com} as the federation. The Unix-service framing (\emph{systemd} manages services, \emph{httpd} serves web, \emph{agentsd} manages the swarm) is genuinely good positioning: it says ``background infrastructure,'' not ``another agent framework,'' which is the correct category to own. Keep ``Harbor'' as the \emph{unit} noun (a harbor is one principal's daemon-scoped domain) and ``Port Daddy'' nowhere in the product --- it does not survive contact with an enterprise procurement officer. The manuscript can keep whatever internal names it likes; the product needs the clinical one.\end{verdict}

\noindent\textbf{The one-line pitch, corrected.} The product reviews offer ``the Delaware Chancery Court and the Visa network for the machine economy'' and ``the UCC / POSIX standard for AI labor.'' These are good \emph{aspirational} lines for the clearinghouse endgame, but they are Phase-5 claims worn on a Phase-1 body. The rigor review supplies the honest Phase-1 line, which is stronger because it is deliverable: closest to \textbf{Lloyd's for autonomous work} --- standard work orders, declared risks, evidence, and (eventually) underwriters --- built on a local \textbf{control plane and flight recorder} for AI agents. Lead with the flight recorder; grow into Lloyd's; mention Visa only once you clear funds.

\noindent\textbf{On neo-feudalism.} The product reviews' ``knights wearing colors, lords posting bonds, invitation-only as a feature'' is vivid and correct as an \emph{account of why high-trust networks are exclusionary} (the bonded-sponsor pattern). Use it in a blog post and a keynote. Do \emph{not} put it in the security documentation, where ``exclusionary by design'' reads as ``we could not solve open admission'' --- which the rigor review shows is a real, priced trade-off (sponsor concentration caps, sponsorship auctions, automatic probation expansion), not a virtue to celebrate.

\section{The assurance-level label: your most valuable product artifact}
The rigor review's five levels are not academic hedging --- they are the single feature that lets you sell honestly and defensibly. Ship this label on every deployment and every marketing claim:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \textbf{Observed} --- effects may occur outside the daemon; evidence is best-effort. (Hook-based integrations start here.)
\item \textbf{Coordinated} --- well-behaved clients use claims, roles, commitments.
\item \textbf{Brokered} --- credentials and selected effects pass through daemon-owned brokers.
\item \textbf{Confined} --- the process has no ambient route around fs/network/tool/credential brokers.
\item \textbf{Attested} --- a remote party can verify the confined runtime and policy measurement.
\end{enumerate}
The product reviews' entire pitch presumes level 4--5 (``mathematically cannot''), but their default integration path --- IDE extension intercepting MCP calls, LLM API proxy, PATH shims --- is level 2--3 (a coordinator, bypassable by a determined same-user process). \emph{Do not claim level 4 while shipping level 2.} The hypervisor work (Phase 2) is what earns levels 4--5, and only ``for the enumerated channel set'' after a red team has hunted the negative space (raw sockets, DNS, \texttt{/proc}, inherited descriptors, git helpers, package managers, child processes, clipboard, provider callbacks).

\section{The phased roadmap}
Sequenced so each phase ships a claim the previous phase earned. The unifying discipline (from the rigor review): \emph{secure the work, then preserve and share it, only then price it.}

\subsection*{Phase 0 --- Repair the spine (weeks, not months)}
Before any new machinery: land the eight consensus corrections from Document 1 (SDT direction, exact-key exclusion + fencing epoch, identity theorem, float-plan buckets, Anchor edge check, WAL durability types, schema migration ledger, and the ``cryptography $\neq$ reality'' scoping). These are cheap and they are the difference between a system a security engineer trusts and one they do not. Ship the assurance-level label and a per-component threat-model table.

\subsection*{Phase 1 --- The one-agent receipt (the wedge MVP)}
The smallest product that proves the thesis, at $n{=}1$:
\begin{itemize}
\item the work-unit object and lifecycle; a typed \emph{acceptance policy} and completion \emph{receipt};
\item an artifact/evidence graph answering why / where / why-not for every claim;
\item structured live-state projection (obligations, grants, pending effects, claim verdicts);
\item a portable task capsule with \emph{same-provider} restart under a minute (event-sourced message array + Git SHA + prefix-cache replay --- the product reviews' mechanism, correctly scoped);
\item an operation journal giving tool idempotency (at-most-once external effects);
\item atomic mutual exclusion on the enforced conflict predicate; oracle-bound closure that rejects free-text ``all green, trust me.''
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Ships the claim:} ``a successor can safely continue after termination, and a human can audit why \emph{done} was accepted.'' That is the compliance wedge. \textbf{Distribution:} pre-compiled binaries (\texttt{npm}/\texttt{pip}/\texttt{brew}) and the IDE extension that boots the daemon silently and intercepts MCP calls --- the product reviews are right that vibe-coders will not install a Rust toolchain. Label this integration \textbf{Coordinated/Brokered (level 2--3)} honestly.

\subsection*{Phase 2 --- Roles, typed messaging, confined effects (earn level 4)}
\begin{itemize}
\item durable \emph{role} mailboxes addressed by role/topic (\texttt{role:test-owner}, not \texttt{agent-492}) so the mailbox survives resurrection; typed performatives (\textsc{inform, request, propose, commit, accept, refuse, challenge, warn, evidence, retract}) with explicit deontic semantics --- \textsc{inform} creates a claim not a truth, a message never carries tool authority because its text asks for an effect;
\item sole-responsibility \emph{role leases} with fencing epochs (calendar avatar, test writer, roadmap owner) --- one accepted epoch per exclusive role, verification kept separate from ownership (a test-writer role must not be the sole approver of its own tests);
\item the effect hypervisor: microVM/VM isolation, seccomp/Landlock/gVisor confinement, cgroup quotas, a network-policy proxy, credential broker (short-lived scoped secrets), tool broker (calendar/GitHub/cloud/payment), transactional outbox;
\item the SLM sidecar as an \emph{untrusted projection service} --- lower privilege than the daemon, no effect authority, agent text as tainted data, provenance required per generated claim, mandatory-obligation IDs computed \emph{outside} the model. Name it ``evidence compactor,'' not ``memory.''
\end{itemize}
\textbf{Ships the claim (red-team gated):} ``Confined (level 4) for the enumerated channel set.'' This is where the psychosis-detection loop lives too --- but framed correctly (below).

\subsection*{Phase 3 --- The review fleet and prospective assurance (the demand generator)}
Build the GitHub review fleet and Lookout/Unspider on the same claim/effect graph. The rigor review's key insight: do not have five agents read the same diff and vote (correlated failure). Use \emph{deliberately different information} --- a property-author who writes tests from the spec \emph{before} seeing the patch, a blind adversarial tester, a security/effect reviewer, a merge-risk reviewer, a protected-CI adjudicator emitting signed receipts. Adversarial test-writing is scored by \emph{mutation kill rate} on a held-out mutation set. This provides adversarial \emph{demand} for evidence and generates the incident corpus that CDM skill-extraction needs. \textbf{Ships:} ``assurance as a purchasable line item'' --- $(1-d)^k$ at a stated price --- which is the clearinghouse's core value made concrete two phases early.

\subsection*{Phase 4 --- Provider-portable resurrection (the flagship demo)}
Publish the neutral task-capsule schema; write provider adapters that reconstruct context but never pretend to restore hidden inference state. Demonstrate the money shot: Claude credits exhausted mid-task, a different provider resumes, no external effect duplicated. \textbf{Names its own attack:} engine substitution (build reputation on a strong model, spend it on a weak one) --- defend with engine attestation in every witnessed outcome, reputation indexed by (principal, engine). \textbf{Ships:} the ``big resume button,'' the single feature that makes the continuity story legible to a buyer in five seconds. Say \emph{task continuity with actor replacement}, never ``perfect neural continuity.''

\subsection*{Phase 5 --- The hosted project institution (Alice, Bob, Carl)}
Start with Alice and Bob under one hosted authority for the non-monotone operations (exclusive roles, revocation, finite budgets, unique settlement); keep personal state in personal harbors. Add the coordination graph (typed modalities: observed/believed/desired/intended/committed/required/permitted/forbidden/forecast), typed-conflict detection (deterministic resource checker $\mid$ SMT for invariants $\mid$ temporal-network for schedules $\mid$ policy block for security $\mid$ steward decision for UX), the durable agentic estate (actor build / instance / memory / relationship / authority, separated), and product-journey traceability. Add Carl only after two-person semantics are stable (the rigor review is right that $n{=}3$ adds coalitions, which is why the charter votes on charter, never on truth or another's namespace). \textbf{This is the remote clearinghouse's real substrate}, and it is more valuable than the shared repo because it explains why the repo exists.

\subsection*{Phase 6 --- The Sealed Harbor (the airlock, hardened)}
Begin with a no-network microVM whose rich result only Derek receives; add dual attested key release, measured policy, whole-worker taint, fixed output channels, signed receipts, and adversarial exfiltration red-teaming (canary bits through network, logs, errors, filenames, length, timing, CPU load, receipt status). Confidential-GPU attestation and special MPC/FHE kernels for narrow fixed computations come later. \textbf{Ships the corrected airlock claim:} bounded, declared, gated release --- not magical secrecy.

\subsection*{Phase 7 --- Economy and underwriting (price it, finally)}
Only now, with real receipts and real failure rates, does the market price them. Bind bonds, provider collateral, insurer coverage, and dispute procedures to principal-owned work orders and evidence. Model correlated failure by provider, model family, toolchain, and shared dependency (the rigor review's repeated warning: do not sum risks as if model/provider/dependency failures were independent). Include buyer misconduct and evidence poisoning in the threat model. The 2-of-3 multisig escrow (product reviews' best financial idea) is the settlement rail; the underwriting layer prices the residual after prevent/contain/checkpoint/observe/adjudicate.

Pair uncapped slashing with principal-aggregated exposure and onboarding cost, or a Sybil just mints a fresh staker. \\

\section{What the product is, in one paragraph}
\textsc{agentsd} is the control plane and flight recorder for AI agents: a local single-writer daemon that mediates every consequential effect, records an auditable evidence-bearing receipt for every unit of work, confines agents to brokered channels, and can resume a task across a provider change without duplicating an external effect. It sells first as compliance and liability containment (the wedge), then as a mutually-confidential clean room for parties who cannot share data or models (the airlock), then as a non-custodial clearinghouse that settles and underwrites machine-to-machine work (the economy). Every claim it makes is labelled with an assurance level it can defend, and every release it permits is explicit and bounded. That is a product a CISO can buy and a regulator can tolerate --- which is worth more than a product that promises magic and gets breached.
