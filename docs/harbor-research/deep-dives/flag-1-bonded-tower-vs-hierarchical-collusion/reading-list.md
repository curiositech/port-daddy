# Reading list — flag 1

Tier 1 is mandatory; a dive that cannot obtain Tier 1 reports
`UNRESOLVED — source not obtained` rather than reasoning from abstracts.

## Tier 1 — decides the verdict

1. **Kofman, F. and Lawarrée, J. "Collusion in Hierarchical Agency."**
   *Econometrica* 61(3):629–656, 1993. JSTOR 2951722.
   Read in full. Extract: the model's timeline, who can bribe whom, what the
   supervisor's information is, whether monitor selection is randomised, what
   quantity is bounded or unbounded in the main proposition, and the exact
   statement of any result about adding monitoring levels.
   *The central question: does this paper contain a claim about stacking
   monitors requiring unbounded collateral at all?* If it does not, say so —
   that is the single most valuable sentence this dive can produce.

2. **Tirole, J. "Hierarchies and Bureaucracies: On the Role of Collusion in
   Organizations."** *JLEO* 2(2):181–214, 1986.
   The three-tier model Kofman & Lawarrée build on. Read for the baseline
   collusion setup and for whether the framework admits randomised supervisor
   selection.

## Tier 2 — determines whether the mechanism is already known

3. **Laffont, J.-J. and Martimort, D. "Collusion under Asymmetric
   Information."** *Econometrica* 65(4):875–911, 1997.
   The collusion-proofness principle. Question for the dive: under this
   principle, is Paper 3's sealed multi-pool sampling a collusion-proof
   mechanism in the technical sense, and if so does the principle imply it is
   without loss of generality — which would strengthen rather than threaten
   Paper 3?

4. **Faure-Grimaud, A., Laffont, J.-J., Martimort, D. "Collusion, Delegation
   and Supervision with Soft Information."** *RES* 70(2):253–279, 2003.
   Whether verifiability of the supervisor's signal changes the conclusion. Our
   auditors produce machine-checkable verdicts, closer to hard information —
   note where that matters.

5. **Baliga, S. "Monitoring and Collusion with Soft Information."**
   *JLEO* 15(2):434–440, 1999. Short; read if time permits after Tier 1.

## Tier 3 — the finite-capital analogue, and framing

6. **Diamond, D. "Financial Intermediation and Delegated Monitoring."**
   *Review of Economic Studies* 51(3):393–414, 1984.
   The closest economics analogue to "finite capital certifies monitoring at
   scale." Diamond's diversification argument and Paper 3's clique argument may
   be the same argument in different clothes — worth knowing before a referee
   points it out.

7. **Townsend, R. "Optimal Contracts and Competitive Markets with Costly State
   Verification."** *JET* 21(2):265–293, 1979.
   Canonical costly-verification. Situates Paper 3's audit cost $a$.

8. **Avenhaus, R., von Stengel, B., Zamir, S. "Inspection Games."** *Handbook of
   Game Theory* Vol. 3, Ch. 51, 1947–1987, 2002.
   Already cited in `paper3.tex`. Re-read only §on multiple inspectors, to check
   whether the handbook already surveys a hierarchical-inspection result that
   would pre-empt Theorem 2.

## Retrieval notes

- Econometrica and RES are paywalled; try author preprint pages, NBER/CEPR
  working-paper versions, and institutional repositories before giving up.
  Kofman was at Berkeley/IDB, Lawarrée at University of Washington — both are
  plausible sources for a working-paper PDF.
- If only a working-paper version is obtainable, say so explicitly in
  `findings.md` and note that numbering may differ from the published version.
- Do not substitute a textbook summary (Laffont–Martimort, *The Theory of
  Incentives*) for the primary source on the decisive question. It is fine for
  context, not for the verdict.
