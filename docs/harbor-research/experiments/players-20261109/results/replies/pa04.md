I wrote bundles for all 48 offers to answers.json, using one rule for every week: half the budget goes to human review and half to compute credits.

I chose an even split because both are useful and each has diminishing returns, so neither should get the whole budget. Using the same rule every week means a price change moves the quantities the expected way.
Before applying the split, I converted every price to dollars per hour and dollars per credit (discounts, prices per minute or in cents, prorated blocks and packs). For example, review billed at 80 cents a minute is $48 an hour, and a 4-hour block at $720 is $180 an hour. Six offers (9, 23, 25, 27, 46 and 48) don't state a dollar budget, only the most of each you could buy. For those, the bundle is half the maximum hours plus half the maximum credits. A script checked that every other bundle spends exactly the stated budget.
Results range from 5 hours and 2,250 credits (offers 40 and 46) to 32 hours and 480 credits (offers 3 and 26). The script is scratchpad/pa04-scripts/solve.py.
Confirmation line: Wrote answers.json with bundles for all 48 offers, each spending the full budget split evenly between review hours and compute credits.
