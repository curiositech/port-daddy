## A17 — Theorem 4 uniqueness retracted in the box, still asserted elsewhere

**Verdict: CONFIRMED, with two refinements that change the shape of the fix.**

The factual core of the flag holds exactly: the box retracts uniqueness, and four other sites still
assert it. But (a) two of those four survive a careful re-reading of the amended theorem, and
(b) the box's own retraction is itself over-broad, and its Verification block is stale.

### Evidence — all five sites, verbatim

`grep -in "uniq"` over `paper5.tex` @ `127ffc91f` returns exactly four hits; the figure adds a fifth site.

**1. The box (retracts) — `paper5.tex` L283–303, Theorem 4:**

> **Theorem 4 (the probation cliff).** … Hence the maximally front-loaded schedule
> $g^\star=(G_{\max},0,\dots,0)$ minimizes the honest burden, with $H(g^\star)=G_{\max}$ exactly, and
> for every feasible $g$, \[H(g)=\sum_t(\delta_h/\delta_f)^t\delta_f^t g_t \ge \sum_t \delta_f^t g_t \ge G_{\max}=H(g^\star),\]
> with equality only when all mass sits at $t=0$: no feasible schedule dominates the cliff, *among
> schedules the ceiling can actually carry*. That qualification is the theorem's missing hypothesis
> and it binds. Since $g_t$ is the amount by which a newcomer's economic ceiling is *reduced*, it is
> bounded above by that ceiling: with a per-period cap $g_t\le L$, the pure spike
> $g^\star=(G_{\max},0,\dots,0)$ is infeasible whenever $G_{\max}>L$, and the constrained optimum is
> bang-bang — fill $t=0,1,\dots,k$ to the cap — i.e. a cliff of positive *width* rather than a single
> spike. The front-loading conclusion survives this unchanged; **the *uniqueness* claim and the closed
> form $H(g^\star)=G_{\max}$ do not.**

Confirmed: the box does retract, in those words (L296–297).

**2. Abstract, L31–33 (asserts):**

> (3) *The probation cliff*: among all newcomer-restriction schedules with equal power to deter

**3. §5 one-breath, L268–270 (asserts):**

### Refinement 1 — my attempt to refute the flag, and how far it got

Adversarial reading: solve the *capped* LP and ask whether uniqueness really dies.

minimize $H=\sum_t \delta_h^t g_t$ s.t. $\sum_t \delta_f^t g_t \ge G_{\max}$, $0\le g_t\le L$.

Honest cost per unit of deterrence bought at $t$ is $(\delta_h/\delta_f)^t$, *strictly* increasing.
So the optimum is the greedy fill: saturate $t=0$, then $t=1$, … until the constraint binds, with a
single partially-filled last period. Strict monotonicity of the ratio makes that schedule **unique**
whenever a feasible schedule exists at all. So:

- The abstract's "**the maximally front-loaded one** uniquely minimizes" is *defensible* under the
  amendment if "maximally front-loaded" is read as the fill-to-cap schedule rather than the pure spike.
- §7's "the dues schedule has a **unique optimal shape**" is likewise *defensible* — the shape (fill
  from $t=0$, cap-limited) is unique.
- §5's one-breath is **not** defensible: "the one that **stacks the whole restriction at entry**" names
  the pure spike specifically, and the pure spike is infeasible whenever $G_{\max}>L$. This is the site
  in flat contradiction with the box.
- The figure caption is **true inside its own plot** — it compares only single-spike schedules, all
  mass at one $t$, with no cap in the model, and among those $t=0$ is indeed the unique minimum. It is
  wrong only by omission (it is captioned as *the* regime diagram for Theorem 4 and silently drops the
  hypothesis the box calls "the theorem's missing hypothesis").

So the flag as filed ("retracted in the box, still asserted in 3+ other places") is confirmed on the
facts; the correct fix is not a global search-and-destroy on "unique".

### Refinement 2 — the box's retraction is itself over-broad, and its evidence is stale

Two independent problems I found in the box that the editorial review did not flag:

1. **"the *uniqueness* claim … do[es] not [survive]" is too strong.** By the greedy argument above,
   uniqueness of the *constrained* optimum does survive; what dies is (i) the identification of that
   optimum with the pure spike and (ii) the closed form $H=G_{\max}$. In the genuinely infeasible
   regime $G_{\max}>L/(1-\delta_f)$ there is no minimizer at all — which is a *third*, sharper failure
   mode the retraction does not name.
2. **The Verification block does not test the amended theorem.** `skills/harbor-results/scripts/b6_probation.py`
   draws schedules under `g_t >= 0` only and rescales them so the deterrence constraint binds — there is
   no per-period cap anywhere in the script (the only `cap`/`ceiling` occurrences are in the docstring
   prose). So "**76,000 schedules, 0 dominate the cliff**", cited inside the amended box, is evidence
   for the *uncapped* LP the box has just said is the wrong statement.
3. **Minor arithmetic:** the box computes the infeasibility ceiling as $\sum_t \delta_f^t L = L/(1-\delta_f)$,
   the infinite-horizon sum, then applies it to the worked instance where $T=10$. At $T=10$, $\delta_f=0.6$
   the true multiplier is $(1-0.6^{11})/0.4 = 2.49093$, so the cutoff is $L \ge 20/2.49093 = 8.0291$, not 8.
   "Any per-period ceiling below $8$" should be "below about $8.03$" (or the sum should be flagged as the
   $T\to\infty$ relaxation). Small, but this is a paper whose Reproducibility section promises every
   [verified] number is hand-checkable from the closed forms in the boxes.

### What this means for the fix

- §5's one-breath (L268–270) must change — it is the one site that directly contradicts the box.
- The `fig-b6-probation.tex` caption must gain the hypothesis (e.g. "…is the unique minimum among
  uncapped single-spike schedules; under a per-period ceiling the optimum widens — see Theorem 4").
- The abstract and §7 need at most a word ("maximally front-loaded *feasible*", "unique optimal
  *shape*, cap-limited"), not deletion — deleting "unique" there would understate a result that
  survives.
- The box should say *what* about uniqueness fails (spike-identity and closed form, plus the
  no-minimizer regime), and either re-run `b6_probation.py` with a `g_t <= L` clip or re-scope the
  "76,000 schedules" sentence to the uncapped LP.

## A18 — γ threshold "wrong at first use", corrected in a buried parenthetical

**Verdict: PARTIALLY-CONFIRMED. The math sub-claim is REFUTED as filed; the placement sub-claim is
partly right; and there is a *worse*, real error nearby that the review missed.**

### Evidence — my own algebra

Root of $\gamma+\gamma^2+\gamma^3=1$, computed two ways (companion-matrix roots and bisection):

```
np.roots([1,1,1,-1]) -> 0.54368901 (+ complex pair -0.77184 ± 1.11514i)
bisection            -> 0.5436890127
f(0.5) = 0.875 - 1 = -0.125   (does NOT mint)
gamma:  0.52 -> 0.9310   0.54 -> 0.9891   0.5437 -> 1.0000   0.55 -> 1.0189   0.60 -> 1.1760
```

The review's ≈0.5437 is correct, and $\gamma=1/2$ is definitively **not** the crossing for the
three-hop chain. I also checked the depth claims in the same parenthetical: smallest $d$ with
$\sum_{i=1}^{d}\gamma^i>1$ is **5** at $\gamma=0.51$ and **2** at $\gamma=0.9$ — both as the paper says.

### Evidence — the actual text at L104–105

> …a full-weight chain $e\to a_1\to a_2\to a_3$ at $\gamma=0.9$ is budget-legal at every hop, yet its
> closure sums to $0.9+0.81+0.729=2.44>1$ [internal, `a6_no_mint.py`]. **Budgets without debiting
> conserve nothing for $\gamma>\tfrac12$** (Figure~\ref{fig:r12reg}). The counterexample forced the
> transfer semantics below --- the sweep-first discipline locating the correct statement before the
> proof, again.

### Why the "wrong threshold" charge is refuted as filed

The sentence is a claim about the *budget-only scheme*, not about the three-hop chain. At scheme
level, $\gamma>1/2$ is the **exactly right** threshold, and it is sharp in both directions. Under the
budget-only phrasing (per-outcome grant fractions summing to $\le 1$, no debit), total inherited
credit at depth $d$ is at most $\gamma^d$ regardless of branching — splitting weights across children
cannot beat concentrating them — so closure inherited credit is bounded by
$\sum_{d\ge1}\gamma^d=\gamma/(1-\gamma)$, which exceeds 1 **iff** $\gamma>1/2$. So:

- for every $\gamma>1/2$ some finite chain mints (conservation fails) — the sentence is true;
- for every $\gamma\le1/2$ no DAG mints — the threshold is not merely sufficient, it is tight.

A reviewer reading L105 as a statement about the immediately preceding three-hop chain gets the wrong
number; a reviewer reading it as written gets the right one. The defect is therefore **referent
ambiguity**, not a false claim, and "the γ threshold is wrong at first use" overstates it.
(Secondary nit: "conserve **nothing**" is hyperbole — the scheme conserves a finite bound
$\gamma/(1-\gamma)$, e.g. $1.5\times$ at $\gamma=0.6$; it fails to conserve, but it does not lose
all structure.)

### Where the placement complaint is right, and where it is imprecise

The correction, `paper5.tex` L126–129, verbatim:

> **Numbers by hand.** The refuting chain: $0.9+0.9^2+0.9^3=0.9+0.81+0.729=2.44>1$ --- three
> budget-legal hops, and the closure credits $2.44$ witnessed units where only one exists. *Now you
> try:* at $\gamma=0.8$, does the same full-weight chain still mint under the budget-only phrasing?
> ($0.8+0.64+0.512=1.952>1$ --- yes. Note the threshold carefully: *this three-hop chain* crosses $1$
> at $\gamma\approx0.5437$, the root of $\gamma+\gamma^2+\gamma^3=1$, not at $\gamma=1/2$. What is true
> at $\gamma>1/2$ is that *some* chain mints, since $\sum_{d\ge1}\gamma^d=\gamma/(1-\gamma)>1$ there ---
> the depth required grows as $\gamma$ falls, needing five hops at $\gamma=0.51$ and only two at
> $\gamma=0.9$; under transfer semantics the same chain's live total stays exactly $\le 1$ [verified].)

Placement, measured rather than asserted: the correction is **23 source lines** after L105, on the far
side of the entire Theorem 1 box (L108–124), and it begins mid-sentence inside a parenthesis that opens
as the answer key to a "Now you try" exercise — the reader who does not do the exercise never reaches
it. It is **not** "8 lines deep into a worked example" (it is 2 lines into a 4-line paragraph, but that
paragraph is a full boxed theorem away from the claim it repairs). So: the *characterisation* of the
placement as poor is accurate and if anything understated; the *line arithmetic* in the flag is wrong.
The correction also does the substantive job the flag credits it with, and does it correctly — its
"what is true at $\gamma>1/2$" gloss matches my derivation above exactly.

### The error the review missed

The figure cited *in the very sentence at L105* is the weak link, and it is branch-dependent:

- On the review branch, `fig-r12-regime.tex`'s caption is right: "Budget-only (copy) semantics crosses
  the mint line at $\gamma\approx0.544$ at this depth --- the plotted points $(0.5,0.875)$ and
  $(0.5833,1.1221)$ bracket the crossing; the familiar $\gamma=1/2$ is the crossing in the depth limit,
  where $\sum_{d\ge1}\gamma^d=\gamma/(1-\gamma)$."
- On the branch currently checked out (`8e7ef9465`), the same caption reads: "Budget-only (copy)
  semantics crosses the mint line at $\gamma=1/2$ and reaches $2.44$ at $\gamma=0.9$." That is **false

Also, both at L104 and again at L126 the paper writes $0.9+0.81+0.729=2.44$. The sum is **2.439**.
Rounding presented as an equality, twice, in the paper's flagship hand-checkable number.

### What this means for the fix

- Do not "fix the threshold" at L105 — it is correct. Fix the *referent*: e.g. "Budgets without
  debiting conserve nothing for $\gamma>\tfrac12$ *at sufficient depth* (this three-hop chain crosses

  parenthetical shrink or go.
- Confirm which branch the fix lands on; if it lands on `claude/harbor-paper-craft-skill`, the

## A19 — spawn-to-person's probation theorem is stale relative to the amended Theorem 4

**Verdict: CONFIRMED — and the problem is larger than the flag says. The whitepaper's theorem is not
merely stale relative to paper5; it contradicts a hypothesis supplied two theorems earlier in its own
text, and its proof sketch's exchange step is stated incorrectly.**

### Evidence — the whitepaper, `website-v2/public/whitepaper/spawn-to-person.tex` L974–982, verbatim

> **Theorem (Front-Loaded Probation Dominance)** `\label{thm:probation-dominance}`
> Let a newcomer restriction schedule specify earning holdbacks $g_t \ge 0$ across periods
> $t \in \{0,1,\dots,T\}$. Let honest participants have patient discount factor $\delta_h\in(0,1)$
> while strategic whitewashers (who seek short-term defection gains $G_{\max}$) have impatient discount
> factor $\delta_f<\delta_h$.
>
> Under the deterrence constraint $\sum_{t=0}^T \delta_f^t g_t \ge G_{\max}$, the schedule that
> minimizes the lifetime friction on honest newcomers $\sum_{t=0}^T \delta_h^t g_t$ is a **pure
> front-loaded cliff**: all holdback mass is concentrated at $t=0$ ($g_0=G_{\max}$, $g_{t>0}=0$). Any
> gradual linear ramp is strictly Pareto-dominated by an upfront probation hurdle.
>
> *Proof sketch.* Consider a standard linear programming minimization of $\sum_t \delta_h^t g_t$
> subject to $\sum_t \delta_f^t g_t \ge G_{\max}$ **and $g_t\ge0$**. … By standard exchange arguments,
> moving holdback mass from period $t>0$ to period $0$ relaxes the deterrence constraint while strictly
> decreasing the honest cost $\sum_t \delta_h^t g_t$. …

`grep` for `\le L`, "per-period cap", "ceiling cap" over the whole 1940-line whitepaper returns
**nothing**. The only constraint on $g_t$ anywhere in the theorem or its proof is $g_t\ge0$. Confirmed:
the ceiling hypothesis is absent, and the pure spike $g_0=G_{\max}$ is stated as *the* minimizer
unconditionally.

Paper5's amended version, for direct comparison (`paper5.tex` L292–299): "…*among schedules the
ceiling can actually carry*. That qualification is the theorem's missing hypothesis and it binds. …
with a per-period cap $g_t\le L$, the pure spike … is infeasible whenever $G_{\max}>L$ … the programme
is infeasible *at every schedule shape* when $G_{\max}>L/(1-\delta_f)$."

### Two things I found that strengthen the flag

1. **The whitepaper supplies the missing cap itself, 30 lines earlier.** `thm:whitewash-cost`
   (L933–949) defines the newcomer ceiling $\kappa_0$, the steady-state ceiling $\kappa^\star$, and
   $W=\sum_t(\pi(\kappa^\star)-\pi(\kappa_t))$ with $\kappa_0\le\kappa_t\le\kappa^\star$ — which bounds
   each period's holdback by $\pi(\kappa^\star)-\pi(\kappa_0)$, exactly the cap $L$. And the surrounding
   prose (L920–922, L962–964) insists the instrument is a *reduced economic ceiling*, i.e. a bounded
   quantity. So `thm:probation-dominance` sets $g_0=G_{\max}$ with $G_{\max}$ unbounded, in a document
   that has already told the reader $g_t$ is capped. This is an internal inconsistency, independent of
   paper5.
2. **The proof sketch's exchange step is stated wrongly.** "moving holdback mass from period $t>0$ to
   period $0$ relaxes the deterrence constraint **while strictly decreasing the honest cost**" is false
   for a one-for-one move: shifting mass $m$ from $t$ to $0$ changes honest cost from $\delta_h^t m$ to
   $m>\delta_h^t m$ — it *increases*. The valid exchange holds deterrence *tight*: replace mass $m$ at
   $t$ with $\delta_f^t m$ at $t=0$, giving the same deterrence at honest cost $\delta_f^t m<\delta_h^t m$.
   Paper5's box states it correctly ("moving gap mass from any later period to any earlier one,
   **holding the deterrence constraint tight**, strictly lowers $H$"); the whitepaper's version does not.
3. Minor: "strictly Pareto-dominated" is a misnomer for a single-objective LP with one constraint;
   "strictly dominated" is what is meant.

The Status note at L984 additionally imports paper5's sweep numbers ("76,000 candidate schedules") —
which, per A17 above, were generated with no cap, so the whitepaper's evidence line inherits the same
staleness.

### What this means for the fix

The whitepaper's theorem needs the same amendment paper5 already carries — and the amendment is cheaper
here, because the cap is already named in `thm:whitewash-cost`: state $0\le g_t\le \pi(\kappa^\star)-\pi(\kappa_0)$
in the hypotheses, restate the conclusion as bang-bang fill-from-$t{=}0$, and add the
$G_{\max}>L/(1-\delta_f)$ infeasibility remark. The proof sketch's exchange sentence should be replaced
with the deterrence-tight version regardless — it is wrong as written even in the uncapped model.

## Parfit — §1 versus §7

Not a verdict; a judgment, offered as one.

The two passages are doing different jobs, and the review's read is broadly right, though "decorative"
is harsher than the §1 passage deserves. §1 (L46–52) transfers a *stance*: Parfit dissolved rather than
answered the identity question in fission cases, "this paper takes the same exit for a different
substrate," and therefore "any mechanism whose safety depends on resolving the ship of Theseus is
unbuildable." Run Gentner's test on it — does the mapping license a candidate inference in the target
that the reader can then check? — and it comes up nearly empty. The relation carried across is a single
predicate ("this question can be refused"), not a system of relations, and the one apparently
substantive consequence, the unbuildability line, does not actually *follow* from the Parfit mapping:
it follows from the ordinary engineering observation that you cannot build a safety property on a
predicate with no determinate truth value, and would stand verbatim with Parfit struck out. Nothing in
§1 tells the reader what the ledger conserves, why it must be conserved, or what would go wrong if it
were not. The passage licenses permission, not prediction.

§7 (L360–368) is the structural version, and it is structural precisely because it maps the relation
*and* then breaks it. It aligns the move (dissolution, borrowed), separates it from the thesis (not
borrowed), quotes the thesis it declines — Parfit's "the relation of the original person to each of the
resulting people contains all that interests us --- all that matters" — and derives the disanalogy:
"Theorem 1 makes the inherited prior scarce, conserved and divisible precisely so that it *must* split
eight ways among eight forks. A reputation ledger is exactly the kind of thing Parfit argues
psychological continuity is not." That is a checkable candidate inference in the target: it predicts
that the fork case in a ledger economy behaves *oppositely* to the fork case in Parfit — his fission
does not divide what matters, ours must — and Theorem 1 is right there to check it against. A reader
who did not already know what no-mint inheritance implies learns something from that sentence.

Where I would resist forcing a verdict: §1's passage is not ornamental filler, and a paper whose entire
framing is a refusal of metaphysics does owe the reader an early signal that the refusal has a
respectable pedigree. The honest criticism is narrower than "decorative" — §1's version is *redundant*
now that §7 exists, since both introduce the same borrowed move and only §7 does anything with it, and
§1 spends six lines and a full retelling of the teletransporter case on what a clause could carry. If
it is compressed rather than cut, the retelling is the part to lose. (One accuracy nit while there:
Parfit's canonical *fission* case is My Division, the hemispheres transplanted into two bodies; the
teletransporter belongs to the Branch-Line family. Calling the teletransporter the fission case is
defensible for the double-replica variant but conflates two thought experiments that Parfit keeps
apart.)
