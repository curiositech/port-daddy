# Continuation after an unknown remote effect

## Decision and scope

The decision is whether a successor may send a second attempt for one work unit after the first caller crashes without a durable local result. A non-idempotent remote effect is counted as `effects += 1`; safety means `effects <= 1`. The finite checker covers one operation `O`, epochs 0 and 1, at most one old call and one successor retry, one effect location, and all interleavings of its declared transitions. It proves no property of deployed Harbor, arbitrary numbers of retries, multiple effect locations, or a real provider's contract.

The durable outbox contains an intent before the first send: work-unit identity, stable operation identity `O`, stable remote key `K`, target, input digest, the authorized effect and epoch 0. The successor inherits the same intent, obligations and uncertainty status. An acknowledgment can be lost after the remote effect; a local result receipt is a distinct durable transition. The remote dedup record, when available, is **atomic with the effect and result**. A local receipt alone cannot repair a missing remote record. A cached result may be read after epoch advancement to reconcile an old operation. Dedup protects this operation's effect count; denying stale actor authority for other effects requires a separate effect-boundary guard.

## Transition system and observations

The state is `(intent, old_sent, old_pending, old_done, old_result, crashed, epoch, remote_fence, observed, retry_sent, retry_pending, retry_done, effects, dedup, local_receipt, stopped, dropped)`. Boolean fields have their literal meanings; `epoch ∈ {0,1}`, `observed ∈ {unknown,absent,present}`, `dedup ⊆ {K,J}`, and `effects ∈ {0,1,2}` on explored shortest paths. `old_pending` and `retry_pending` include calls in transit that the crashed caller cannot inspect. A network loss marks the old call terminal **without** setting `old_result`; it cannot produce a success receipt. Remote commit is an atomic effect-boundary transition. A crash leaves in-flight calls intact and may occur before or after remote commit and before or after a durable local receipt. A successor promotion changes the assigned actor; in the readback adapter it additionally installs a durable remote fence serialized with remote effects. Remote receipt lookup/readback and local receipt persistence occur separately.

The caller's observation is its durable intent, crash/epoch assignment, acknowledged remote-fence installation, last readback result, known retry send, local receipt and stop status. It cannot see remote pending calls, network loss, actual effect count or dedup internals. A diagnostic `absent` is a snapshot, not a certificate that an unseen old call will never arrive. The checker constructs two reachable worlds with identical caller observations after an `absent` read: in one the old call remains pending, in the other it was lost. In the first, old remote commit remains enabled. Thus, with no dedup and no remote fence, retry is outside the common safe-policy set. Examples of safe policies are never resending while awaiting authoritative evidence, and stopping for reconciliation. These are examples from the no-second-send argument, not an exhaustive synthesized controller. If authoritative external reconciliation proves an outcome by a stronger contract, it may enlarge the set; this model does not assume such a contract.

For an observation class `C`, let `Safe(C) = ⋂_{s∈C} {π : every finite modeled continuation trace from s under policy π satisfies effects <= 1}`. The quantifier includes every enabled network and remote transition. A policy that sends a retry is assessed through both pending remote completions; the send step alone is never counted as a safety proof. If evidence refines `C` to `C' ⊆ C` while the policy and transition contracts stay fixed, then `Safe(C) ⊆ Safe(C')`. This monotonicity is about safety over the declared finite continuations; it promises neither completion nor a viable fair schedule. Operationally a continuation capsule needs the stable identity, obligations and evidence that determine the same safe-policy class. It need not reproduce an actor's whole transcript merely to preserve this decision, though other obligations may demand more data.

### Adapters and allowed actions

| Adapter | Exact capability and observation | Action after crash | Safety condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Atomic dedup and result | Remote serializes check of stable `K`, effect and durable result record at one effect boundary. Pending or completed duplicate requests with `K` cannot create another effect. | Retry `O,K`, or fetch its result, across actor epochs. | `K` stays bound to this logical operation and the result/dedup record remains until no old or successor retry can be admitted. This row proves duplicate-effect safety for `O,K`, not a general stale-epoch authority rule. |
| Authoritative readback and fence | A durable remote epoch fence rejects epoch 0 at the **actual effect boundary**. A later linearizable read of `O` returns exact committed outcome and result, or absence, ordered after that fence. | Persist a present result; on absence, send one epoch-1 attempt. | The fence is installed before the read; all earlier commits are reflected; old requests cannot commit after it. An in-flight epoch-1 attempt would need its own admission discipline before another retry. |
| Neither | No durable dedup/result contract and no remotely enforced fence/readback pair. | Stop and seek authoritative external reconciliation. | No automatic retry from silence, timeout, outbox entry or missing local receipt. |

The readback row is stronger than a plain GET. A linearizable negative GET alone is insufficient: an old request still in transit can commit after the GET. The remote fence must order with the effect and reject stale calls **when they actually reach that boundary**; a control-plane owner flag or a check only before send does not do this. An authoritative read must also resolve the specific operation and its result, rather than infer it from a coarse current resource value that may have changed for another reason.

## Finite checks and counterexamples

Run `python3 skills/harbor-results/scripts/continuation_observation_model.py` from the repository root. It is standard-library-only and import-safe; direct execution prints deterministic JSON and writes no files. BFS exhausts 23 honest dedup states, 24 honest fenced-readback states and 17 honest stop states, with no `effects > 1` and no local success receipt without a remote effect. State counts are for the exact finite alphabet above; they are neither a population estimate nor an unbounded proof. Four weakened variants yield shortest traces:

| Weakened guard | Shortest trace length | Decisive suffix |
|---|---:|---|
| Retry after a negative read alone | 8 | read absent; old call commits; retry commits |
| Admit stale epoch at effect boundary | 8 | fence; read absent; old call commits despite fence; retry commits |
| Change operation key on retry | 7 | old `K` commits; successor retries with `J`; second effect commits |
| Drop dedup result while retry admissible | 8 | old `K` commits; record evicted; same-key retry commits again |

Each trace begins with durable intent, first send, crash and successor promotion. The checker asserts honest safety and requires a violating witness for every mutation. Its shortest paths are shortest **within these transitions**, not minimum network failures in a production system.

## Safety argument and progress boundary

For dedup, induction over effect-boundary steps suffices: the only first commit of `O,K` atomically installs its result; any later `K` request reads that record and makes no effect. This induction requires the record's retention and stable identity conditions above. For fenced readback, any old commit either precedes the fence and is visible in the ordered read, or follows the fence and is rejected. If the read says absent, at most the single admitted epoch-1 attempt can commit; if present, there is no retry. The stop adapter never sends a second attempt. These arguments are conditional on the adapter contracts; the script checks their finite realization and exposes violations when selected conditions are removed.

Safety does not imply completion. Dedup permits progress only if the remote store remains durable, duplicate calls rendezvous with the record, retries are eventually delivered, and the operation eventually finishes or reports an explicit permanent error. Fenced readback permits progress only if fence installation and ordered read eventually complete and the successor attempt is eventually delivered. Under neither capability, conservative stop preserves safety but may remain unresolved indefinitely; operator or provider reconciliation is an additional capability, not a fairness consequence. A durable outbox plus blind at-least-once delivery is a baseline for not losing work, but it does not establish at-most-once non-idempotent effects. A durable outbox paired with stable-key remote idempotency is the direct baseline for safe automatic continuation.

## Relation to existing work and operational value

The result-record baseline is established prior art. RIFL requires a unique RPC identifier, an atomic durable completion record containing the result, retry rendezvous and guarded garbage collection; its paper also distinguishes in-progress and stale calls. [Lee et al., *Implementing Linearizability at Large Scale and Low Latency*, §§3–4](https://web.stanford.edu/~ouster/cgi-bin/papers/rifl.pdf). The Harbor contribution here is a bounded decision table and falsification harness for a specific cross-actor continuation question, not a new generic RPC impossibility theorem or a novelty claim.

Paper 2's controllability discussion motivates making the remote effect boundary the enforcement point; Paper 5's migration model concerns inherited score attribution and does not settle unknown external effects. The [corpus reading ledger](corpus-reading-20260928.md) identifies those gaps. The operational test for a candidate provider is concrete: can it bind an operation ID through retries, atomically retain and return its result, or install an effect-boundary fence and provide exact ordered readback? If neither contract can be demonstrated, the work unit remains pending for reconciliation. No provider API, Harbor deployment, or field outcome was tested here.
