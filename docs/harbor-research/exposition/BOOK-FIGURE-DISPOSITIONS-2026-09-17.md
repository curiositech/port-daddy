## Figures that must remain distinct

Similarity of vocabulary or visual family is not evidence of duplication. The following pairs should both remain, provided each passes its own acceptance test.

1. **Containment versus ceremony.** Figure 1.12 (`fig:swk-delegation-chains`) is a conceptual containment claim: coordination lineage is nested within cryptographic authorization. Chapter 2’s `fig:anchor-handshake-ladder` and `fig:anchor-delegation-inline` are protocol evidence: exact messages, offline boundaries, and per-hop provenance. Preserve the containment figure and the ceremony/delegation figures because they answer different reader questions.

2. **Qualitative account versus quantitative amount.** Figures 3.13 and 3.14 are not duplicates. Figure 3.13 says three residual descriptions are one debt account; Figure 3.14 computes the content and timing contributions on a common scale. They may share a page or adjacent exposition, but they should not be collapsed into one grammar.

3. **Cycle condition versus fractional cochain arithmetic.** Current Figure 8.6, `fig:fh-cycle-vs-cut` (source `website-v2/public/whitepaper/figures/fig-fh-cycle-vs-cut.tex`, PDF p. 506, folio 488), shows the topological condition: the same disagreement is visible on a cycle and invisible on a path. Current Figure 8.7, `fig:fh-cochain-hand` (source `website-v2/public/whitepaper/figures/fig-fh-cochain-hand.tex`, PDF p. 508, folio 490), shows a different result: the same size-3 edge disagreement under three topologies, including the hand-solved fractional potential `x=(0,0.75,1.5,2.25)` and residual `r=1.5`. Both should remain because one proves *when topology exposes the lie* and the other shows *how the fractional least-squares residual is produced*. The present 8.7 drawing prints only edge values and residuals; to earn its separate place, its next acceptance pass should make the fractional potential/residual construction visible in the figure rather than leaving it solely in following prose.

4. **Transfer primitive versus incident composition.** Current Figure 8.3, `fig:fh-xfer` (source `website-v2/public/whitepaper/figures/fig-fh-xfer-ceremony.tex`, PDF p. 495, folio 477), is the four-message cross-harbor sequence: which two messages cross the boundary, what each binds, and why Harbor B verifies alone after message 3. Keep both: 8.3 owns the exact transfer ceremony; 8.12 owns ordering across four primitives. Do not duplicate the four-message ceremony inside the worked incident. The Book already correctly suppresses the Chapter 6 copy under `\ifpdbook` and points to Chapter 8, consistent with the adjudication in `docs/harbor-research/exposition/critique/ADJUDICATION-2026-09-08.md:71`.
