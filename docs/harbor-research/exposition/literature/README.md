Four outside reviews of the Book, one per part, fifty ideas in all, each written by a reviewer told to ignore the Book's own framing and look everywhere: what other fields call each idea, who has solved or attacked it and by what means, and whether the formulation holds. Every citation in the four files is marked `[verified]` (fetched or confirmed by a live search this session) or `[unverified]`; nothing was invented, and where a search found no prior work the file says so, because that is itself a finding.

## The short answer

The Book is on firm ground where it matters most, and the reviewers were surprised by how much of the literature review the chapters had already done themselves: Bainbridge, Endsley, Green and Swets, Dorfman, Sleator–Tarjan, Locke, Parfit, Akerlof, Douceur, Friedman–Resnick, Myerson–Satterthwaite, Rochet–Tirole, Ostrom, FLP, Dechter–Meiri–Pearl, Herlihy–Kozlov–Rajsbaum, Hansen–Ghrist, PeerReview, and Certificate Transparency are all cited, all real, and all applied correctly. No theorem was found to be wrong as stated. What the reviews found instead falls into three bins, and the bins are the work list.

**Known results restated under private names.** The information floor is the classical covering bound from group testing; the split-digest theorem is Schmeidler–Yaari comonotonicity; the costly-escalation threshold is Spence's signalling model (the chapter even says "the Spence mold" without citing Spence); "reputation is amortized verification" is Kreps–Wilson and Fudenberg–Levine's continuation value; the non-forgeable-identity necessity theorem is a Douceur/Cheng–Friedman special case; the Myerson–Satterthwaite boundary is the textbook impossibility instantiated numerically. Part I adds two of the same kind: the fencing epoch on the single-writer rail is the fencing token Chubby shipped (Burrows 2006) and Kleppmann named, and "run the world twice" in the sealed room is self-composition (Barthe, D'Argenio, and Rezk 2004), a real verification technique re-derived under a plain-English description. None of these is a defect in the mathematics. Each is a place where the Book should say the public name first and its own second, and each file's closing table lists the coinage against the term the field uses.

**Prior work the Book never engaged.** The inspection tower does not cite Kofman–Lawarrée (1993) or Tirole (1986), the two canonical papers on collusion between an auditor and the audited, nor the cryptoeconomic literature (TrueBit, Arbitrum's bisection, proof-of-stake slashing) that converged on the same "who audits the auditor" shape. No-mint inheritance has a twenty-five-year-old cousin in Levien's Advogato max-flow trust metric. The operator's instrument reinvents Sheridan's and Parasuraman–Sheridan–Wickens's levels of automation and ignores Klein's naturalistic decision making. Context paging under a corrupted pin oracle sits next to Lykouris–Vassilvitskii's algorithms with predictions. The federation chapter cites OpenID Federation, UCAN, and macaroons but never ActivityPub, Matrix, or SPKI/SDSI, the three production federations closest to its own design. The personal-identity grounding takes Parfit's move without answering Williams's intuition flip or Olson's animalism. Part I's edges are the same shape: the reference-monitor discussion never reaches seL4, gVisor, or Firecracker, which is where its own same-machine-adversary open problem lives; capability attenuation cites Macaroons but not Miller's object-capability thesis or SPKI/SDSI; the declassification apparatus stops at Sabelfeld and Myers and never reaches Sabelfeld and Sands' dimensions; the SPRT canary is missing Jung and Paxson's threshold random walk; the WAL caveat lacks Pillai et al.'s crash-consistency measurements; and the Anchor chapter neither claims nor disclaims the algorithm-confusion attacks RFC 8725 catalogues. These are the citations the next revision owes, and most are a paragraph each.

**Contested or open, and honestly labelled.** The deontic tractable-then-NP-complete frontier has a near-identical dichotomy in the regulatory-compliance line (Colombo Tosatto, Governatori, van Beest), which paper 6 admits it had not searched; the fragment and the choice of conflict-freedom as the decision problem survive as novel, the framing does not. Cross-harbor two-of-three settlement is an open problem the Book already labels as one. Coalition cancellation is a boundary the Book's own new method draws around itself. The bandit critique of scalar reputation is unproven by the authors' own admission. Part I's one taxonomy of its own, the assurance ladder (Observed, Coordinated, Brokered, Confined, Attested), is not wrong but is not yet placed against Sheridan's levels of automation or the Common Criteria's assurance levels, the two public ladders it most resembles.

## What the Book gets right that most whitepapers do not

Three things recur across all four reviews. The chapters carry real related-work sections with real citations, so the reviewers' job was to find what was missing rather than what was false. Every claim is labelled by kind, so a reviewer could tell a theorem from a design invariant from a hypothesis without guessing. And the Book already names its own limits (the five silences, coalition cancellation, the unquantified subsidy structure, the stipulated discount factor), so the reviews mostly confirm the Book's own map of its edges rather than redraw it.

## One factual correction found

Part III: an earlier edition's bibliography cited Liu and Skrzypacz (2014) as *Review of Economic Studies*; the confirmed venue is *Journal of Economic Theory* 151, and the current chapters already carry it (ledger row LR-312 verified this rather than re-applying it).

## What happens next

Each file ends with a reading list per idea and a table of private coinages against public names. The revision that follows should, in this order: add the missing canonical citations (a paragraph each, listed above); put the public name before the private one wherever the closing tables show a coinage; reframe the deontic frontier against the compliance-checking dichotomy; engage ActivityPub, Matrix, and SPKI/SDSI in the federation chapter's related work; place the assurance ladder beside levels of automation and the Common Criteria; state the algorithm-confusion boundary in the Anchor chapter's limitations; and correct the Liu–Skrzypacz venue. None of these changes a theorem. The first pass of that revision is on the branch `wave-16/credit-the-canon`, with one row per find in `docs/harbor-research/CRITIQUE-LEDGER.md` (the `LR-` rows).

The second pass, `wave-18/contested-and-open` (`LR-5xx` rows), took the third bin, where a citation was never the fix and a source had to be read. Four rows, three of them findings and the fourth a question that closed without a change. The assurance ladder is **not** a restatement: Sheridan and Verplank grade the allocation of the work, the Common Criteria's EALs grade how much independent evaluation a claim received before deployment, and the ladder grades what an adversary would have to defeat for one runtime claim about one mechanism to be false — three independent axes, and the chapter now exhibits the corners. The deontic frontier is **orthogonal** to Colombo Tosatto, Governatori and van Beest rather than inside or outside their dichotomy: their tractability turns on literals-versus-formulae in the obligation, and $\mathcal{L}_c$ sits on the hard side of that coordinate and is polynomial anyway, because their hardness comes from quantifying universally over a process model's exponentially many traces and $\mathcal{L}_c$ has no process model to branch. The Anchor chapter turned out not to be silent on algorithm confusion but self-contradicting — its properties table claimed the ProVerif result its limitations section disclaimed — and the prose now carries the attack, the counter-trace, and the model. The Liu–Skrzypacz venue needed no change and was re-verified from the publisher record rather than trusted from the earlier row.
