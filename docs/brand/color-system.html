Port Daddy — Color as an Information Language

A wheel-complete palette where every hue carries a meaning. Harmony first, semantics by construction.

The helm mark already speaks in four colors. This rounds them into a full hue hexad — so the brand can narrate state: what's confirmed, what's pending, what failed, what a machine did on its own. The point isn't decoration. It's that a glance should carry information.

The harmony scheme

  • Spine: cobalt (218°) and amber (42°) sit ~176° apart — a complementary pair. Maximum contrast, the two loudest signals: "control" vs "attention."
  • Fill: the remaining hues land at roughly even stops around the wheel (seafoam 165, violet 262, magenta 326, mayday 358) — an extended hexad. Even spacing = maximal categorical distinguishability.
  • Cohesion: every hue shares a high-chroma, mid-value register on ink, so they read as one family even while being six categories. (Tints/shades give per-hue ramps.)
  • Neutrals: a slate ramp (idle/historical) + ink/paper grounds carry everything that isn't a signal — so signals stay rare and meaningful.

The tokens & their jobs

Each hue is a semantic role, not a decoration. The rule: if a color appears, it means something.
