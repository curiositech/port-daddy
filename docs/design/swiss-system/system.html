Port Daddy · design system · draft 1

The Swiss system, taken off the page and onto the screen.

One grotesk. An 8pt grid nothing leaves. Colour that carries meaning rather than mood. Six dot states, eight border patterns, four motion durations — and hard rules about how few of them you may use at once.

§ 01 · type

One grotesk, working at every size.

Müller-Brockmann did not pair faces; he set one well. Archivo is the grotesk here — a real Helvetica-lineage design with a variable width axis, so the oversized numerals are an expanded cut of the same family rather than a second font pretending to belong. IBM Plex Mono carries machine truth: ids, timestamps, hexes, anything the system asserts rather than says.

Chosen by register, not by habit. The house typography reference sorts faces by the register a thing is written in, and this book and this site sit in Quiet Swiss / neo-grotesque. Its libre answer for that register is Archivo — which is also its named replacement for Helvetica and Neue Haas, on the grounds that "neutral" stopped being neutral the moment everyone agreed it was.

Rejected, and why. Inter is "competent and invisible to the point of anonymity". Geist "ships with every Next.js starter — I scaffolded this an hour ago". Fraunces is "the soft serif that became the indie-SaaS display serif", and a display serif fights a Swiss grid rather than sitting in it. Those three plus a gradient are a recognised look — the one that reads as generated rather than designed. The research report this system descends from used all three; it comes back to the repository re-cut onto this stack.

Two families, and a mono makes three. The pairing rule is one workhorse plus one voice, capped at two families — with a monospace permitted as the third when it carries code or data. Here the workhorse and the voice are the same family at different widths, which is the tightest form of the rule, and IBM Plex Mono is the third, doing only machine truth.

§ 02 · the crossing

Type that changes colour because of what is behind it.

The headline is drawn once in ink and once in paper. The paper copy is clipped to exactly the slab's rectangle, so the switch is a hard edge, not a blend — and it costs nothing per frame: the clip is written into two custom properties on resize and never touched again. Drag the window and watch the edge stay exact.

A spawn is not a soul. It is a position in a ledger that other positions are willing to settle against.
§ 03 · colour

Hue is the meaning, not the mood.

Four slab colours, one per part of the argument, and they never trade places. A slab is always full-bleed, always square-cornered, always carries paper-coloured text, and never has an outline or a shadow — the colour is the boundary. In dark mode the hue is identical and only the lightness moves, so a part is the same colour on paper and on a screen at night.

Status tones are a separate register and never borrow a part colour, with one deliberate exception: running is the machine blue, because a thing in flight is the kernel working.

§ 04 · liveness

Six states. One dot. No spinner beside it.

A dot may pulse only while fresh heartbeat or stream evidence is actually arriving. Every other state is still. A dot that animates when nothing is happening teaches the eye to ignore movement — and then the one moving dot that matters goes unread.

6
runningWork is in flight right now and evidence is arriving.pulses
okA durable receipt exists. The claim is settled.still
warnProceeding, but into something the operator should see.still
errorFailed, with a typed code and exactly one next action.still
blockedWaiting on a human, a guard, or an arbitration.still
idleStopped and making no way. Not a failure.still

The dot restates a colour the row already carries in its left stripe, so the state survives colour-blindness and monochrome printing. There is no seventh state: "unknown" is idle with the text saying so, because inventing a colour for absence of knowledge is how status systems rot.

§ 05 · fractional borders

Eight patterns. Use two. Mix none.

Three lineages meet here: register marks from offset lithography, colour blocks from Swiss modernism, and instrumentation from Bauhaus and Braun. Each pattern must mark something specific — panel scope, selection, focus, state. Three or more in one surface reads as decorated chaos, which is the opposite of restraint.

Use · primary state

The two-at-most rule, shown both ways

Left: exactly two patterns, each meaning something — a left stripe for state (2.4) and corner brackets for panel scope (2.1). Right: five at once, drawn deliberately badly.

Two patterns

The stripe says which state this row is in. The brackets say where the panel ends. Remove either and you lose a specific piece of information — which is the test.

Five patterns

Stripe, dashed outline, corner brackets, a gradient and a drop shadow. Nothing here marks anything; the chrome is standing in for a hierarchy the layout does not have.

The diagnostic. Remove every fractional border. Does the surface still communicate its hierarchy through colour, spacing and type alone? If yes, the brackets are refinement. If no, they are hiding the absence of a system.

§ 06 · motion

Four durations. Two easings. Transform and opacity only.

Everything that moves moves on the compositor — no width, no height, no top, no left, no filter, nothing that forces layout. That is what "jitter-free" actually means in practice, and it is why this page does not stutter when you scroll it during an animation.

4
Tap · 120ms
--t-tap · acknowledge a press
Move · 180ms
--t-move · a state changes
Enter · 260ms
--t-enter · a thing arrives
Slab · 420ms
--t-slab · a full-bleed block resolves

Click or focus-and-press any panel. Both easings are asymmetric on purpose: --ease-out for anything the operator caused, so it answers instantly and settles; --ease-in-out for anything the system caused, so it reads as a considered move rather than a jolt. Every animation on this page stops dead under prefers-reduced-motion, including the dot's halo.

§ 07 · signal faces

Every page flies one flag.

Two-block micro-faces that keep the real flag's colours, from the 1969 International Code of Signals. They carry a registered meaning and are never decorative aliases — a page that flies Papa is saying "prepared to proceed", and if it is not, it must not fly Papa.

M · Mike — stopped, making no way

Romeo has no single-letter meaning in the 1969 code — it is the procedure acknowledgement "Received", and the "way is off my ship" gloss is 1931 folklore that survives on decorative flag charts. This system does not repeat it.

§ 08 · the rules

What earns its place, and what dates immediately.

Earns its place
  • Brackets ≥ 12px, landing on the 8pt grid
  • Each pattern marks one specific thing — scope, focus, state
  • One accent, at most two, against a strict neutral matrix
  • The grid underneath is invisible but real
  • Removing the brackets would cost refinement, not hierarchy
Dates immediately
  • Brackets under 10px — reads as a render glitch
  • Three or more bracket styles in one view
  • Decoration without semantics: every card bracketed, so none mean anything
  • Three accent colours competing on one panel
  • Dashed strokes as persistent UI — always reads as a draft
  • A box-shadow stacked under a fractional border — collapses the whole idea
