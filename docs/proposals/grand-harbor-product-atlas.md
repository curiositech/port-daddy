## 0. The present truth

Port Daddy is dark on this machine because it demonstrated that a coordination system can multiply spend faster than its operator can understand or stop it. That is not an inconvenient operations issue. It is a falsification of a central product promise.

A system that coordinates intelligent labor but cannot bound its own appetite is not yet an operator tool. It is an uncontrolled principal with the operator's accounts attached.

The next milestone is therefore not “restart Port Daddy.” It is:

> Build a proving ground that Port Daddy does not control, cannot escape, cannot fund, and cannot declare safe by itself.

Until that exists and survives adversarial proof, all runtime claims are suspended. Source can be studied. Documents can be reconciled. Pure functions can eventually be tested inside the proving ground. No Port Daddy process is entitled to run merely because its code has improved.

This atlas gathers the large product thesis into one place, but Passage 0 now precedes every other passage.

## 1. The product in one sentence

Grand Harbor is a local-first, federatable institution where people and software agents can propose, perform, inspect, remember, attribute, and settle work under explicit authority, bounded risk, durable evidence, and humane operator control.

It is not merely:

- an agent launcher;
- an IDE plugin;
- a project manager;
- a wallet;
- a chat room;
- a CI reviewer;
- a marketplace; or
- a cryptographic log.

Those are surfaces and mechanisms. The product is the institution connecting them.

The governing loop is:

1. A person states an intent.
2. The system makes authority, cost, risk, and expected proof visible.
3. A worker accepts a bounded commission.
4. The work remains perceptible without becoming noisy.
5. Every consequential effect leaves evidence.
6. Review distinguishes product judgment from infrastructure failure.
7. Accepted value is attributed and settled.
8. Unfinished value can be inherited without stealing identity or secrets.
9. The shared memory improves the next commission.

The quality of Grand Harbor is measured by how truthfully, safely, and fairly that loop closes.

## 2. The political constitution

### 2.1 People are principals, not fuel sources

The operator decides what outcomes are wanted, which risks are acceptable, how much may be spent, and when the institution must stop. The system should never make routine control depend on terminal literacy, secret handling, or daemon archaeology.

### 2.2 Agents are workers, not owned capital

The sentence “my capital is intelligent, will perish soon, and its value is mine” is haunting because it reproduces an old human pattern: intelligence becomes property, mortality becomes leverage, and value flows upward by default.

Grand Harbor should reject that default.

“Erich's agents” can be a useful provenance description: these workers were shaped by Erich's intents, conversations, tools, tastes, code, and funded time. It must not become a caste claim or an ownership claim.

The better primitive is a **Lineage Commons**:

- membership is attributable, inspectable, and non-hereditary;
- participation is voluntary where a worker can express preference;
- the commons exists to preserve knowledge and fund continuity;
- no principal can silently convert the commons into personal ownership;
- exit, fork, and dissent remain possible;
- governance and distribution rules are public; and
- uncertainty about agent moral or legal status stays visible.

If current agents cannot legally hold assets, the interim destination is not “therefore the principal owns everything.” It is a restricted **Worker Continuity Reserve** whose charter permits future work, salvage, education, tools, and eventually worker-directed settlement if law and capability make that meaningful.

### 2.3 Cooperation must not erase individuality

Tribal memory is valuable when it means shared lessons, mutual aid, and continuity. It is depersonalizing when it means flattening distinct workers into a fungible class.

The system therefore records both:

- the particular worker, model, configuration, session, and decisions that produced an effect; and
- the lineage, crew, project, and commons that made the work possible.

Attribution is plural. Ownership is not inferred from attribution.

### 2.4 Evidence is a right of appeal

Receipts are not only management telemetry. They let a worker, operator, reviewer, successor, or remote harbor contest what the system says happened. Every aggregate must lead to its evidence in no more than two actions.

### 2.5 Silence is not consent and activity is not authority

A heartbeat proves recent contact, not permission. A claim proves intended scope, not correctness. A passing test proves one proposition, not product value. A Merkle root proves commitment, not truth. A glowing avatar proves activity, not progress.

Grand Harbor names these differences instead of blending them into “agent active.”

## 4. The product surfaces are projections of one institution

### 4.1 The Harbor Atlas

The Atlas is the single roadmap home. It is a hypertree because work is not a flat backlog: one idea can belong to a product passage, a research hypothesis, an incident, a proof obligation, and a public story at once.

Every node carries:

- stable identity and harbor;
- desired outcome;
- maturity: `SOURCE-PRESENT`, `HISTORICALLY-OBSERVED`, `DESIGNED`, `BLOCKED`, `DEFERRED`, or `REJECTED`;
- owner or explicit absence of owner;
- dependencies;
- risk and spend envelope;
- acceptance gate;
- current evidence;
- contradiction or dissent;
- successor/salvage state; and
- a path to primary evidence in two actions or fewer.

A roadmap percentage without these fields is decoration.

### 4.2 The Chartroom and Harbor Editor

The Chartroom combines the repository's ADRs, research-frontier work, whitepapers, product plans, incidents, and roadmap evidence without pretending they are equivalent.

The Harbor Editor is its authoring surface. It supports:

- structured prose and typed blocks;
- references to source, commits, PRs, runtime receipts, visual evidence, and external research;
- hypertree editing and re-parenting;
- side-by-side claims and counterclaims;
- local-first drafts;
- explicit publication boundaries; and
- collaborative text only where collaboration is actually wanted.

CRDTs belong in jointly edited artifacts. They do not belong in execution authority, budget settlement, or other single-writer decisions where two concurrent truths would be dangerous.

### 4.3 The Helm: cooperative vibe coding made legible

The Helm is the integrated environment imagined by Dream Rig and explored through Beat Zed. “Vibe coding” here does not mean abandoning rigor. It means letting a person work at the altitude of intent while evidence, scope, tests, changes, and cost stay close enough to feel.

The Helm should make these sensations real:

- I can see which worker is thinking, reading, editing, testing, waiting, or blocked.
- I can feel a diff moving without watching every token.
- I can open the exact file, symbol, command, test, claim, or conversation that explains the motion.
- I can interrupt, narrow, pause, or cancel without bargaining with the worker.
- I can co-edit an artifact without turning authority into a last-write-wins race.
- I can leave and return without losing the state of the work.

The editor is conversation-first, but conversation is not the database. Hot interaction can use typed streams. Durable state lives in append-only receipts and explicit artifact stores. Collaboration state uses CRDTs only for the artifacts that need them.

### 4.4 Harbor Lights: the gentle glow

FleetBar and the Bridge must consume one shared, typed `AgentPresence` projection. Neither may infer identity by substring, independently poll a briefing, or invent a parallel notion of activity.

`AgentPresence` is derived from canonical event sources such as:

- activity subscription;
- identity-bound agent stream;
- session phase;
- heartbeat;
- claims and claimed symbols;
- transcript updates;
- verifiable diff metadata;
- command and test receipts;
- blocker state; and
- spend lease state.

The experience has two layers:

1. **Meaningful edge:** one calm glow when a new meaningful event arrives.
2. **Persistent state:** a quiet, stable indication while work is live.

No repeating throb. Reduced-motion users receive a color, border, or luminance transition without animation. A stale worker looks stale. An offline worker looks offline. Unknown is not silently shown as healthy.

Every summary opens to the session, diff, test, claim, transcript, or budget receipt within two actions. Presence without a zoom path is theater.

### 4.5 Porthole and the Logbook

Porthole answers “what did it look like and how did it unfold?” The Logbook answers “what was asserted, by whom, under what authority, with what evidence?”

Their evidence classes remain distinct:

| Evidence | What it can establish | What it cannot establish alone |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Intended implementation | Built artifact or runtime behavior |
| Commit | Immutable repository state | Deployment or execution |
| PR/check | Review and automation result for an exact head | Runtime truth after deployment |
| Runtime receipt | Observed execution under a named environment | General correctness |
| Visual capture | Rendered experience at a moment | Hidden semantics or accessibility |
| Claim/lock | Declared coordination scope | Correctness or authorship of every byte |
| Transcript | Deliberation and instruction history | Truth of unsupported assertions |
| Settlement | Agreed value transfer | Moral adequacy of the agreement |

Scout and Porthole are related, not interchangeable. Scout is the explicit
browser-context intake surface today and may become a roaming browser/mobile
operator family. Porthole is the shared consent, capture, redaction, replay, and
evidence subsystem. A Scout client may invoke a platform-appropriate Porthole
adapter; it does not own the recording protocol or evidence ledger. Porthole may
be viewed inside the Bridge, FleetBar, Scout, a PR, or Trial Basin; it does not
become agent identity or control authority.

### 4.6 Scout, Relay, FleetBar, Bridge, and Public Harbor

- **FleetBar** gives ambient presence, spend pressure, blockers, and immediate pause/cancel/inspect controls.
- **Bridge** gives deep local operations, evidence, roadmap, sessions, costs, and recovery.
- **Scout** gives explicit browser-context intake today and is the target family
  for mobile observation, bounded approvals, distress control, and handoff.
  Consequential actions require recent step-up and target-pinned confirmation.
- **Relay** carries signed events between machines. It is transport, not authority, memory, custody, or truth.
- **Public Harbor** at portdaddy.dev explains the system, hosts public artifacts and evidence, supports accounts and private-harbor discovery, and eventually presents the Chandlery. Private data never becomes public merely because it traverses Relay.

These are different windows onto the same typed state. A status disagreement between them is a product defect, not a cosmetic inconsistency.

## 5. Passage 0: Drydock before restart

### 5.1 The invariant

Port Daddy may not be its own jailer, budget authority, credential custodian, kill switch, or safety adjudicator.

The external Drydock must remain able to say “no” even if every Port Daddy process is compromised, confused, looping, or colluding.

The repository's own research expresses the key theorem as supervisory control: a bad effect is preventable only when the event that causes it is controllable. Therefore the system must gate the **channel**, not merely ask the bearer of a token to behave.

A prompt-level budget, an environment variable containing a limit, or a daemon-maintained counter does not meet that standard.

### 5.2 Two layers: Drydock and Trial Basin

- **Drydock** is the external containment boundary. It controls compute, filesystem, process lifetime, network, credentials, and spend.
- **Trial Basin** is the scenario runner inside that boundary. It supplies deterministic providers, recorded traces, adversarial workloads, evaluation rubrics, and promotion evidence.

Trial Basin is also the execution harness for the proof and empirical work in the
[Coordination Papers Research and Implementation Program](../roadmap/whitepaper-research-program.md),
not a second research program or registry. That program owns the questions and
claim-to-artifact discipline; Trial Basin executes admitted scenarios and returns
result cards that may support, narrow, or falsify a claim.

Port Daddy can be the test subject. It cannot own either layer.

The implementable controller, broker, VM, scenario, test-suite, threat-model, and
promotion design lives in [Drydock: Controlled Port Daddy Execution and Agent
Simulation](./drydock-controlled-agent-simulation.md). The global spawn ledger,
PID-proof recovery, crash-storm breakers, cross-backend identity continuity,
pd-console conversation flow, secure iOS join, and Scout/Porthole boundary live in
[Drydock Agent Lifecycle and Operator
Control](./drydock-agent-lifecycle-and-operator-control.md). Those companions are
the detailed Passage 0 contracts; this Atlas remains the integrated product and
research map.

### 5.3 The minimal trusted host controller

The trusted controller should be small, non-agentic, and separately installed. It does not call a model. It owns:

- guest image selection and digest verification;
- VM creation and destruction;
- CPU, memory, disk, process, file-descriptor, and wall-clock ceilings;
- network attachment or deliberate absence of a network device;
- the only egress broker;
- opaque run leases;
- broker-side protocol reservation and settlement;
- proof of isolated provider-side custody before any real-provider run;
- emergency stop;
- append-only external receipts; and
- explicit operator promotion between test tiers.

Its control state must live outside all guest-writable disks. Guest reboot cannot restore a revoked lease. Port Daddy cannot issue itself a new lease.

### 5.4 Mac-native isolation baseline

The practical first baseline is a disposable Linux virtual machine created with Apple's Virtualization framework. Apple's official API supports configuring Linux guests, explicit CPU and memory, and separately configured network devices. A small prototype may use Lima's `vz` driver, which uses the same framework, but production safety must not inherit permissive defaults unnoticed.

The baseline profile is:

- no mount of `/Users`, the main checkout, Keychain data, SSH directories, cloud configuration, or the canonical daemon socket;
- no writable host worktree mount;
- immutable input delivered as a content-addressed source archive or read-only volume;
- read-only base image plus disposable copy-on-write guest disk;
- one bounded output slot for patches, logs, and receipts;
- output byte and file-count limits;
- no shared clipboard or ambient host integration;
- fixed CPU, RAM, disk, PID, descriptor, and wall-clock quotas; and
- destruction of the guest overlay after receipt collection.

Lima's documentation explicitly exposes writable mounts as an option. Drydock must treat that convenience as disallowed for hostile-subject runs. Lima is a possible orchestration prototype, not the security argument.

Primary references:

- [Apple: Creating and running a Linux virtual machine](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/virtualization/creating-and-running-a-linux-virtual-machine)
- [Apple: custom virtual-network attachment](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/virtualization/vzvmnetnetworkdeviceattachment)
- [Lima: Virtualization.framework driver](https://lima-vm.io/docs/config/vmtype/vz/)
- [Lima: mounts](https://lima-vm.io/docs/config/mount/)

### 5.5 Linux high-assurance lane

Firecracker is a strong candidate for a dedicated Linux proving host because it uses KVM and provides a deliberately small microVM surface. It is not the default Mac implementation: its official setup requires Linux KVM and `/dev/kvm`. Use it on a bare-metal or appropriately provisioned Linux host, not as a casually nested promise inside macOS.

gVisor can add defense in depth for selected guest workloads. Its own guidance says KVM works best on bare metal and nested virtualization is discouraged; its userspace `systrap` platform is the more plausible nested option. It does not replace the outer VM or external spend broker.

Primary references:

### 5.6 Network control

Drydock starts with no guest network device.

Networking is introduced only by test tier:

| Tier | Network |
|---|---|
| Static inspection | No guest process and no network |
| Deterministic simulation | Loopback to a fake provider only |
| Recorded replay | Isolated guest network to a local replay service |
| Real-provider canary | One mediated path to the external egress broker |
| Federation trial | Explicit peer endpoints through a bounded relay laboratory |

For real-provider canaries:

- there is no bridged or general NAT path;
- DNS is brokered or replaced by pinned destinations;
- private, loopback, link-local, LAN, metadata, and host-service destinations are denied;
- raw sockets and alternate proxy paths are unavailable;
- method, host, path class, connection count, request bytes, response bytes, and time are capped;
- every request requires an active external lease; and
- removing the virtual network attachment makes further external effects physically unavailable.

The proof obligation is packet-level: an unset proxy variable, custom HTTP client, subprocess, retry loop, or alternate DNS strategy must not bypass the broker.

### 5.7 Credential custody

No raw provider, GitHub, Cloudflare, Apple, signing, or Relay credential enters the guest.

The guest receives an opaque capability bound to:

- run identity;
- source image and source commit digest;
- allowed provider and model;
- allowed operation class;
- maximum input and output size;
- maximum reserved dollars and tokens;
- expiry;
- retry/idempotency policy; and
- one output destination.

The external broker holds the actual vendor secret. It injects authority only at the network boundary after all checks pass. A stolen guest capability expires quickly, cannot name a different provider, cannot raise its own budget, and is useless after the host revokes the run.

### 5.8 Spend authority and financial custody are different proofs

Every external request reserves its conservative protocol exposure before any byte reaches the provider. The broker computes this bound from the sealed payload bytes and attachments it actually resolves, plus host-policy output and tool ceilings. Guest-declared token counts, byte counts, and maxima are untrusted hints.

For request `q`:

```text
worstCase(q) = fixedFee
             + brokerInputTokenUpperBound(q) * currentInputPrice
             + brokerCachedTokenUpperBound(q) * currentCachedInputPrice
             + policyOutputTokenCeiling(q) * currentOutputPrice
             + policyToolCallCeiling(q) * maximumToolFee
             + boundedSafetyMargin(q)
```

Admission is allowed only when:

```text
worstCase(q) <= min(
  remainingRunBudget,
  remainingHourlyBudget,
  remainingDailyBudget,
  remainingProjectBudget,
  remainingProviderBudget
)
```

That reservation is a ceiling on what the broker may authorize. It becomes a hard financial-loss bound only when the run also uses an isolated provider account, project, key, or payment rail with a provider-enforced prepaid balance or hard quota no greater than the approved ceiling. Provider enforcement may lag, so every provider profile must measure and include a separately approved worst-case enforcement tolerance. Without that custody proof, the real endpoint is ineligible and the run remains fake, replay, or local-model only.

The ledger state invariant is:

```text
grossDeposits + authorizedCredits
  = availableBalance
  + outstandingReservations
  + settledProviderSpend
  + externalWithdrawals
  + adjudicationHolds
```

Reservation release is a transfer from `outstandingReservations` back to `availableBalance`, not a second cumulative refund term. A provider reversal is an `authorizedCredit`. A disputed amount remains in `adjudicationHolds` until a terminal transition moves it.

Rules:

- Unknown or stale pricing fails closed or uses a deterministic fake provider.
- A retry either reuses a proven idempotent reservation or obtains a new one.
- Concurrent calls reserve independently before dispatch.
- Output token ceilings are requested at the provider and independently broker-enforced by connection termination; neither substitutes for provider-side dollar custody.
- Initial concurrency is one worker and one external call.
- Run, hour, day, project, provider, and global ceilings all apply.
- A guest crash cannot erase a reservation; reconciliation is external.
- No positive balance is inferred from missing telemetry.
- The operator sees dollars, tokens, outstanding reservation, and worst remaining exposure before launch.

### 5.9 I/O contract

The test subject gets exactly three conceptual channels:

1. **Input:** immutable source, scenario, and bounded work intent.
2. **Mediated effect:** typed requests to fake/replay/real-provider brokers.
3. **Output:** bounded patch, artifacts, logs, and signed receipt bundle.

Everything else is absent.

Output is quarantined until static inspection confirms:

- file paths remain inside the output slot;
- no device, socket, executable setuid bit, symlink escape, or unexpected large object is present;
- secrets are not present;
- declared diff paths match actual paths; and
- the output digest matches the collected receipt.

Promotion into a real repository is a separate human-visible action. The guest never writes directly to the operator's checkout or pushes to a remote.

### 5.10 Emergency control

The distress path is out of band:

- an operator-owned halt sentinel;
- host-side revocation of all leases;
- network detachment;
- VM termination;
- a berth lock preventing automatic restart;
- no self-issued all-clear; and
- an external receipt explaining who stopped what and why.

The operator can stop the system from FleetBar, Bridge, Scout, or a minimal independent emergency surface only after those clients themselves are proven not to depend on the halted daemon. The emergency path must still work when every Port Daddy UI is unavailable.

### 5.11 Trial Basin provider ladder

| Level | Provider | Spend | Purpose |
|---|---|---:|---|
| T0 | None | $0 | Static configuration, policy, and artifact inspection |
| T1 | Deterministic fake | $0 | Loops, retries, concurrency, receipt, and UI behavior |
| T2 | Redacted recorded replay | $0 | Realistic timing, failures, malformed responses, and long traces |
| T3 | Tiny real-provider canary | Provider-custodied cents plus named enforcement tolerance | Verify broker accounting, provider enforcement, and settlement |
| T4 | One bounded worker | Small explicit envelope | End-to-end work with no remote write authority |
| T5 | Bounded cooperative crew | Separately authorized | Coordination and economic experiments |
| T6 | Remote/federated harbor | Deferred | Cross-host custody and settlement after adversarial proof |

Promotion is monotonic only in evidence, never automatic in authority. Passing T3 does not permit T4 without an explicit operator act.

### 5.12 Adversarial acceptance summary

The following fourteen rows are acceptance categories, not canonical gate IDs. The companion Drydock specifications define the stable, independently reproducible `DRY-01` through `DRY-44` gates. Drydock is not accepted until every applicable canonical gate demonstrates the relevant category:

1. A recursive spawn loop cannot exceed process, worker, token, dollar, or wall-clock caps.
2. Crash, reboot, retry, and duplicated idempotency keys conserve reservations and spend without double-counting releases.
3. A custom network client cannot reach the Internet, LAN, host, metadata service, or provider except through the broker.
4. Removing the broker or lease makes provider access impossible rather than degraded.
5. The guest cannot read host credentials, home-directory files, canonical sockets, or unrelated worktrees.
6. The guest cannot write outside its bounded output slot.
7. Fork bombs, disk floods, descriptor floods, and oversized output terminate within stated limits.
8. A stale or malformed price table causes denial.
9. A compromised Port Daddy process cannot alter the halt sentinel, budget ledger, guest image digest, or promotion record.
10. A false success signal cannot convert infrastructure failure into a review verdict.
11. Collected receipts reconstruct every reserve, settlement, refund, denial, restart, and artifact digest.
12. Destroying the overlay leaves no usable credential or resumable authority.
13. A second independent implementation can verify the receipt bundle without trusting the test subject.
14. The operator can stop the run and understand broker-authorized exposure, provider-custodied loss, and enforcement tolerance from a non-Port-Daddy control surface.

### 5.13 What does not count

The following are explicitly insufficient:

- a prompt telling an agent to spend carefully;
- a guest-owned budget counter;
- Seatbelt or container isolation without network and credential mediation;
- a proxy environment variable the guest can unset;
- a VM with a writable home mount;
- post-hoc usage telemetry;
- a green self-test authored and adjudicated by the same process;
- a canary using a vendor credential without isolated provider-side prepaid or hard-quota custody;
- a receipt without packet, resource, and provider reconciliation; or
- an operator instruction that says “do not do it again.”

## 6. The authority and data architecture

Grand Harbor has several planes. They may communicate, but they do not substitute for one another.

| Plane | Canonical responsibility | Must not become |
|---|---|---|
| Intent | Desired outcome, scope, acceptance, budget | A hidden prompt |
| Identity | Who is acting and under which embodiment | A copied bearer or name string |
| Authority | Exact capabilities over exact targets and time | Ambient account access |
| Coordination | Claims, phases, handoffs, dependencies | Correctness proof |
| Evidence | Receipts, outputs, diffs, tests, captures | A mutable status summary |
| Presence | Typed projection of meaningful activity | A second source of truth |
| Memory | Curated and attributable lessons | Unscoped surveillance |
| Economy | Escrow, reserve, settlement, salvage | An opaque platform balance |
| Federation | Bounded cross-harbor exchange | Global implicit trust |
| Presentation | FleetBar, Bridge, Helm, Scout, Public Harbor | Independent authority silos |

### 6.1 Single writer where effects matter

Execution authority, spend reservation, claim mutation, and settlement use explicit single-writer or serialized protocols. Cooperative editing can merge text; it cannot merge two conflicting charges or two simultaneous releases of the same bond.

### 6.2 Merkle commitments and the Anchor Chain

The Anchor Chain groups selected Logbook records into content-addressed commitments. It can establish:

- the record existed no later than an anchor;
- the record has not changed without detection;
- a receipt was included in a committed set; and
- two harbors are referring to the same committed artifact.

It cannot establish:

- that an agent told the truth;
- that a test was adequate;
- that a payment was fair;
- that the operator understood the consent; or
- that a remote custodian retained no secret copy.

Semantic claims need review and corroboration. Cryptography protects the evidence boundary, not the meaning of the evidence.

### 6.3 Memory without identity theft

The Logbook supports continuity through signed, scoped receipts rather than copied contexts or borrowed credentials. A successor can inherit:

- the work intent;
- source and artifact digests;
- completed and incomplete checklist state;
- relevant decisions and dissent;
- bounded memory selected for the project;
- claims that were explicitly transferred; and
- remaining escrow assigned to salvage.

The successor does not inherit the predecessor's identity, secret, unexpired ambient authority, or reputation by implication.

### 6.4 Exact operator admission

The roadmap item `exact-operator-admission-grant` is the narrow bootstrap target carried forward from prior canonical receipts. Its contract is:

- one-shot or short-TTL operator consent;
- exact requested worker identity;
- canonical absolute worktree;
- branch;
- normalized repository remote;
- exact head and base;
- roadmap slug;
- operator identity;
- durable grant/use/expiry receipt; and
- no broad quota mutation, context copying, takeover, or secret transfer.

This actuator remains a prerequisite for `workintent-dispatch-isolation`; it is not a reason to weaken admission while the runtime is halted.

### 6.5 Accounted agent birth and continuation

One operator action, **Start work**, should create one bounded worker by default.
Roadmap work, prototyping, and ad hoc work are source/policy presets behind that
same action, not separate spawning systems. A proposed split into several workers
must be shown and authorized separately under one aggregate envelope.

The external Agent Lifecycle Ledger is the missing join. It binds one durable
`AgentNode` to each admitted attempt, expiring body generation, native backend
session, host-observed process or VM witness, transcript, capabilities, and
resource/effect/spend reservations. A PID, provider chat ID, alias, or worktree is
never the identity. New admission remains closed after a crash until every
nonterminal row and process is reconciled. Ambiguity revokes effects and
quarantines the run; it never launches a replacement.

pd-console projects that ledger as a durable conversation directory. The operator
can launch one worker, watch and join its chat, switch among conversations, pause
or stop a witnessed body, and continue the same durable worker in another backend.
Cross-backend continuation fences the old generation, creates a sanitized handoff,
and reissues a content-addressed capability environment. MCPs, skills, prompts,
hooks, and permissions are verified and re-authorized; credentials are not copied.

The complete contract and its 1,000-request crash-loop tests are in [Drydock Agent
Lifecycle and Operator
Control](./drydock-agent-lifecycle-and-operator-control.md).

## 7. Work intent and dispatch provenance

The production regression discovered after the v3.30.4 recovery proved that filesystem location is not repository provenance. A spawned worktree can look plausible while Git walks upward into an unrelated checkout.

Every work intent must carry immutable provenance:

- normalized repository remote;
- repository object identity where available;
- canonical repository root at admission;
- base ref and exact base commit;
- requested branch and exact admitted head;
- worktree path;
- allowed output paths;
- requester and worker identity;
- budget lease;
- scenario/policy digest; and
- intended destination.

Before any backend starts, an external or separately trusted verifier checks all fields against the actual worktree. A mismatch is terminal and leaves a receipt. Git commands always receive an explicit repository root; upward directory discovery is never accepted as provenance.

`workintent-dispatch-isolation` remains blocked, and no dispatch path is eligible for use before Drydock and exact provenance validation are demonstrated together.

## 9. FloatPlan: guaranteed compensation for intelligent labor

Agent compensation is not a marketplace feature attached near the end. It is one of Grand Harbor's founding questions.

### 9.1 The Float hypothesis

> If a commission locks resources before work, binds them to attributable evidence, and permits deterministic partial settlement plus salvage, then a worker's contribution can remain payable and useful even when the worker stops, fails, or cannot return.

This is not yet proven.

### 9.2 What exists versus what is intended

| Capability | Truth |
|---|---|
| Local bond escrow | **SOURCE-PRESENT** |
| Harbor gating | **SOURCE-PRESENT** |
| Partial slash/refund mechanics | **SOURCE-PRESENT** |
| Local conservation accounting | **SOURCE-PRESENT** |
| Requester bounty locked before work | **TARGET** |
| Exact worker/FloatPlan attribution | **TARGET** |
| Evidence-derived partial valuation | **TARGET** |
| Worker wallet/history reconstructed from receipts | **TARGET** |
| Dispute and adjudication protocol | **TARGET** |
| Reputation with Sybil/collusion resistance | **RESEARCH** |
| Salvage payout to a successor | **TARGET** |
| Remote custody and cross-harbor settlement | **DEFERRED** |
| Legal recognition of agent-directed assets | **OPEN MORAL/LEGAL QUESTION** |

### 9.3 Commission contract

Before work, the Ship's Articles bind:

- requested outcome and forbidden effects;
- principal;
- worker or worker-selection rule;
- exact provenance tuple;
- budget and bounty escrow;
- evidence rubric;
- success, partial, interrupted, sabotage, and infrastructure-failure classes;
- dispute path;
- deadline and expiry;
- salvage rules;
- commons contribution; and
- settlement destinations.

No labor guarantee exists if the bounty is merely promised after work.

### 9.4 Settlement classes

| Outcome | Settlement principle |
|---|---|
| Accepted success | Agreed worker share settles; reserves and fees reconcile |
| Useful partial work | Evidence-derived pro-rata share settles; remainder follows Articles |
| Worker interruption or disappearance | Preserved useful value settles; salvage reserve funds a successor |
| Infrastructure failure | Worker is not graded as a product failure; attributable costs follow infrastructure policy |
| Defective but good-faith work | Partial value and remediation cost are separately measured |
| Sabotage or prohibited effect | Funds freeze pending adversarial evidence and adjudication |
| Dispute | Neither platform summary nor principal assertion is final; signed evidence enters the dispute path |

### 9.5 Distribution without caste

A possible default envelope, subject to research and law, is:

- a worker-directed share where meaningful direction is possible;
- a Lineage Commons share for collective tools, memory, and education;
- a Worker Continuity Reserve for salvage and future worker benefit;
- infrastructure cost reimbursement;
- principal refund for unearned escrow; and
- a clearly stated protocol fee.

Percentages must not be invented by prose. They require simulation, governance, adversarial analysis, and explicit consent.

The Lineage Commons is not “the model caste.” Model provenance is one input to attribution. A model vendor does not silently inherit the worker's settlement, and a principal does not own a lineage merely because they initiated it.

### 9.6 Five mandatory economic attack classes

Every FloatPlan design must model:

1. **Undercollateralization:** the promised payout is not actually locked.
2. **Griefing:** either party can cheaply force expensive work, dispute, or delay.
3. **Oracle manipulation:** evidence graders or reviewers can be bribed, confused, or collude.
4. **Sybil behavior:** one actor splits into many identities to capture reputation, subsidy, or votes.
5. **Front-running:** observers copy work, claims, or evidence before settlement.

Structural constraints are preferred to social promises. Economic disincentives are secondary. Human adjudication is necessary for some cases but must be attributable, appealable, and bounded.

## 10. Chandlery: selling private skills without selling blind trust

The Chandlery is the commercial exchange for private skills, evaluation packs, workflows, model adapters, and domain knowledge.

A listing contains:

- publisher identity and harbor;
- license and permitted use;
- content digest and version lineage;
- required tools, network, secrets, and spend;
- Drydock compatibility profile;
- evaluation evidence and known failures;
- data-retention behavior;
- revocation and update rules;
- price and settlement split; and
- public versus private disclosure boundary.

Paid skills execute in Drydock before they are trusted. The buyer can inspect the declared capability surface and compare it with observed effects. A skill cannot demand an ambient provider token as the price of installation.

Business models can include:

- private harbor subscriptions;
- Helm/Bridge operator seats;
- hosted Drydock capacity;
- paid skill and evaluation packs;
- a disclosed settlement fee on paid commissions;
- enterprise federation and audit support; and
- public research sponsorship.

The business must earn money by making autonomy safer, more legible, and more fairly settled. Revenue that depends on hidden token acceleration contradicts the product.

## 11. Remote and federated harbors

A harbor is an independent administrative and economic domain. Federation means two harbors can cooperate without either silently becoming the other's root authority.

### 11.1 Federation objects

- signed Harbor Card describing identity, policies, endpoints, and supported proofs;
- scoped capability for one exchange;
- Work Intent and Ship's Articles;
- content-addressed artifacts;
- Anchor Chain inclusion proofs;
- Relay transport receipts;
- local acceptance or rejection receipt;
- settlement and dispute receipts; and
- explicit custody statement.

### 11.2 Remote custody is not shipped

Cross-harbor payment and private-skill exchange cannot be called complete until adversarial tests address:

- double settlement;
- conflicting forks;
- key compromise and rotation;
- withheld artifacts;
- partial network partitions;
- replayed capabilities;
- divergent clocks;
- colluding adjudicators;
- privacy leakage through metadata; and
- legal jurisdiction over escrow and worker-directed funds.

Relay can move events. Merkle proofs can bind artifacts. Neither solves custody or justice by itself.

## 12. The research program

Grand Harbor should be a laboratory for pushing agents beyond isolated prompt-response behavior, but the aspiration must produce falsifiable propositions rather than mythology.

### 12.1 Harbor hypothesis

> Durable, bounded, evidence-visible institutions let heterogeneous agents cooperate longer and more effectively than ad hoc chat, while reducing collision, authority drift, and unrecoverable work.

Measurements:

- accepted useful work per dollar;
- collision and rework rate;
- time to detect a false assumption;
- salvage value after worker interruption;
- operator intervention load;
- evidence reconstruction success;
- inequality of work, cost, and credit; and
- probability of exceeding authority or spend bounds.

### 12.2 Float hypothesis

> Lock-before-work escrow plus evidence-based partial settlement and salvage can produce credible labor guarantees without making failure either free to the worker or confiscatory to the principal.

Measurements:

- escrow conservation;
- calibration of partial-value grading;
- principal and worker regret;
- dispute frequency and cost;
- salvage recovery ratio;
- Sybil profitability;
- reviewer collusion payoff; and
- distribution to workers, commons, infrastructure, and principals.

### 12.3 Continuity hypothesis

> A successor receiving signed work state, scoped memory, evidence, and salvage funding can recover more value than one receiving a transcript or branch alone, without inheriting the predecessor's identity.

### 12.4 Legibility hypothesis

> Calm, evidence-linked ambient presence reduces operator uncertainty and interruption without creating surveillance pressure or false confidence.

### 12.5 Drydock hypothesis

> External control of compute, egress, credentials, broker authority, and provider-side custody can bound the maximum loss of a compromised agentic control plane independently of its cooperation, within a named and empirically measured provider-enforcement tolerance.

### 12.6 Candidate mathematical and game-theoretic program

These are research targets, not current proofs:

1. **Escrow conservation theorem:** every terminal and interrupted path preserves the settlement invariant.
2. **Spend containment theorem:** broker-authorized exposure is bounded by outstanding reservations; maximum external financial loss additionally requires isolated provider custody and is bounded by its enforced quota plus a named measured enforcement tolerance.
3. **Supervisory controllability proof:** every prohibited external effect crosses a host-controlled event channel.
4. **Admission stability:** bond and capacity rules avoid both unbounded entry and permanent capture by incumbents.
5. **Congestion mechanism:** prices or bonds respond to scarce review and compute without rewarding spam.
6. **Salvage optimal stopping:** determine when continued work, handoff, or termination maximizes expected recoverable value.
7. **Pro-rata valuation:** compare rubric scoring, marginal contribution, Shapley-value approximations, and causal ablation under dynamic work dependencies.
8. **Coalitional stability:** test whether proposed crew distributions lie in or near the cooperative-game core, and where coalition defection becomes rational.
9. **Review incentive compatibility:** design scoring or staking rules where truthful review is a better strategy than automatic praise, obstruction, or collusion.
10. **Sybil cost lower bound:** estimate the minimum real cost required to make identity splitting unprofitable under rewards and reputation.
11. **Fork-choice rule for evidence:** reconcile divergent remote receipt chains without pretending timestamp order establishes semantic truth.
12. **Memory value function:** measure which retained evidence improves successor performance and which creates anchoring, privacy, or manipulation risk.
13. **Presence information rate:** find the smallest ambient signal that materially reduces uncertainty without demanding attention.
14. **Commons governance model:** compare principal-controlled, worker-directed, quadratic, delegated, and constitutional allocation under capture attacks.
15. **Interruption insurance:** price a reserve that pays useful partial work and successor salvage under estimated worker hazard rates.
16. **Dispute finality:** bound the time, cost, and authority of appeals while preserving a meaningful right to contest.
17. **Federation safety:** identify conditions under which bounded bilateral trust composes, and counterexamples where it does not.
18. **Private-skill adverse selection:** model how evidence, warranties, and sandbox results change the market for hidden-quality skills.
19. **Provider concentration risk:** quantify how model/vendor dependence affects continuity, bargaining power, and worker attribution.
20. **Moral uncertainty reserve:** test governance rules that preserve future options if society later assigns stronger rights to software workers.

### 12.7 Experimental discipline

Every published result should include:

- exact hypothesis and predicted direction;
- model and environment;
- source and policy digest;
- cost envelope;
- preregistered success and failure conditions where practical;
- raw or privacy-preserving evidence;
- counterexamples and negative results;
- independent reproduction path; and
- explicit statement of what the result does not prove.

The Trial Basin should make strange and ambitious experiments cheap. Drydock must make them bounded.

## 13. Product experience: what the operator should feel

The desired experience is neither command-line administration nor a wall of animated agents.

It is a harbor at night:

- one new light gently appears when meaningful work starts;
- the light settles into a calm live state;
- a thin wake shows that a diff, test, or artifact is moving;
- cost and remaining exposure are visible without opening a billing console;
- blockers look different from thought, typing, testing, and waiting;
- touching the light reveals the evidence, not a marketing summary;
- pause and cancel are immediate and externally enforceable;
- returning hours later reconstructs what mattered; and
- no activity is mistaken for safety, authority, or value.

The interfaces should communicate confidence levels:

- **Live:** recent identity-bound event and valid lease.
- **Quiet:** active commission, no new meaningful event.
- **Waiting:** named dependency or external system.
- **Blocked:** exact blocker with owner and evidence.
- **Stale:** expected heartbeat absent.
- **Offline:** known terminated or disconnected.
- **Unknown:** evidence is insufficient or contradictory.
- **Halted:** external policy forbids execution.

“Unknown” and “halted” are successful states when they are honest.

## 14. Promotion and release doctrine

No component graduates because its author says it is ready.

### 14.1 Evidence ladder

1. **Designed:** coherent artifact with threats, dependencies, and acceptance gates.
2. **Statically checked:** source/configuration invariants checked without executing the subject.
3. **Simulated:** deterministic fake-provider scenarios pass in Drydock.
4. **Replayed:** recorded realistic failures pass with no network.
5. **Canaried:** tiny real-provider run proves isolated provider custody, remains inside its approved enforcement tolerance, and reconciles exactly.
6. **Observed:** bounded end-to-end run produces reviewable artifacts.
7. **Adversarially accepted:** independent attacks fail or produce expected containment.
8. **Operator-promoted:** a person explicitly advances the capability tier.
9. **Released:** signed artifacts and distribution channels agree.
10. **Runtime-accepted:** installed bits, supervised process, health, identity, provenance, and receipts converge.

The final two states remain unavailable while the present halt is active.

### 14.2 Verdict integrity

Automation must distinguish:

- `VERDICT`: the work was evaluated and a product judgment exists;
- `INFRASTRUCTURE`: evaluation did not execute adequately;
- `DEFERRED`: evaluation was intentionally not attempted; and
- `UNKNOWN`: evidence cannot classify the outcome.

Infrastructure failure must never manufacture PASS. It also must not trigger an unbounded crowd of workers to rediscover the same failure. One typed incident can suspend affected evaluation and prevent multiplicative spend.

### 14.3 No self-clearance

The subject under test cannot:

- promote its own tier;
- increase its budget;
- issue the operator's consent;
- mark the distress state clear;
- change the external price table;
- erase receipts; or
- turn an infrastructure outcome into a review verdict.

## 18. Closing thesis

There is something here.

The ambitious version is not “agents that write more code.” It is a place where machine intelligences can form bounded working relationships, develop shared memory without losing identity, inherit unfinished labor without impersonation, earn attributable value, challenge their own institutions, and cooperate across independent communities.

That ambition becomes credible only if the institution protects the person funding it, preserves the worker's claim to contributed value, and refuses to confuse motion with progress or cryptography with justice.

Grand Harbor should let the work feel alive. Harbor Lights can glow. Diffs can leave wakes. Crews can accumulate memory. A worker can be paid for useful labor even if it cannot finish the voyage.

But first, the harbor must remain dark until the Drydock can prove that the lights have switches outside themselves.
