Held-out moved with in-loop — not against it.

If my iterations had been overfit to the visible 12, the held-out cohort (Ben/Ngozi/Devon) would have plateaued or regressed. Instead held-out Appeal moved +2.0 across rounds (in-loop moved +3.0). The held-out cohort actually had higher starting scores by sheer luck of sympathetic readers, and v4 closed the gap rather than widening it.

The structural changes — name the pain, enforce the gate, make trust delegable, surface the audit — generalized across personas I wasn't optimizing for. That's the strongest evidence the synthesis was real architecture, not a target chase.

§ 06
Honest gaps · what v5 would address
surfaced across all 15 personas in round 3
Gap 01 · Sam
Power mode — opposite of Simple
Simple hides chrome for solo users. Power should expose the policy file. cat ~/.portdaddy/policies/spider.toml · editable in $EDITOR · version-controlled · pd policy validate --probe.
Affects · Sam, Hiro, Aisha
Gap 02 · Tomas, Priya
Drill-through on the enterprise footer
v4 shows "Audit · Datadog" as a label. Tomas's "Bob in compliance" will click expecting: last 5 audit events, retention period, log schema, signing CA, SOC 2 report PDF. Static badges fail the procurement bar.
Affects · Tomas, Priya, Casey
Gap 03 · Maya, Aisha
Fleet-as-graph, not fleet-as-list
v4 surfaces symbol-level claims one at a time. Conflict prediction across in-flight claims requires a topology view: who's about to collide, what touches what, where the merge will hurt.
Affects · Maya, Aisha
Gap 04 · Daria
Define "Run probe" semantics
If the button runs a synthetic violation against the live sandbox and shows the deny, it's the most important button in the panel. If it re-fetches text, it's worse than nothing. Document it or kill it.
Affects · Daria, Casey
Gap 05 · Devon, Aisha
Playground / demo fleet mode
Devon (and any newcomer) has nothing to authorize without setup. A "demo fleet" that spawns 3 fake agents on a sample repo so the user can try approve/halt/resume before installing turns curiosity into commitment.
Affects · Devon, new users
Gap 06 · Lin
Simple mode should keep cost cap
v4's Simple hides too much — editable cost cap was Lin's best "I'm in control" handle and it disappeared. Cut line should be "infrastructure vs control," not "team chrome vs solo." Keep the thermostat.
Affects · Lin, Marcus
Gap 07 · Hiro
Auto-detect mode, don't ask
Picker is a workaround. Default install should detect: solo machine, one project, no remote PD → Simple. Multi-project + remote sessions → Team. Org SSO present → Org. Picker as override, not entry point.
Affects · Hiro, Lin
Gap 08 · Ben (HELD)
Scope-keyed mode persistence
Freelancers carry a per-client mental model. pd config set --scope=acme:* mode=team cost-cap=$2 · popover honors it · invoice export per scope. Mode-per-scope, not mode-per-app.
Affects · Ben, contractors generally
Gap 09 · Jordan
Real squad rollup behind scope toggle
v4's scope toggle changes the label. The dashboard hasn't moved — same data, different word. Org mode needs: total spend per squad, top-5 HITL approvers/deniers, audit-export-to-CSV, policy-applied report.
Affects · Jordan, Priya
§ 07
The meta-finding
one architecture · four lenses · three rounds
What the 15 personas, scored 135 times, jointly revealed

The same architecture pleased every persona that wasn't economically threatened by it.

The Round-1 synthesis identified one architecture serving four personas: delegated trust with audit visibility, enforced not promised. Three iterations later, that prediction held. Every persona that found their workflow inside this architecture moved positive — the safety researcher, the buyer, the skeptic, the post-incident EM, the OSS maintainer, the no-code founder, the VP, the junior, the contractor.

The only personas who didn't move were the two who were structurally adversarial to the product getting better: Aisha (the build-it-herself ML platform engineer whose job security depends on her org NOT buying a tool that overlaps her platform) and Devon (the student looking for HN cred, where "looks enterprise-y" is mildly negative). Their stuck scores are the calibration evidence — the agents are scoring honestly, not flattering the design.

The one persona whose Appeal moved down in v3 (Aisha, -1) and then back up in v4 (+3) traces the most interesting curve: as v3 got closer to her platform's territory she was more threatened; as v4 added the API/SDK gestures (symbol-level claims, attestation hashes), her calculus flipped from "build" to "integrate." Same product, two directions of motion in two iterations, driven by where it landed against her job role.

§ 08
What this is & isn't
methodological caveat
Honest about the apparatus

This is a structured persona-driven design eval, not a real A/B test.

The scores are LLM judgments of LLM-generated personas critiquing a static mockup. There's a hall-of-mirrors quality. Three things keep it useful: (1) each persona has a different constraint set they're optimizing against — what they reject is more reliable than what they praise; (2) aggregating across many constraints surfaces architectural gaps that don't depend on any one persona being "right"; (3) the held-out cohort acts as a partial overfit check.

What this is good for: finding the structural fault lines a single critique would miss, surfacing where features address one constraint while violating another (Aisha's competitor lens), generating a punch list that holds up to scrutiny across many perspectives. What it is not good for: predicting actual adoption rates, validating specific UI affordances, or substituting for real users in front of real screens. The next step is putting v4 in front of 5 real engineers who match the Maya/Jordan/Sam/Casey profiles.
