§ 04
The four dream features
one per persona · the thing that flips them
Maya · The Switcher
Collision Shield
A menu-bar badge that lives in the icon. "3 sessions safe · 0 collisions · 1 file claim." Clicking it shows: "Claude-iTerm has lib/db.ts lines 40–120. Claude-VSCode is waiting on that file. Claude-worktree-2 is on a different branch and clear." One-click release & hand-off. The invisible problem becomes visible in the menubar at all times, without her opening anything.
Why it flips her: Every other dev tool waits for her to ask. This one whispers "I've got you" while she keeps coding. Directly addresses the pain she walked in with. She installs it that minute.
Jordan · The Buyer
PR Replay
A reviewer-side popover that selects a PR and shows every HITL prompt the agent saw, every approval the engineer clicked, every file the swarm touched, with cost and elapsed time inline — collapsed into a 30-second scrub bar. Loom for agent sessions, attached to the PR.
Why it flips him: Turns "agents did stuff" into "here's exactly what the agent did, what the human authorized, and what it cost." That's a defensible budget line. He buys seats for all 24 engineers.
Sam · The Power User
Scriptable Trust Policies
YAML config, hot-reloaded. auto_approve: { class: [linter, indexer], network: none, cost_lt: 0.10 }. A panel showing "today PD auto-approved 47 actions on your behalf, saving 47 context switches; here's the audit log; here's the one that flagged." Plain config, visible audit, every auto-approval inspectable.
Why it flips them: Makes the popover a flow-defender instead of a flow-interrupter. Sam writes 2,000 words about it. Compares it to AWS IAM but for agents. Evangelizes.
Casey · The Skeptic
Enforced Sandbox + Signed Audit
Every agent action runs in a per-task sandbox with a declared allowlist (files, network egress, secrets), enforced by an OS-level mechanism (seatbelt/landlock). Violations hard-fail. Every action is signed and exported as OpenTelemetry to the existing collector, with a 90-day signed audit log.
Why it flips her: She'll grant a fair internal pilot if she can demo pd guard actually preventing a misbehaving agent from touching ~/.ssh/, with the attempt landing in Datadog in 5 seconds. Make the enforcement layer the headline.
§ 05
The convergence
where four dreams become one
Synthesis · what the four dreams actually want

One feature, four lenses: delegated trust with audit visibility, enforced not promised.

Maya wants the tool to silently shield her from collisions she doesn't see. Sam wants to delegate trust via policy. Jordan wants to review the delegated zone after the fact. Casey wants the delegation to be OS-enforced, not just labeled. Different verbs. Same architecture.

The HITL gate at the front of the current mockup is the opposite of this — it interrupts before there's evidence, every time, with no policy memory. Replacing the "approve every swarm" model with "define your zone of autonomy, get pinged only on exceptions, replay the audit log on demand" is the meta-feature that simultaneously serves all four personas.

§ 09
What's obviously missing
in a single sentence
The one-sentence answer

The mockup sells aesthetics; the product is coordination — name the actual pain, expose the underlying primitives, and the four personas resolve into one motion.

Every persona's "missing piece" reduces to the same root cause: PD's actual differentiating substrate — sessions, claims, salvage, guard, the daemon, the coordination primitives — is hidden underneath a beautiful agent-launcher. Surface those primitives in the user's own words ("Claude," "worktree," "collision," "budget," "audit") and the panel stops being a productivity toy and starts being a platform.

The dream feature isn't one feature. It's making the existing platform legible — at four altitudes (Maya's machine, Sam's editor, Jordan's PR review, Casey's threat model) — without changing what's underneath. The work that's already shipped is bigger than the panel shows.
