\subsection{Build the layer: a gate that reads the record and keeps the time}
\label{sec:sealed-gate}

\noindent\emph{Express lane: the gate is built three times. The first
applies the declared function to whatever the worker hands in, and a worker
that hands in half the record sends a bit the contract never released. The
second reads the committed record and opens when the worker asks. Its value
is right, and the time it leaves is a message. The third works the value
out by itself and lets the clock open the slot. A walk of two rooms as one
system finds no state in 1,870 where Erin can tell two records of one
parity apart, and a worker that could choose among 9 things to show her is
left with 1.}

The boundary above ends on a gap. The checked model's \texttt{submit}
carries no payload, so no mutation of it can launder, and
Figure~\ref{fig:sealed-laundering-fork} is arithmetic and not a run.
\pdkernelname{} builds the missing piece here: a worker that hands the gate
a value, a gate that has to decide what to do with it, and a clock.

A \emph{room} holds one record. Derek commits it, and a worker inside reads
it and hands the gate values. Erin sees what the gate lets out and the time
on the room's clock when it left. The work order gives the room one
\emph{slot}: a count of the record's low bits that may leave, and the time
they leave (Listing~\ref{alg:sealed-gate-slot}). One low bit is the parity
$g(s)=s\bmod 2$ of Property~\ref{thm:sealed-noninterference}.

\pdlistingroom{22}
\pdexcerpt[caption={A slot, and what the decision core is shown of a
room.},label={alg:sealed-gate-slot}]{ch03-slot}

Every run below is one room in two worlds, printed side by side. Derek's
record is 0 in one world and 2 in the other, the pair of the example above.
The slot declares their parity, which is 0 in both, so Erin must see the
same thing in both columns.

\subsubsection{Naive: the function of what is handed in}

The obvious gate follows the contract's words. The worker computes a result
and hands it in. The gate applies the declared function to it and lets that
out through the slot, once (Listing~\ref{alg:sealed-gate-handed}).

\pdlistingroom{9}
\pdexcerpt[caption={The first try. \texttt{low} keeps the low bits that the
slot declares.},label={alg:sealed-gate-handed}]{ch03-handed}

This gate is not careless. It uses its slot once, and nothing wider than
the declared bits can pass it. With a worker that hands in the record it is
right (Listing~\ref{alg:sealed-gate-run-honest}).

\pdlistingroom{15}
\pdrun[caption={A worker that hands in the record, at the first gate.
Nobody here is asked who they are: the names say who would
act.},label={alg:sealed-gate-run-honest}]{honest-handed}

Erin sees a 0 in both worlds. A reader who tested this gate with an honest
worker would pass it.

\subsubsection{Break: half the record}

The worker is Erin's agent, and nothing makes it hand in the record. It can
hand in anything it can compute from the record.
Figure~\ref{fig:sealed-laundering-fork} proposed $t=\lfloor s/2\rfloor$
(Listing~\ref{alg:sealed-gate-run-launder}).

\pdlistingroom{16}
\pdrun[caption={The break. The gate applies the parity function to half the
record.},label={alg:sealed-gate-run-launder}]{launder-handed}

Half of 0 is 0, and half of 2 is 1. The gate took the parity of each, as it
was written to, and let out 0 in one world and 1 in the other. Erin now
knows the record's second bit, which the contract never released. The gate
held the opening to one bit and had no say in which bit went through it.
This is the laundering attack of the boundary above, as a run.

\subsubsection{Break again: the right value at a chosen time}

The property's hypothesis says what to repair: the gate should read the
record Derek committed. What the worker hands in then becomes a request to
open (Listing~\ref{alg:sealed-gate-prompt}).

\pdlistingroom{11}
\pdexcerpt[caption={The second try. The value comes from the committed
record, and the worker says when.},label={alg:sealed-gate-prompt}]{ch03-prompt}

The worker that hands in half the record now gets nowhere
(Listing~\ref{alg:sealed-gate-run-launder-prompt}).

\pdlistingroom{6}
\pdrunlines[caption={The first break, repaired. What is handed in no longer
reaches Erin.},label={alg:sealed-gate-run-launder-prompt}]{launder-prompt}{1,5,8-9,14-15}{1, 5, 8\textendash 9 and 14\textendash 15}

The gate still opens when the worker asks. A worker that has read the
record can ask at once in one world and a tick later in the other
(Listing~\ref{alg:sealed-gate-run-timing}).

\pdlistingroom{18}
\pdrun[caption={The second break. The value is the same in both worlds, and
the time is not.},label={alg:sealed-gate-run-timing}]{timing-prompt}

Erin sees a 0 in both worlds, and it is the right value. It left at time 0
in one world and at time 1 in the other, and she reads the record's second
bit off her clock. Section~\ref{sec:sealed-budget} counts this channel: a
release that may pick one of $w$ times carries $\log_2 w$ bits beyond its
payload. The property above set timing outside its model. This gate meets
that property's hypothesis and still leaks.

\subsubsection{Fix: the gate works it out, and the clock opens it}

The two faults are one. The worker chose something that Erin can see: the
value under the first gate, and the time under the second. \pdkernelname{}
takes both choices away. The decision is in the decision core
(\weld{prereq:sec:prereq-build}{decision core}), and the core makes it when
the clock moves and at no other moment. \texttt{tick} is given the room and
the time. Something leaves only if \texttt{due} finds that the slot's time
has come and \texttt{spent} finds the slot unused, and what leaves is
worked out by \texttt{source} and \texttt{declared}
(Listing~\ref{alg:sealed-gate-tick}).

\pdlistingroom{16}
\pdexcerpt[caption={The decision the core makes each time the clock
moves.},label={alg:sealed-gate-tick}]{ch03-tick}

Those four functions are the gate's guards, in two pairs. One pair answers
the first break (Listing~\ref{alg:sealed-gate-what}).

\pdlistingroom{18}
\pdexcerpt[caption={The guards on what leaves. A line marked
\texttt{MUTANT} is compiled only into a build of the core that has that
guard removed, and the verification below runs each of those
builds.},label={alg:sealed-gate-what}]{ch03-what}

\texttt{source} reads the record its owner committed, and \texttt{declared}
keeps the bits the slot names. The worker may still hand the gate values.
The core keeps the last one, and neither guard reads it. The worker that
hands in half the record changes nothing
(Listing~\ref{alg:sealed-gate-run-launder-core}).

\pdlistingroom{15}
\pdrun[caption={The first break, on the kernel's
gate.},label={alg:sealed-gate-run-launder-core}]{launder-core}

The other pair answers the second break
(Listing~\ref{alg:sealed-gate-when}).

\pdlistingroom{16}
\pdexcerpt[caption={The guards on when it
leaves.},label={alg:sealed-gate-when}]{ch03-when}

\texttt{due} compares the time the core is given with the time the slot
names, and asks nothing of the worker. \texttt{spent} lets the slot be
used once. A worker that asks early in one world and late in the other is
answered at one time in both
(Listing~\ref{alg:sealed-gate-run-timing-core}).

\pdlistingroom{16}
\pdrun[caption={The second break, on the kernel's
gate.},label={alg:sealed-gate-run-timing-core}]{timing-core}

Nothing leaves when the worker hands a value in. The parity leaves when the
clock reaches time 2, in both worlds at once.

\keyidea[Take the choice away]{Whatever a worker can choose and a recipient
can see is a channel, whether it is a value or the moment a value arrives.
Narrowing the choice makes the channel smaller. It is closed when the
choice is gone: the gate works out what leaves from what the owner
committed, and the clock says when.}

\Needspace{12\baselineskip}
\subsubsection{Verify: two rooms as one system}

\paragraph{Every pair of worlds.}
Property~\ref{thm:sealed-noninterference} is about pairs of runs, and its
proof idea builds two worlds side by side. The kernel's tests do the same
with the kernel's own gate. Two rooms are set up as one system, with
records that differ, and a walk visits every state the pair can reach.
Which moves the two rooms share says what a worker knows
(Listing~\ref{alg:sealed-gate-pair}).

Derek's commit and the clock are one move in both rooms. So is anything a
worker does before it has read the record, since it has nothing to go on.
After the read, the worker in each world moves by itself, and the walk
tries every way the two can differ. In each state it asks one question of
two records with the same parity: has Erin seen the same thing in both
rooms? Every room is also held to a rule written apart from the core, which
says what Erin should have seen by each time: nothing until the slot's
time, and then the record's low bits, once.

\pdlistingroom{29}
\pdexcerpt[caption={Every move a pair of rooms can make. A worker that has
not read the record does the same thing in both
worlds.},label={alg:sealed-gate-pair}]{ch03-pair}

The bounds are small. Records run from 0 to 3, the slot declares one bit at
time 2, the clock runs to time 3, and each worker hands in up to 2 values
from 0 to 3. Two of the six pairs of records have one parity, as the
example above counted (Listing~\ref{alg:sealed-gate-worlds}).

Under the first gate Erin has seen different things in 5,760 of 8,046
states. In 1,280 of those, both rooms let something out at the same time
and the values differ, which is laundering. The second gate has none of
that kind, and all 4,800 of its differences are in time. The kernel's gate
has no difference of either kind in 1,870 states, and in every state it
had let out what the rule says.

\pdlistingroom{22}
\pdrun[caption={Every state of two rooms whose records have one parity,
under each gate.},label={alg:sealed-gate-worlds}]{worlds}

\paragraph{Every view.}
A count of states says that a gate leaks and not how much. The last column
of the listing does. It takes one record and counts the different things a
worker can make Erin see by the time the clock stops
(Figure~\ref{fig:sealed-views}).

\input{figures/fig-sealed-views}

Under the first gate the worker picks a bit and one of 4 times to send it,
or sends nothing. That is $2\times4+1=9$ things, whatever the record is.
Theorem~\ref{thm:sealed-capacity} gives one release of $b=1$ bit at one of
$w=4$ times a capacity of $b+\log_2 w=3$ bits, which is 8 things. The ninth
is silence. The theorem assumes that the number of releases is fixed, and
this gate lets the worker decide whether there is one. Under the second
gate the bit is the record's own, and the worker keeps the 4 times and the
silence, which is 5 things. Under the kernel's gate there is 1, and a
choice among 1 carries nothing.

\paragraph{Any record, any time.}
The walk stops at records of 2 bits and a clock that runs to 3. Kani lifts
both bounds (\weld{anchor:sec:correct}{Kani}). It compiles the core's own
\texttt{tick} and asks a solver about every input at once. A statement
about two runs needs two rooms in one harness, which is the
self-composition of the proof idea above, done on the code
(Listing~\ref{alg:sealed-gate-kani-pair}).

\pdlistingroom{27}
\pdexcerpt[caption={Two rooms in one Kani harness. Their records are any 64
bits with one declared part.},label={alg:sealed-gate-kani-pair}]{ch03-kani-pair}

\texttt{two\_rooms} builds any two rooms that Erin cannot tell apart so
far. They have one slot, used in both or in neither, and their records
have one declared part. Records of 0 and 2 under a slot of 1 bit are one
such pair, and the solver has every record of 64 bits to pick from.
Everything else is its to choose as well, what each worker handed in
included. The harness moves the clock to any time, and its 2 assertions
say that the same thing leaves both rooms and that they are alike
afterwards. Its two \texttt{cover} lines
make the solver find a case in which something leaves two rooms with
different records, and a case in which the workers handed in different
values. Without them the assertion could hold for want of any case to
try.

That is one step. A second harness shows that a commit and a submission let
nothing out. So two rooms that start alike are alike after any number of
moves. That sentence is an induction, and the tool checked its two premises
and not the sentence (Listing~\ref{alg:sealed-gate-kani}).

\pdlistingroom{11}
\pdrunlines[caption={Kani~0.68 on the core's
gate.},label={alg:sealed-gate-kani}]{kani-release}{1-11}{1\textendash 11}

The first verdict is a control. It puts the same question to a gate that
works from what the worker handed in, and that harness fails as it must.

\paragraph{Each guard removed.}
This build has four guards, and the kernel has a build with each one taken
out. The walk is run on each (Listing~\ref{alg:sealed-gate-guards-walk}),
and so are the harnesses (Listing~\ref{alg:sealed-gate-kani-guards}).

The first row is the first break, inside the core. The second lets the
whole record out, and Erin tells the worlds apart with no help from the
worker: that build still has 1 view of each record. The third opens early
when the worker has asked, which is the second break.

\pdlistingroom{23}
\pdrun[caption={The walk again, on four builds of the core with one guard
removed from each.},label={alg:sealed-gate-guards-walk}]{worlds-guards}

\pdlistingroom{14}
\pdrunlines[caption={The harnesses again, on the same four
builds.},label={alg:sealed-gate-kani-guards}]{kani-release}{1,12-24}{1 and 12\textendash 24}

The last row is the one to study. A core that lets the parity out a second
time shows Erin the same thing in both worlds, in every one of 1,870
states, because both rooms repeat themselves alike. The walk of pairs
cannot see that guard at all. The rule each room is held to sees it in
1,080 states, and Kani sees it. Two rooms that are wrong in the same way
look the same. A statement that compares two runs with each other needs a
second check beside it, one that compares a run with what should have
happened.

\pdscope{The walk covers records of 2 bits, one slot of one bit and a clock
of 3 ticks, within the bounds that Listing~\ref{alg:sealed-gate-worlds}
prints. Its counts are of what can happen, with no chances attached. The
harnesses cover one move of two rooms, with any record of 64 bits and any
slot. That rooms which are alike stay alike over a whole run is argued
from two of them.}

\begin{pdboundary}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
The gate does the work. The declared part is one the gate can work out
from the record, so the worker adds nothing to what leaves, and a room that
nobody works in lets out the same parity. A room is rented for work the
gate cannot redo. For that work the gate cannot ask whether an answer is
the declared function of the committed record, and
Section~\ref{sec:sealed-budget} offers a budget in place of silence. That
is this chapter's next build.

Time is a count of ticks that the room's host supplies. Nothing here says
how long a step takes, and a recipient with a finer clock than the gate's
is outside the model. A record that Derek commits after the slot's time
leaves at the next tick, so the time of a release still tells Erin when he
committed.

Nobody is asked who they are. Whoever reaches the core first commits the
record, the worker included. The signed work order of
\S\ref{sec:sealed-design} is not built, and neither is its measured
hardware.

Only the gate is built. A worker that sends what it read by some other way
is the business of the two fences, and no test here runs one.
\end{pdboundary}
