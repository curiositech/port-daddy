\paragraph{Ranking skills: the best match need not help.}
The same split applies when the items ranked are skills rather than agents.
A skill router scores a skill against a task by a fit of the form of
Eq.~\ref{eq:fit}: text and declared capability matched to the query. A
reader who decides payment for the skill (the settlement reader) needs
something else, the skill's \pdterm{uplift}: the change in the expected outcome,
as finally graded and paid, when the skill is made available to the
task, against the same task without it.

\begin{pdclaim}{Theorem}{Fit Is Not Uplift}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
\label{thm:ls-fit-uplift}
Suppose each reader's optimal selections at every budget are the top sets
of its own priority order, as in Theorem~\ref{thm:comonotone}. On any skill
catalog that contains a high-fit skill with zero uplift and a lower-fit
skill with positive uplift, no single scalar head serves both the router,
which reads fit, and the settlement reader, which reads uplift.
\end{pdclaim}
\begin{proof}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
Let $A$ be the high-fit skill with zero uplift, such as a procedure the
agent already performs without help, and $B$ the lower-fit skill with
uplift $\mathrm{uplift}(B)>0$. Then
$\bigl(\mathrm{fit}(A)-\mathrm{fit}(B)\bigr)\bigl(\mathrm{uplift}(A)-\mathrm{uplift}(B)\bigr)<0$, a
strict reversal. By Theorem~\ref{thm:comonotone}, no shared head serves
both orders. The construction is the one in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:split-ranker}, with uplift in place of regret.
\end{proof}

\noindent For real catalogs the hypothesis is plausible and unproved.
SkillsBench reports that curated skills raise the average pass rate from
$33.9\%$ to $50.5\%$, with gains ranging from $+4.1$ to $+25.7$ points by
model--harness configuration~\pdcite{li2026skillsbench}. Fit is computed
from the skill's text and the task. Uplift belongs to the skill together
with the agent that uses it. The same study finds that focused skills of
at most three modules beat larger bundles~\pdcite{li2026skillsbench}, so
adding matching material can raise fit and lower value. Routing may use
fit. A skill's \pdterm{royalty} is what its licensor is paid for the skill's
use. Royalties and deranking should read uplift
(\pdchapref{he}{The Harbor Economy}%
\ifpdbook
, \S\ref{he:sec:he-licence-split}%
\fi
).
