% Reader question: what does each worst-case query certificate guarantee?
% Claim (thm:zoom-advantage): (a) plain halving's ratio
%   F/[2k ceil(log2(F/k))+4k] exceeds one iff d=k/F<1/12; (b) Hwang's
%   generalized binary splitting, with k known, uses at most log2 C(F,k)+k
%   queries, so its ratio is at least 1/(H(d)+d), which exceeds one iff
%   d < d_H = 0.2271 (root of H(d)+d=1).
% Evidence: thm:zoom-advantage; exact halving formula on separate dyadic
%   intervals; Hwang curve is the closed form 1/(H(d)+d), a lower bound on
%   F/[log2 C(F,k)+k]. Worked points: F=2500,k=10 (halving 200 queries,
%   ratio 12.5; Hwang 101.1, ratio 24.7) and F=4096,k=32 (halving 576, 7.1;
%   Hwang 298.2, 13.7) [checked: closed form, math-ls/hwang_gbs.py].
% The halving curve is piecewise hyperbolic, not a step function. Neither
% threshold is an observed crossover. No measured point is mixed in.
% Grammar: log-density plot, two certificate curves, two worked bounds each.
\begin{figure}[H]

\caption{Two sufficient certificates, not measured performance curves.
Plain halving's ratio is piecewise hyperbolic, with jumps at inverse powers of
two, and guarantees fewer queries than flat inspection below $d=1/12$. Hwang's
splitting, for a tester who knows $k$, guarantees it below $d\approx0.227$.
