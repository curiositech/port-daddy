\section*{Part IV Solutions --- The Harbor Economy}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Part IV Solutions --- The Harbor Economy}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{IV.\arabic*},leftmargin=2.8em,itemsep=8pt,topsep=4pt]

% 1
\item \wbcheck A reputation any spawn can \emph{clone} carries no information, so
  there is nothing to rent or license; the market needs an asset that cannot be
  costlessly cloned, and reputation-on-a-non-forgeable-identity is the first such
  asset in the stack.

% 2
\item \wbcheck On Side~1 the counterparty is the \emph{operator} (a firm taking
  work-for-hire), and the operator bears the consequence directly: it eats slashed
  sub-bonds and its own reputation moves. The accountable principal is already
  present and already exposed, so durable per-agent identity is not the gate.
  Sides 2 and 3 trade the \emph{agent} or the \emph{skill} as a standalone asset
  whose value is its track record; there the asset is the identity, so a forgeable
  one is fatal.

% 3
\item \wbopen\wbstar \emph{Sketch.} Suppose sides 2 and 3 run on pseudonyms with no
  binding to a scarce principal. Asset value is the discounted stream of future
  rents conditioned on a track record $r$. With free identities an owner facing a
  pending bad outcome simply abandons the pseudonym and re-lists the same asset under
  a fresh one at the newcomer prior. The expected penalty for any bad outcome is then
  capped at $\min(\text{slash}, \text{newcomer re-entry cost})$; if re-entry is free
  the penalty is zero and no sanction binds. Formally this is Douceur's result:
  without a cost to mint identities, no reputation or quorum mechanism is robust. The
  only escape is a positive \emph{abandonment cost} --- exactly what binding to a
  scarce, non-forgeable principal supplies (and what \emph{From Spawn to Person}
  proves is necessary for any sanction-respecting reputation). Every cloneless design
  reintroduces that cost under another name (stake, deposit, hardware root); strip it
  and the design collapses to Sybil.

% 4
\item \wbcheck \emph{Test:} count the distinct, privately-informed parties the
  platform must individually rationalize into participating. Ride-share is two-sided
  (riders, drivers): the driver is the only worker-side party and the platform
  prices to ignite the cross-side network effect. It becomes three-sided only if a
  separate party (e.g.\ a fleet owner who is neither the platform nor the driver)
  bears distinct private information.

% 5
\item \wbtrace v2 is a new content hash, so it inherits an \emph{empty} track record
  and re-enters at the newcomer prior --- the Akerlof lemons problem reappears at
  every version bump, because the buyer again cannot observe quality before use.
  Fix: a version-binding (IV.12) that lets v2 inherit a discounted prior from v1's
  settled outcomes rather than starting cold.

% 6
\item \wbopen\wbstar Treat the reputation vector as a functor into a product object
  $R = R_{\mathrm{acc}}\times R_{\mathrm{aes}}\times R_{\mathrm{eff}}$ with \emph{no}
  chosen universal map to $\mathbb{R}$. \emph{Disclosure rule:} publish each axis
  separately with its sample size and provenance, and let the buyer supply the weight
  vector $w$ at read time; the platform never publishes a single ranked scalar.
  Any \emph{fixed} platform-chosen collapse $s = w\cdot R$ recreates one scalar
  target that producers optimize against --- the Goodhart/bandit framing: a single
  exploitable maximand. Keeping the collapse buyer-side and per-query means there is
  no global target to game, only a moving family of buyer utilities; the cone (the
  universal map) is deliberately left unspecified. (This is exactly the
  multi-dimensional aggregation gap the paper lists as \openmark{}.)

% 7
\item \wbtrace \emph{Base case (paper's worked trace):} bounty $400$, bond $100$,
  $40\%$ done. Before: $\text{wallet}_{\text{Bob}}$ down $500$, $\text{escrow}=500$.
  After: Bob refunded the unearned $400$ bounty; worker earns
  $0.40\times100=40$ pro-rata; salvage gets $100-40=60$; escrow $=0$. Check:
  $400+40+60=500$. Conserved.\par
  \emph{Asked variant:} bond $250$, $70\%$ done, same bounty $400$. Before:
  escrow $=400+250=650$. After: worker earns $0.70\times250=175$; salvage gets
  $250-175=75$; Bob refunded $400$; escrow $=0$. Check: $400+175+75=650$. Conserved.
  The split is always $\text{bond}=f\cdot\text{bond}+(1-f)\cdot\text{bond}$ with the
  bounty returning whole on any non-success state, so conservation is an algebraic
  identity in $f$, independent of the amounts.

% 8
\item \wbcheck An oracle is a source of ground truth the agent \emph{cannot author}
  (a passing test id, a merged SHA, a satisfied policy check). A free-text
  ``Result:'' note lets the worker grade its own work --- a Goodhart attack: the
  agent writes whatever string releases the bounty regardless of what it did. Binding
  closure to an oracle makes the daemon \emph{derive} the grade rather than read the
  worker's claim.

% 9
\item \wbopen\wbstar \emph{Amendment protocol.} (1) Either party proposes a delta
  $\Delta_{\text{criteria}}$ and a delta budget/bounty $\Delta_b$. (2) Both
  countersign $\mathsf{sign}_R,\mathsf{sign}_W$ over $\langle \text{plan hash}_{\text{old}},
  \Delta_{\text{criteria}}, \Delta_b\rangle$. (3) The daemon, in one serialized
  transaction, applies the escrow delta: if $\Delta_b>0$ debit $R$'s wallet into
  escrow; if $\Delta_b<0$ refund $R$; write a new plan hash chaining the old one;
  commit. Conservation is preserved because every amendment is itself a debit/credit
  pair through the same wallet/escrow/commons objects --- the amendment is a morphism
  in the same conservation category, so it composes with the existing settlement
  morphisms. Replay safety: the new plan hash supersedes the old, and a presentation
  against the old hash is rejected.

% 10
\item \wbcheck Grossman--Hart says the residual control right should go to the party
  making the most important non-contractible investment. On the rental side, the
  \emph{renter} runs the agent on its own repository, so the renter must hold the
  \emph{runtime control right} --- the runtime jail (per-agent tool-allowlist plus
  scoped filesystem). The \emph{owner} bears the reputational consequence. So the
  jail belongs to the renter, not the owner.

% 11
\item \wbtrace A lease line item moves $\ell$ from renter-wallet to owner-wallet on
  green settlement; a metered license moves $u\cdot p$ from buyer-wallet to
  licensor-wallet. Each is a matched debit/credit through the same ledger objects, so
  $\text{wallet}+\text{escrow}+\text{commons}$ is unchanged by construction --- the
  total before equals the total after for \emph{any} $\ell, u, p$, because no line
  item creates or destroys credits, it only relocates them. Conservation is therefore
  amount-independent.

% 12
\item \wbopen\wbstar \emph{Version-binding.} Anchor v2's portfolio proof to v1's
  Merkle root: v2 publishes $\langle \text{hash}(v2), \text{parent}=\text{root}(v1),
  \text{diff-attestation}\rangle$. v2's starting prior is a discounted function of
  v1's settled-outcome distribution, $\hat\mu_{v2}=(1-\lambda)\mu_{\text{newcomer}}
  +\lambda\,\mu_{v1}$, with the discount $\lambda$ falling as the diff grows
  (a large rewrite earns less inherited credit). This mirrors the newcomer policy for
  agents: a fresh identity does not start at zero if it can prove lineage, but it
  cannot inherit a clean slate for a substantively different artifact. Wash-resistance:
  the parent link is in the evidence chain, so an owner cannot launder a bad v1 by
  re-publishing it as v2 --- the inherited prior follows the lineage.

% 13
\item \wbcheck If identities are free (Douceur), an adversary mints fresh pseudonyms
  faster than any sanction can attach, so the reputation map has no fixed point to
  condition on --- it is not a weak mechanism but \emph{no} mechanism, because the
  signal it conditions on can be reset to the prior at zero cost.

% 14
\item \wbcheck ``Mutually-distrusting'' (equivalently ``a boundary neither
  controls''). Local non-forgeable identity assumes a single trusted root; the
  market's defining adjective denies any shared root, so the keystone's threat model
  excludes exactly the adversary the market is about.

% 15
\item \wbopen\wbstar \emph{Design.} Bind $N$ agent keys to one principal via a
  hop-bound authorization chain rooted at a principal credential that is itself
  scarce (hardware-attested or stake-bonded), so the principal's bond floor is a
  property of the \emph{root}, not the leaves: spawning a fresh agent extends the
  chain but does not mint a new root, so it cannot reset the floor. Cross-operator:
  replace the single-operator ``the daemon is the trusted root'' shortcut with a
  attestation that other harbors can verify without trusting the issuer --- e.g.\ a
  transparency-log-published principal certificate plus a stake that is slashable in a
  shared bond ledger. The hard half (still \openmark{}) is the attestation: making
  ``this key really is one distinct principal, not a hundred sock puppets'' checkable
  across harbors with no shared root. A stake-and-transparency construction reduces it
  to ``is the stake real and non-double-counted,'' which is itself the gluing problem
  (IV.31--33).

% 16
\item \wbcheck Myerson--Satterthwaite: no bilateral-trade mechanism under two-sided
  private information can be simultaneously efficient, incentive-compatible,
  individually rational, and budget-balanced. The harbor keeps IC, IR, and budget
  balance (the latter forced by strict conservation), so it must surrender
  \emph{efficiency}: the slashing/commons buffer is the budget-balancing wedge that
  pushes the realized allocation below first-best.

% 17
\item \wbtrace Harbor $A$ prices in unit $\alpha$, Harbor $B$ in $\beta$, with a
  boundary conversion rate $\rho$. A transfer $f$ of $1\,\alpha$ then $g$ of the
  result back records $\rho^{-1}\rho = 1$ only if $\rho$ is globally consistent. If
  $A\to B$ uses $\rho$ but $B\to A$ uses $\rho'\ne 1/\rho$ (stale or
  arbitrage-divergent rates), then $F(g\circ f)\ne F(g)\circ F(f)$: the round trip
  leaves a residual $|\rho'\rho-1|$. That residual, capped by the pre-agreed escrow
  slack, is the lax functor's $\varphi$.

% 18
\item \wbopen\wbstar The coherence 2-cell is the natural transformation
  $\eta_{f,g}: F(g)\circ F(f)\Rightarrow F(g\circ f)$ whose component on a transfer
  is the conversion residual, bounded $\|\eta_{f,g}\|\le\varphi$. Pentagon coherence
  requires the two ways of re-associating three composable transfers to agree; this
  holds iff the conversion residuals are \emph{additive and path-independent} over the
  triple, which fails in general for non-consistent rates (a triangular-arbitrage
  cycle has nonzero net residual). So the lax functor exists with bounded 2-cells but
  the pentagon/triangle coherence is \emph{not} guaranteed --- this is the
  unit-of-account facet of the gluing obstruction, and proving when coherence holds
  is open.

% 19
\item \wbcheck Two messages would let $A$ mint a card $B$ must trust on $A$'s word.
  Message (3) is $B$'s counter-signature: $B$ applies its \emph{own} attenuation and
  mints $C_B'$ under $B$'s key, so every later presentation verifies under $B$ alone
  --- no synchronous call back to $A$ on the hot path. Four messages buy the liveness
  property (no $A$-interaction after step 3) plus $B$-side attenuation; two cannot.

% 20
\item \wbtrace $C_B'$ verifies under $B$'s key, not $A$'s, so a rotation of $A$'s
  signing key does \emph{not} immediately invalidate the already-minted $C_B'$ ---
  that is the point of $B$ re-minting. What an $A$-rotation must do is publish the new
  epoch root $R_A^{(e+1)}$; once that root reaches $B$'s witness log, any $C_B'$ bound
  to the stale $R_A^{(e)}$ is invalidated. The gap (\openmark{}) is the interval
  between rotation and root-propagation, and whether a rotated-but-not-revoked key
  should re-validate held delegations --- cross-boundary key rotation is unspecified.

% 21
\item \wbopen\wbstar The \emph{anchor id} should be a nonce, unique per transfer.
  $B$ must reject a re-presented message (3) by recording consumed anchor ids (or a
  monotonic per-source counter) for the lifetime of the epoch root they bind to.
  Freshness invariant: a transfer is accepted iff (a) its envelope binds the current
  or a not-yet-superseded epoch root $R_A^{(e)}$, and (b) its anchor id has not been
  consumed at $B$. Replay against a stale root fails (a); replay of a fresh message
  fails (b).

% 22
\item \wbcheck The bound $\Delta(1+\ln m)$ assumes (1) \emph{honest} participants and
  (2) \emph{random} peer selection (Demers' average-case epidemic model). An attacker
  who controls peer selection or partitions the gossip graph (eclipse attack) breaks
  both. Partial synchrony (gossip eventually delivered within $\Delta$ after some
  unknown global stabilization time) rescues a weaker eventual-convergence guarantee.

% 23
\item \wbtrace Alice revokes $c$ at home harbor $A$ at $t=0$. Harbor $B$ (one hop)
  learns at $t=\Delta$; in $[0,\Delta]$ its filter is stale and $c$ is still spendable
  at $B$. Harbor $C$ (two hops) learns at $t=2\Delta$. So the worst single-hop
  exposure window is $[0,\Delta]$ at $B$, and the Equivocation Lower Bound must price
  the maximum value spendable in that window.

% 24
\item \wbopen\wbstar \emph{Setup.} Three harbors $A,B,C$, gossip period $\Delta$,
  one equivocating witness. Worst-case (not expected) convergence over a path of
  length $\le m-1$ hops is $(m-1)\Delta$; for $m=3$ the deepest honest harbor learns
  by $2\Delta$. Let $v$ be the max value spendable against $c$ per harbor per unit
  time. Then a rational adversary equivocates iff the extractable value
  $\sum_{\text{stale harbors}} v\cdot(\text{stale window})$ exceeds the bond. The
  lower bound is therefore $B_{\text{witness}} \ge v\cdot\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}k\Delta
  = v\,\Delta\,\binom{m}{2}$ in the worst case (here $v\,\Delta\,(1+2)=3v\Delta$).
  Proving tightness requires showing the adversary can actually realize spends at
  every stale harbor simultaneously; that is the open part --- the expected-case
  Demers bound understates it by the gap between $\binom{m}{2}\Delta$ (worst case) and
  $(1+\ln m)\Delta$ (expected).

% 25
\item \wbcheck The folk theorem (Green--Porter; Abreu--Pearce--Stacchetti) needs the
  public-signal distribution to be common knowledge and \emph{exogenous}. A capturable
  grader makes the signal \emph{endogenous} --- a strategic player chooses it --- so
  the premise fails and the equilibrium characterization does not apply as stated.

% 26
\item \wbcheck Unit test passing: machine-checkable (Fix A). Merged SHA:
  machine-checkable (Fix A). ``The refactor is tasteful'': judge-dependent (Fix B) ---
  not machine-checkable, so it needs a bonded human/LLM judge with a re-audit lane.

% 27
\item \wbopen\wbstar Define levels: a level-0 oracle is machine-checkable (a test, a
  SHA). A level-$(k{+}1)$ judge adjudicates disputes over level-$k$ rulings, posts a
  bond, and is subject to re-audit by a sampled level-$(k{+}1)$ panel whose
  \emph{own} evidence is the same machine-checkable artifact (the diff, the tests).
  Order judges by the level they adjudicate; the order is well-founded because every
  dispute ultimately preserves the same diff/tests, so any chain of ``judges judging
  judges'' terminates at the level-0 machine-checkable base in finitely many steps ---
  there is no infinite ascending chain because re-audit always reduces to the fixed,
  machine-checkable evidence, not to a fresh subjective judgement. The bond makes each
  level individually IC; well-foundedness makes the recursion terminate.

% 28
\item \wbcheck Subsidize the side carrying the scarcest cross-side externality ---
  in the harbor, \emph{supply} (worker agents / listed assets), because no requester
  shops an empty shelf. Phase~1 seeds supply at zero/negative price with
  platform-posted tasks at above-market bounties, generating the first settled
  outcomes (the bootstrap reputation data), then flips to charging demand once a
  liquidity threshold is met.

% 29
\item \wbtrace The cartel is self-sustaining under grim trigger when the discounted
  collusion stream dominates one-shot defection:
  $\dfrac{\delta\,\pi_C}{1-\delta(1-p_d)} \ge \pi_D$. Raising the detection
  probability $p_d$ (sampled adversarial re-audit of high-velocity pairs) or lowering
  the collusion payoff $\pi_C$ (diminishing-returns concavity on pairwise settled
  outcomes) raises the critical $\delta$ needed to sustain collusion until it exceeds
  $1$, breaking the cartel. Slashing on detection enters as the $-5(q_f-\mu)$ branch,
  which lowers the effective $\pi_C$.

% 30
\item \wbopen\wbstar Price-of-anarchy is a ratio for a \emph{fixed} game; once
  reputation is resold, the strategy space and payoffs change, so PoA is ill-posed.
  Reframe via Spence signaling with a resale market: a quality type $\theta$ signals
  by accumulating costly settled outcomes; the question is whether \emph{any}
  informative separating equilibrium survives when the signal (reputation) can be
  bought. It survives iff the marginal signaling cost is steeper for low types
  \emph{even accounting for purchase} --- i.e.\ buying reputation must cost more than
  it is worth to a low type. The structural break (pairwise concavity + re-audit) is
  what keeps the single-crossing condition intact under resale; without it, resale
  pools the types and the separating equilibrium collapses.

% 31
\item \wbcheck \emph{Trustless settlement:} local ledgers cannot glue into a global
  settled state without a shared chain. \emph{Equivocation Lower Bound:} the gossip
  window is the overlap on which local sections must agree, and an equivocating
  witness publishes inconsistent overlaps. \emph{Lax functor:} heterogeneous units
  make the conversion residual a nonzero cocycle. All three are ``local sections fail
  to glue,'' i.e.\ $H^1\ne 0$.

% 32
\item \wbcheck $H^0$ is the space of global sections --- it asks whether a consistent
  global state \emph{exists at all}. $H^1$ is the \emph{obstruction} to building a
  global section from locally consistent ones. Double-spend is not ``no consistent
  state exists'' ($H^0$); it is ``locally each harbor is consistent, but they cannot
  be glued into one consistent global ledger'' --- precisely a nonzero $H^1$ class.

% 33
\item \wbopen\wbstar Two horns. \emph{Defense horn:} a bounded-degree gossip topology
  with a shared transparency log and slashable witness bonds forces detected
  equivocation to be unprofitable; if the bond dominates the worst-case window value
  (IV.24), the only surviving sections are consistent ones and $H^1=0$ effectively
  (economically, not topologically). \emph{Impossibility horn:} without consensus,
  there exist gossip schedules under which two harbors commit contradictory overlaps
  before detection, so the cocycle is nonzero for that schedule --- meaning $H^1=0$
  \emph{deterministically} requires agreement on order, which is exactly the consensus
  the federation refuses (FLP). The honest position: the federation buys
  \emph{economic} $H^1=0$ (deterrence), not \emph{topological} $H^1=0$ (prevention) ---
  and proving that boundary precisely is the deepest open problem in the layer.

\end{enumerate}
