\caption{The commitment lifecycle with its oracle-bound closure gate. A commitment
is a durable, \emph{violable} promise (Cohen \& Levesque's persistent goal): it may
end \emph{done}, \emph{abandoned}, or \emph{superseded}. The load-bearing gate is
Law~2 (I6): a transition to \emph{done} is \emph{refused} --- the promise stays open
--- unless the agent supplies a reference drawn from the finite oracle vocabulary,
re-verified by the daemon at closure time; free-text notes cannot close a promise.
Law~1 (I5) closes the complementary Goodhart hole: the deadline is derived by the
daemon from a scope policy --- the agent picks the \emph{work}, never the
\emph{deadline}. That finite vocabulary is precisely the kernel's honesty boundary
(OP-5): it can mechanically verify ``the test you named passed,'' never ``the
refactor is good.''}
