\caption{The specialization boundary (Thm.~\ref{thm:specialization}) at a
three-agent pool, and the shape of the error the superseded threshold made.
\emph{Solid}: the correct Erlang-C boundary $g_A(\rho,c)$, which \emph{saturates}
at the capacity ratio ($g\to c=3$) --- past a certain load the boundary is only
asking the specialist to replace the pool's throughput, never more.
\emph{Dashed}: the threshold this paper originally proposed,
$\tilde g=1+(c-1)\rho/(1-\rho)$, which \emph{diverges}. The two cross exactly once,
at $\rho\approx0.307$ for $c=3$, so the proposed form errs in \emph{both}
directions: in the left lens it certifies a sole owner who loses on mean response
time, in the right lens it rejects one who wins. The marked point is the worked
instance in the text ($\lambda_F=2/\text{hr}$, $\mu_{\mathrm{spec}}=3/\text{hr}$,
three generalists at $1.2/\text{hr}$ each): the correct boundary is $2.483$ and the
premium $2.5$ clears it, while $\tilde g=3.5$ would have sent the work to the pool.
A direct sweep against simulation rejects $\tilde g$ at $z=-15.7$ and $z=+32$ on the
two sides [internal, \texttt{b8\_specialization.py}, seed 20260816].}
