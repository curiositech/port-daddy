\caption{Durability by fault class --- the single most important correction to the
kernel's foundational promise, and exactly Gray \& Reuter's
atomicity-versus-durability distinction (the ``D'' in ACID is a separate promise).
The unqualified claim ``a write that returned success survives a crash'' is true for
a \emph{process} crash (I1a: the OS page cache persists past the dying daemon) but
\emph{not guaranteed} for an OS crash or power loss (I1b), because under normal
synchronization the WAL is synced only at checkpoints --- the seductive belief that
``normal sync is safe in WAL mode'' conflates crash-\emph{consistency} (true) with
power-loss \emph{durability} (false). The remedy for I1b is full synchronization or
a checkpoint on the commit path; the kernel does not pay that cost by default, and
the prose must not imply it does. We split the invariant accordingly: I1a is
\textsf{implemented}; I1b is \textsf{not guaranteed} under the default WAL
configuration.}
