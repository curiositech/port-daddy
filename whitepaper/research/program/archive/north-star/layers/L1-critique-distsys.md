## Steelman (distributed-systems framing)

At its strongest, this dossier makes a genuinely good move: it reframes multi-agent coordination as a **protocol stack with an asynchronous, lossy, partially-failing transport**, and refuses to pretend the agents are co-located processes sharing memory. That instinct is correct and underexploited in the LLM-agent literature, which mostly treats "the conversation" as a benign transcript rather than a fault-tolerant distributed computation.

Three things the dossier gets right in my field's terms:

1. **It identifies the conversation as a distributed termination problem, not a chat log.** Naming Dijkstra-Scholten (diffusing computations) and Chandy-Lamport (consistent snapshots) for "has this conversation tree quiesced" is exactly the right reduction. Most agent frameworks detect "done" by timeout or by the orchestrator getting bored. The dossier correctly says: *no pending commitments in the branch* is a **global stable predicate** over a distributed computation, and stable-predicate detection is a solved-but-nontrivial subfield. That is the single sharpest insight in the document.

2. **It binds the soundness floor to FLP.** Invoking Fischer-Lynch-Paterson to say "quiescence detection must be eventually-correct with a failure detector, not perfect" is the right humility. It means termination is a **liveness property under an unreliable failure detector** (Chandra-Toueg ◇W territory), and the dossier at least gestures at this. The honest header — "real where it touches the kernel, a specification everywhere it touches the agents" — is the kind of claim a distributed-systems reviewer respects, because it doesn't oversell consistency it hasn't proven.

3. **The Saga reduction for cancellation is correct.** Treating a cancelled `cfp`-that-already-awarded as needing compensating transactions (Garcia-Molina & Salem) is precisely how you reason about partial-failure rollback without distributed locks. The conversation tree is a long-lived business transaction; compensation, not 2PC, is the right model. That instinct is sound.

So the steelman is: **this is one of the few agent-coordination designs that treats the medium as a network and the dialogue as a distributed protocol with terminal states, provenance, and compensation — rather than as prompt-stuffing with extra steps.**

## Gaps & inconsistencies (prioritized)

**1. The consistency model is never stated — this is the foundational omission.** A distributed-systems expert's first question is: *what consistency guarantees does the shared state offer, and what does a performative observe?* The dossier inventories `tube` (at-least-once, ordered), `pheromone` (decaying), `commitments` (daemon-clocked), `transcript-store` — but never says whether these compose into a coherent consistency model. At-least-once delivery + a performative taxonomy means **every performative handler must be idempotent**, and idempotency is never named as a wire-contract obligation. `inform` re-delivery, a double-processed `award`, a replayed `cancel` — these are routine under at-least-once, and the dossier's felicity-conditions section (which is otherwise good) does not include "is this act a duplicate?" This is more fundamental than felicity conditions: it's the difference between a protocol that works under the stated transport and one that corrupts state on the first redelivery.

**2. The single daemon is a single point of failure and a hidden consistency oracle — unexamined.** Everything load-bearing routes through one Express daemon on `localhost:9876` with SQLite. The dossier's whole distributed-termination apparatus (Chandy-Lamport, FLP) is *premised on no global clock and no central observer* — but PD **has** a central observer: the daemon owns deadlines, the breach monitor, the Arbiter, the commitment clock. So which is it? Either (a) the daemon is a linearizable central coordinator, in which case quiescence detection is **trivial** (it's a local query against SQLite, FLP doesn't bite, and citing Dijkstra-Scholten is theater), or (b) the agents are genuinely distributed and the daemon is just another partition-prone participant, in which case the daemon's SQLite is a consistency bottleneck and you owe a story for daemon crash/restart mid-conversation. The dossier wants the intellectual credit of (b) while shipping the architecture of (a). **This contradiction must be resolved explicitly.** My read: PD is fundamentally a *centralized* coordinator with distributed *clients*, which is a totally respectable design — but then the FLP/Chandy-Lamport framing is over-claimed and should be scoped to "what happens when the daemon itself is unavailable or an agent partitions from it," which is the only place the impossibility results actually bite.

**3. Exactly-once / idempotency / message dedup is absent from the envelope.** Following from (1): the envelope has `inReplyTo`, `conversationId` (designed) — but no idempotency key, no dedup window, no delivery-receipt semantics. FIPA hand-waves this too, so citing FIPA doesn't cover you. Under at-least-once you need either (a) idempotent effects keyed by message ID, or (b) an effectively-once layer (dedup store + ack). The dossier should engage **Lamport's "Time, Clocks, and the Ordering of Events" (1978)** and the exactly-once-delivery-is-impossible / effectively-once-is-achievable distinction (cf. the Kafka/event-sourcing literature, and Helland's "Life Beyond Distributed Transactions" 2007 / "Idempotence Is Not a Medical Condition" 2012). This is the canon the dossier most conspicuously skips, and it's *more* central to "the conversation as a fault-tolerant protocol" than the speech-act philosophy it leads with.

**4. Causal ordering is missing.** `conversationId` + `inReplyTo` gives you a reply tree, but not a **causal order** across concurrent branches. In a nested protocol (B2), two sub-conversations can produce `inform`s whose relative order matters for the supervisor's `decide`. Without vector clocks or at minimum a happened-before relation (Lamport), the digest in H2 cannot reconstruct a consistent causal history, and the q-accumulation function in C3 may aggregate over an inconsistent cut. The dossier cites Chandy-Lamport (snapshots) but **not Lamport's happened-before**, which is the prerequisite. A consistent snapshot is meaningless without a defined causal order to be consistent *with*.

**5. The failure detector is named in passing but never specified — and it's the crux.** Open-problem 1 says "eventually-correct with a failure detector," which is exactly right, but no failure detector is designed. G3 ("when is silence a `failure`?") *is* the failure-detector spec and the dossier files it as a sub-bullet. In a real system this is the highest-stakes tunable: too-aggressive suspicion → false `failure` performatives → spurious re-`cfp` → duplicated work; too-lax → distress starvation and stuck conversations. The dossier should commit to the **Chandra-Toueg failure-detector hierarchy** (is PD targeting ◇P? ◇S? ◇W?) and state which class quiescence detection requires. As written, "presence/heartbeat substrate (L0 provides)" treats the hardest distributed-systems decision in the stack as a solved dependency.

**6. Inconsistency with the L0 honesty canon: "loud-fail" vs at-least-once redelivery.** ADR-0045 loud-fail is invoked repeatedly. But under at-least-once delivery, a perfectly healthy system will *routinely* redeliver, reorder, and time-out-then-succeed. If every protocol anomaly is a "typed visible signal," the operator drowns in false alarms from normal network behavior — the loud-fail discipline collides with the transport's own non-determinism. The dossier needs to distinguish **benign transport non-determinism** (dedup silently, this is correct) from **genuine protocol violation** (surface loudly). It currently treats "silent drop = bad" as axiomatic (gap 14), but under at-least-once, *silent dedup of a redelivery is mandatory, not a sin*. This is a real consistency conflict between two of the project's own canon ADRs (0045 honesty vs. the tube's at-least-once semantics) and the dossier doesn't catch it.

**7. Capability attenuation (E4/open-problem 8) needs a monotonicity proof, not a prose assertion.** "B's caps ⊆ A's" across delegation + nesting is a lattice-monotonicity property. The dossier rightly connects it to the anchor-protocol ProVerif proofs, but ProVerif proves *secrecy/authenticity of the crypto chain*, not *monotonicity of the capability set under composition*. Those are different theorems. This wants a separate treatment — either a TLA+/Alloy model of the capability lattice under delegate+spawn, or an explicit reduction showing the coordination-attenuation invariant is entailed by the crypto-attenuation invariant. The dossier flags the question but conflates the two proof obligations.

**8. Backpressure (gap 13) is under-theorized as priority scheduling.** "Coalesce `inform`, never delay `distress`" is a **priority-inversion** problem (the dossier names it, good) but the solution space is real and uncited: this is a multi-class queueing / scheduling problem with a strict-priority class (distress) that must be **non-starving for lower classes** while **never blocked by them**. Under at-least-once with redelivery, naive priority queues deadlock or livelock. Engage the actual literature (priority inheritance; the credit-based flow control of modern transports; LMAX/Disruptor-style backpressure). "Rate-limit a performative class" is the right phrase but it's one sentence where it needs a discipline.

## What an expert would add

- **State the CAP/PACELC position outright.** During a partition between an agent and the daemon, does the agent (a) block (CP — refuse to act without the coordinator), or (b) proceed optimistically and reconcile (AP — act, risk a conflicting claim, compensate via Saga later)? This single choice determines everything downstream and is currently unstated. Given the architecture, PD looks **CP** (the daemon is the authority; partitioned agents should block), which is *fine* and arguably correct for a coordination guard — but then the FLP framing applies only to the daemon-unreachable edge, and the dossier should say so.

- **An idempotency/dedup layer in the envelope spec**, keyed by a content-addressed message ID, with a stated dedup window, plus the rule that *all* performative effects (claim, award, commitment-open) are idempotent by construction. This is non-negotiable under the stated at-least-once transport and is the cheapest highest-leverage fix.

- **Lamport happened-before + a causal-delivery option for the envelope** (even if default is FIFO-per-channel), so the digest and q-accumulation operate over a consistent cut.

- **A concrete failure-detector spec** placed at the L0/L1 seam: heartbeat interval, suspicion timeout, the suspect→`failure` promotion rule, and the Chandra-Toueg class targeted. Tie the false-positive rate directly to the spurious-re-`cfp` cost.

- **Engage the canon the dossier skips:** Lamport 1978 (clocks/ordering); Chandra-Toueg 1996 (unreliable failure detectors — *the* paper for "eventually-correct quiescence"); Helland 2007/2012 (idempotence beyond distributed transactions — directly on-point for "conversation as fault-tolerant protocol"); Birman's virtual synchrony / process-group literature (the closest classical analogue to a fleet of agents joining/leaving a coordinated group with consistent membership — strikingly absent given G's directory/presence concerns are *exactly* group-membership-service problems). Optionally Lamport's Paxos/Raft only if any state is actually replicated (it isn't today — single SQLite — so *don't* cite consensus; that would be over-claiming the other direction).

- **A daemon-crash recovery story.** The daemon is the consistency oracle; SQLite WAL gives durability but the dossier never says what happens to in-flight conversations across a daemon restart. Are conversationIds recovered? Are pending `award`s re-driven? This is the actual fault-tolerance question for *this* architecture, and it's more important than the distributed-quiescence theory the dossier foregrounds.

## Verdict

**Not complete, not yet consistent — but the bones are unusually good for the genre.**

The dossier is *completionist on vocabulary* (speech acts, FIPA, GPGP, deontic logic) and *gestures correctly* at the deep distributed-systems problems (termination detection, Sagas, FLP). But it is **incomplete on the load-bearing distributed-systems substrate**: no consistency model, no idempotency/dedup contract, no causal order, no failure-detector spec, and — most damagingly — **an unresolved contradiction between the centralized-daemon architecture it ships and the leaderless-distributed framing it borrows for intellectual weight.**

Three things must change before paper-ready:

1. **Resolve the centralization contradiction (gap 2).** Declare PD a CP system with a central coordinator (daemon) and distributed clients. Re-scope FLP/Chandy-Lamport/Dijkstra-Scholten to the *only* place they bite — daemon-unreachable or agent-partition — and admit that in the common case quiescence is a local SQLite query. This converts theater into honest engineering and is fully compatible with the project's own ADR-0045 honesty discipline.

2. **Add the missing substrate layer between A (message) and B (protocol):** idempotency keys + dedup window, Lamport happened-before / causal delivery, and a concrete failure detector with its Chandra-Toueg class. Without this, the performative taxonomy is unsound under the project's *own stated* at-least-once transport.

3. **Reconcile loud-fail with at-least-once (gap 6).** Define the line between benign transport non-determinism (dedup silently) and genuine protocol violation (fail loud). As written, two canon ADRs contradict each other and the dossier doesn't notice.

Do those three and the layer is consistent with both the stack and my field's canon. The speech-act/GPGP scholarship is already strong; the distributed-systems scholarship is currently a layer of citations (Dijkstra-Scholten, Chandy-Lamport, FLP, Sagas) deployed *for the hard problems the system mostly sidesteps via centralization*, while the canon for the problems it *actually has* under at-least-once delivery (Lamport ordering, Chandra-Toueg detectors, Helland idempotence, Birman group membership) is missing. Fix the citation/architecture mismatch and this is a defensible distributed-protocol paper.
