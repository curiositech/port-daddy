"I have strong canon grounding now. The FIPA skill is decisive: it confirms the dossier's three best moves (protocols as first-class FSMs, the happy-path/robustness boundary, roles-not-identities) and exposes one it underweights (roles vs. identities). I have enough to write the expert review.

## Steelman (multi-agent systems framing)

Read charitably, this dossier is the strongest piece of MAS systems thinking I have seen applied to an LLM-agent OS, because it refuses the field's two recurring sins simultaneously. It does not commit the **emergent-coordination fallacy** ("give the agents good prompts and coordination will arise") — it takes FIPA's position (b) explicitly: pre-specified, inspectable interaction protocols so that even a dumb backend can participate correctly by pattern-following. And it does not commit the inverse sin of **protocol-as-API-spec conflation** — it correctly separates the *performative* (envelope, communicative act) from the *content language + ontology* (payload), which is precisely the FIPA ACL / SL split that most "agent framework" authors collapse. That A1–A3 decomposition is genuinely literate: distinguishing rational effect (what the daemon records as having happened) from perlocution (what the sender intended) is the FIPA SL feasibility-precondition-vs-rational-effect distinction, and naming it as "the home of dishonesty" is the right place to mount ADR-0045's honesty discipline.

The single best move in the whole document is gap-2 framed as a thesis: **"naming a pattern without its terminal + exception states *is* the failure."** This is exactly FIPA-00025's own honest boundary — interaction protocols specify the happy path; robustness (timeouts, out-of-sequence, cancellation, duplicates) is a *different layer*. The dossier internalizes that boundary rather than papering over it, and it pushes further than FIPA by demanding compensation semantics (Sagas) for the cancellation case FIPA explicitly leaves open. That is a real contribution, not a summary.

The layered honesty is also methodologically correct for a systems paper: the BUILT/DESIGNED/VISION ledger, checked against `grep` of the actual `lib/`, is the right epistemic hygiene. The most load-bearing single sentence in the dossier — *"every L1 honesty claim is conditional on non-forgeable identity (ADR-0040), which is unbuilt"* — is exactly the observation a MAS security reviewer would lead with: ACL performatives are worthless without an authenticated `sender`, because Contract-Net, felicity conditions, and commitment counterparties all presuppose you know who is speaking. Recognizing that the entire semantic layer floats on an unbuilt identity substrate is intellectually honest in a way most MAS papers (which simply *assume* honest agents) never are.

And the GPGP read is, at the headline level, correct: it imports the C(DL)/C(Do) commitment distinction, the enables/facilitates/hinders/redundant relationship typing, the power factor, and quiescence-as-termination. The catch that shipped commitments are *solo-against-a-clock* while GPGP commitments are *social* (A-to-B) is a sharp, true observation that most implementers miss entirely.

## Gaps & inconsistencies (prioritized)

**1. (P0 — field-concept misuse) The dossier never states whether L1 is closed or open coordination, and conflates Contract-Net-the-protocol with Contract-Net-the-market.** It treats `cfp→bid→award` as "the local skeleton of the L3 market." But Smith-1980's Contract-Net is *not* a market — it has no prices, no utility-maximizing bids, no equilibrium; it is a *task-announcement-and-mutual-selection* protocol where the bid is a capability/suitability claim, not a willingness-to-pay. Calling it "the auction once bids carry bonds" papers over a genuine mechanism-design discontinuity: Contract-Net is *not* incentive-compatible and Smith never claimed it was. The moment you bolt bonds + reputation onto it you are no longer doing Contract-Net, you are doing a sealed-bid auction with all of Myerson/Vickrey's incentive constraints — which the dossier's own open-problem 7 (Sybil-resistant Contract-Net) half-admits but does not name as the regime change it is. **Cite and engage:** Nisan et al. 2007 (Algorithmic Game Theory) and Sandholm's own work — the dossier cites leveled-commitment but not the auction theory that governs the bid step.

**2. (P0 — completionist) The common-knowledge problem is entirely absent.** This is the largest *conceptual* hole. The dossier's termination story ("no pending commitments in the branch") and its agreement performatives (`agree`, `propose`/`accept`) silently assume that when A tells B something, it becomes shared knowledge. The MAS canon (and the epistemic-logic literature behind even the Contract-Net reading) is emphatic: broadcast ≠ mutual knowledge ≠ common knowledge, and *coordinated action under uncertainty requires common knowledge, which is provably unattainable over a lossy channel* (the coordinated-attack / two-generals result). The dossier invokes FLP for consensus impossibility but misses the *epistemic* impossibility that bites a coordination protocol far sooner: an `agree` that A sent and B received does **not** give A common knowledge that B will act. Every "synchronized" act (debate-then-decide, fan-in barrier, mutual commitment "X if you do Y") sits on this fault line. This belongs as a starred open problem and is not even gestured at.

**3. (P1 — inconsistency with its own stack) Roles vs. identities — the dossier specifies coordination by identity throughout, which contradicts FIPA's core abstraction it claims to inherit.** The envelope (A2) carries `sender`/`receiver` *identity*; the protocol patterns (B) are never specified in terms of *roles* (Initiator/Responder/Auctioneer/Bidder). FIPA-00025's load-bearing insight is that protocols bind to roles, not identities, precisely so one agent can play multiple roles, roles can change hands mid-conversation, and any compliant backend can satisfy a role. A coordination layer whose protocol FSMs are not role-parameterized will re-encode identity coupling — the exact anti-pattern ("Identity-Based Routing") FIPA warns produces failures that appear only at swap/scale time. The dossier's protocol-composition section (B2) cannot actually work without roles, because a nested Contract-Net-inside-supervisor-worker requires the *same agent* to be Responder in the outer and Initiator in the inner conversation. This is a real inconsistency, not a nitpick.

**4. (P1 — completionist) The GPGP import is shallower than the dossier admits, even after self-flagging q-accumulation.** It correctly flags the missing q-accumulation function, but TÆMS is a *task-structure* formalism, not just a relationship taxonomy: it has a subtask hierarchy with quality/cost/duration distributions on leaf methods, and the coordination relationships (enables/facilitates) are *derived over that structure*. Without the task structure, "power factor 0.0–1.0" is a free-floating number with no semantics — in GPGP the power of a `facilitates` edge is computed from how much it shifts the quality/duration distribution, not assigned by hand. The dossier treats the relationship type and power factor as primitives to store on a commitment; GPGP treats them as *consequences of a task structure*. Importing the leaves without the tree is exactly the "headline only" sin the dossier accuses the seed of — and it commits a milder version itself.

**5. (P1 — BDI grounding is asserted, not used).** The dossier cites Rao-Georgeff, Cohen-Levesque, Bratman, and maps "obligation→adopted-desire, prohibition→negative-desire." But it never engages the actual BDI result that matters here: **commitment strategies** (blind / single-minded / open-minded — Rao-Georgeff 1991). The whole question of *when an agent may drop a commitment* (the negotiability index, the decommitment penalty, the renegotiation triggers) IS the commitment-strategy question, formalized 35 years ago. Cohen-Levesque's *persistent goal* (drop only when achieved, believed unachievable, or its motivation is gone) is the precise specification the dossier's "lifecycle (open/done/abandoned/superseded)" is an under-theorized shadow of. The dossier name-drops these papers in the bibliography but does not let them do work — a MAS reviewer will read that as citation-as-decoration.

**6. (P2 — distributed-systems rigor) The quiescence/FLP framing is good but the dossier picks the wrong half of the canon for the actual problem.** Dijkstra-Scholten / Chandy-Lamport detect termination of a *diffusing computation with reliable channels and no crashes*. The dossier's setting has crashes (it says so) and unreliable agents. The correct frame is **failure-detector-based termination** (Chandra-Toueg's ◇W) layered over a snapshot, and the honest claim is "eventually-correct termination modulo a failure detector," which it does gesture at — but it should also note that **its `cancel`/Saga compensation interacts badly with snapshot-based detection**: a compensating action issued after a snapshot can resurrect a "terminated" branch. That composition hazard (termination detection + compensation) is the genuinely novel hard problem and it is not stated.

**7. (P2 — missing mechanism) No deadlock / circular-wait treatment.** The coordination delegation chain (F2) handles ping-pong loops, but `enables` relationships plus commitments-with-counterparties create classic *commitment deadlock* (A waits on B's commitment, B waits on A's). GPGP's own failure-mode list (commitment cascade) is a sibling of this. The dossier has loop detection on *delegation* but nothing on *wait-for cycles in the commitment graph* — a distinct graph, a distinct cycle-detection problem.

**8. (P3 — taxonomy) Felicity conditions are named but not specified per-act, and the dossier omits the `not-understood` performative**, which is FIPA's canonical loud-fail-on-the-wire act (gap 14 reinvents it under a new name without citing it). Using FIPA-00037's `not-understood` would ground the "loud-fail protocol surface" in the standard instead of presenting it as novel.

## What an expert would add

- **A common-knowledge / agreement-attainability section** as a first-class open problem, with the coordinated-attack impossibility cited, and an explicit statement of which protocols require common knowledge (synchronized barriers, mutual commitment) vs. which tolerate mere mutual knowledge (fire-and-forget `inform`). This is the deepest thing missing.
- **Role-parameterized protocol FSMs.** Rewrite section B so each pattern is `{roles, role→legal-transitions, terminal states, exception edges, timeout policy}`. Then composition (B2) becomes role-rebinding across nested `conversationId`s, which actually works.
- **A commitment-strategy column** mapping the GPGP lifecycle to Cohen-Levesque persistent-goal conditions and Rao-Georgeff blind/single/open-minded strategies, so "when may abandon" has a formal answer rather than four enum values.
- **A TÆMS task-structure assumption** stated as an L0/L1 dependency: relationship type and power factor are *derived* from a task structure with quality/cost/duration distributions on methods — name where that structure lives, or honestly downgrade power-factor to "operator-assigned heuristic, not GPGP-derived."
- **Mechanism-design honesty on Contract-Net→market.** State plainly that bonded Contract-Net is a *different mechanism* (sealed-bid auction) with its own incentive-compatibility burden, cite Nisan et al. / Vickrey, and stop calling it "Contract-Net" past the bond boundary.
- **The termination × compensation composition hazard** as its own starred problem (snapshot-then-compensate can un-terminate a branch).
- **Adopt FIPA's `not-understood`** as the named loud-fail act rather than inventing gap-14 from scratch.
- **A commitment wait-for-graph deadlock detector** distinct from the delegation loop detector.

## Verdict

**Not yet complete; largely consistent within itself but inconsistent with the MAS canon it claims to inherit.**

As a *systems honesty document* it is paper-ready and exemplary — the BUILT/DESIGNED ledger and the "everything is conditional on unbuilt identity" thesis are the strongest parts and should anchor the paper's framing. As a *multi-agent-systems contribution* it is roughly 80% there on breadth and about 50% there on depth: it correctly enumerates the families and catches several real seed gaps, but it imports GPGP, BDI, and Contract-Net at headline depth and then commits milder versions of the same "headline only" sin it accuses the seed of.

Three things must change before it is paper-ready to a MAS audience:

1. **Add the common-knowledge / agreement-attainability problem.** Its absence is the one gap a MAS reviewer will treat as disqualifying, because it undermines the termination and mutual-commitment stories the dossier rests on.
2. **Make protocols role-parameterized, not identity-parameterized**, to resolve the internal inconsistency between the envelope's `sender`/`receiver` and the composition section that cannot work without roles.
3. **Stop calling bonded Contract-Net "Contract-Net."** Name the mechanism-design regime change and cite the auction-theory canon, or the L1→L3 "market skeleton" claim reads as a category error to anyone who has read Smith-1980 and Nisan-2007.

Fix those three and let the four cited-but-idle papers (Cohen-Levesque, Rao-Georgeff, FIPA-00025, FIPA-00037) actually do work, and this becomes a genuinely strong layer. Until then it is a superb engineering ledger wearing a MAS theory it has read the abstracts of.",
