## Source lineages (one per miner)

### Miner 1

These four sources converge on one idea: a resource-bounded agent cannot be rational by re-deriving what to do at every instant (decision theory) nor by executing a fixed script to completion (traditional programs) — Rao & Georgeff's "commitment paradox" (1995). The resolution is intentions as a THIRD, first-class, persistent mental attitude (alongside beliefs and desires) that survives belief/desire changes and is dropped only under explicit termination conditions. Rao & Georgeff (1991) axiomatize this as commitment strategies (blind / single-minded / open-minded) via modal U-axioms; Bratman, Israel & Pollack (1988) ground it as plans-that-filter-deliberation, with a filter-override mechanism mediating the irreducible stability-vs-revisability tension. The GAP for LLM agents: they re-derive intentions from a prompt every turn, so an obligation evaporates when the context window rolls over; a BDI commitment is durable STATE with an owner, success/drop/impossibility conditions, and a reconsideration policy — exactly what Port Daddy can persist in SQLite.

### Miner 2

Three of the four sources are the same paper — Tufiș & Ganascia, "A Normative Extension for the BDI Agent Model" — which grafts a deontic layer onto classic BDI: an agent carries an Abstract Norm Base (norms it *recognizes* are in force) distinct from a Norm Instance Base (norms it has *internalized* and that are currently active), and resolves conflicts by ranking the worst consequence of maximal non-conflicting plan subsets. The fourth (Jenkins & Jarvis, "BDI Agents: A Soft Model for Organisations?") supplies the Bratman point that intentions are commitments whose purpose is to *bound future deliberation* — once intended, you stop reconsidering. The CRUX for Port Daddy: the Arbiter regiments prohibitions ("can't") by making forbidden states unreachable, but obligations ("must") are intrinsically violable — the literature handles them not by regimentation but by representing each obligation as a tuple ⟨Modality, Activation, Expiration, Content, Sanction, Reward⟩, monitoring activation/expiration against beliefs, detecting violation when the deadline/expiration passes with content unsatisfied, and applying a sanction that re-enters deliberation as a weighted (negative) desire. Responsibility = an obligation instance that lives in working memory, is monitored continuously, and bites via sanction if dropped.

### Miner 3

Coordination-as-contract runs from Smith's Contract Net Protocol (a task is announced, agents bid, the manager awards, and the award is a mutual binding "contract" between manager and contractor with explicit message types: task-announcement, bid, award, report) through Decker-Lesser's GPGP, which reframes commitments as SOCIAL constraints posted to autonomous local schedulers — directed at specific agents (per Castelfranchi/Shoham), carrying a negotiability index (high/medium/low) and an estimated utility, breakable only with notification and only when the largest-positive-utility-change schedule justifies it. Ostrom supplies the institutional layer: a fleet sharing one codebase is a commons, and her 8 principles (defined boundaries, proportional cost/benefit, collective choice, monitoring accountable to the monitored, GRADUATED sanctions, cheap fast conflict resolution, right to organize, nested enterprises) tell you when advisory coordination is self-governing versus a "daemon monarchy." The two PD-native skills close it formally: mechanism-design makes truthful, responsible behavior incentive-compatible via collateralized bonds + multi-oracle settlement, and game-theory proves advisory claims are a Nash equilibrium only under observable immutable history + persistent (non-Sybil) identity + a sufficiently long shadow of the future, sustained by a graduated trigger strategy rather than grim trigger. The throughline: `pd roadmap pop atomic-claim` is already a degenerate Contract Net award; bonding + graduated sanctions + reputation are exactly the missing terms that turn a claim into an OWNED, ongoing obligation.

### Miner 4

Three threads converge. (1) The runtime-verification-for-agents skill (Port Daddy FORMAL_VERIFICATION_PLAN.md, Section 3) defines the Arbiter: an independent agent that compiles formal invariants (TLA+ safety/liveness) into online bounded monitors that stream over state transitions, fire on violation, and route through a remediation decision tree (alert/auto-remediate/halt) — solving the "quis custodiet" recursion with a trivially-correct meta-monitor reaper. (2) Weyns' Engineering Self-Adaptive Systems establishes the non-negotiable architectural separation between the *managed system* (domain logic) and the *managing system* (a MAPE-K feedback loop whose sole responsibility is keeping machine-readable adaptation goals true), with hierarchical loops at separated time-scales (reactive→tactical→strategic) and explicit escalation when the capability envelope is exceeded. (3) The mislabeled "kephart-autonomic" file actually carries Beale's "Rise up, Revolt!" — the insight that systems must *refuse to let the operator internalize blame* for a property the system itself is responsible for maintaining: when tests go red or the roadmap goes inconsistent, the monitor must own the obligation and attribute the failure to a named component, not let it silently accumulate. Together they say: an agent becomes responsible by being the explicit, separated, always-running owner of a continuously-checked property over an event stream, with a remediation contract and a self-healing watchdog.

### Miner 5

Port Daddy already operates as a "building department": it enforces invariants (Arbiter, mapped to a BondedCommons TLA+ spec), records what happened (activity log, feedback stream, roadmap_item_status_events), and gates expensive action with money (bonds escrow/slash + budget-guard throttle/kill). The intellectual lineage is Ostrom's commons-governance ("monitoring without sanctions is failure" — quoted verbatim in lib/bonds.ts) crossed with classic admission-control/back-pressure systems theory (lib/budget-guard.ts) and TLA+ safety invariants. The conspicuous structural absence is *time-bound obligation*: every accountability primitive is either instantaneous (a claim succeeds or fails right now) or economic (a bond is escrowed then refunded/slashed on exit) — none of them holds an agent to a promise that comes due *later*. ADR-0033 itself names this gap ("claims have no TTL today... a follow-up ADR can add a stale claim sweeper") and never closes it, and there is no per-actor track record anywhere in lib/ or cli/.

### Miner 6

The "make an LLM agent responsible for an ongoing job" problem has accreted a stack of widely-tried answers, each grafted from an adjacent tradition: role prompting from instruction-following, critic/debate panels from LLM-as-judge (Zheng et al. 2023) and self-refine, eval/CI gates from software testing, cron/always-on loops from ops automation, reflection/ReAct (Yao et al. 2022, Shinn Reflexion) from chain-of-thought, and memory/scratchpads from agent-memory work. The unifying intellectual move is the same in every case: simulate responsibility by stacking text-generation behaviors (more instructions, more self-talk, more re-runs, more notes) rather than instituting an accountable, persistent OWNER with standing obligations that outlive any single context window. They all conflate "produced correct output once" or "described an intention to maintain X" with "is on the hook for X being true tomorrow." Each is a legitimate component, but none, alone or stacked, creates durable ownership — which is precisely the gap the deep mechanisms must close.
