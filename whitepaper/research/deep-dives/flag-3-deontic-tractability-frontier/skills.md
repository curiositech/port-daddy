# Skills — flag 3

## Primary

**`fipa-00037-communicative-act-library`** — the most directly relevant skill in
the catalog for this dive, and the one whose community is most likely to hold
the prior art. Paper 6's commitments (standing prohibitions, conditional
obligations, exclusive claims with intervals, chained deadlines) are close to
FIPA's commitment and obligation semantics, and the multi-agent-systems
community has been formalizing normative conflict for two decades under names
this program has not searched.

Use it for vocabulary, not for verdicts: it supplies the terms to search with
(commitment, obligation discharge, normative conflict, institutional power) so
the sweep can find prior art that uses none of our words.

**`game-theoretic-agent-incentives`** — bears on Part II. Its treatment of when a
coordination function needs a designated authority versus a mechanism is the
same question Paper 6's inventory table answers, and its warning against "just
add enforcement" is the same instinct as Paper 6's "the theorems relocate
authority rather than abolishing it."

## Secondary

**`harbor-exposition`** — for Q7 and Q9. The dichotomy framing is an exposition
decision as much as a citation one: naming Theorem 1a/1b a dichotomy is exactly
the skill's "word-as-affection" move, where using the field's real term is how
you show respect for the reader who knows it.

**`productive-discourse-facilitator`** — genuinely applicable here, in its
steelmanning discipline. Paper 6 *falsified its own whitepaper's proposed
threshold in both directions* and shipped the correction. When comparing against
prior art, hold the prior work to the same standard: state the strongest version
of the claim that it already covers this ground, and only then answer it.

## Not applicable

`ux-friction-analyzer`, `multi-agent-authoring-product-bar` — product-facing
skills, no bearing on a literature comparison.
