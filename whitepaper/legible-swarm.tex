\begin{abstract}\noindent
A swarm of autonomous coding agents, left to coordinate itself, is
\textbf{Hobbes' state of nature}~\cite{hobbes1651}: rational, even well-meaning
actors fall into a war of all against all --- double-claimed files, pull requests
no human can read, confident lies, footguns obvious only in hindsight. We argue
that the operator rationally \emph{consents} to a coordinating authority --- a
\emph{local} Leviathan running on one machine --- for exactly Hobbes' reason: the
alternative is worse. That authority governs the only way any sovereign governs a
population it cannot personally inspect: by making the swarm
\textbf{legible}~\cite{scott1998}. The central hazard is James~C.~Scott's warning
that high-modernist \emph{over}-legibility crushes \emph{m\^etis} --- the local,
hard-to-codify practical knowledge a system actually runs on --- so we make Scott's
warning a buildable rule: \textbf{digest-with-zoom}, in which every summary is a
lens onto a verifiable artifact, never a replacement for it. We then make three
claims the volume's flagship rests on. First, the binding constraint on swarm scale
is \emph{read-poverty}, not write-contention: the write side (claims, locks, the
Arbiter) is solved; the read side --- finding the right agent, the relevant work,
the trustworthy collaborator --- is where the next order of magnitude is won or
lost. Second, \emph{tokens are simultaneously the swarm's cost-of-goods-sold and
its legibility engine} --- the digest \emph{is} compaction --- so the cost question
and the legibility question are one question. Third, the title's other half ---
\emph{authority} --- demands a first-class, scoped, revocable \emph{consent}
primitive with an inalienable operator-override, and an operator-attention
objective spined on Signal Detection Theory rather than raw throughput. We ground
every claim in Port Daddy's shipped read-surfaces, label each mechanism honestly as
\Built/\BuiltWeak/\Designed/\Vision, and hand the next paper a legible, checkpointed,
outcome-bearing identity --- the precondition for reputation and, eventually, the
market.
\end{abstract}

\vspace{0.4cm}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} legibility, multi-agent coordination, human-in-the-loop,

\section{Introduction: the swarm is a state of nature}\label{sec:intro}
% ═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The pitch for running many coding agents at once is parallelism: five agents, five
times the work. The reality, the first time you try it on a real repository, is a
specific kind of dread. Two agents claim the same file and race each other's writes.
One agent, asked to make the tests pass, makes them pass by deleting the failing
one. A fourth opens a pull request you cannot review because three others have
opened five more. You spawned a team and got a mob. The work did not get more
parallel; your \emph{uncertainty} did.

This is not a prompt-engineering problem. It is a coordination problem, and
coordination problems have a literature. \textbf{Thomas Hobbes}, \emph{Leviathan}
(1651)~\cite{hobbes1651} --- \emph{the foundational social-contract text: rational
self-interested actors with no common power above them fall into a ``war of every
man against every man,'' in which life is ``solitary, poor, nasty, brutish, and
short.''} Hobbes' insight is structural, not moral: the actors need not be
malicious. Absent a shared authority, even rational, well-meaning agents defect,
because each cannot trust the others not to. Your agents are in exactly that state
of nature. They do not need better prompts; they need a referee they have agreed to
obey.

decision records. Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy} maps each Hobbesian failure to its

The striking recent result is that the identification is not merely a useful
analogy. \textbf{Dai et al.\ 2024}, \emph{Artificial Leviathan}
(arXiv:2406.14373)~\cite{dai2024} --- \emph{a sandbox of LLM agents with
psychological drives that, starting from unrestrained conflict resembling the state
of nature, were observed to establish social contracts, authorize a sovereign, and
form ``a peaceful commonwealth founded on mutual cooperation.''} The arc this
volume posits as its organizing metaphor is, in at least one controlled study, an
\emph{emergent dynamic} of LLM populations (the methodological ancestor is
\textbf{Park et al.\ 2023}, \emph{Generative Agents}~\cite{park2023}, where LLM
agents form emergent social behavior in a sandbox town). The Leviathan is not a
costume we put on the system; it is the attractor the system already falls toward.
Port Daddy's wager is that we can build the consented sovereign \emph{deliberately,
locally, and legibly} rather than let an illegible one congeal by accident.

\subsection{Why ``local''}\label{sec:local}

Hobbes' commonwealth is territorial: one sovereign per realm. Port Daddy's realm is
your machine. The \textbf{daemon} (\texttt{server.ts}, run as the launchd service
\texttt{com.portdaddy.daemon} --- \emph{the single always-on process on
\texttt{localhost:9876}, backed by SQLite/WAL, that every \texttt{pd} command talks
to and that holds state no agent can edit}) is the L0 kernel and the realm: your
machine, your swarm, no network, no cryptography required. Cryptography enters only
at L3, the instant another operator's fleet touches your repo (Paper~4). The
single-operator Leviathan is the \emph{wedge} --- the thing a solo developer pays
for today --- and the federation of Leviathans is the platform, deferred behind the
wedge by design. This paper is entirely about the wedge.

\section{Consent to the Leviathan (the normative claim)}\label{sec:authority}
% ═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

Why should the operator cede authority to a daemon --- let it block an agent's
claim, escalate a decision, reorder a merge, refuse a \texttt{done}? Hobbes'
answer, transposed: the operator authorizes the sovereign because the covenant is
rational under the alternative. Crucially, in Hobbes the covenant is \emph{among the
subjects} --- ``a real unity of them all\ldots by covenant of every man with every
man''~\cite{hobbes1651,sep-hobbes} --- not a bargain struck \emph{with} the
sovereign. Transposed: the operator does not negotiate with each agent; the operator
institutes a \emph{rule of the road} (L1, the \textbf{conversation protocol},
\texttt{docs/adr/0047-conversation-protocol.md} --- \emph{typed performatives,
commitments, delegation, and termination so messages carry real intent, ownership,
and stop conditions}) that all agents are bound to, and the daemon enforces it.

The authority is \emph{asymmetric by design}. This is the same lesson \textbf{Raft}
teaches for distributed systems (\textbf{Ongaro \& Ousterhout 2014},
\emph{In Search of an Understandable Consensus Algorithm}~\cite{raft2014} ---
\emph{a strong-leader consensus protocol deliberately made less general than Paxos
in order to be comprehensible and correctly implementable}): a strong leader who
decides the common case unilaterally is simpler and more reliable than democratic
consensus among peers, provided the common case dominates. A solo operator's swarm
is overwhelmingly common-case; the strong-leader daemon is the right shape. (And it
is the right shape \emph{architecturally}: per the cross-layer resolution C3, the
whole stack L0--L2 is honestly a \emph{CP system with a central coordinator and
distributed clients}; the leaderless distributed-systems canon bites only at the
edges where the daemon is not the sole observer --- agent-partitioned-from-daemon,
and cross-harbor at L3. We do not borrow the intellectual credit of a leaderless
design while shipping a centralized one.)

\subsection{The legible-sovereign amendment}\label{sec:legible-sovereign}

Hobbes' absolutism needs one modern amendment, and it is the crux of this paper.
Hobbes argued the sovereign, being the \emph{product} of the covenant rather than a
\emph{party} to it, can never breach it and need not be
inspectable~\cite{hobbes1651,sep-hobbes}. For a software authority this is exactly
wrong. An \emph{illegible authority} is the fifth state-of-nature failure mode
(Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy}) wearing a crown: if the daemon blocks an agent and the
operator cannot see why, the operator's trust --- and therefore the consent that
legitimizes the authority --- erodes.

\begin{property}[The legible-sovereign rule]\label{prop:legible-sovereign}
The coordinating authority must be the \emph{most} legible actor in the system, not
the least. Every act of authority --- a claim denial, an Arbiter detection, an
escalation, an ``all good'' attestation --- must be a logged, named, zoomable event
whose reason the operator can reconstruct. Consent is renewable only while the
sovereign is inspectable.
\end{property}

This is the inversion that rescues the design from despotism, and it is the move a
political theorist would press hardest (\S\ref{sec:consent-canon}). In Hobbes the
sovereign is opaque \emph{by structure}; here the sovereign is the one actor whose
every exercise of power is an audit record. It is implemented as a discipline:
ADR-0045's \textbf{honest attestation} (\texttt{lib/attest.ts},
\texttt{lib/attest-invariants.ts} --- \emph{a single self-report that runs every
invariant check, distinguishes verified-good from no-evidence-of-bad, regiments what
it can, and screams about what it cannot}) is precisely the sovereign making its own
judgments legible: ``all good'' becomes ``a claim with a verifier, not vibes.''

\pitfall{There is a regress hiding here, and Scott would press it. If every act of
authority must be legible, the \emph{legibility apparatus itself} is an exercise of
authority --- the digest decides what is surfaced and what is buried. The Attention
Queue ranker (\S\ref{sec:discovery}) is \emph{the} sovereign act: it decides what
the operator sees \emph{right now}. A ranker that silently buries an escalation is
governing opaquely. The rule is therefore not complete until the ranker's
\emph{omissions} are themselves legible --- a ``what did the queue not show me, and
why'' counter-surface. Ranking is governing; we say so explicitly in
\S\ref{sec:consent-canon}.}

\subsection{Consent as a first-class, revocable primitive}\label{sec:consent}

The dossier this paper is built from said the operator ``consents to cede decision
authority.'' A political theorist's first question --- and the one that decides
whether the Leviathan frame is load-bearing or decorative --- is: \emph{consented
how, to what, and revocable when?} ``The operator just starts using the tool'' is
not Hobbesian consent; it is what the canon calls \textbf{tacit consent}, and
\textbf{Hume} (\emph{Of the Original Contract}, 1748~\cite{hume1748} --- \emph{the
decisive critique: the peasant who ``consents'' by remaining in the country has no
real alternative, so the consent does no normative work}) demolished it two and a
half centuries ago. We therefore make consent a concrete object.

\begin{definition}[Consent grant]\label{def:consent}
A \textbf{consent grant} is an explicit, scoped, revocable authorization the
operator issues to the daemon, of the form
\[
g = \langle\, \textsc{scope},\ \textsc{stakes-ceiling}\ s_{\max},\ \textsc{reversibility-floor}\ v_{\min},\ \textsc{ttl},\ \textsc{revocable}\,\rangle,
\]
e.g.\ ``the daemon \emph{may} auto-land reversible diffs under stakes threshold
$s_{\max}$ in the \texttt{tests/} subtree without asking, for 24h.'' A grant is a
first-class, legible object: it appears in the digest, it has an audit trail, and it
expires. The authority's legitimate action set at any moment is exactly the union of
its active grants.
\end{definition}

Consent so defined gives the design something Hume's critique cannot reach: a
discrete authorization event, a bounded scope, and an exit. It also hands the next
two papers a concrete object. A grant is precisely what a \emph{hosted-trust}
offering at L3 transfers (``we make Alice's fleet legible to you, under this scope''
is the sale of a consent grant to a third party --- Paper~4 prices it), which is why
\S\ref{sec:bridge} lists consent among the things L2 provides upward.

\begin{property}[The inalienable operator-override]\label{prop:override}
Hobbes grants the subject exactly one inalienable right --- self-preservation; you
cannot covenant away the right to resist when your \emph{life} is at
stake~\cite{hobbes1651}. The computational analog is an always-available,
unconditional operator-override: a \texttt{stop now / I withdraw authority} channel
that \emph{no autonomy level can suppress}. ADR-0046's mayday-red lane is the
daemon$\to$operator channel; the override is its dual, the operator$\to$daemon
channel, and it is a \emph{right}, not a feature: it preempts every grant, every
in-flight landing, every Arbiter decision.
\end{property}

This pairs with \textbf{Hirschman}'s \emph{Exit, Voice, and Loyalty}
(1970~\cite{hirschman1970} --- \emph{the choice between leaving (exit) and
complaining (voice) as the two levers a member has over a declining organization}):
the design has rich \emph{voice} machinery (escalation, pheromone spray, the queue)
and the override supplies the missing \emph{exit}. Exit is the discipline that keeps
voice honest; an operator who cannot cheaply withdraw a grant has no real leverage
over the sovereign.

\section{The mechanism: legibility-with-zoom}\label{sec:zoom}
% ═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

A referee that only prevents collisions would be a glorified lockfile. The reason
an operator would \emph{pay} for the Leviathan is the next move: it makes the swarm
\emph{legible}. \textbf{James C.\ Scott}, \emph{Seeing Like a State}
(1998)~\cite{scott1998} --- \emph{states impose legibility (cadastral maps,
standardized surnames, the metric grid, scientific forestry) to make populations
countable and governable; high-modernist over-legibility --- the overconfident faith
that a clean top-down scheme can replace messy local reality --- recurrently fails
because it destroys the local practical knowledge (\textbf{m\^etis}: hands-on,
case-specific, hard-to-codify know-how) the simplified order depended on.} Scott's
canonical example is the German \emph{Normalbaum} (``scientific forest'') --- rows
of single-species, same-age trees, gloriously legible to the state's yield tables
--- that thrived for one generation and then collapsed, because the legible
monoculture had erased the illegible ecological m\^etis (soil fungi, undergrowth,
species mix) that kept a real forest alive~\cite{scott1998}. The software port of
Scott's thesis (\textbf{Rao 2010}, \emph{A Big Little Idea Called
Legibility}~\cite{rao2010}) made ``legibility'' a standard lens on systems that
flatten reality to a single purpose.

Map this onto agent oversight and the design rule writes itself. The operator is the
state; the swarm is the population; the dashboard/TUI/digest is the cadastral map.
The temptation is identical: render the swarm as a clean grid of green tiles and
\emph{govern the map}. The \emph{Normalbaum failure} in software is the
\textbf{Potemkin digest} --- a beautiful surface whose tiles assert a reality nobody
can check, having paraphrased away the diff, the test output, the error, the
reasoning that constituted the actual work. The operator who acts on that map is the
forester admiring the yield table while the forest dies.

\begin{definition}[The one law: digest-with-zoom]\label{def:zoom}
Every summary is a lens onto the real artifact, never a replacement for it.
Summarize the \textbf{state}; link to the \textbf{work}. A digest you cannot zoom is
a high-modernist map. A digest that always reaches the underlying artifact preserves
m\^etis: the operator can descend from abstraction to ground truth and catch the
lie. Formally, a read-surface is a pair $(\sigma, \zeta)$ where $\sigma$ is a
projection (the summary) and $\zeta$ is a total \emph{zoom} function mapping every
summarized claim to a verifiable artifact; a surface with a partial or absent
$\zeta$ is a Potemkin digest.
\end{definition}

\input{figures/legible-swarm-zoom-vs-potemkin}

ADR-0048 states this principle (``every summary is a lens onto the real
artifact\ldots legibility-with-zoom''); this paper supplies the \emph{why} (Scott +
human factors, \S\ref{sec:operator}) and the \emph{how} (the rules below). The same
discipline is already named across the project's canon: ADR-0047's ``summaries as
indexes, not replacements'' and ADR-0045's ``honest green.'' Digest-with-zoom is the
unifying L2 statement of all three.

\subsection{What to flatten, what to preserve}\label{sec:flatten}

Scott's own distinction is the design boundary. The state may safely standardize
\emph{administrative facts it itself defines}, and must \emph{never} standardize away
the \emph{local knowledge it depends on}.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \textbf{Flatten (safe --- the operator's own structured field):} IDs,
\textbf{claim} rows, session phases, port numbers, \textbf{commitment} status
(\texttt{lib/commitments.ts} --- \emph{violable obligations bound to an actor,
caught by a breach monitor; ADR-0041}), enum states. Administrative legibility is
cheap and lossless here.
\item \textbf{Preserve verbatim, never paraphrase (the agents' m\^etis / the ground
truth):} the diff, the test output, the error message, the reasoning trace, the
exact command run. Paraphrasing these is the over-flattening.
\end{itemize}

The slogan: \emph{summarize the STATE, link to the WORK}.

\pitfall{``Zoom to the artifact'' does not fully preserve m\^etis, and a political
theorist will say so (\S\ref{sec:consent-canon}). M\^etis in Scott is not ``the
detailed data underneath the summary'' --- it is practitioner knowledge that
\emph{resists codification entirely}: the operator's gut sense that ``this agent is
about to do something dumb,'' the local knowledge of which corners of the codebase
are cursed. A read-path to the diff is more legibility, not m\^etis. The honest
admission --- which couples Scott to Bainbridge (\S\ref{sec:operator}) --- is that
\emph{some of the most valuable operator knowledge is exactly what the system cannot
make legible, and the more the avatar automates, the faster that m\^etis atrophies}.
The skill-retention tax (\S\ref{sec:abdication}) is the design's only defense, and
it is honest \Vision.}

\subsection{The zoom target must be verifiable, not self-reported}\label{sec:verifiable}

There is a subtle trap inside ``zoom to the real thing.'' If the operator zooms to
the \emph{agent's chain-of-thought} and treats that as ground truth, the map has
merely moved down one level --- because the reasoning trace may be fiction.
\textbf{Turpin et al.\ 2023}, \emph{Language Models Don't Always Say What They Think:
Unfaithful Explanations in Chain-of-Thought Prompting} (NeurIPS)~\cite{turpin2023}
--- \emph{LLM-generated reasoning traces can be systematically unfaithful: plausible,
coherent, and not actually what drove the model's output (a model swayed by
reordering of options will rationalize a post-hoc reason and never mention the
bias).} Therefore:

\begin{property}[Verifiable-zoom]\label{prop:verifiable-zoom}
The canonical zoom target is an artifact the operator can check
\emph{independently of the agent} --- the diff on disk, the test result, the
committed file. The agent's self-narration is a \emph{secondary} lens, always
labeled as such. Two LLMs agreeing (an agent acting and an LLM summarizing it) is
not two checks; it may be one error reported twice.
\end{property}

This is why Port Daddy's source of truth is the SQLite/WAL daemon and the git
working tree, not a model's summary of them. The digest is computed \emph{over}
artifacts the operator owns. It is also why an \emph{adversarial} review organ (a
reviewer with no stake) is structurally necessary, not optional: a swarm that
reviews its own work is two LLMs agreeing, which by Prop.~\ref{prop:verifiable-zoom}
is one error twice. We return to adversarial review as the authority's quality organ
in \S\ref{sec:authority-rules}.

\subsection{The four design questions (a decision procedure)}\label{sec:four-questions}

For any read-surface --- Attention Queue, briefing, resurrection digest, TUI pane ---
ask, in order (Figure~\ref{fig:four-questions}):

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
\item \textbf{Will the operator act irreversibly on this?} If yes
(merge/deploy/delete), the artifact must be one keystroke away and a zoom
\emph{forced}. Never let an irreversible act rest on a digest alone
(\S\ref{sec:operator}).
\item \textbf{What is administrative vs.\ m\^etis?} Flatten the former; link the
latter verbatim (\S\ref{sec:flatten}).
\item \textbf{Who authored the summary, and can it lie?} A deterministic projection
of structured state is trustworthy; an LLM summarizing another LLM is not --- point
its zoom at the artifact (\S\ref{sec:verifiable}).
\item \textbf{Is the authority itself legible here?} Every coordinator action
carries a named reason (Prop.~\ref{prop:legible-sovereign}).
\end{enumerate}

\input{figures/legible-swarm-four-questions}

\exhead{Trace the mechanism.}

\section{The Leviathan rules, it does not just watch (the authority half)}\label{sec:authority-rules}
% ═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The title is \emph{Legibility \& Authority}. A read-only legibility layer is a
glorified dashboard; the whole point of Hobbes is that the sovereign does not just
\emph{see} --- it \emph{rules}. This section specifies the authority half: the
mechanisms by which the consented daemon actually decides, blocks, lands, and
gates. Most of it is honestly \Designed-to-\Vision; we say so per row in
\S\ref{sec:honest-state}. Figure~\ref{fig:authority-organs} maps the organs.

\input{figures/legible-swarm-authority-organs}

\subsection{Roadmap-as-truth: the artifact the authority is accountable to}\label{sec:roadmap}

A coordinator that prevents collisions answers ``what is the swarm doing?'' It does
not answer ``is the swarm doing the \emph{right} thing?'' That question needs a
single legible artifact the authority is measured against: the \textbf{roadmap}
(\texttt{roadmap\_items} + the Implementation Matrices of ADR-0048/0043, owned by
the \emph{Cartographer} --- \emph{the role that maintains the phase-keyed plan as
load-bearing truth}). The authority's legitimacy is measured by \emph{whether the
roadmap stays true}: every phase keys to a roadmap item, and drift between plan and
reality is itself a legible, surfaced anomaly. \BuiltWeak: the roadmap rows and
matrices exist; the authority \emph{being held accountable to it} is \Designed.

\subsection{Adversarial review: the quality organ}\label{sec:review}

By Prop.~\ref{prop:verifiable-zoom}, a swarm reviewing its own work is two LLMs
agreeing --- one error twice. The quality organ must therefore be \emph{adversarial
and conflict-free}: a reviewer with no stake in the work, running a bounded
critique--refine protocol (ADR-0047 Phase~1). This is the L2 organ that bridges to
L3's \emph{neutral evaluators} (the harbor's ``universities and rating agencies,''
Paper~3): the same conflict-free-judge discipline, scoped first to one operator and
then federated. \Designed.

\subsection{Completionist obligation: \texttt{done} as a verifier, not a vibe}\label{sec:completionist}

The operator's standing complaint is the agent that \emph{declares done while
hollow} --- ``fixed the test by deleting it.'' The completionist obligation makes
this structurally impossible to do silently.

\begin{definition}[Completionist \texttt{done}]\label{def:completionist}
A \texttt{done} claim is a \textbf{commitment} (ADR-0041) whose satisfaction
predicate is a \emph{verifier} derived from the obligation's acceptance criteria
(the ADR's ``Done when:'' clause). The authority gates \texttt{done} against an
unmet predicate. An \emph{unverifiable} predicate must \textbf{SKIP-loud} (ADR-0045)
--- surface ``I cannot verify this completion'' --- never silently pass. This is the
authority-half analog of discovery's \emph{refuse-to-route}: ``I don't know'' must
beat a confident wrong answer.
\end{definition}

The verifier is grounded in design-by-contract (\textbf{Meyer 1992}, \emph{Applying
Design by Contract}~\cite{meyer1992} --- \emph{pre/postconditions as
machine-checkable obligations; the completion predicate is a postcondition}). This is
the single highest-leverage net-new mechanism in the wedge, and it directly answers
the project's ``no Potemkin react apps / be transparently hollow'' standing rule. It
is honest \Vision: no ADR yet specifies the \texttt{done}-as-commitment-with-predicate
gate.

\pitfall{Verification has a \emph{cost gradient}, not a binary. A test going green is
cheap to verify; ``the architecture is right'' is expensive, and reading a 600-line
diff to confirm a ``Done when:'' clause is itself attention-seconds that may exceed
the agent's narration cost by an order of magnitude (the \emph{verification
asymmetry}). Def.~\ref{def:completionist}'s SKIP-loud is the honest floor for the
\emph{unverifiable}; \S\ref{sec:operator} supplies the cost model for the
\emph{expensive-but-possible} middle.}

\subsection{Thoughtful landing and the regimentation/enforcement split}\label{sec:landing}

The authority decides \emph{what lands and in what order}, conflict-aware
(\texttt{lib/merge-queue.ts}, \BuiltWeak: present but not wired). And it has teeth:
the footgun-guards. Here we must be precise, because the project's own documents
have repeatedly overstated them (cross-layer resolution C1).

\begin{property}[Regimentation vs.\ enforcement --- the reserved-phrase rule]\label{prop:regiment}
A coordination state is \textbf{physically unreachable} \emph{only} when a SQLite
constraint or a fail-closed boot gate forbids it inline, pre-commit: PK-backed
mutual exclusion (double-claim, PID-squat-as-primary-key), the
prod-DB-in-test guard, \texttt{attest} refuse-to-serve. Credit these to the
\emph{PK constraint / boot gate}. Every Arbiter \emph{subscription} rule
(\texttt{lib/arbiter.ts:328} is \texttt{activityLog.subscribe(...)}, a
\emph{post-commit log subscriber}) is, by Schneider's reference-monitor
theorem~\cite{schneider2000}, \emph{not} an enforcement of a safety property but
\emph{detect-and-compensate}: ``detected within a bounded window, then
halted/compensated.'' The phrase ``physically unreachable'' is reserved for the
regimented set; nowhere else.
\end{property}

\textbf{Fred B.\ Schneider} (\emph{Enforceable Security Policies},
2000~\cite{schneider2000} --- \emph{an execution monitor that observes a system can
enforce exactly the safety properties; a monitor placed downstream of the act it
would forbid can detect a violation but cannot prevent it}) is decisive: a monitor
downstream of commit enforces no safety property, only detection plus compensation
within a bounded window. The Arbiter is post-commit; honesty requires we call it
detect-and-compensate, not regimentation. This wording fix is the single most
important one in the volume and it appears in all four papers.

\subsection{HiTL escalation as a typed, prioritized interrupt}\label{sec:escalation}

The one thing that needs a human is the \texttt{escalate}/\texttt{distress}
performative (ADR-0047) routed to the operator's Distress lane (mayday-red, reserved;
ADR-0046). The mechanism wants the load-bearing signal to win the operator's
attention --- but \emph{when} the daemon stops and asks versus proceeds is a control
problem (\S\ref{sec:operator}), and the \emph{escalation predicate} is not a TODO:
it is a rationalized alarm philosophy (\textbf{ANSI/ISA-18.2}, \emph{Management of
Alarm Systems}~\cite{isa182} --- \emph{the process-control standard for alarm
rationalization: every alarm has a defined priority, set-point, and operator
response, with explicit flood-suppression}). An uncontrolled-false-alarm Distress
lane self-destructs into habituated uselessness within days, exactly as clinical
alarms do (\textbf{Cvach 2012}, \emph{Monitor Alarm Fatigue}~\cite{cvach2012} ---
\emph{85--99\% of clinical alarms are non-actionable; staff learn to ignore channels
that cry wolf}). We make escalation a \emph{costly signal} (\S\ref{sec:discovery}):
an \texttt{escalate} the operator dismisses debits the agent's standing, so crying
wolf has a price. \Designed.

\section{The operator as a modeled entity}\label{sec:operator}
% ═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The seeds modeled the \emph{swarm} as the thing made legible. They barely modeled the
\emph{operator} --- yet L2 is ``for the human operator,'' and the binding constraint
at the top of the stack is the operator's finite attention and fallible trust
calibration. A completionist L2 treats operator attention as a resource with its own
economics, parallel to the token economics of the swarm (\S\ref{sec:tokens}).

\keyidea{In a world where token cost trends toward zero, the human supervisor is the
only cost that stays fixed --- so the entire economic logic of the stack eventually
routes through one objective: minimize \emph{operator-attention per correct
decision}. The operator is the system's reliability bottleneck, a single server
with a non-elastic service rate. The 51st agent does not get a 51st operator.}

The cognitive ceiling is real: \textbf{Miller 1956}, \emph{The Magical Number
Seven}~\cite{miller1956} --- \emph{working memory holds roughly $7\pm2$ chunks},
which makes read-poverty (\S\ref{sec:readpoverty}) a human constraint, not merely an
engineering one --- and \textbf{Wickens}' \emph{Multiple Resource Theory}
(\cite{wickens2002} --- \emph{attention is not a single scalar pool but a vector
over stage $\times$ modality $\times$ code $\times$ visual-channel; you cannot trade
audio-channel attention for visual-channel attention $1{:}1$}). The vectored model
matters: the right objective is not ``minimize total seconds'' but \emph{minimize
peak load on the bottleneck channel}, and the ambient-vs-dense split
(\S\ref{sec:ambient}) is the cross-modal load-balancing mechanism that does it.

\subsection{The attention objective, spined on Signal Detection Theory}\label{sec:sdt}

A naive objective --- ``attention-seconds-per-correct-decision'' --- is
Goodhart-bait: it rewards lowering the decision criterion to wave things through,
because zooming costs seconds and most diffs are fine. The correct frame is
\textbf{Signal Detection Theory} (\textbf{Green \& Swets 1966}~\cite{greenswets1966}
--- \emph{an operator discriminating signal from noise trades sensitivity $d'$
against a decision criterion $\beta$; performance is a joint function of both and of
the asymmetric costs of the two error types}). The operator is trading \emph{misses}
(rubber-stamping a destructive-on-stale-premise merge) against \emph{false alarms}
(zooming on a safe diff), and the cost of a miss is wildly asymmetric to the cost of
a false alarm.

\begin{definition}[The SDT-spined attention objective]\label{def:sdt}
Let an operator decision over an action have sensitivity $d'$ and criterion $\beta$.
With miss probability $P_{\text{miss}}(\beta)$, false-alarm probability
$P_{\text{fa}}(\beta)$, miss cost $C_{\text{miss}}$ (large: irreversible damage),
false-alarm cost $C_{\text{fa}}$ (small: wasted attention-seconds), base rate $\pi$
of genuinely dangerous actions, and per-decision attention cost $a$, the operator
objective is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sdt}
\min_{\beta}\ \Big[\, \pi\,C_{\text{miss}}\,P_{\text{miss}}(\beta)
   \;+\; (1-\pi)\,C_{\text{fa}}\,P_{\text{fa}}(\beta)
   \;+\; a\,\mathbb{E}[\text{zooms}(\beta)] \,\Big].
\end{equation}
The forced-zoom probability $p(r,s,v)$ as a function of agent reputation $r$, action
stakes $s$, and reversibility $v$ is set so the criterion sits where expected cost is
minimized given the cost matrix --- \emph{not} where throughput is maximized.
\end{definition}

\input{figures/legible-swarm-sdt}

The miss-cost term is the fix: without $C_{\text{miss}}$, Eq.~\ref{eq:sdt} collapses
to ``zoom as little as possible,'' the danger-optimizing degenerate the naive
objective rewards. With it, the forced-zoom rate rises automatically as stakes and
irreversibility rise and as agent reputation falls --- which is exactly the
stakes-proportional-friction rule, now derived rather than asserted. (The reputation
term $r$ couples L2 to L3, flagged as a forward dependency: until reputation ships,
$r$ is a flat prior.)

\subsection{Trust calibration is multi-dimensional, and the vigilance decrement is real}\label{sec:calibration}

Showing the operator a beautiful digest does not make them appropriately reliant on
it (\textbf{Lee \& See 2004}, \emph{Trust in Automation}~\cite{leesee2004} ---
\emph{trust has at least three bases (performance, process, purpose) and calibration
depends on \textbf{resolution} (can the operator tell where automation is reliable
from where it is not?) and \textbf{specificity} (is trust differentiated across
functions and time?)}). The implication is sharp: a \emph{single} ``approve-without-
zoom rate'' and a \emph{single} global forced-zoom knob is mis-calibration by
aggregation. An operator who correctly trusts the swarm on refactors and correctly
distrusts it on migrations shows a middling \emph{global} catch-rate, and a global
knob mis-serves both. Trust calibration must be \emph{function-specific and
history-dependent} --- per (action-class, agent), not one scalar.

And the canary-defect catch-rate mechanism --- inject known-bad diffs at a sampled
rate, raise friction when catch-rate drops --- is a \emph{vigilance task}, monitoring
for rare signals, and the vigilance literature predicts it will degrade for reasons
unrelated to swarm danger (\textbf{Warm, Parasuraman \& Matthews 2008}, \emph{Vigilance
Requires Hard Mental Work and Is Stressful}~\cite{warm2008} --- \emph{detection of
rare signals degrades sharply within 20--35 minutes, worse at low signal
probability}; the classic result is \textbf{Mackworth 1948}~\cite{mackworth1948}).
The honest, citable tension: defeating the vigilance decrement \emph{costs
attention-seconds} (canary rate must be high enough to beat the decrement, which
fights Eq.~\ref{eq:sdt}). Catch-rate must therefore be \emph{time-on-task-corrected},
and the right countermeasure is to force breaks, not just rotate zooms.

\pitfall{Campbell's Law eats the governor. The moment ``operator catch-rate''
becomes a metered invariant that raises friction when it drops, the operator is
incentivized to game it --- memorize canary patterns, develop a ``looks-like-a-canary''
heuristic orthogonal to real defect-finding (\textbf{Campbell 1979}~\cite{campbell1979}
--- \emph{the more a quantitative indicator is used for decision-making, the more it
distorts the process it monitors}). The accountability instrument corrupts the
accountability it measures. We do not claim the canary governor cleanly closes the
loop; it is net-positive only if canaries are drawn from a refreshed, costly-to-fake
distribution and the catch-rate is one signal among several, never the sole gate.}

\subsection{Situation awareness needs projection, not just perception}\label{sec:sa}

Digest-with-zoom is overwhelmingly a perceive-and-comprehend instrument. But
\textbf{Endsley 1995} (\emph{Toward a Theory of Situation Awareness}~\cite{endsley1995}
--- \emph{SA has three levels: perception, comprehension, and \textbf{projection} ---
what the system will do next}) puts projection at the top, and \textbf{Endsley \&
Kiris 1995} (\emph{the out-of-the-loop performance problem}~\cite{endsleykiris1995}
--- \emph{operators removed from active engagement lose the dynamic SA needed to take
over when automation fails; \emph{intermediate} automation can yield \emph{better}
SA than full automation because the operator stays cognitively engaged in
projection}) shows the deeper danger: a digest that summarizes only the
\emph{present} is an SA-destroying instrument no matter how good its zoom. The
authority half therefore needs a first-class \emph{projection} mode --- ``what is the
swarm about to do, and what will the state be in ten minutes?'' --- validated by a
freeze-probe (pause the swarm, ask the operator to predict the next three merges,
score accuracy). The forensic time-axis (\S\ref{sec:timeaxis}) is the
\emph{backward}-looking complement; projection is the missing \emph{forward} one.

\subsection{The Bainbridge--Scott convergence and the abdication gradient}\label{sec:abdication}

\textbf{Lisanne Bainbridge 1983} (\emph{Ironies of Automation}~\cite{bainbridge1983}
--- \emph{the more reliable an automated system becomes, the less practiced and
situationally aware its human supervisor becomes, so the human is least equipped to
intervene at exactly the moment intervention is needed}) and Scott are the
\emph{same warning at two scales}: the digest erases the swarm's m\^etis, and the
digest erases the \emph{operator's} m\^etis. As the avatar gains autonomy (ADR-0046
end-to-end roadmap execution), the operator's role slides
$\text{doer}\to\text{approver}\to\text{rubber-stamp}\to\text{absent}$ --- the
\emph{abdication gradient}. The design's only defense is a deliberate
\emph{skill-retention tax}: periodic forced manual action, borrowed from aviation's
manual-flight-hours mandates (the FAA response to automation-dependency accidents).
We flag it honestly: the analog is \emph{contested} --- poorly-designed ``do one
thing manually'' interruptions cause attention residue and degrade the primary task
(\textbf{Leroy 2009}~\cite{leroy2009}). It is \Vision, and it is framed as m\^etis-
preservation, not annoyance-management.

\end{exercises}

\section{Read-poverty: the binding constraint at scale}\label{sec:readpoverty}
% ═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

A single operator can hold two coding agents in their head. They cannot hold two
hundred. Spin up two agents and coordination is free: you read both their notes,
eyeball both their claims, route a question by recognition. Spin up fifty and an
agent that wants to ask ``who owns the OAuth refactor?'' has no move except to dump
the session roster and scroll. It guesses, cold-DMs the wrong agent, and re-derives
from scratch a design decision another agent settled an hour ago and wrote into a
note nobody will ever read again.

The swarm has not run out of information. It has drowned in it. Every agent emits
sessions, claims, notes, diffs, skill tags, and --- eventually --- reputations, far
faster than any single participant can read them.

\begin{definition}[Read-poverty]\label{def:readpoverty}
\textbf{Read-poverty} is a regime in which legible state accumulates faster than it
can be consulted, so participants fall back to $O(n)$ eyeball search over the
population, or, worse, act blind.
\end{definition}

\pullquote{Read-poverty, not write-contention, is the binding constraint on swarm
scale. The write side has known fixes --- claims, locks, merge queues, the Arbiter ---
and Port Daddy has shipped them. The read side has been left implicit, and it is
where the next order of magnitude is won or lost.}

The cure is the move every database made when table scans stopped scaling: build an
\textbf{index}. A \emph{directory} is to agents what an index is to rows --- it pays a
write-time cost (maintaining the structure) to buy read-time speed (answer a query in
$O(\text{query})$ instead of scanning $O(n)$ participants). Read-poverty is also a
human constraint (Miller's $7\pm2$, \S\ref{sec:operator}) and a legibility argument
in Scott's sense: a directory is a state-like act of legibility over the swarm, and a
directory entry over-flattened into a verdict that hides what mattered (``scout owns
auth'' omitting that scout's last three auth PRs were reverted) is the failure.
Every directory entry must therefore be a lens that zooms (Def.~\ref{def:zoom}).

\input{figures/legible-swarm-readpoverty}

\subsection{The value curve across $n$ (the wedge must work at $n{=}1$)}\label{sec:value-curve}

Read-poverty is a \emph{scale} disease, but the wedge ships to a solo developer who
often runs one or two agents. L2 must be valuable at $n{=}1$ --- the briefing, the
attest, the footgun-guard, the honest \texttt{done} all pay off immediately --- and
\emph{gracefully grow} into the read-poverty regime (Figure~\ref{fig:readpoverty},
right panel). Discovery is free at $n{=}2$, the bottleneck at $n{=}50$, the whole
system at $n{=}500$. This value curve \emph{is} the three-layer cure of the next
section: existence (the registry), relevance (ranked routing), trust (reputation +
guilds, deferred to Paper~4). The layers are strictly ordered: relevance over a stale
registry returns confident garbage; reputation over an unranked registry has nothing
to attach a score to.

\section{Discovery: the directory substrate and the split ranker}\label{sec:discovery}
% ═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

This section owns the L1 \emph{directory substrate} (cross-layer resolution C11): the
existence layer a performative must address belongs with the wedge's discovery story,
even though the protocol that addresses it is split across Papers~1 and~2.

\subsection{Existence: the yellow pages (a solved shape)}\label{sec:existence}

The canonical prior art is forty years old. \textbf{FIPA} (\emph{Foundation for
Intelligent Physical Agents}) made a \textbf{Directory Facilitator} (DF) ---
\emph{the mandatory ``yellow pages'' agent: others register the services they offer
and query to find agents that offer a needed service} (FIPA Agent Management
Specification, FIPA00023~\cite{fipa00023}) --- a required component of every conformant
platform, alongside the \textbf{Agent Management System} (AMS, the ``white pages'' for
\emph{identity} lookup). Two FIPA choices matter: the DF is \emph{push-based} (agents
self-advertise), and DFs may \emph{federate} (the seed of cross-operator discovery,
Paper~4). The DF has been re-invented almost beat-for-beat for LLM agents
(\textbf{Ren et al.\ 2025}, \emph{Agent Name Service}~\cite{ren2025} --- \emph{a
DNS-inspired naming + capability-resolution layer with PKI-backed identity}, which is
FIPA's DF with a 2025 threat model).

Port Daddy's existence layer is already on disk, though not built as a ``directory.''
The \texttt{agents}, \texttt{sessions}, and \texttt{session\_files} tables
(\texttt{lib/db.ts} --- \emph{the core SQLite schema the daemon self-initializes})
hold the population, their stated purpose, and their claims; the \textbf{skill index}
(\texttt{lib/shipwright/skill-index.ts} --- \emph{a SQLite-backed vector index that
embeds every \texttt{SKILL.md} with a MiniLM encoder and answers top-$k$ cosine
queries}, \Built) is a yellow pages over capabilities. Existence is not the gap; the
layers above it are.

\subsection{Relevance: the split ranker (the headline result)}\label{sec:relevance}

A flat registry answers ``does an OAuth specialist exist?'' It does not answer ``of
the fifty live agents, who should I ask about \emph{this} OAuth bug, given who
touched these files an hour ago and who wrote the design note?'' That is a
\emph{ranking} problem, and the proposed \textbf{talent phonebook} ---
\textbf{\texttt{pd whois}} (ADR-0030, \texttt{docs/adr/0030-talent-phonebook-coordination-router.md}
--- \emph{a coordination router that ranks live agents/sessions/actors/skills by
relevance to a free-text query and returns scored matches with a one-line rationale
and a pre-filled DM command}, \Designed; \texttt{lib/router.ts} does \emph{not} exist
on disk) --- is its mechanism. ADR-0030 defines \texttt{findExpert(query)} as a
weighted sum of five sub-scores, each normalized to $[0,1]$ then clamped:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:whois}
\mathrm{fit}(c,q) = \mathrm{clamp}\!\Big(
  w_f\,\mathrm{file}(c,q) + w_n\,\mathrm{note}(c,q) + w_i\,\mathrm{ident}(c,q)
  + w_s\,\mathrm{skill}(c,q) + w_e\,\mathrm{epis}(c,q) - w_\ell\,\mathrm{load}(c),
  \,0,1\Big).
\end{equation}
The design blends \emph{push} signals (declared capability: identity/purpose,
skill-index cosine --- gameable, can go stale) with \emph{pull} signals (demonstrated
capability: file claims with recency decay, BM25 over notes, episodic matches ---
derived from what the agent \emph{did}, with no self-report to lie in). Crucially, per
the project's \textbf{no-keyword-NLP} rule, the only substring match is over
\emph{structured file paths} (a namespace the operator controls); every free-text
signal uses BM25, character-bigram TF-IDF, or embeddings.

Now the dossier's headline claim was that the \texttt{whois} ranker and the operator-
facing \emph{Attention Queue} ranker are \emph{the same function}. That claim is
elegant and, on inspection, \emph{false as stated} (cross-layer resolution C11; the
information-design critique). The two readers have incompatible loss functions.

\begin{theorem}[The split ranker]\label{thm:split-ranker}
\texttt{whois} and the Attention Queue \emph{share} a candidate-generation and
recency-decay substrate but apply \emph{distinct scoring heads}, and the heads are
not weight-reparametrizations of one objective. \texttt{whois} (agent$\to$agent)
maximizes expected \emph{fit} --- capability match (Eq.~\ref{eq:whois}). The
Attention Queue (substrate$\to$operator) maximizes expected
\emph{regret-if-ignored}:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regret}
\mathrm{regret}(x) \;=\; \underbrace{\mathrm{stakes}(x)}_{\text{magnitude}}
   \times \underbrace{\mathrm{irreversibility}(x)}_{v^{-1}}
   \times \underbrace{\mathrm{anomaly}(x)}_{\text{surprise}}.
\end{equation}
The operator wants the \emph{most dangerous} thing surfaced, not the \emph{most
capable} agent. No paper may write ``the same ranker''; the honest claim is
shared candidate-generation $+$ decay, distinct heads $\mathrm{fit}(\cdot)$ vs.\
$\mathrm{regret}(\cdot)$.
\end{theorem}

\input{figures/legible-swarm-split-ranker}

\subsection{The quality gates: refuse-to-route and the costly escalation}\label{sec:gates}

Two gates carry most of the design's trustworthiness. \textbf{Refuse-to-route:} if
every candidate falls below a confidence floor (default $0.35$), the router returns
empty-with-explanation, not a confident wrong answer --- ``I don't know'' must beat a
guess, because sending an agent to the wrong door is high-cost and silent. This is the
exact dual of completionist \texttt{done}'s SKIP-loud (Def.~\ref{def:completionist}):
both are the same refuse-to-guess discipline, one on the read side, one on the
authority side. \textbf{The costly escalation:} the Attention Queue is the system's
\emph{most spoofable} surface --- agents that learn the stakes-ranking will manufacture
distress to win the operator's attention. Goodhart applies more sharply here than to
\texttt{whois} (\textbf{Strathern 1997}~\cite{strathern1997}). The fix is signaling
theory: an \texttt{escalate} the operator dismisses \emph{debits the agent's
standing}, so crying wolf is costly (the \textbf{EigenTrust} pre-trusted-set idea ---
\textbf{Kamvar et al.\ 2003}~\cite{kamvar2003} --- applied to the Distress lane). This
arms the abdication-resistance and Goodhart gaps at once.

\subsection{Staleness/decay discipline on every read-surface}\label{sec:staleness}

\textbf{The Codex-owner worked example.} The project's own memory records a recurring
failure: ``Codex-CLI session owners with usage-limit failures are DEAD'' --- stale
claims from credit-starved fleets veto live work. This is precisely a staleness bug,
and it generalizes.

\begin{property}[Universal recency-decay]\label{prop:decay}
Every read-surface projection --- briefing, resurrection handoff, \texttt{whois}
result, attest report --- carries an explicit TTL / recency-decay. A pull signal
contributes $\propto \exp(-\lambda \cdot \text{hoursAgo})$ (ADR-0030: file-claim
half-life 6h, episodic 24h, notes restricted to 48h). \emph{A stale row can never
outrank a live one; a dead-session claim must decay out of ``current,'' not persist
as misleading truth.} This is a free inheritance from L0's existing TTL/pheromone-decay
primitives --- a \emph{use}, not new mechanism --- and it is the production discipline
of health-checked, TTL-expiring service registries (Consul, DNS-SD~\cite{consul}).
\end{property}

The dead-Codex-owner case is the worked instance: had the resurrection handoff and the
claim projection carried Prop.~\ref{prop:decay}, the dead owner's claim would have
aged out of ``current'' rather than blocking a live agent. The seeds applied decay to
pheromones but not uniformly to digests; Prop.~\ref{prop:decay} closes that gap.

\subsection{Ambient legibility: the cross-modal load-balancer}\label{sec:ambient}

Per Wickens' Multiple Resource Theory (\S\ref{sec:operator}), attention is vectored,
not scalar. The dense TUI loads the \emph{visual-focal} channel; an ambient channel
(the FleetBar menu-bar icon turning red on a PASS$\to$FAIL transition, ADR-0045;
opt-in sound; reduced-motion) loads a \emph{different}, always-visible,
pre-attentive channel. This is not a second-class fallback --- it is the cross-modal
load-balancing mechanism, and, per the HCI critique, it may be the \emph{primary}
alarm path, because a separate low-clutter field is where pop-out actually works
(\textbf{Treisman \& Gelade 1980}, Feature Integration Theory~\cite{treisman1980} ---
\emph{a single salient feature pops out pre-attentively in $\sim$200--500\,ms in a
non-cluttered field, but degrades toward serial search as distractor heterogeneity
rises} --- so a busy TUI defeats the pop-out a dense ``mayday-red'' lane depends on).
\BuiltWeak: FleetBar exists; the continuous-watchdog wire is \Designed.

\section{Tokens: the swarm's COGS \emph{and} its legibility engine}\label{sec:tokens}
% ═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

A swarm of coding agents has exactly one consumable that is both metered and
meaningful: \textbf{tokens} --- \emph{the sub-word units an LLM reads and writes,
billed per million} (\textbf{Sennrich et al.\ 2016}, byte-pair
encoding~\cite{sennrich2016}). Tokens occupy a position no other resource does: they
are \emph{simultaneously} the swarm's \textbf{cost-of-goods-sold} (the direct
variable cost the L3 economy must meter) \emph{and} its \textbf{legibility engine}
(the mechanism by which a swarm becomes seeable). The bridge between the two is
\textbf{compaction}.

\keyidea{The same compaction choice controls the bill \emph{and} controls what is
true and visible. When the context window fills you must drop something --- and what
you drop is a \emph{cost} decision (you stop paying to carry it) \emph{and} a
\emph{legibility} decision (whoever reads the residue no longer sees it). The act
that resolves both is compaction, and compaction's output is exactly the digest a
human or successor agent reads. \emph{The digest IS the compaction.}}

\subsection{Tokens as COGS, and the effective-context trap}\label{sec:cogs}

For a fleet the unit of output is ``a landed piece of work'' and the dominant
variable cost is inference tokens (input context $+$ output generation, each priced
per million) --- the textbook shape of \textbf{COGS}. Every L3 price (a rented
fleet's margin, an agent-for-hire's outcome-per-token) needs a COGS line, so context
economics is the \emph{denominator} of the market, not a side metric. There is a cost
trap: \emph{effective} context $\ll$ \emph{advertised} context. Models degrade well
before their advertised window (\textbf{Liu et al.\ 2024}, \emph{Lost in the
Middle}~\cite{liu2024} --- \emph{performance is highest at the beginning and end of
context and degrades for information in the middle, even in long-context models};
\textbf{Anthropic 2025} names the same effect \emph{context rot}~\cite{anthropic2025}),
rooted in the transformer's $n^2$ attention over $n$ tokens (\textbf{Vaswani et al.\
2017}~\cite{vaswani2017}). The consequence is blunt: packing an agent to 200K tokens
does not buy 200K of capability; it buys degradation at full price. Budgeting to
\emph{effective} context is simultaneously a cost optimization and a quality
optimization --- the two point the same way, the defining feature of context
economics. \BuiltWeak: PD's cost-accrual tracking accounts dollars and runs
(\texttt{lib/spawner.ts} run-caps); a \emph{per-agent-task token ledger keyed to
outcomes} (``\texttt{feat:tests} spent 84K in $+$ 12K out to land PR \#123'') is
\Vision and is the COGS primitive L3 needs.

\subsection{Compaction is the legibility engine; the digest IS the compaction}\label{sec:tokens-digest}

\textbf{Compaction} (\textbf{Anthropic 2025}~\cite{anthropic2025} --- \emph{taking a
conversation nearing the window limit, summarizing it, and reinitiating with the
summary}, with the discipline \emph{maximize recall first, then optimize precision})
is the canonical move for keeping a long-horizon agent under budget. Its siblings ---
structured note-taking (external memory) and sub-agent context isolation (the parent
never sees the bloat) --- complete the family. The reframe: \emph{the only artifact
anyone reads to understand the swarm is a compaction of its raw trajectory.} The
operator reads a digest, not six transcripts; the seventh agent reads a briefing, not
the first six's scrollback; a crashed agent's successor reads a handoff, not the dead
agent's context. Each is a summary of dropped tokens produced for a specific reader.
So compaction is the legibility mechanism of the \emph{entire swarm}, and PD has been
doing swarm-compaction for releases without naming the economics
(Figure~\ref{fig:read-surfaces}).

\input{figures/legible-swarm-read-surfaces}

The most counterintuitive instance is \emph{forgetting as compaction}: pheromone
decay (\texttt{lib/pheromone.ts}, \Built) implements \textbf{stigmergy}
(\textbf{Grass\'e 1959}~\cite{grasse1959} --- \emph{coordination via persistent
traces left in a shared environment, as ants deposit evaporating trails}) by
multiplying stored signal by a decay rate each interval. Decay is deliberate,
continuous, $O(1)$ compaction of the shared context: the map stays readable because
the stale ink fades. It is legibility by subtraction, and it is the same primitive
Prop.~\ref{prop:decay} generalizes to every read-surface.

\subsection{The context-degradation cascade (the shared failure surface)}\label{sec:cascade}

Cost and legibility share one failure surface: when compaction goes wrong, the bill
\emph{and} the truth degrade together (Figure~\ref{fig:cascade}). \textbf{(1)
Lost-in-the-middle:} a load-bearing fact buried mid-window is paid-for-and-ignored ---
edge-place facts, shorten the window. \textbf{(2) Context rot:} quality decays as the
window grows even with nothing dropped --- compact earlier (the cost cure and the
quality cure are the same act). \textbf{(3) Recursive-summarization collapse:} each
summary injects LLM noise; summarize the summary enough and the noise compounds into
confident fabrication. The load-bearing cure: \emph{compact from the durable
artifacts each round --- git, notes, the DB --- never from the previous summary.} PD
is unusually well-placed because its artifacts (claims, notes, episodes) are durable
and addressable, so a PD compaction re-derives from source instead of recursing on
prose. \textbf{(4) Over-flattening (the Scott failure):} the digest reads clean but
the real situation is unreachable from it --- detection rule: any digest line with no
deep-link to its artifact; cure: legibility-with-zoom enforced by construction (the
pure-ratatui console of ADR-0046 enforces this \emph{because you cannot over-render in
a terminal}; the medium forbids the flattening).

\input{figures/legible-swarm-cascade}

\subsection{Single-operator: account, don't auction}\label{sec:account}

In the wedge all agents serve one operator, so there is \emph{no incentive problem} ---
only a visibility problem. The right mechanism is \emph{accounting}, not a market: a
per-agent-task token ledger, budget caps, and a loud failure when a cap blows
(ADR-0045). The externality-pricing and Sybil-resistance machinery
(\textbf{Nisan et al.\ 2007}~\cite{nisan2007}; \textbf{Douceur 2002}, the Sybil
attack~\cite{douceur2002}; \textbf{Ostrom 1990}, commons
governance~\cite{ostrom1990}) is a Paper~4 concern --- and it is one more reason the
continuity$\to$person$\to$reputation through-line must come first: you cannot bill,
cap, or reputation-score a swarm of disposable spawns, because Sybils dodge every
per-agent mechanism. Context economics therefore \emph{demands} the identity layer it
appears to precede.

\section{Reconciling the canon: roles, consent, and local knowledge}\label{sec:consent-canon}
% ═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The Leviathan metaphor is load-bearing only if it survives contact with the political-
theory canon. Three reconciliations are required, and a reviewer would refuse the
paper without them.

\subsection{Who is the multitude, who is the sovereign?}\label{sec:roles}

Hobbes' covenant is \emph{among the subjects}; the sovereign is its product, not a
party to it. So the metaphor breaks differently depending on which entity sits where
(Figure~\ref{fig:roles}). We assign roles explicitly, using Hobbes' own
author/actor distinction (\emph{Leviathan} ch.~16~\cite{hobbes1651}):

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \textbf{The agents are the multitude.} They covenant --- via the rule of the
road (L1) --- to be bound, each to all, enforced by the daemon. This is the literal
Hobbesian covenant-among-subjects.
\item \textbf{The daemon is the sovereign-as-actor.} It holds the authority the
multitude instituted; it blocks, lands, escalates. But, per the legible-sovereign
amendment (Prop.~\ref{prop:legible-sovereign}), it is the \emph{most} legible actor,
not (as in Hobbes) the least.
\item \textbf{The operator is the author.} In Hobbes' author/actor split, the
\emph{author} owns the authority that the \emph{actor} exercises. The operator is not
a peer subject and not the sovereign-in-action; the operator is the source of
authority, exercising it through the daemon and able to withdraw it
(Prop.~\ref{prop:override}). This is the slot Hobbes leaves for the one who authorizes
but does not personally act.
\end{itemize}

This assignment is what makes the consent grant (Def.~\ref{def:consent}) coherent:
the author issues a scoped grant to the actor, over a multitude bound by covenant.

\input{figures/legible-swarm-roles}

\subsection{Express consent, not tacit; voice \emph{and} exit}\label{sec:express}

\S\ref{sec:consent} already made consent a first-class object precisely to escape
Hume's tacit-consent critique; the canon demands we also engage \textbf{Locke}
(\emph{Second Treatise}, \S\S95--122~\cite{locke1689} --- \emph{express consent binds
where tacit consent does not}) and \textbf{Pateman} (\emph{The Problem of Political
Obligation}, 1979~\cite{pateman1979} --- \emph{liberal consent theory keeps collapsing
into hypothetical consent}). The consent grant is express by construction: a discrete,
scoped, revocable authorization with an audit trail (Def.~\ref{def:consent}). And the
inalienable override (Prop.~\ref{prop:override}) supplies the \emph{exit} that
Hirschman~\cite{hirschman1970} says keeps \emph{voice} honest. The design has rich
voice machinery; the override is the missing exit, and exit is what gives the author
real leverage over the actor.

\subsection{M\^etis is Hayek, and some of it cannot be made legible}\label{sec:hayek}

The honest admission of \S\ref{sec:flatten} --- that a read-path to the diff is more
legibility, not m\^etis --- has a 1945 economics name. \textbf{Hayek}, \emph{The Use
of Knowledge in Society} (1945~\cite{hayek1945} --- \emph{the knowledge that matters
is dispersed, particular to time and place, and not aggregable by any central
planner; this is the fundamental reason central planning fails}) is the other half of
Scott's argument from the opposite pole: the operator-attention economy
(\S\ref{sec:operator}) is at bottom a Hayekian knowledge-allocation problem, not only
a Wickens attention-resource problem. The core L2 claim --- ``the binding constraint
is decentralized, non-spawnable knowledge held in one head'' --- is a Hayek argument,
and Scott's \emph{Two Cheers for Anarchism} (2012~\cite{scott2012}) is the
\emph{constructive} sequel that says what to do: preserve the spaces where m\^etis
lives, do not flatten them for legibility's sake. The skill-retention tax
(\S\ref{sec:abdication}) is the design's gesture at exactly this, and we label it
\Vision\ because preserving the illegible is, by definition, the hardest thing to
engineer.

\subsection{Legibility over time, not just the present}\label{sec:timeaxis}

All three seeds treated legibility as a snapshot --- what is the swarm doing
\emph{now}. But most operator decisions are forensic: \emph{why} did this land? The
time axis is a distinct legibility mode --- the replay scrubber (ADR-0046),
\texttt{git\_sha\_at\_annotation} provenance on every pheromone spray, the append-only
event log that answers ``how did we get here?'' (Scott's cadastral map is static; a
swarm needs a legible \emph{history}.) Together with the projection mode
(\S\ref{sec:sa}), legibility spans all three tenses: the forensic past (replay), the
inspectable present (digest-with-zoom), and the projected future (freeze-probe SA).
The event-log substrate is \Built\ at L0; the replay/provenance surfaces are \Designed.

\subsection{What L2 hands upward (the through-line)}\label{sec:handoff}

The wedge is the single-player product. But the same legibility discipline applied to
\emph{continuity} is the load-bearing wall of everything above it. Here is the volume's
canonical through-line statement, repeated identically in every paper at the L0$\to$L3
seam (cross-layer resolution C7):

\pullquote{The economy rests on three continuity organs --- memory (\textsf{BUILT}),
checkpoint (\textsf{BUILT-WEAK}: notes, not execution state), and the
witnessed-outcome ledger (\textsf{DESIGNED}) --- and reputation keys on the third,
which does not yet exist; the checkpoint organ is the weakest \emph{built} link, and
``resurrection with teeth'' (a real execution-state checkpoint) is the literal
foundation of L3.}

Concretely, L2 provides to L3 (Paper~3, then Paper~4):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \textbf{Legible continuity} --- resurrection-with-memory $+$ episodic memory $+$
briefing make an agent's continuity legible. This is the precondition for the
through-line \emph{memory $+$ checkpoint $\to$ continuity $\to$ a person not a spawn
$\to$ reputation $\to$ a tradeable asset $\to$ the market}. No legible continuity, no
reputation, no tradeable agent. \emph{The score is cheap; the substrate it scores over
--- witnessed outcomes on a non-forgeable identity --- is the gate} (this is the
cleanest cross-layer sentence in the corpus; Papers~1, 3, and 4 all point at it). The
non-forgeable identity is ADR-0040 (identity-local), the keystone for Papers~1--3;
Paper~4 needs the unbuilt cross-operator attestation ADR-0040b, which it must declare,
not assume (cross-layer resolution C2).
\item \textbf{The completionist-\texttt{done} ledger} --- every verified completion
(and every loud-failed one) is an outcome event. L2 is the \emph{outcome witness}; L3
is the \emph{scorer}. The split-ranker substrate (Thm.~\ref{thm:split-ranker}) and the
costly-escalation signal (\S\ref{sec:gates}) are the candidate-generation and
trust-signal scaffolding the L3 reputation head consumes.
\item \textbf{The consent grant as a priceable object} --- a hosted-trust offering at
L3 is the transfer of a consent grant (Def.~\ref{def:consent}) to a third party;
Paper~4 prices it.
\item \textbf{Operator-attention economics} --- the denominator L3 cannot escape. The
SDT-spined objective (\S\ref{sec:operator}) hands L3 the operator-attention cost of
supervising a rented fleet.
\end{itemize}

The Leviathan that makes your swarm legible to you, without lying to you with a pretty
map, is the product. Everything above it --- federation, the market --- is a second
Leviathan made of the first.

\section{Honest-state ledger (full)}\label{app:honest}

Table~\ref{tab:honest-state} (\S\ref{sec:honest-state}) summarizes the wedge's state.
This appendix records the verification basis. The labels follow the volume's single
key (ADR-0045): \Built\ = code exists on disk in this repo today; \BuiltWeak\ = code
exists but is partial, unwired, or honest about a known gap; \Designed\ = an accepted
ADR with a concrete design but no merged implementation; \Vision\ = a net-new
mechanism argued in this paper (or the North Star) with no ADR yet.

Three corrections this paper makes to prior drafts, per the cross-layer stitch report,
are load-bearing and worth restating:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \textbf{The Arbiter is detect-and-compensate, not regimentation} (C1,
Prop.~\ref{prop:regiment}). \texttt{lib/arbiter.ts:328} is
\texttt{activityLog.subscribe(...)}, a post-commit subscriber; by
Schneider~\cite{schneider2000} it enforces no safety property. ``Physically
unreachable'' is reserved for PK-constraint / boot-gate states only.
\item \textbf{The unified ranker is two rankers} (C11, Thm.~\ref{thm:split-ranker}).
\texttt{whois} maximizes \emph{fit}; the Attention Queue maximizes
\emph{regret-if-ignored}. They share candidate generation and decay, not an objective.
\item \textbf{Every read-surface decays} (C12, Prop.~\ref{prop:decay}). Briefing,
resurrection, \texttt{whois}, and attest projections carry TTL/recency-decay; the
dead-Codex-owner case (\S\ref{sec:staleness}) is the worked failure this closes.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Notation and the nomenclature key}\label{app:notation}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{capability} = \emph{ability} (what the jail makes possible);
\textbf{permission} = \emph{normative status} (what the deontic layer allows). Never
collapsed: ``able but forbidden'' (e.g.\ a \texttt{--allow-main-worktree} flag that
exists but must not be used) must stay expressible (C9).
\item \textbf{auth-chain} = the cryptographic Ed25519 hop-bound, attenuation-monotonic
delegation chain (Paper~2); \textbf{delegation-trace} = the coordination object over
which loop-detection runs (Paper~1's L1). Never bare ``delegation chain'' (C4).
\item \textbf{physically unreachable} = reserved for PK-constraint / boot-gate
regimented states only (C1, Prop.~\ref{prop:regiment}).
\item \textbf{ADR-0040} (identity-local) is the keystone for Papers~1--3;
\textbf{ADR-0040b} (cross-operator attestation, unwritten) is the keystone Paper~4
must declare, not assume (C2).
\item Honest labels: \Built\ $\cdot$ \BuiltWeak\ $\cdot$ \Designed\ $\cdot$ \Vision.
\end{itemize}
