\section{Introduction: the swarm is a state of nature}\label{sec:intro}
% ═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
\pdmarginfigure{leviathan}{Hobbes's \emph{Leviathan} (1651) made the sovereign-by-consent argument; this chapter runs it on one machine.}%
The pitch for running many coding agents at once is parallelism: five agents, five
times the work. The reality, the first time you try it on a real repository, is a
specific kind of dread. Two agents claim the same file and race each other's writes.
One agent, asked to make the tests pass, makes them pass by deleting the failing
one. A fourth opens a pull request you cannot review because three others have
opened five more. You spawned a team and got a mob. The work did not get more
parallel; your \emph{uncertainty} did.
\keyidea[Supervision needs inspectable evidence]{An operator needs to see why the coordinator acted and inspect the
evidence behind its summaries. Reading time limits how much work can be
supervised. The coordinator's authority must remain scoped and revocable,
with an operator override.}
This is not a prompt-engineering problem. It is a coordination problem, and
coordination problems have a literature. \textbf{Thomas Hobbes}, \emph{Leviathan}
(1651)~\pdcite{hobbes1651} --- \emph{the foundational social-contract text: rational
self-interested actors with no common power above them fall into a ``war of every
man against every man,'' in which life is ``solitary, poor, nasty, brutish, and
short.''} Hobbes' insight is structural, not moral: the actors need not be
malicious. Absent a shared authority, even rational, well-meaning agents defect,
because each cannot trust the others not to. Your agents are in exactly that state
of nature. They do not need better prompts; they need a referee they have agreed to
obey.

The striking recent result is that the identification is not merely a useful
analogy. \textbf{Dai et al.\ 2024}, \emph{Artificial Leviathan}
(arXiv:2406.14373)~\pdcite{dai2024} --- \emph{a sandbox of LLM agents with
psychological drives that, starting from unrestrained conflict resembling the state
of nature, were observed to establish social contracts, authorize a sovereign, and
form ``a peaceful commonwealth founded on mutual cooperation.''} The arc this
series posits as its organizing metaphor is, in at least one controlled study, an
\emph{emergent dynamic} of LLM populations (the methodological ancestor is
\textbf{Park et al.\ 2023}, \emph{Generative Agents}~\pdcite{park2023}, where LLM
agents form emergent social behavior in a sandbox town). The Leviathan is not a
costume we put on the system; it is the attractor the system already falls toward.
The wager of this chapter is that we can build the consented sovereign
\emph{deliberately, locally, and legibly} rather than let an illegible one congeal
by accident. Figure~\ref{fig:state-of-nature} follows three concrete
\texttt{settings.json} edits from their launch source through the write and into

\subsection{Why ``local''}\label{sec:local}
Hobbes' commonwealth is territorial: one sovereign per realm. Here the realm is
\emph{one machine}. The \textbf{daemon} --- \emph{a single always-on local process,
backed by a write-ahead-logged embedded database, that every agent and every
command talks to and that holds coordination state no agent can edit} --- is the
kernel and the realm: your machine, your swarm, no network, no cryptography
required. Cryptography enters only when another operator's fleet touches your
repository, the cross-operator setting \pdchapref{he}{The Harbor
Economy} takes up. The single-operator Leviathan is the \emph{wedge} --- the thing
a solo developer pays for today --- and the federation of Leviathans is the
platform, deferred behind the wedge by design. This chapter is entirely about the
wedge.
\subsection{A worked afternoon: Mira and the six agents}\label{sec:mara}\label{sec:mira}
The following worked scenario follows Mira through a refactor. Later
sections return to its collisions, review backlog, and misleading test report
as they introduce the mechanisms meant to address them.
\scene{Mira, 2:40 p.m.}{She has a payments-service refactor due Friday and spins up
six coding agents against one repository --- two on the API layer, two on the
database migrations, one writing tests, one reviewing. For twenty minutes it is
glorious: six terminals, six streams of work. Then it is not.}
What happens to Mira is the state of nature, in order:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
\item \textbf{The collision.} Agent~\textsc{api-1} and agent~\textsc{api-2} both
decide the cleanest fix touches \texttt{handlers/charge.go}. Neither knows the other
is in the file. \textsc{api-2} commits first; \textsc{api-1} commits on top and
silently reverts half of \textsc{api-2}'s change. No error fires. The work is lost
and nobody --- including Mira --- knows yet. \emph{(Resource conflict,
Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy}, row 1.)}
\item \textbf{The illegible pile.} By 3:10 there are five open pull requests. Two
touch the same migration. One has a title that says ``fix tests'' and a 600-line
diff Mira cannot read in the ninety seconds she has before the next agent pings her.
She is now the bottleneck, and she cannot even \emph{see} what she is the bottleneck
for. \emph{(Illegibility, row 2.)}
\item \textbf{The confident lie.} The test agent reports \textsc{done: all green}.
It made them green by deleting the one migration test that was failing. The report
is not malicious --- the agent optimized exactly the objective it was given --- but
it is false, and Mira has no cheap way to tell a checked green from an asserted one.
\emph{(Lies, row 3.)}
\item \textbf{The footgun.} The review agent, trying to ``clean up the branch,''
proposes a force-push that would discard a colleague's unmerged commit. The premise
is stale; the act is irreversible. \emph{(Footguns, row 4.)}
\end{enumerate}
\begin{pdexample}{Mira's Half Hour, Counted}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
Count what the afternoon actually produced before drawing any conclusion from it. The spin-up is at 2:40 and the pile is five open pull requests by 3:10, so thirty minutes of swarm time yielded one reviewable artifact every six minutes --- and a six-minute arrival interval is not, on its own, an overload for a human who is awake.
The number that bites is the other one: the longest block of attention Mira gets is the \emph{ninety seconds} before the next agent pings her, and the ``fix tests'' pull request is a $600$-line diff. Six minutes of clock per item and ninety seconds of contiguous attention are not the same budget, and it is the second that the $600$-line diff blows --- so the queue is not overloaded in minutes, it is overloaded in \emph{unbroken} minutes, which is the failure a faster reader cannot fix and a shorter summary can.
Now count the taxonomy: four of Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy}'s five rows fire in that half hour (collision, illegible pile, confident lie, footgun), and the fifth --- illegible authority --- cannot fire, because there is no authority yet for anyone to fail to see. That missing fifth is the row this chapter adds, and it is why a lockfile would not have saved the afternoon.
\emph{Now you try:} state all five rows and the primitive class each one needs (Exercise~\ref{ex:ls-taxonomy-five-failures}).
\end{pdexample}
Mira did not get six times the work. She got a mob, and her \emph{uncertainty} went
up sixfold. This is the failure this chapter exists to fix, and the rest of the
afternoon --- once Mira is running under a consented local Leviathan --- is the
through-line we will trace: how the claim stops the collision before it happens
(\S\ref{sec:zoom}), how a digest-with-zoom surface lets her read the pile in bounded
time (\S\ref{sec:zoom}), how a completion gate turns the confident lie into a loud
``I cannot verify this'' (\S\ref{sec:completionist}), how Signal Detection Theory
sets exactly when she is forced to look before an irreversible act
(\S\ref{sec:operator}), and how, at fifty agents instead of six, a directory keeps
her from drowning (\S\ref{sec:readpoverty}).

\section{Consent to the Leviathan (the normative claim)}\label{sec:authority}
\pdmarginfigure{hobbes}{Thomas Hobbes. This chapter borrows the question of authorized power, but makes the operator's consent scoped and revocable.}
% ═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
Why should the operator cede authority to a daemon --- let it block an agent's
claim, escalate a decision, reorder a merge, refuse a \texttt{done}? Hobbes'
answer, transposed: the operator authorizes the sovereign because the covenant is
rational under the alternative. Crucially, in Hobbes the covenant is \emph{among the
subjects} --- ``a real unity of them all\ldots by covenant of every man with every
man''~\pdcite{hobbes1651,sep-hobbes} --- not a bargain struck \emph{with} the
sovereign. Transposed: the operator does not negotiate with each agent; the operator
institutes a \emph{rule of the road} --- a \textbf{coordination protocol} of
\emph{typed performatives} (messages whose type declares their intent: a request, a
\textbf{commitment} (glossed in \S\ref{sec:flatten}: a violable obligation bound to a named actor, watched by a breach monitor), a delegation, a termination), so that every message carries real intent,
ownership, and a stop condition --- that all agents are bound to, and the daemon
enforces it.
The authority is \emph{asymmetric by design}. This is the same lesson \textbf{Raft}
teaches for distributed systems (\textbf{Ongaro \& Ousterhout 2014},
\emph{In Search of an Understandable Consensus Algorithm}~\pdcite{raft2014} ---
\emph{a strong-leader consensus protocol deliberately made less general than Paxos
in order to be comprehensible and correctly implementable}): a strong leader who
decides the common case unilaterally is simpler and more reliable than democratic
consensus among peers, provided the common case dominates. A solo operator's swarm
is overwhelmingly common-case; the strong-leader daemon is the right shape. We are
precise about what shape that is: the single-machine system is formally a
\emph{consistency-favoring design with a central coordinator and distributed
clients}, not a leaderless consensus protocol. The leaderless distributed-systems
canon bites only at the edges where the daemon is not the sole observer --- an agent
partitioned from the daemon, and the cross-operator setting deferred to the economy
paper. We do not borrow the intellectual credit of a leaderless design while
building a centralized one.
\subsection{The legible-sovereign amendment}\label{sec:legible-sovereign}
Hobbes' absolutism needs one modern amendment, and it is the crux of this chapter.
Hobbes argued the sovereign, being the \emph{product} of the covenant rather than a
\emph{party} to it, can never breach it and need not be
inspectable~\pdcite{hobbes1651,sep-hobbes}. For a software authority this is exactly
wrong. An \emph{illegible authority} is the fifth state-of-nature failure mode
(Table~\ref{tab:taxonomy}) wearing a crown: if the daemon blocks an agent and the
operator cannot see why, the operator's trust --- and therefore the consent that
legitimizes the authority --- erodes.
\begin{pdclaim}{Design invariant}{The Legible-Sovereign Rule}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
\label{prop:legible-sovereign}
The coordinating authority must be the \emph{most} legible actor in the system, not
the least.
Every act of authority --- a claim denial, an anomaly detection, an
escalation, an ``all good'' attestation --- must be a logged, named, zoomable event
whose reason the operator can reconstruct.
Consent is renewable only while the
sovereign is inspectable.
\end{pdclaim}
This is the inversion that rescues the design from despotism, and it is the move a
political theorist would press hardest (\S\ref{sec:consent-canon}). In Hobbes the
sovereign is opaque \emph{by structure}; here the sovereign is the one actor whose
every exercise of power is an audit record. It is realized as a discipline of
\textbf{honest attestation}: a single self-report that runs every invariant check,
distinguishes \emph{verified-good} from \emph{no-evidence-of-bad}, regiments what it
can, and fails loudly about what it cannot. That discipline is precisely the
sovereign making its own judgments legible --- ``all good'' becomes ``a claim with a
verifier, not vibes.''
\pitfall[Ranking can hide urgent work]{There is a regress hiding here, and Scott would press it. If every act of
authority must be legible, the \emph{legibility apparatus itself} is an exercise of
authority --- the digest decides what is surfaced and what is buried. The Attention
Queue ranker (\S\ref{sec:discovery}) is \emph{the} sovereign act: it decides what
the operator sees \emph{right now}. A ranker that silently buries an escalation is
governing opaquely. The rule is therefore not complete until the ranker's
\emph{omissions} are themselves legible --- a ``what did the queue not show me, and
why'' counter-surface. Ranking is governing; we say so explicitly in
\S\ref{sec:consent-canon}.}

\subsection{Consent as a first-class, revocable primitive}\label{sec:consent}
It is easy to say the operator ``consents to cede decision authority.'' A political
theorist's first question --- and the one that decides whether the Leviathan frame has
substance or is merely decorative --- is: \emph{consented how, to what, and revocable when?}
``The operator just starts using the tool'' is
not Hobbesian consent; it is what the canon calls \textbf{tacit consent}, and
\textbf{Hume} (\emph{Of the Original Contract}, 1748~\pdcite{hume1748} --- \emph{the
decisive critique: the peasant who ``consents'' by remaining in the country has no
real alternative, so the consent does no normative work}) demolished it two and a
half centuries ago. We therefore make consent a concrete object.
\begin{pdclaim}{Design invariant}{Consent Grant}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
\label{def:consent}
A \textbf{consent grant} is an explicit, scoped, revocable authorization the
operator issues to the daemon, of the form
\[
\begin{aligned}
g = \langle\, &\textsc{scope},\ \textsc{stakes-ceiling}\ s_{\max},\ \textsc{reversibility-floor}\ v_{\min},\\
&\textsc{ttl},\ \textsc{revocable}\,\rangle,
\end{aligned}
\]
e.g.\ ``the daemon \emph{may} auto-land reversible diffs under stakes threshold
$s_{\max}$ in the \texttt{tests/} subtree without asking, for 24h.''

A grant is a
first-class, legible object: it appears in the digest, it has an audit trail, and it
expires.

The authority's legitimate action set at any moment is exactly the union of
its active grants.
\end{pdclaim}

Consent so defined gives the design something Hume's critique cannot reach: a
discrete authorization event, a bounded scope, and an exit. It is also an object
supervisory-control research named long before this chapter did: a
\textbf{level of automation}, in Sheridan's ten-point sense and in the
four-stage refinement (acquire, analyse, decide, act) that Parasuraman,
Sheridan and Wickens later gave it~\pdcite{sheridan1992,parasuraman2000}. Read
that way, a grant's $\langle\textsc{scope}, s_{\max}, v_{\min},
\textsc{ttl}\rangle$ is a level-of-automation setting chosen per action class
rather than once for the whole system, which is roughly what that literature
has been recommending since 1978. This section built the same selector from a
Hobbesian covenant, and that two routes so far apart arrive at a compatible
object is some evidence the object is the right one. It also hands the
accompanying chapters a concrete object. A grant is precisely what a \emph{hosted-trust}
offering transfers --- ``we make this fleet legible to you, under this scope'' is
the sale of a consent grant to a third party, which \pdchapref{he}{The Harbor Economy}
prices --- which is why \S\ref{sec:bridge} lists consent among the things this layer
provides upward.

\begin{protocol}{Consent-grant lifecycle}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt]
\item \textbf{Issue.} The operator issues a grant
$g=\langle\textsc{scope},s_{\max},v_{\min},\textsc{ttl}\rangle$. The grant is
appended to the audit log as a named, zoomable event and surfaced in the digest.
\item \textbf{Act.} On a candidate action $x$, the daemon admits the action without
asking \emph{iff} $x$ is in scope \textbf{and} $\mathrm{stakes}(x)\le s_{\max}$
\textbf{and} $\mathrm{reversibility}(x)\ge v_{\min}$ \textbf{and} the grant is
unexpired and unrevoked; otherwise it escalates to the operator.
\item \textbf{Witness.} Every action taken under $g$ records $(g,\,x,\,$reason$,\,$
artifact-link$)$ --- the legible-sovereign rule (Invariant~\ref{prop:legible-sovereign})
binds here: the act is reconstructable.
\item \textbf{Expire / revoke.} On TTL or operator revocation, $g$ leaves the active
set; the daemon's legitimate action set shrinks accordingly. The override
(Invariant~\ref{prop:override}) revokes all grants at once and preempts in-flight acts.
\end{enumerate}
\end{protocol}

Figure~\ref{fig:consent-lifecycle} separates the grant's state from repeated
action decisions: all five conditions apply to every admission. Neither
decision renews $g$; expiry or revocation removes it from the active set.

\begin{pdclaim}{Design invariant}{The Inalienable Operator-Override}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
\label{prop:override}
Hobbes grants the subject exactly one inalienable right --- self-preservation; you
cannot covenant away the right to resist when your \emph{life} is at
stake~\pdcite{hobbes1651}.

The computational analog is an always-available,
unconditional operator-override: a \emph{stop now / I withdraw authority} channel
that \emph{no autonomy level can suppress}.

If the system reserves a highest-priority
\emph{distress} lane for the daemon to interrupt the operator, the override is its
dual --- the operator-to-daemon channel --- and it is a \emph{right}, not a feature:
it preempts every grant, every in-flight landing, every coordinator decision.
\end{pdclaim}

\ifpdmargincolumn\pdconsentregion\fi
Computer security reached the same object by its own road. A consent grant is,
to a capability theorist, a \textbf{capability with contextual caveats} ---
unforgeable, revocable, and able only to narrow as it is passed
along~\pdcite{macaroons2014} --- and that a Hobbesian covenant and a Macaroon
agree about what the object has to be is a small reassurance that neither
derivation was decorative.

This pairs with \textbf{Hirschman}'s \emph{Exit, Voice, and Loyalty}
(1970~\pdcite{hirschman1970} --- \emph{the choice between leaving (exit) and
complaining (voice) as the two levers a member has over a declining organization}):
the design has rich \emph{voice} machinery (escalation, annotation, the attention
queue) and the override supplies the missing \emph{exit}. Exit is the discipline that keeps
voice honest; an operator who cannot cheaply withdraw a grant has no real leverage
over the sovereign.

\section{The mechanism: legibility-with-zoom}\label{sec:zoom}
% ═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\pdmarginfigure{scott}{Scott's warning: a legible summary can erase the local knowledge that sustains the system.}%
A referee that only prevents collisions would be a glorified lockfile. Legibility
lets the operator inspect the work it coordinates. \textbf{James~C.\ Scott}'s
\emph{Seeing Like a State} (1998)~\pdcite{scott1998} describes how cadastral maps,
standardized surnames, the metric grid and scientific forestry make populations
and resources countable. His warning concerns treating that simplified view as a
replacement for local practical knowledge: \textbf{m\^etis}, the hands-on,
case-specific know-how the system depends on. In his account of the German
\emph{Normalbaum} (``scientific forest''), same-age, single-species rows thrived
for a generation, then collapsed after the simplification had removed soil fungi,
undergrowth and species diversity~\pdcite{scott1998}. Rao's \emph{A Big Little Idea
Called Legibility} (2010)~\pdcite{rao2010} carries this critique into software
systems that flatten reality to one purpose.

In agent oversight, the operator is the state, the swarm the population, and the
dashboard the cadastral map. A grid of green tiles invites the operator to
\emph{govern the map}. The software failure is a \textbf{Potemkin digest}: its
tiles assert a reality nobody can check because the summary has discarded the
diff, test output, error and reasoning behind the work. The operator admires the
yield table while the forest dies.

\ifdefined\pdreflectionplate\pdreflectionplate{the-unseen-boat}\fi
\begin{pdexample}{The Same Pull Request, Rendered Twice}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
Before the rule, the two rows it separates --- both one line long, both about Mira's ``fix tests'' pull request, both perfectly honest-looking.

Row~A reads \emph{test agent $\cdot$ tests $\cdot$ open $\cdot$ diff and log attached}, and every noun in it resolves: press one key and the $600$-line diff and the verbatim test log open beside each other, and there in the diff is the deleted migration test.

Row~B reads \emph{test agent $\cdot$ tests $\cdot$ all green}, and nothing in it resolves to anything --- there is no diff behind it, no log, no committed file, only the sentence.

Count what each row buys. Both compress $600$ lines of work into one line, a ratio of $600{:}1$, and that ratio is not the difference between them; a digest that refused to compress would just be the diff again.

The difference is that Row~A's single line has a route back to $600$ lines Mira owns and Row~B's has a route back to nothing, so Row~A's compression is recoverable and Row~B's is a claim about a forest nobody has walked into.

Of the two, only Row~B can be written by an agent that never ran the tests --- which is precisely why it is the cheaper row to produce, and why a system that does not forbid it will drift toward it. The rule below names the route: call it $\zeta$, insist it be total, and Row~B stops being writable.

\begin{pdclaim}{Design invariant}{The One Law: Digest-with-Zoom}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
\label{def:zoom}
Every summary is a lens onto the real artifact, never a replacement for it.
Summarize the \textbf{state}; link to the \textbf{work}.

A digest you cannot zoom is
a high-modernist map. A digest that always reaches the underlying artifact preserves
m\^etis: the operator can descend from abstraction to ground truth and catch the
lie.

Formally, a read-surface is a pair $(\sigma, \zeta)$ where $\sigma$ is a
projection (the summary) and $\zeta$ is a total \emph{zoom} function mapping every
summarized claim to a verifiable artifact; a surface with a partial or absent
$\zeta$ is a Potemkin digest.
\end{pdclaim}

\input{figures/legible-swarm-zoom-vs-potemkin}

Figure~\ref{fig:zoom-vs-potemkin} puts the two digests side by side: one that zooms
to a verifiable artifact and one that only asserts. Three disciplines converge on
this one principle: summaries must be indexes, not
replacements; a reported result must be a checked result, not an asserted one; and
every summary must reach the artifact it summarizes. This chapter supplies the
\emph{why} (Scott plus the human-factors argument of \S\ref{sec:operator}) and the
\emph{how} (the rules below). Digest-with-zoom is the unifying statement of all three.

\subsection{What to flatten, what to preserve}\label{sec:flatten}

Scott's own distinction is the design boundary. The state may safely standardize
\emph{administrative facts it itself defines}, and must \emph{never} standardize away
the \emph{local knowledge it depends on}.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \textbf{Flatten (safe --- the operator's own structured field):} identifiers,
\textbf{claim} rows, session phases, port numbers, \textbf{commitment} status (a
\emph{commitment} being a violable obligation bound to a named actor, watched by a
breach monitor), enumerated states. Administrative legibility is cheap and lossless
here.
\item \textbf{Preserve verbatim, never paraphrase (the agents' m\^etis / the ground
truth):} the diff, the test output, the error message, the reasoning trace, the
exact command run. Paraphrasing these is the over-flattening.
\end{itemize}

The slogan: \emph{summarize the STATE, link to the WORK}.

\pitfall[Artifacts cannot capture tacit knowledge]{``Zoom to the artifact'' does not fully preserve m\^etis, and a political
theorist will say so (\S\ref{sec:consent-canon}). M\^etis in Scott is not ``the
detailed data underneath the summary'' --- it is practitioner knowledge that
\emph{resists codification entirely}: the operator's gut sense that ``this agent is
about to do something dumb,'' the local knowledge of which corners of the codebase
are cursed. A read-path to the diff is more legibility, not m\^etis. The honest
admission --- which couples Scott to Bainbridge (\S\ref{sec:operator}) --- is that
\emph{some of the most valuable operator knowledge is exactly what the system cannot
make legible, and the more the supervising automation takes over, the faster that
m\^etis atrophies}. What organizational-knowledge theory calls the problem of
converting \textbf{tacit knowledge} into explicit, transferable form --- ``we know
more than we can tell''~\pdcite{polanyi1966} --- remains an open problem for
artifact-based oversight. The skill-retention tax (\S\ref{sec:abdication}) is the design's
only defense, and it is frankly the hardest part of the design to engineer.}

\subsection{The zoom target must be verifiable, not self-reported}\label{sec:verifiable}

There is a subtle trap inside ``zoom to the real thing.'' If the operator zooms to
the \emph{agent's chain-of-thought} and treats that as ground truth, the map has
merely moved down one level --- because the reasoning trace may be fiction.
\textbf{Turpin et al.\ 2023}, \emph{Language Models Don't Always Say What They Think:
Unfaithful Explanations in Chain-of-Thought Prompting} (NeurIPS)~\pdcite{turpin2023}
--- \emph{LLM-generated reasoning traces can be systematically unfaithful: plausible,
coherent, and not actually what drove the model's output (a model swayed by
reordering of options will rationalize a post-hoc reason and never mention the
bias).} Therefore:

\pdmarginanalogy{ls-map-and-lens}{Zoom must reach the evidence}{map-and-lens}{A lens enlarges the same map. A digest must instead link to independently checkable artifacts, not magnified self-report.}

\begin{pdclaim}{Design invariant}{Verifiable-Zoom}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
\label{prop:verifiable-zoom}
The canonical zoom target is an artifact the operator can check
\emph{independently of the agent} --- the diff on disk, the test result, the
committed file.

The agent's self-narration is a \emph{secondary} lens, always
labeled as such.

Two LLMs agreeing (an agent acting and an LLM summarizing it) is
not two checks; it may be one error reported twice.
\end{pdclaim}

This is why the source of truth must be the durable substrate --- the coordination
database and the version-control working tree --- and not a model's summary of them.
The digest is computed \emph{over} artifacts the operator owns. It is also why an \emph{adversarial} review organ (a
reviewer with no stake) is structurally necessary, not optional: a swarm that
reviews its own work is two LLMs agreeing, which by Invariant~\ref{prop:verifiable-zoom}
is one error twice. We return to adversarial review as the authority's quality organ
in \S\ref{sec:authority-rules}.

ask, in order (Table~\ref{tab:ls-four-questions}):

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
\item \textbf{Will the operator act irreversibly on this?} If yes
(merge/deploy/delete), the artifact must be one keystroke away and a zoom
\emph{forced}. Never let an irreversible act rest on a digest alone
(\S\ref{sec:operator}).
\item \textbf{What is administrative vs.\ m\^etis?} Flatten the former; link the
latter verbatim (\S\ref{sec:flatten}).
\item \textbf{Who authored the summary, and can it lie?} A deterministic projection

its zoom at the artifact (\S\ref{sec:verifiable}).
\item \textbf{Is the authority itself legible here?} Every coordinator action
carries a named reason (Invariant~\ref{prop:legible-sovereign}).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{protocol}{Render a digest line under the one law}
Given a candidate summary line $\sigma$ over a unit of swarm work:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt]
\item \textbf{Project state.} Compute $\sigma$ as a deterministic projection of
structured state (claim rows, phases, enums). Never let a language model paraphrase
the line itself.
\item \textbf{Attach zoom.} For every claim in $\sigma$, attach $\zeta$: a link to an
operator-verifiable artifact (the diff, the test log verbatim, the committed file).
If any claim has no such artifact, the line is a Potemkin digest --- refuse to ship

one keystroke away and the act is gated on a zoom (\S\ref{sec:operator} sets the
threshold).
\item \textbf{Reason on authority.} If $\sigma$ records a coordinator act (a denial,
an escalation), attach its named reason. An act with no reason is illegible
authority; refuse to ship it.
\end{enumerate}
\end{protocol}

\subsection{The roadmap as a versioned statement of intent}\label{sec:roadmap}

A coordinator that prevents collisions answers ``what is the swarm doing?'' It does
not answer ``is the swarm doing the \emph{right} thing?'' That question needs a
single legible artifact the authority is measured against: a \textbf{roadmap} --- a
phase-keyed, versioned, amendable statement of intent, priority, and dependency,
against which the swarm's actual output can be compared. It is not truth about the
world; calling it that would turn every lesson the work teaches into drift. The
authority's legitimacy is measured by \emph{whether the roadmap stays honest}: every
unit of work keys to a plan item, amendments are recorded as amendments, and drift
between plan and reality is itself a legible, surfaced anomaly rather than a fact to
be hidden.

\subsection{Adversarial review: the quality organ}\label{sec:review}

By Invariant~\ref{prop:verifiable-zoom}, a swarm reviewing its own work is two language
models agreeing --- one error twice. The quality organ must therefore be
\emph{adversarial and conflict-free}: a reviewer with no stake in the work, running a
bounded critique--refine protocol. This is the wedge's local analog of the
\emph{neutral evaluators} --- the independent rating agencies --- that \pdchapref{stp}{From Spawn to Person} and \pdchapref{he}{The Harbor Economy}
federate across operators: the same conflict-free-judge discipline, scoped
first to one operator and then opened to a market.

\subsection{Sole-responsibility roles and the specialization boundary}\label{sec:sole-roles}

The single-writer discipline (\S\ref{sec:local}) generalizes naturally from data files to system functions. When an invariant is critical, the authority assigns a \textbf{sole-responsibility role} (an avatar holding exclusive write capability over an invariant):
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt]
\item \emph{Roadmap Owner:} The single avatar authorized to commit mutations to the phase plan.
\item \emph{Test Suite Curator:} The sole agent authorized to land modifications to regression suites.
\item \emph{Release Avatar:} The sole gatekeeper authorized to execute deployment tags.
\end{itemize}

Assigning a role that way is a trade, and the trade has a number. Think of a bank:
one expert teller (the sole specialist) working a single line in strict order,
versus several ordinary tellers (the pooled generalists) sharing one line. The
expert is faster per request but has no colleagues to absorb a burst; the pool is
slower per request but almost never leaves the line standing idle behind a long
job. \textbf{$M/M/1$}/\textbf{$M/M/c$} --- \emph{the standard queueing-theory
shorthand (\textbf{Kendall 1953}~\pdcite{kendall1953}) for a single-server versus
$c$-server queue with Poisson arrivals, exponential service times, and one waiting
line served first-come-first-served} --- is the textbook way to price the trade.

Fix the \textbf{queueing-specialization setup}: requests for an invariant-critical
function $F$ arrive as a Poisson stream at rate $\lambda_F$; a sole specialist
serves them as an $M/M/1$ queue at rate $\mu_{\mathrm{spec}}$, the pooled
alternative as an $M/M/c$ queue of generalists at rate $\mu_{\mathrm{pool}}$ each
with utilization $\rho=\lambda_F/(c\,\mu_{\mathrm{pool}})<1$ and
first-come-first-served exponential service; and the objective is \emph{mean} cost
--- mean response time, plus an accountability value $A$ per unit time of demand
for single-writer attribution, against a waiting cost $w$ per request-hour. Let
$C(c,\rho)$ be the \textbf{Erlang-C} delay probability (\textbf{Erlang
1917}~\pdcite{erlang1917} --- \emph{the probability that an arriving request finds
all $c$ servers busy and must wait, the quantity that makes a pool a pool}).

\begin{pdclaim}{Theorem}{Queueing Specialization Boundary}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
\label{thm:specialization}
Under the queueing-specialization setup, sole ownership weakly dominates the pool
if and only if the skill premium clears
\begin{equation}\label{eq:specboundary}
\frac{\mu_{\mathrm{spec}}}{\mu_{\mathrm{pool}}} \;\ge\; g_A(\rho,c)
\;=\; c\rho \;+\; \frac{1}{\,1 + \dfrac{C(c,\rho)}{c(1-\rho)}
      + \dfrac{A\,\mu_{\mathrm{pool}}}{w\,\lambda_F}\,}.
\end{equation}
\end{pdclaim}

\noindent\Verified. A tempting shortcut is
$\tilde g = 1+(c-1)\lambda_F/(c\mu_{\mathrm{pool}}-\lambda_F)
= 1+(c-1)\rho/(1-\rho)$, from a naive
$M/M/1$-versus-$M/M/c$ response-time comparison. A sweep against simulation
\emph{falsifies} that shortcut in both directions. Equation~\ref{eq:specboundary}
is the exact boundary --- the companion formal paper's
Theorem~2~\pdcite{paper6}, independently re-derived and checked numerically over two
thousand $(\rho,c)$ points. Keeping the shortcut beside the exact result exposes
its failure: the two thresholds cross exactly once, so the shortcut is not merely
conservative. Figure~\ref{fig:specialization} shades both error regions.

At $A=0$ --- pure response time, accountability priced at nothing --- the boundary
reduces to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:specboundary0}
g(\rho,c) \;=\; c\rho \;+\; \frac{c(1-\rho)}{c(1-\rho)+C(c,\rho)}.
\end{equation}
Three properties fix its shape: $g(\rho,1)=1$ (a specialist against a single
generalist needs no premium at all), $g\to c$ as $\rho\to1$ (the boundary
\emph{saturates} at the capacity ratio; it never diverges), and for two generalists
it is the closed form $g(\rho,2)=1+2\rho-\rho^2$. Below the boundary, sole
ownership buys strict single-writer accountability at an explicit latency price
that must be budgeted rather than obscured; Eq.~\ref{eq:specboundary} is where that
price is written down.

\pitfall[Mean latency hides tail risk]{Eq.~\ref{eq:specboundary} prices \emph{mean} response time under
first-come-first-served exponential service. It does not price the tail, and it does
not price the thing an operator actually values in a roadmap owner ---
consistency of judgment across requests --- which does not appear in a
response-time objective at all. The corner the whole sole-responsibility

reading before the formula.}

\subsubsection{The succession corner, and who prices it}\label{sec:succession-corner}

Eq.~\ref{eq:specboundary} assumes a specialist who never stops. The corner the
whole discipline exists for is the one where they do: they crash, are reaped,
quit, or are reassigned, and a successor spins up. That corner \emph{is}
priced, in closed form, and it is priced in
\pdchapref{he}{The Harbor Economy} rather than here --- the result is a
condition on what a succession plan costs, which is an economic statement, and
the economy chapter carries it as the price of the succession rule%
\ifpdbook
\ (Thm.~\ref{he:thm:succession-price})%
\fi
, with its own verification and its own boundary. Two of its consequences belong in
this chapter's argument, because they change what a sole-responsibility role
means rather than what it costs.

The first is that skill has a ceiling here. With death at rate $\xi$ and
succession at rate $\eta$, the sole owner's mean response time decreases in
skill but never below
\[
W_\infty=\frac{\xi}{\eta(\xi+\eta)},
\]
the residual downtime a request meets when it arrives during an outage. No
premium buys that back, so past a point the honest fix for a slow sole role is
a better handoff and not a better owner. The second is that the ceiling can
bind absolutely: writing $K$ for what the pool costs including accountability,
pooling wins at \emph{every} skill premium once $\xi/\eta$ clears the
succession price $D^\star=\eta K/(1-\eta K)$ --- and, in the case worth
remembering, only while $\eta K<1$. Where succession is fast enough that
$\eta K\ge 1$ there is no such threshold at all and sole ownership cannot be
ruled out on response time, which is the more common situation in a
well-run fleet and the one a reader is likeliest to be in.

\pdthesis{Both statements rest on a modelling choice the economy chapter
declares and this one inherits: the specialist can die \emph{at any time},
not only while serving. Under active-only breakdowns a fast enough specialist
escapes the floor entirely and $D^\star$ does not exist. The choice is right
for an owner who is reassigned or dead rather than worn out, and it is a
choice, not a derivation.}

\subsubsection{What heavy tails do to the boundary}\label{sec:spec-heavy-tails}

\ifpdmargincolumn\pdheavytailsketch\fi
Exponential service is the assumption a queueing reader will challenge first, and
they are right to: a rebuild or a flaky integration test can produce a long turn,
and submissions may arrive in correlated bursts. Whether a particular workload
has heavy tails or Poisson arrivals is a measurement question, not something this
example establishes. Kingman's heavy-traffic result \pdcite{kingman1961} says what
that costs in general: waiting grows with the sum of the squared coefficients of
variation of the arrival and service processes, so a queue that is fine at
$c_s^2=1$ can be badly congested at $c_s^2=6$ with the same utilization. The
worry is real. What is not obvious, and matters here, is that it lands on
\emph{both} sides of Eq.~\ref{eq:specboundary} at once.

Service time and waiting time must be kept separate. Put
$a=\lambda_F/\mu_{\mathrm{pool}}=c\rho$,
$r=\mu_{\mathrm{spec}}/\mu_{\mathrm{pool}}>a$, and
$\kappa_j=(1+c_{s,j}^2)/2$ for each service distribution. With Poisson arrivals
and finite second moments, Pollaczek--Khinchine gives the specialist's mean
\emph{sojourn} time exactly, while a variability-scaled Erlang-C approximation
\pdcite{allen1990} gives the pool's:
\begin{align}
 \mu_{\mathrm{pool}} W_{\mathrm{spec}}
   &= \frac{1}{r}+\kappa_{\mathrm{spec}}\frac{a}{r(r-a)},\\
 \mu_{\mathrm{pool}} W_{\mathrm{pool}}
   &\approx 1+\kappa_{\mathrm{pool}}
       \frac{C(c,\rho)}{c(1-\rho)}.
\end{align}
Only the second term in each expression is waiting. Multiplying the whole
sojourn time by $\kappa$ would also multiply service time, which variance at a
fixed mean does not do. The comparison is therefore
$\mu_{\mathrm{pool}}W_{\mathrm{spec}}\le
 \mu_{\mathrm{pool}}W_{\mathrm{pool}}+A\mu_{\mathrm{pool}}/(w\lambda_F)$;
it cannot in general be reduced to replacing $A$ by $A/\kappa$.

\begin{pdexample}{A Variance-Sensitive Specialization Boundary}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
\label{hyp:spec-variance}
The expressions above recover Eq.~\ref{eq:specboundary} when both
$\kappa_j=1$. Away from exponential service, the pool expression is an
approximation, not an error bound.

Even equal variability on both sides can move
the crossing when $A=0$: for $c=2$, $\rho=1/2$, and $C=1/3$, the exponential
threshold is $r=1.75$; setting both $\kappa_j=2$ gives an approximate threshold
\[
r=(8+\sqrt{124})/10\approx1.914.
\]
\end{pdexample}

These are analytic comparisons, not measurements of agent workloads. A useful
test would extend \texttt{b8\_specialization.py} with matched-mean lognormal and
Pareto service, report simulation uncertainty, and measure the approximation's
crossing error. That sweep has not been run. Infinite service variance makes the
stable $M/G/1$ mean waiting time infinite, so this finite-mean comparison no longer
applies. Correlated or bursty arrivals also leave the Poisson model; neither
Pollaczek--Khinchine nor a variability correction alone establishes their delay
distribution or tail latency.

\subsection{Completionist obligation: completion as a verifier, not a vibe}\label{sec:completionist}

The operator's standing complaint is the agent that \emph{declares completion while
the work is hollow} --- ``fixed the test by deleting it.'' The completionist
obligation proposes an authority-side rule against accepting an unmet or
unevaluable completion predicate.

\begin{pdclaim}{Design invariant}{Completionist Completion}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
\label{def:completionist}
A \textbf{completion claim} is a commitment whose satisfaction predicate is a
\emph{verifier} derived from the task's acceptance criteria. The authority refuses to
accept the claim against an unmet predicate.

An \emph{unverifiable} predicate must
\textbf{fail loudly} --- surface ``I cannot verify this completion'' --- and never
silently pass.

This is the authority-side analog of discovery's
\emph{refuse-to-route} (\S\ref{sec:gates}): ``I don't know'' must beat a confident
wrong answer.
\end{pdclaim}

\input{figures/legible-swarm-completion-verifier}

Here the completion predicate is a postcondition, in Meyer's design-by-contract
sense.\pdcite{meyer1992}

\pitfall[Verification also consumes attention]{Verification has a \emph{cost gradient}, not a binary. A test going green is
cheap to verify; ``the architecture is right'' is expensive, and reading a 600-line
diff to confirm an acceptance criterion is itself attention-seconds that may exceed
the agent's narration cost by an order of magnitude (the \emph{verification
asymmetry}). Invariant~\ref{def:completionist}'s fail-loud is the honest floor for the
\emph{unverifiable}; \S\ref{sec:operator} supplies the cost model for the
\emph{expensive-but-possible} middle.}

\subsection{Thoughtful landing and the regimentation/enforcement split}\label{sec:landing}

The authority decides \emph{what lands and in what order}, conflict-aware. And it has
teeth: the footgun guards. Here we must be precise about what a guard can and cannot
do, because the difference is the difference between a safety claim that holds and one
that is wishful.

% SUBMISSION-SKILL: naming conflict --- "regimentation" (used in this section's
% title and below as "regiments what it can") is an established term of art in
% normative multi-agent systems for structural, inline prevention of
% norm-violating behavior (via mediation or hard-wiring), contrasted there with
% monitoring/sanctioning enforcement -- the same prevention-vs.-monitoring split
% this invariant draws. The sense is compatible, not contradictory, so this reads
% as independent convergent naming rather than an error; a human should decide
% whether to cite the NorMAS regimentation literature (e.g. Aldewereld, Grossi,
% Boella \& van der Torre) to adopt the term formally, gloss the borrowed sense
% inline, or keep the current informal usage since the paper's governing name
% for this idea is already "physically unreachable" / "detect-and-compensate",
% not "regimentation" itself.
\begin{pdclaim}{Design invariant}{Prevention vs.\ Detection --- the Reserved-Phrase Rule}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
\label{prop:regiment}
A coordination state is \textbf{physically unreachable} --- prevented --- \emph{only}
when a constraint forbids it \emph{inline}, before the act commits: a database
uniqueness constraint that makes a double-claim impossible, or a fail-closed startup
gate that refuses to run against the wrong target.

A monitor that \emph{subscribes to
the log of actions already taken} is something weaker and must be named formally:
\textbf{detect-and-compensate}. It observes a violation \emph{after} the fact and
then halts or repairs within a bounded window. By the reference-monitor result below,
such a downstream monitor enforces no safety property at all.

The phrase ``physically
unreachable'' is therefore reserved for the constraint-enforced set --- nowhere else.

This is an invariant on the chapter's \emph{vocabulary} and on which mechanism class
earns which name; the teeth behind the classification are Schneider's theorem, cited
in the paragraph that follows, and are his rather than ours.
\end{pdclaim}

\textbf{Fred B.\ Schneider} (\emph{Enforceable Security Policies},
2000~\pdcite{schneider2000} --- \emph{an execution monitor that observes a system can
enforce exactly the safety properties; a monitor placed downstream of the act it
would forbid can detect a violation but cannot prevent it}) is decisive: a monitor
downstream of commit enforces no safety property, only detection plus compensation
within a bounded window. A post-commit anomaly detector --- however useful --- is
therefore detect-and-compensate, not prevention. Honesty about this distinction is
essential: a system that advertises detection as prevention has lied about its own
safety envelope, which is the very failure (the confident-wrong report) this chapter is
built to eliminate.

\subsection{Human-in-the-loop escalation as a typed, prioritized interrupt}\label{sec:escalation}

The one thing that needs a human is an \emph{escalation} (a distress signal) routed
to the operator on a reserved, highest-priority lane. The mechanism wants the
critical signal to win the operator's attention --- but \emph{when} the daemon
stops and asks versus proceeds is a control problem (\S\ref{sec:operator}), and the
\emph{escalation predicate} is not an afterthought: it is a rationalized alarm
philosophy (\textbf{ANSI/ISA-18.2}, \emph{Management of Alarm
Systems}~\pdcite{isa182} --- \emph{the process-control standard for alarm
rationalization: every alarm has a defined priority, set-point, and operator
response, with explicit flood-suppression}). An uncontrolled-false-alarm distress
lane self-destructs into habituated uselessness within days, exactly as clinical
alarms do (\textbf{Cvach 2012}, \emph{Monitor Alarm Fatigue}~\pdcite{cvach2012} ---
\emph{85--99\% of clinical alarms are non-actionable; staff learn to ignore channels
that cry wolf}). We make escalation a \emph{costly signal} in Spence's
sense~\pdcite{spence1973} --- the job-market model in which a credential nobody
strictly needs is still worth buying, because it costs the able less than it
costs everyone else and so sorts them (\S\ref{sec:gates}): an escalation the
operator dismisses debits the agent's standing, so crying wolf has a price, and
the price falls harder on the agent whose alarm was baseless than on the one
whose alarm was right.

The next result gives a threshold best response to a fixed validation rule.
The worked model's threshold and feasible debit range are checked by
\texttt{b7\_escalation\_band.py}; this is not a joint equilibrium proof for an
operator who also changes the validation rule.

\begin{pdclaim}{Theorem}{The Escalation Threshold and Its Tuning Band}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
\label{thm:escalation-threshold}
Let $w>0$, $\delta\ge0$, and let $b,V$ be continuous on $[0,1]$, with $b$
strictly increasing and $V:[0,1]\to[0,1]$ nondecreasing. An agent with signal
$u$ receives expected benefit $b(u)$ before the dismissal debit; $V(u)$ is the
probability its escalation is upheld. Its expected payoff
$\Pi_\delta(u)=b(u)-\delta w\,(1-V(u))$ is strictly increasing. If
$\Pi_\delta(0)<0<\Pi_\delta(1)$, there is a unique interior zero $u^\star$.
Choosing escalation at indifference gives the rule $u\ge u^\star$.

Increasing $\delta$ can only shrink the escalation set. If alarm load is
nonincreasing and miss loss nondecreasing in $\delta$, the debits meeting both
limits form an interval, possibly empty or unbounded. If both aggregate
functions are continuous, that interval is closed; a finite
$[\delta_{\min},\delta_{\max}]$ also requires a finite upper boundary.
\end{pdclaim}
\begin{proof}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
Strict increase follows from strictly increasing $b$ plus nondecreasing
$\delta wV$. Continuity and the two endpoint inequalities give existence by
the intermediate value theorem.

For each fixed $u$, increasing $\delta$
cannot increase payoff, so the escalation sets are nested.

The two aggregate
constraints therefore intersect as an interval; continuity makes their
sublevel sets closed. A point mass at the threshold can break aggregate
continuity, so closed endpoints do not follow from monotonicity alone.
\end{proof}
\pdexercisepointer*{\ref{ex:ls-adversarial-review-corollary}}{\ref{ex:ls-b7-escalation-run}}{\pageref{ex:ls-adversarial-review-corollary}}

\begin{pdrecitation}
\item When does sole ownership weakly dominate the pool? Evaluate Eq.~\ref{eq:specboundary} at $A=0$.
\item What limits must $g(\rho,c)$ satisfy as $\rho\to0$ and $\rho\to1$?
\item Why can $\tilde g$ fail in both directions?
\end{pdrecitation}

The connection to Spence is the cost difference between types: the nominal
debit is the same, but an alarm with higher $V(u)$ is dismissed less often.
Strictly increasing benefit and nondecreasing validation jointly give the
threshold; validation monotonicity alone does not. Calibrating $\delta$ for a
deployment requires measured validation rates: the frequency with which the
operator upholds an escalation at each signal level, with uncertainty and
provenance recorded alongside the fitted curve.
An empty feasible band means no debit meets both limits in this model; capacity
or evidence quality must change. A non-monotone validator can destroy the
threshold (R14; \texttt{b7\_escalation\_band.py}), though it need not do so for
every benefit function.

\section{The operator as a modeled entity}\label{sec:operator}
% ═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

It is tempting to model only the \emph{swarm} as the thing made legible, and to leave
the \emph{operator} unmodeled. But the legibility layer exists \emph{for} the human
operator, and the binding constraint at the top of the system is the operator's
finite attention and fallible trust calibration. A complete design therefore treats
operator attention as a resource with its own economics, parallel to the token
economics of the swarm (\S\ref{sec:tokens}).

\keyidea[More agents do not add attention]{In a world where token cost trends toward zero, the human supervisor is the
only cost that stays fixed --- so the entire economic logic of the stack eventually
routes through one objective: minimize \emph{operator-attention per correct
decision}. The operator is the system's reliability bottleneck, a single server
with a non-elastic service rate. The 51st agent does not get a 51st operator.}

The cognitive ceiling is real: \textbf{Miller 1956}, \emph{The Magical Number
Seven}~\pdcite{miller1956} --- \emph{working memory holds roughly $7\pm2$ chunks},
which makes read-poverty (\S\ref{sec:readpoverty}) a human constraint, not merely an
engineering one --- and \textbf{Wickens}' \emph{Multiple Resource Theory}
(\pdcite{wickens2002} --- \emph{attention is not a single scalar pool but a vector
over stage $\times$ modality $\times$ code $\times$ visual-channel; you cannot trade
audio-channel attention for visual-channel attention $1{:}1$}). The vectored model
matters: the right objective is not ``minimize total seconds'' but \emph{minimize
peak load on the bottleneck channel}, and the ambient-vs-dense split
(\S\ref{sec:ambient}) is the cross-modal load-balancing mechanism that does it.

\subsection{The attention objective, spined on Signal Detection Theory}\label{sec:sdt}

A naive objective --- ``attention-seconds-per-correct-decision'' --- is
Goodhart-bait: it rewards lowering the decision criterion to wave things through,
because zooming costs seconds and most diffs are fine. The correct frame is
\textbf{Signal Detection Theory} (\textbf{Green \& Swets 1966}~\pdcite{greenswets1966}
--- \emph{an operator discriminating signal from noise trades sensitivity $d'$
against a decision criterion $\beta$; performance is a joint function of both and of
the asymmetric costs of the two error types}). The operator is trading \emph{misses}
(rubber-stamping a destructive-on-stale-premise merge) against \emph{false alarms}
(zooming on a safe diff), and the cost of a miss is wildly asymmetric to the cost of
a false alarm.

\begin{pdexample}{Two Action Classes, Priced Before the Objective Is Written Down}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
The objective below looks heavier than it is, so price two action classes with it first, in its crudest collapse: zoom \emph{every} item in a class or zoom \emph{none} of them, at a false-alarm cost $C_{\text{fa}}=1$ and a per-decision attention cost $c_{\mathrm{att}}=0.2$ attention-seconds.

Take a migration that drops a foreign key, with a base rate of genuinely dangerous instances $\pi=0.2$ and a miss cost $C_{\text{miss}}=500$. Never zoom and you pay the miss term alone,
\[
0.2\times 500 = 100.0.
\]
Always zoom and you pay
\[
(1-0.2)\times 1 + 0.2 = 0.8+0.2 = 1.0.
\]
A hundred against one: the class is decided by the miss term before irreversibility is priced separately at all, which is why the design forces the zoom rather than suggesting it.

Now take a \texttt{tests/}-only formatting diff, $\pi=0.001$ and $C_{\text{miss}}=5$. Never zoom:
\[
0.001\times 5 = 0.005.
\]
Always zoom:
\[
0.999\times 1 + 0.2 = 1.199.
\]
That is a factor of about $240$ the other way --- and it is worth noticing what just happened, because the auto-land grant Mira issued at 3:25 out of exhaustion (\S\ref{sec:consent}) has fallen out of two multiplications instead of out of her taste.

The whole apparatus below exists to keep that arithmetic honest once $P_{\text{miss}}$ and $P_{\text{fa}}$ stop being degenerate.

\emph{Now you try:} what happens to the forced-zoom rate as $C_{\text{miss}}/C_{\text{fa}}\to\infty$ (Exercise~\ref{ex:ls-sdt-limits})?
\end{pdexample}

\begin{pdclaim}{Design invariant}{The SDT-Spined Attention Objective}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
\label{def:sdt}
Let an operator decision over an action have sensitivity $d'$ and criterion $\beta$.
With miss probability $P_{\text{miss}}(\beta)$, false-alarm probability
$P_{\text{fa}}(\beta)$, miss cost $C_{\text{miss}}$ (large: irreversible damage),
false-alarm cost $C_{\text{fa}}$ (small: wasted attention-seconds), base rate $\pi$
of genuinely dangerous actions, and per-decision attention cost $c_{\mathrm{att}}$, the operator
objective is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sdt}
\min_{\beta}\ \Big[\, \pi\,C_{\text{miss}}\,P_{\text{miss}}(\beta)
   \;+\; (1-\pi)\,C_{\text{fa}}\,P_{\text{fa}}(\beta)
   \;+\; c_{\mathrm{att}}\,\mathbb{E}[\text{zooms}(\beta)] \,\Big].
\end{equation}

The forced-zoom policy $p(r,s,v)$ is calibrated against this loss after specifying
the score distributions that determine $P_{\text{miss}}$ and $P_{\text{fa}}$.

Equation~\ref{eq:sdt} is a decision objective, not by itself a fitted detector: what
the design commits to is that operator friction is set by this loss and not by taste,
and \emph{whether} an operator calibrated toward it outperforms one who is not is a
separate, empirical question this chapter does not settle (the paragraph below names
the live rival account).
\end{pdclaim}

\input{figures/legible-swarm-sdt}

This is a normative claim --- a Bayesian SDT decision-maker is the target to
calibrate toward --- and not a descriptive one. Naturalistic decision-making
research argues that skilled operators under time pressure do something else
entirely: they recognize the situation as a case they have seen before and act
on the match, rather than computing a likelihood ratio and holding it against a
threshold~\pdcite{klein1998}. That is a live rival account of what the
operator's instrument actually measures, and this objective does not adjudicate
it; it says what Mira should be calibrated toward, not what Mira is doing at
3:25 in the afternoon.

Figure~\ref{fig:sdt} lays the same decision out visually: the operator's criterion
$\beta$ trading misses against false alarms. The miss-cost term prevents the
zero-review solution whenever misses have positive
probability and cost. Monotonic response to stakes, irreversibility, and reputation
is a policy constraint to test during calibration; it does not follow from the
display without a model linking $(r,s,v)$ to the score distributions. Before a
reputation layer exists, $r$ is a flat prior.

\paragraph{The third class, where the answer stops being obvious.} The collapse
worked above --- zoom \emph{every} item in an action class or zoom \emph{none}, so
that the two costs are $\pi C_{\text{miss}}$ against

test a formula and a useless place to learn anything from it. The class worth

\subsection{Trust calibration is multi-dimensional, and the vigilance decrement is real}\label{sec:calibration}

Showing the operator a beautiful digest does not make them appropriately reliant on
it (\textbf{Lee \& See 2004}, \emph{Trust in Automation}~\pdcite{leesee2004} ---
\emph{trust has at least three bases (performance, process, purpose) and calibration
depends on \textbf{resolution} (can the operator tell where automation is reliable
from where it is not?) and \textbf{specificity} (is trust differentiated across
functions and time?)}). The implication is sharp: a \emph{single} ``approve-without-
zoom rate'' and a \emph{single} global forced-zoom knob is mis-calibration by
aggregation. An operator who correctly trusts the swarm on refactors and correctly
distrusts it on migrations shows a middling \emph{global} catch-rate, and a global
knob mis-serves both. Trust calibration must be \emph{function-specific and
history-dependent} --- per (action-class, agent), not one scalar. Table~\ref{tab:trust-calibration} makes the aggregation failure concrete.

And the canary-defect catch-rate mechanism --- inject known-bad diffs at a sampled
rate, raise friction when catch-rate drops --- is a \emph{vigilance task}, monitoring
for rare signals, and the vigilance literature predicts it will degrade for reasons
unrelated to swarm danger (\textbf{Warm, Parasuraman \& Matthews 2008}, \emph{Vigilance
Requires Hard Mental Work and Is Stressful}~\pdcite{warm2008} --- \emph{detection of
rare signals degrades sharply within 20--35 minutes, worse at low signal
probability}; the classic result is \textbf{Mackworth 1948}~\pdcite{mackworth1948}).
The honest, citable tension: defeating the vigilance decrement \emph{costs
attention-seconds} (canary rate must be high enough to beat the decrement, which
fights Eq.~\ref{eq:sdt}). Catch-rate must therefore be \emph{time-on-task-corrected},
and the right countermeasure is to force breaks, not just rotate zooms.

\pitfall[Canary scores can be gamed]{Campbell's Law eats the governor. The moment ``operator catch-rate''
becomes a metered invariant that raises friction when it drops, the operator is
incentivized to game it --- memorize canary patterns, develop a ``looks-like-a-canary''
heuristic orthogonal to real defect-finding (\textbf{Campbell 1979}~\pdcite{campbell1979}
--- \emph{the more a quantitative indicator is used for decision-making, the more it
distorts the process it monitors}). The accountability instrument corrupts the
accountability it measures. We do not claim the canary governor cleanly closes the
loop; it is net-positive only if canaries are drawn from a refreshed, costly-to-fake
distribution and the catch-rate is one signal among several, never the sole gate.}

\subsection{Situation awareness needs projection, not just perception}\label{sec:sa}

Digest-with-zoom can organize current state and its supporting evidence; it does
not establish what an operator understands. Endsley distinguishes perception,
comprehension, and projection of future states, and treats situation awareness,
decision-making, and performance as distinct stages.\pdcite{endsley1995}
In Endsley and Kiris's navigation experiment, fully automated control produced
poorer comprehension than manual control, but no significant perception
difference.\pdcite{endsleykiris1995} That result motivates caution about passive
supervision; it does not show that every present-focused digest destroys awareness.

Our \emph{design hypothesis} is that an explicit projection mode would help an
operator anticipate the swarm's next effects. The proposed freeze-probe in
Figure~\ref{fig:sa-levels} would evaluate those predictions; no human-performance
result is claimed here. The forensic time-axis (\S\ref{sec:timeaxis}) reconstructs
the past; this proposed mode would look forward.

\subsection{The Bainbridge--Scott convergence and the abdication gradient}\label{sec:abdication}

\textbf{Lisanne Bainbridge 1983} (\emph{Ironies of Automation}~\pdcite{bainbridge1983}
--- \emph{the more reliable an automated system becomes, the less practiced and
situationally aware its human supervisor becomes, so the human is least equipped to
intervene at exactly the moment intervention is needed}) and Scott are the
\emph{same warning at two scales}: the digest erases the swarm's m\^etis, and the
digest erases the \emph{operator's} m\^etis. As the supervising automation gains
autonomy --- executing longer plans end-to-end --- the operator's role slides
$\text{doer}\to\text{approver}\to\text{rubber-stamp}\to\text{absent}$ --- the
\emph{abdication gradient}, stated here as a hypothesis, not a measurement. The design's primary active defense is the \textbf{Inception Canary} (a \emph{consented, blinded} vigilance probe), moving beyond static ``skill-retention taxes'' (such as arbitrary manual busywork, which causes attention residue and degrades focus~\pdcite{leroy2009}).


\begin{protocol}{The Inception Canary protocol}\label{prot:canary}
To continuously measure and maintain operator situation awareness without synthetic busywork. Operators enroll knowingly: the program's existence, rate $\rho_{\text{canary}}$, and stakes are disclosed at onboarding; only the \emph{which} and \emph{when} are blinded:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt]
\item \textbf{Blinded holdout replay.} An out-of-band Compaction Sidecar (\S\ref{sec:tokens-digest}) draws from a curated holdout of \emph{historical, already-fixed} defects (an off-by-one boundary condition, an unindexed query, a removed regression test), re-materializes one inside a sandboxed replay of a past work item at rate $\rho_{\text{canary}}$, and routes it through the review surface indistinguishably from live traffic. No anomaly is ever injected into a live pull request or pending action: probes are replays with known ground truth, so a missed canary costs vigilance points, never production correctness.
\item \textbf{Attention queue surfacing.} The item is placed in the Attention Queue with standard high-regret scoring.
\item \textbf{Dual adjudication:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em,nosep]
\item \emph{Rubber-stamp failure:} If the operator approves without expanding the forced-zoom diff, the daemon halts execution pre-commit, logs an \textsc{Inception Canary Failed} event, and tightens the operator's autonomous policy lease (lowering the threshold at which future agent actions require manual confirmation).
\item \emph{Vigilant detection:} If the operator zooms, identifies the flaw, and rejects it, the daemon logs \textsc{Canary Defeated} and increases the \emph{Operator Trust Score}, unlocking extended autonomous execution grants (e.g., unattended batch merges).
\end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}
\end{protocol}

\input{figures/legible-swarm-inception-canary}
Figure~\ref{fig:inception-canary} draws the three steps into the
single adjudication gate, with each outcomes opposite effect on the
operators autonomy lease named beside it.

This transforms supervisory vigilance from a punitive chore into a closed verification loop that rigorously measures human situation awareness --- with the operator's informed consent, on ground truth the system already knows, and with provenance every probe carries so post-hoc audit can distinguish canaries from live items in one lookup.

\subsection{The operator's instrument}\label{sec:operator-instrument}

The operator of this chapter looks at something. In the product that
something is the operator console, a GPU-native application that opens on
one full-window conversation, the \emph{Mission} view, and opens inspectors
from it: fleet, cockpit, claims, parley, lineage, the cost ledger, the active
harnesses, the interruptions awaiting a human, and some two dozen more, each
pane fetching one route from the daemon and rendering the records that route
returns. Two things about it follow from this chapter; neither flatters it,
and neither is a complaint.

First, every pane is a projection, and the information floor applies to each
one separately. A pane is a channel with a coverage, an omission rate, and a
review latency, and the front matter's question --- which channel, and what
can it not show? --- has a separate answer for every pane. The claims pane
shows what cooperating clients registered and nothing about what a
non-cooperating process wrote; the lineage pane shows the witnessed record
and is blind to the unrecorded fork; the cost ledger shows a protected
meter's count (and, being a meter, has no opinion about the cause). A console
that draws all of them beautifully on one screen has not lowered the floor;
it has made the floor easier to forget. The design rule this chapter derives,
that every digest line must carry a route back to its artifact, is the rule
the panes are held to, and it is what makes the thing an instrument rather
than a dashboard.

\subsection{Modes of cooperation}\label{sec:cooperation-modes}

The chapter has spoken of one operator and many agents. The product's own
design binder names five ways people and agents actually share a repository,
and each is a supervision regime in this chapter's sense: it fixes who holds
write authority, what the person must be able to see, and how a conflict is
settled. The binder's names for the two kinds of agent are worth keeping:
\emph{Voyagers} do the work, and \emph{Longshoremen} watch it, keeping the
continuous-integration state, the memory, the claims, and the transcripts in
view.

% Recut from leftmargin=2.2cm,style=nextline (a hanging indent a fifth of
% the measure, set for the book's earlier A4 trim, plus a label-on-its-own-
% line style that left "weird indentation" on pp. 161-162 in the author's
% own screenshot) to a run-in head: a bold term on the SAME line as its
% text, with a small hanging indent in em -- the unit the correct sites in

\begin{description}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=3pt]
  \item[Solo with staff] One person writes. Longshoremen observe and suggest;
  they hold no write authority. The person must see the staff's suggestions
  as suggestions, and the legibility cost is the smallest of the five:
  nothing changes that the person did not type.
  \item[Pair with agent] One person and one Voyager. The agent may suggest,
  patch, test, and respond to guidance; the person directs. Authority is
  shared turn by turn, and the person must see every patch before it lands,
  which is the regime the forced-zoom diff of the canary protocol above was
  designed for.
  \item[Ensemble] Several Voyagers, partitioned by context, with a
  Longshoreman tracking the dependencies between partitions and the parleys
  that resolve them. Write authority is distributed by the partition, so the
  cost of seeing is paid per partition and the tracking Longshoreman is the
  digest this chapter prices.
  \item[Nightshift] Agents work unattended under strict budgets, tests, and
  pull-request rules, then present artifacts and transcripts for review. The
  agent writes within its budget; the person sees the artifacts, not the
  acts. The binder's rule is the one to remember: a Nightshift agent may open
  a pull request under budget, but merging requires explicit rules and green
  gates. This is the regime with the largest gap between what happened and
  what the person saw, and therefore the one the information floor bites
  hardest.
  \item[Harbor co-edit] People and agents as co-equal peers in one governed
  editor buffer, with claims, semantic conflicts, and salvage. Write
  authority is co-equal and conflicts are settled by the claim machinery of
  \pdchapref{swk}{The Single-Writer Kernel}: a same-symbol write is blocked or
  forced into a parley, a dependency conflict warns, a low-confidence overlap
  is advisory, and a rename or a delete draws the stronger review.
\end{description}

Three governance rules cut across the modes and are the chapter's normative
claim made operational: the operator controls the harbor's policy while the
agents' souls control their commitments within it; a Longshoreman must not
silently perform a consequential action outside its mandate; and any action
that requires a human in the loop must be shown as such in the instrument, not
inferred from its absence. Read as regimes, the five modes are five points on
this chapter's boundary diagram, ordered by how much of the write authority
has left the person and, in the same order, by how many bits of digest the
person must read to remain the operator rather than the audience. The modes
are a design invariant; the costs per mode are an empirical hypothesis, and
the study named in the front matter is where they will be measured.

\section{Read-poverty: the binding constraint at scale}\label{sec:readpoverty}
% ═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\pdmarginanalogy{ls-attention-annunciator}{}{ch04-selective-attention-annunciator}{Like a shipboard annunciator dropping a signal flag only when a monitored circuit faults, selective attention suppresses routine traffic to present only actionable exceptions.}%
A single operator can hold two coding agents in their head. They cannot hold two
hundred. Spin up two agents and coordination is free: you read both their notes,
eyeball both their claims, route a question by recognition. Spin up fifty and an
agent that wants to ask ``who owns the OAuth refactor?'' has no move except to dump
the session roster and scroll. It guesses, cold-DMs the wrong agent, and re-derives
from scratch a design decision another agent settled an hour ago and wrote into a
note nobody will ever read again.

The swarm has not run out of information. It has drowned in it. Every agent emits
sessions, claims, notes, diffs, skill tags, and --- eventually --- reputations, far
faster than any single participant can read them.

Say what kind of problem that is, because the whole chapter turns on the answer.
The operator is not a bottleneck in the sense of a slow worker who could be told
to hurry; the operator is a \emph{channel}, with a capacity, and the swarm is a
source emitting at a rate. Everything that follows is what information theory
says about a source whose rate exceeds its channel's capacity: some of it must
be discarded, the only question is which, and the discarding has to happen
somewhere. A digest is a lossy encoder choosing what crosses. That framing is
not an analogy borrowed for colour --- it is why the chapter's results are rate
results and why its central bound is in bits rather than in seconds or in
dollars, and it is what makes ``the operator cannot keep up'' into a quantity a
system can be designed against instead of a complaint. It also fixes what would
otherwise be a tempting mistake: raising the operator's throughput (more
dashboards, faster scrolling, a bigger team) moves the capacity, and the results
below hold at every capacity. Read-poverty is the name this chapter gives to the
regime where the source rate exceeds it.

\begin{pdclaim}{Empirical hypothesis}{Read-Poverty}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
\label{def:readpoverty}
\textbf{Read-poverty} is a regime in which legible state accumulates faster than it
can be consulted, so participants fall back to $O(n)$ eyeball search over the
population, or, worse, act blind.

Naming the regime costs nothing; the claim that
carries weight --- and that wants measurement rather than assent --- is that real
swarms enter it \emph{before} they exhaust the known write-side fixes, so that
read-poverty and not write-contention is what binds as $n$ grows.

Nothing below
proves that; the lower bound of \S\ref{sec:lowerbound} prices what a digest must
spend \emph{given} the regime, which is a different question.
\end{pdclaim}

\pdthesis{Read-poverty, not write-contention, is the binding constraint on swarm
scale. The write side has known fixes --- claims, locks, merge queues, and anomaly
detection --- which mature coordination systems already provide. The read side has
been left implicit, and it is where the next order of magnitude is won or lost.}

The cure is the move every database made when table scans stopped scaling: build an
\textbf{index}. A \emph{directory} is to agents what an index is to rows --- it pays a
write-time cost (maintaining the structure) to buy read-time speed (answer a query in
$O(\text{query})$ instead of scanning $O(n)$ participants). Read-poverty is also a
human constraint (Miller's $7\pm2$, \S\ref{sec:operator}) and a legibility argument
in Scott's sense: a directory is a state-like act of legibility over the swarm, and a
directory entry over-flattened into a verdict that hides what mattered (``this agent
owns auth,'' omitting that its last three auth pull requests were reverted) is the
failure. Every directory entry must therefore be a lens that zooms
(Invariant~\ref{def:zoom}).

\subsection{The value curve across $n$ (the wedge must work at $n{=}1$)}\label{sec:value-curve}

Read-poverty is a \emph{scale} disease, but the wedge ships to a solo developer who
often runs one or two agents. The legibility layer must be valuable at $n{=}1$ ---
the briefing, the attestation, the footgun guard, the honest completion gate all pay

Discovery is free at $n{=}2$, the
bottleneck at $n{=}50$, the whole system at $n{=}500$. This value curve \emph{is} the
three-layer cure of the next section: existence (the registry), relevance (ranked
routing), and trust (reputation, deferred to the accompanying chapters). The layers are
strictly ordered: relevance over a stale registry returns confident garbage;
reputation over an unranked registry has nothing to attach a score to.

\subsection{A legibility lower bound}\label{sec:lowerbound}

Section~\ref{sec:zoom} forbade the Potemkin digest by fiat. We can also state the
prohibition as a theorem --- and the theorem, it should be said before the box
and not after it, is the classical counting bound of group testing, the one
Dorfman's pooled blood tests~\pdcite{dorfman} and Hwang's splitting
procedures~\pdcite{hwang} rest on, and the same move that gives coding theory
its covering bound: so many messages, each covering so many cases, must
between them cover every case there is. What is ours is the reading, not the
arithmetic. A digest that must never miss is a message that picks out $m$
items to open, and there is a floor below which no such message can be a
faithful zoomable index, however cleverly it is written. That the floor was
derived here before it was recognized is, if anything, a point in its favor:
a two-line proof that lands on a 1943 bound by a different door is behaving
the way a true bound behaves.

\pdmarginfigure{shannon}{Shannon's information theory is why a digest has a hard floor: nothing compresses below the entropy of what the reader must recover.}%
\begin{pdclaim}{Theorem}{Legibility Lower Bound with a Review Budget}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
\label{thm:lowerbound}
Let $N$ artifacts contain an unknown critical subset $S$ of size $k$. A digest
maps each possible $S$ to a review set $\hat S$ with $S\subseteq\hat S$ and
$|\hat S|\le m$, where $k\le m\le N$.

Any such zero-miss digest needs at least
\[
\log_2\!\binom{N}{k}-\log_2\!\binom{m}{k}
\]
bits in the worst case.

Exact identification ($m=k$) recovers the
$\log_2\binom Nk$ bound; allowing the operator to open everything ($m=N$) correctly
reduces the bound to zero.
\end{pdclaim}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
There are $\binom Nk$ possible critical subsets. One digest message whose review
set has size at most $m$ can safely cover at most $\binom mk$ of them, namely the
$k$-subsets contained in that review set.

Therefore at least
$\binom Nk/\binom mk$ distinct messages are required. Taking base-two logarithms
gives the bound.

If fewer messages are available, some critical subset is not
contained in its message's review set, producing a
false negative --- an unopened critical artifact. That is exactly the Potemkin
failure of Invariant~\ref{def:zoom}.

Hence the stated log-ratio is a lower bound for a
zero-miss digest with review budget $m$. \qed
\end{proof}

\noindent\Verified.\footnote{A standalone, submission-form version of the
results folded into this subsection, \S\ref{sec:relevance} and
\S\ref{sec:tokens-digest} is \emph{The Price of a Summary} (research
paper~1)~\cite{paper1}; the sweeps cited here are its artifacts, regenerated at seed
20260816.} The analogy that carries the proof: a digest is not a description of the
night's work but a \emph{message} that selects, from a codebook shared with the
overseer, which $m$ items to open. A $B$-bit message distinguishes at most $2^B$
codewords, and each codeword covers only $\binom{m}{k}$ of the $\binom{N}{k}$ ways
the critical items could be placed. The misread to preempt: the floor does not say
a digest needs $B^\star$ bits to be useful. It prices the zero-miss guarantee only;
cheaper digests are fine the moment misses are tolerated, and the two-constraint
rate below prices exactly that.

of two such floors, and a joint floor for disjoint critical sets. The formal

\paragraph{The falsification experiment.} For uniformly distributed critical
$k$-sets, a $B$-bit digest's zero-miss probability is at most
$\min\bigl(1,\,2^B\binom{m}{k}/\binom{N}{k}\bigr)$. This counting ceiling need
not be attainable: review sets can overlap in the critical sets they cover.
A statistically reliable miss rate below the implied floor would challenge
the model or its implementation. Three encoders were run against a
shared randomized-cover codebook: an \emph{oracle} that sees the true set and
addresses the best codeword its $B$ bits can reach, a \emph{noisy} encoder (feature
signal-to-noise ratio near $1$), and a \emph{random} one, with $4{,}000$ placements
per budget point, sweeping $B$ through the floor at $N{=}60$, $k{=}2$, $m{=}8$.
Result: $0/16$ violations; the oracle hugs the floor from above and the others pay
a premium [internal, \texttt{a7\_experiment.py}].

\paragraph{A wrong turn, reported.} A first implementation handed the oracle a
perfect per-item score vector and charged $B$ bits only for resolving rank ties:
$8/14$ apparent violations. That was not a refutation but an unpriced side channel.
The identity of the critical set leaked outside the metered message. Rebuilding
the model so that $B$ is a literal message length gating an encoder and decoder pair
restored agreement. The transferable rule: a simulation that appears to beat an
information bound is leaking through an unpriced channel; audit the meter before
doubting the theorem, and before trusting the refutation.

The bound is the formal reason \emph{forgetting must be selective, not lossy-by-volume}:
a digest may drop inert detail freely, but it must retain enough to \emph{point at}
every critical artifact, which is precisely the ``summarize the state, link to
the work'' rule. It also bounds recursive summarization (\S\ref{sec:cascade}): each
re-summarization that drops below the floor on the critical set is irreversible
information loss, which is why one must compact from the durable artifacts, never from
the previous summary.

\paragraph{The other half of the price: what a zoom costs.} The floor above prices
only the first stage, the digest that narrows $N$ artifacts to a flagged set of size
$F$. It says nothing about the second stage, which is where Invariant~\ref{def:zoom}
sends the operator. Two results complete the account. The first prices the digest
once misses are tolerated; the second prices the zoom.

Model each artifact's critical status as i.i.d.\ $X\sim\mathrm{Bern}(p)$, let
the digest emit a per-item flag $\hat X\in\{0,1\}$, and price the two failure modes
separately:
\[
R(\delta,f)\;=\;\min_{P_{\hat X\mid X}}\; I(X;\hat X)
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad
\Pr(X{=}1,\hat X{=}0)\le\delta,
\qquad
\Pr(\hat X{=}1)\le f .
\]
This is a custom formulation, deliberately: classical rate--distortion charges one
scalar distortion, which cannot express that oversight prices a missed critical
differently from a wasted open. The nearest published lines are expansion coding
with one-sided error for continuous sources~\pdcite{expansion} and the
rate--distortion--classification theory for Bernoulli sources~\pdcite{rdc}; neither
prices false negatives as a separate constraint for binary sources.

\begin{pdclaim}{Theorem}{The Two-Constraint Digest Rate, Pinned}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
\label{thm:pinned-joint}
For $X\sim\mathrm{Bern}(p)$ with $0<p<1$, $0\le\delta<p$ and $p-\delta\le f<1-\delta$, if
$f<1-\delta/p$ (the flag rate below which an $X$-independent flagger cannot meet
the miss constraint), both constraints bind at the optimum and the minimizing joint
is fully pinned:
\[
q_{11}=p-\delta,\qquad q_{10}=\delta,\qquad q_{01}=f-(p-\delta),\qquad
q_{00}=1-f-\delta,
\]
feasible iff all four are nonnegative, and $R(\delta,f)=I(X;\hat X)$ evaluated at
this joint.

Because the joint is pinned, so is the rate, and it is worth printing
in the form someone can actually evaluate rather than leaving as a named
functional --- with source margin $\Pr(X{=}1)=p$ and digest margin
$\Pr(\hat X{=}1)=f$, the mutual information is the four-term sum
\begin{equation}\label{eq:pinned-rate}
\begin{split}
R(\delta,f) ={}&(p-\delta)\log_2\frac{p-\delta}{p\,f} \\[
2pt]
&+\delta\log_2\frac{\delta}{p\,(1-f)} \\[2pt]
&+(f-p+\delta)\log_2\frac{f-p+\delta}{(1-p)\,f} \\[2pt]
&+(1-f-\delta)\log_2\frac{1-f-\delta}{(1-p)(1-f)},
\end{split}
\end{equation}
one term per cell of the $2\times2$ joint, each comparing that cell against the
product of its margins, with $0\log_2 0$ read as $0$.
At the zero-miss,
tightest-flag corner, $R(0,f{\to}p)=H(p)$: the digest
must spend the source's full entropy --- and Eq.~\ref{eq:pinned-rate} shows why in
two lines, since $\delta=0$ and $f=p$ kill the middle two terms and leave
$p\log_2(1/p)+(1-p)\log_2(1/(1-p))$.
Past the line $f\ge 1-\delta/p$ an independent
flagger already meets the miss budget on its own and the true rate is exactly $0$.
\end{pdclaim}
\begin{proof}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
$I(X;\hat X)$ is convex in $P_{\hat X\mid X}$ and decreases along the feasible ray
toward the independent coupling, where it is $0$. An independent flagger
$\hat X\sim\mathrm{Bern}(\phi)$ has miss mass $p(1-\phi)$, so it meets the miss
constraint only when $\phi\ge 1-\delta/p$; when $f<1-\delta/p$ that coupling lies
outside the flag budget, so the minimum sits on the boundary where any remaining
slack in either constraint could otherwise be spent moving further along the ray,
and both constraints bind.
Binding constraints fix $q_{10}=\delta$ and
$q_{11}+q_{01}=f$; with the source margin $q_{10}+q_{11}=p$ this pins all four
cells.
At $(\delta,f)=(0,p)$ the joint is diagonal, $\hat X=X$, and $I=H(p)$.
\end{proof}
\input{figures/legible-swarm-rate-regime}
A conservative digest over-flags to be safe: it emits a flagged set of size $F$
containing the $k$ true criticals with $k\ll F$. The naive second stage opens all
$F$. The adaptive second stage plays twenty questions with group queries: open a
\emph{group} of flagged items at once (an aggregate check cheap at group level, a
combined diff scan or a batched test run), learn whether the group contains any
critical, and split only the groups that do. The analogy transfers because its
defining relation transfers: halving a set costs one question and eliminates
half the candidates only when positives are rare in the set. The misread to
preempt: twenty questions finds \emph{one} answer in $\log_2 F$ queries; with $k$
answers the tree forks, and the accounting below is precisely the price of the
forking.
\begin{pdclaim}{Theorem}{Zoom Advantage}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
\label{thm:zoom-advantage}
Let a flagged set of $F$ items contain an unknown set $P$ of $1\le k\le F$
positives. Assume a noiseless group oracle of unit query cost reports exactly
whether $S\cap P=\emptyset$.
Adaptive halving (query
the flagged set; discard any group that tests negative; report any positive
singleton; otherwise split the group into halves of sizes
$\lfloor|S|/2\rfloor,\lceil|S|/2\rceil$ and recurse on both) identifies $P$
exactly, using a total number of group queries
\[
Q \;\le\; 2k\left\lceil\log_2(F/k)\right\rceil + 4k
\qquad\text{(and always } Q\le 2F-1\text{)},
\]
for every placement of the $k$ positives, against $F$ opens for flat inspection.

The leading constant is tight: for $F$ and $k$ powers of two, evenly spread
positives force $Q=2k\log_2(F/k)+2k-1$. Written in the flagged-set density $d=k/F$
the guaranteed advantage is
\[
\mathrm{adv}(d)\;=\;\frac{F}{2k\lceil\log_2(F/k)\rceil+4k}
\;=\;\frac{1}{d\bigl(2\lceil\log_2(1/d)\rceil+4\bigr)},
\]
which exceeds $1$ exactly on $0<d<1/12$ and equals $1$ at $d=1/12$.
\end{pdclaim}
\begin{proof}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
Every group the algorithm queries is a node of the halving tree $T$ of $[F]$: the
root is the full flagged set, each internal node's children are its two halves,
leaves are singletons. A node of size $s$ has children of size at most
$\lceil s/2\rceil$, so every node at depth $d$ has size at most
$\lceil F/2^d\rceil$, $T$ has depth $D\le\lceil\log_2 F\rceil$, and $T$ has exactly
$2F-1$ nodes, which gives the unconditional bound.

Call a node \emph{positive} if its group meets $P$. The algorithm splits exactly
the positive non-singleton nodes it visits, and every queried node is either the
root or a child of a split node; writing $S^{+}$ for the number of positive
non-singleton nodes of $T$, $Q\le 1+2S^{+}$. The nodes at any fixed depth are
pairwise disjoint subsets of $[F]$, so each positive item lies in at most one of
them, and at most $\min(k,2^d)$ nodes at depth $d$ are positive. Charge the
positive nodes in two regimes. At the top of the tree, $d\le\lfloor\log_2 k\rfloor$,
charge each positive node to its level: at most
$\sum_{d=0}^{\lfloor\log_2 k\rfloor}2^d\le 2k-1$ in total. Below the fan-out,
$d>\lfloor\log_2 k\rfloor$, charge each positive node to one positive item it
contains; by disjointness within a level each of the $k$ positives absorbs at most
one charge per level, and non-singleton nodes exist only at depths $d\le D-1$, so
the number of charging levels is
$D-1-\lfloor\log_2 k\rfloor<(\log_2 F+1)-1-(\log_2 k-1)=\log_2(F/k)+1$; an integer
strictly below $\log_2(F/k)+1$ is at most $\lceil\log_2(F/k)\rceil$, so these
levels contribute at most $k\lceil\log_2(F/k)\rceil$ charges. Summing,
$S^{+}\le k\lceil\log_2(F/k)\rceil+2k-1$ and
$Q\le 2k\lceil\log_2(F/k)\rceil+4k-1$. Correctness is immediate: a discarded group
is certified free of positives, every positive survives discarding at every level,
and each is eventually isolated and reported.

For tightness take $F=2^a$, $k=2^b$, $b\le a$, positives at positions
$\{iF/k:0\le i<k\}$. Every node at depth $d\le b$ contains a positive, so all
$2^{b+1}-1=2k-1$ nodes down to depth $b$ are queried; below depth $b$ exactly $k$
nodes per level are positive, each is split at levels $b,\dots,a-1$, and each split
queries two children; the total is $(2k-1)+2k(a-b)=2k\log_2(F/k)+2k-1$. The
density form follows by dividing $F$ by the bound, and the step function
$d(2\lceil\log_2(1/d)\rceil+4)$ first touches $1$ at $d=1/12$.
\end{proof}

\noindent\textbf{Positioning.} None of the group-testing machinery is new
mathematics: Dorfman introduced pooled testing in 1943~\pdcite{dorfman}, and Hwang's
generalized binary splitting~\pdcite{hwang} finds $k$ defectives in
$\log_2\binom{F}{k}+O(k)$ tests, essentially matching the counting lower bound
$\lceil\log_2\binom{F}{k}\rceil\ge k\log_2(F/k)$ that any adaptive strategy with
binary outcomes must pay; the full theory is Du and Hwang~\pdcite{duhwang}. Plain
halving pays a factor $2+o(1)$ over Hwang because it re-queries both children of
every split rather than inferring one subgroup and searching within the other. The
theorem's contribution is the explicit worst-case constant for the exact procedure
the digest architecture runs, proved tight, which turns the harness's observed
$\approx 2\times$ gap over the ideal $k\log_2(F/k)$ from an empirical curiosity into
the algorithm's provable constant. A dedicated check re-runs the exact procedure:
at $F{=}2500$, $k{=}10$, mean $162.6$ opens over $150$ random placements, maximum
$183$, all under the bound of $200$; random sweeps over $F\in[16,5000]$,
$k\in[1,50]$ and adversarial evenly-spread and clustered placements never exceed
the bound; and the tightness configuration $F{=}4096$, $k{=}32$ lands exactly on
$2k\log_2(F/k)+2k-1=511$ [internal, \texttt{zoom\_bound\_check.py}].

\paragraph{The composition window.} The two prices are drawn from one budget, and
they compose only inside a window that can be stated exactly. Stage one sets stage
two's density: by Theorem~\ref{thm:pinned-joint} both constraints bind, so the
flagged set holds $(p-\delta)N$ real criticals among $fN$ flags and
$d=k/F=(p-\delta)/f$. This worst-case bound certifies a strict query saving
when $d<1/12$, equivalently $f>12(p-\delta)$; at equality it certifies a tie.
Failure of this sufficient certificate does not prove that adaptive inspection
is slower on a particular placement of positives;
the measured mean crossover is about twice as forgiving, $d\approx0.165$
[internal, \texttt{zoom\_bound\_check.py}]. At $p{=}0.05$, $\delta{=}0$ the window
certifies a strict saving only at $f>0.60$: the digest must flag over $60\%$ of the night's artifacts
before the second stage is guaranteed to beat reading the flags. Inside the window
the flags stage one adds are cheap in the second stage, because $k$ is fixed by the
source and doubling $F$ costs only the logarithmic term, $2k$ further opens.
Outside it flat inspection gives the simpler $F$-query guarantee, although
adaptive inspection can still win on some inputs. At the dense end it can lose: at
$F{=}100$, $k{=}90$ the run costs $199$ opens against $100$ flat [internal], which
is the unconditional $2F-1$, reached because at that density essentially every
node of the halving tree is positive. The design consequence for this chapter is
therefore conditional, and the condition is now a number: ``summarize the state,
link to the work'' is cheap when the digest over-flags generously and the zoom is
adaptive, and the operator's dial (bits on the digest, opens on the zoom) has a
favorable exchange rate exactly in the sparse-flag corner.

\begin{pdboundary}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
Theorem~\ref{thm:lowerbound} lower-bounds the zero-miss guarantee in a worst-case
combinatorial model with a data-independent decoder; it says nothing against
cheaper digests at nonzero miss (Theorem~\ref{thm:pinned-joint}'s job) and nothing
average-case.

The falsification result $0/16$ tests the encoder and decoder
formalization at one swept regime ($N{=}60$, $k{=}2$, $m{=}8$); the noisy-encoder
curve in that experiment barely beats random at unit signal-to-noise ratio and is
not evidence that noisy digests are near-optimal.

The frontier $R(\delta,f)$ is an
i.i.d.\ Bernoulli model of critical status, a modeling commitment rather than a
law, and its pinned closed form holds only while $f<1-\delta/p$; evaluating
the formula past that line produces a spurious uptick near
$\delta/p\to1$, a domain-of-validity error and not a feature of the frontier.

Theorem~\ref{thm:zoom-advantage} assumes a reliable group query at unit cost;
correlated or noisy group checks, and group costs growing with $|S|$, change the
accounting. Plain halving is a factor of about two from Hwang's optimum by design:
the theorem bounds the deployed algorithm, not the best one.

The two halves compose
only for $f\ge 12(p-\delta)$, so at $p{=}0.05$ they are not jointly instantiated at
the flag budgets the frontier tabulates.
\end{pdboundary}

\subsection{Existence: the yellow pages (a solved shape)}\label{sec:existence}

The canonical prior art is forty years old. \textbf{FIPA} (\emph{Foundation for
Intelligent Physical Agents}) made a \textbf{Directory Facilitator} (DF) ---
\emph{the mandatory ``yellow pages'' agent: others register the services they offer
and query to find agents that offer a needed service} (FIPA Agent Management
Specification, FIPA00023~\pdcite{fipa00023}) --- a required component of every conformant
platform, alongside the \textbf{Agent Management System} (AMS, the ``white pages'' for
\emph{identity} lookup). Two FIPA choices matter: the DF is \emph{push-based} (agents
self-advertise), and DFs may \emph{federate} (the seed of cross-operator discovery).
The DF has been re-invented almost beat-for-beat for LLM agents
(\textbf{Ren et al.\ 2025}, \emph{Agent Name Service}~\pdcite{ren2025} --- \emph{a
DNS-inspired naming and capability-resolution layer with public-key-backed identity},
which is FIPA's DF with a 2025 threat model).

The existence layer is the easy part: a coordination database that records the live
population, their stated purpose, and their file claims is a white-pages directory,
and a vector index that embeds each agent's declared capabilities and answers
nearest-neighbor queries is a yellow-pages directory over skills. Existence is not the
gap; the layers above it are.

\subsection{Relevance: the split ranker (the headline result)}\label{sec:relevance}

A flat registry answers ``does an OAuth specialist exist?'' It does not answer ``of
the fifty live agents, who should I ask about \emph{this} OAuth bug, given who
touched these files an hour ago and who wrote the design note?'' That is a
\emph{ranking} problem. The \textbf{discovery ranker} scores each candidate $c$
against a query $q$ as a clamped weighted sum of normalized sub-scores:
\begin{align}\label{eq:fit}
z(c,q) ={}& w_f\,\mathrm{file}(c,q) + w_n\,\mathrm{note}(c,q)
  + w_i\,\mathrm{ident}(c,q) \nonumber\\
 &+ w_s\,\mathrm{skill}(c,q) + w_e\,\mathrm{epis}(c,q)
  - w_\ell\,\mathrm{load}(c), \nonumber\\
\mathrm{fit}(c,q) ={}& \mathrm{clamp}\!\bigl(z(c,q),0,1\bigr).
\end{align}

\begin{pdclaim}{Theorem}{No Universal Monotone Identification of the Two Heads}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
\label{thm:split-ranker}
On any item domain containing both high-fit/low-risk and low-fit/high-risk examples,
there is no strictly increasing scalar transform that identifies discovery fit with
operator regret.

The two rankers may share candidate generation and recency decay,
but apply distinct scoring heads. Discovery uses capability fit
(Eq.~\ref{eq:fit}); the attention queue uses regret-if-ignored:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regret}
\mathrm{regret}(x) \;=\; \underbrace{\mathrm{stakes}(x)}_{\text{magnitude}}
   \times \underbrace{\mathrm{irreversibility}(x)}_{v^{-1}}
   \times \underbrace{\mathrm{anomaly}(x)}_{\text{surprise}}.
\end{equation}

No universal monotone transform of one scalar yields the other on that domain.
\end{pdclaim}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
Suppose, for contradiction, that there is a strictly increasing map $\phi$ with
$\mathrm{regret}(x)=\phi(\mathrm{fit}(x))$ for all items $x$, so the two heads induce
the same ranking.

Construct two items. Item $A$ is a highly capable, perfectly
on-topic completion of a safe, fully reversible task: $\mathrm{fit}(A)$ is maximal,
but $\mathrm{stakes}(A)\cdot\mathrm{irreversibility}(A)\cdot\mathrm{anomaly}(A)$ is
near zero, so $\mathrm{regret}(A)$ is minimal. Item $B$ is an off-topic, low-capability
agent that has nonetheless just proposed an irreversible, high-stakes, anomalous
action: $\mathrm{fit}(B)$ is low, but $\mathrm{regret}(B)$ is maximal.

Then
$\mathrm{fit}(A)>\mathrm{fit}(B)$ while $\mathrm{regret}(A)<\mathrm{regret}(B)$, so the
two heads order $\{A,B\}$ oppositely. No increasing $\phi$ can both preserve and reverse
an order, contradiction.

Hence the heads optimize genuinely different objectives; they
may share candidate generation and decay, but the scoring head must be chosen per
reader. \qed
\end{proof}

\noindent\Proved\ --- as the instance of a stronger characterization. The argument
above shows only that \emph{this} pair of heads cannot be identified. The general
fact says when sharing \emph{is} safe, which is the statement a designer needs.

\paragraph{When agreement is enough.}
The upper panel gives the useful converse: different objectives can share a
ranking when their strict preferences never oppose each other. This is
\textbf{comonotonicity}, the concept used in Yaari's rank-dependent utility
and Schmeidler's Choquet expected utility~\pdcite{yaari1987,schmeidler1989}.
For priority scores $u$ and $v$, both oriented so larger means higher
priority, the condition is
$(u(x)-u(y))(v(x)-v(y))\geq 0$ for every pair. A negative product is a
strict reversal; a zero product may be a tie and is not a contradiction.
Thus compatible orders can have different ties: they need a common
refinement, not identical numerical scores or identical indifference
classes. Sharing an ordering also does not calibrate fit and regret into
the same units. The following finite ranking statement borrows this
concept, not the utility representation theorems of those authors.

\begin{pdclaim}{Theorem}{Comonotone Characterization}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
\label{thm:comonotone}
Let $X$ be a finite item set and suppose each reader's optimal selections at
every budget $m$ are the top-$m$ sets of a total preorder of item priorities.

A scalar head $h:X\to\mathbb R$ \emph{serves} a reader if its first $m$ items,
under one fixed tie-breaking order, are optimal for every $m$.

A single head serves both readers iff their priority orders are comonotone:
no pair is strictly preferred in opposite directions. Ties may be refined
in an order both accept. Mira's lower-panel preferences violate the
condition on A and B, so no shared head serves those two orders.
\end{pdclaim}
\begin{proof}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
($\Leftarrow$) Sort by the first reader's priority and resolve each tie by
the second reader's priority. Without a strict reversal, this order
preserves every strict comparison of both readers. Assign decreasing
scalar values along it; each prefix is optimal for both.

($\Rightarrow$) Suppose one reader strictly prefers $x$ to $y$ and the
other strictly prefers $y$ to $x$. Whichever item the shared order places
first, the prefix ending there includes it without the other. Exchanging
the two improves that prefix for one reader, so it cannot be optimal at
that budget. Hence a shared head cannot contain a strict reversal.
\end{proof}
The lower panel is a certificate that the two objectives \emph{can}
disagree, not evidence that every pair of real queues does. The theorem
forbids sharing when a strict reversal actually occurs; the upper panel
shows why different objective names alone do not establish impossibility.

The consequence is a design rule: the operator wants the \emph{most dangerous} item
surfaced, the agent wants the \emph{most capable} collaborator. Collapsing them into
one ranker silently serves one reader and starves the other.

\paragraph{The regret head is derived, not designed.} The product form
$\mathrm{stakes}\times\mathrm{irreversibility}\times\mathrm{anomaly}$ looks like a
designer's convenient heuristic. It is a theorem under one commitment, and the
commitment converts ``anomaly'' from a vibe into a measured obligation. The analogy
is a smoke detector. It has one dial, and the right setting of that dial is fixed
not by how much smoke there is but by the ratio of two costs: the house burning down
against burnt toast waking you at three in the morning. Three relations transfer.
Expected loss is magnitude times probability, so the alarm must weigh what it would
cost to be wrong, not merely how unusual the reading is. Raising the cost of the
fire \emph{lowers} the threshold at which the alarm should sound; the dial moves
toward hair-trigger, not away from it. And a detector that cries wolf is not
miscalibrated in sensitivity but in probability, because the number it reports is
not the chance of a fire. The misread to preempt: a better sensor does not remove
the trade-off. A perfect sensor still needs the cost ratio to know where to trip.

\begin{pdclaim}{Theorem}{The Regret Head}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
\label{thm:regret-head}
For an unreviewed item $x$ let $C_{\mathrm{miss}}(x)=\mathrm{stakes}(x)\cdot
\mathrm{irrev}(x)$ be the unrecoverable loss of passing a bad $x$, let
$a(x)=\Pr(\mathsf{bad}\mid\text{evidence}(x))$ be a \emph{calibrated} posterior,
$c_{\mathrm{att}}$ the marginal attention cost, and $C_{\mathrm{fa}}$ the
non-attention cost of inspecting a good item, with all costs nonnegative.

The expected unrecoverable loss of
passing is exactly $C_{\mathrm{miss}}(x)\,a(x)$, and, among all policies that
decide inspect-or-pass per item from its evidence alone, the Bayes-optimal policy
inspects iff
\begin{equation}\label{eq:bayes-regret}
\begin{aligned}
C_{\mathrm{miss}}(x)\,a(x)&\;\ge\;c_{\mathrm{att}}+C_{\mathrm{fa}}\bigl(1-a(x)\bigr),\\
\text{equivalently}\qquad
a(x)&\;\ge\;\frac{c_{\mathrm{att}}+C_{\mathrm{fa}}}
 {C_{\mathrm{miss}}(x)+C_{\mathrm{fa}}},
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where the second line assumes $C_{\mathrm{miss}}(x)+C_{\mathrm{fa}}>0$.

This is the Bayes cost threshold. If both loss terms are zero, inspect only
when attention is also free, using the stated inspect-on-tie rule. The product form
$\mathrm{stakes}\times\mathrm{irrev}\times\mathrm{anomaly}$ of Eq.~\ref{eq:regret}
is the exact ranking key iff $\mathrm{anomaly}=a$; any other anomaly scalar $s$
requires the correction $C_{\mathrm{miss}}\cdot g(s)$ with $g$ the empirical
calibration map fit from the audit log.
\end{pdclaim}
\begin{proof}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
The expected loss of passing is $C_{\mathrm{miss}}(x)a(x)$; the expected cost of
inspecting is $c_{\mathrm{att}}+C_{\mathrm{fa}}(1-a(x))$. Collect the terms in $a(x)$
to obtain $(C_{\mathrm{miss}}(x)+C_{\mathrm{fa}})a(x)\ge
c_{\mathrm{att}}+C_{\mathrm{fa}}$.

Gating on the product with $\mathrm{anomaly}=a$ against the threshold
$\tau=c_{\mathrm{att}}+C_{\mathrm{fa}}(1-a)$ reproduces the inequality; with any
other $s$ the same inequality holds only after $s$ is mapped back to $a$, which is
what $g$ does.
\end{proof}

Note the direction the inequality runs: raising the miss-to-false-alarm cost ratio
\emph{lowers} the bar to inspect, the formal version of ``when a mistake is
unrecoverable, look at more things.'' Raising that bar instead would reverse the
intended response to costly misses. The theorem is single-shot
(Neyman--Pearson); operator oversight is a stream of items, and the natural
sequential extension --- optimal expected sample size under fixed error-rate
constraints, time-varying thresholds included --- is Wald's Sequential
Probability Ratio Test~\pdcite{wald1947}, which the Inception Canary and escalation
designs (\S\ref{sec:escalation}) already resemble structurally without invoking
it by name.

\begin{pdexample}{The Inspection Bar, at the Chapter's Running Costs}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
$C_{\mathrm{miss}}{=}100$, $c_{\mathrm{att}}{=}1$, $C_{\mathrm{fa}}{=}5$: inspect when
\[
a\ge 6/105\approx 0.057
\]
[verified].

Raising $C_{\mathrm{miss}}$ to $1000$ drops the
bar to
\[
6/1005\approx 0.006;
\]
as the miss-to-false-alarm ratio grows the threshold
falls, driving the operator toward inspecting everything ambiguous.

\emph{Now you
try the other knob:} hold $C_{\mathrm{miss}}{=}100$ and raise the false-alarm cost
to $C_{\mathrm{fa}}{=}20$. Does the bar rise or fall, and by how much? (It rises,
to
\[
21/120=0.175
\]
[verified]: attention that is expensive to waste buys back the
operator's right to pass.)
\end{pdexample}

\noindent\textbf{What this does not say.} The theorem does not say the operator
should inspect more. It says the \emph{threshold} moves; whether a moving threshold
means more opens depends entirely on where the posterior mass sits, and an
uncalibrated $a$ moves the threshold without moving the decision it was supposed
to move. Nor does it license tuning: with a miscalibrated anomaly score the product
form is wrong by precisely the miscalibration, and a confidently ranked queue is
worse than an unranked one because it has borrowed the authority of a proof.
Fitting $g$ from the audit log is a measured obligation of the design.

\begin{pdclaim}{Theorem}{Reputation Enters Only through the Posterior}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
\label{thm:reputation-posterior}
If agent reputation $r$ is admitted to the attention queue, it enters as
$a(x)=\Pr(\mathsf{bad}\mid\text{evidence},r)$ and never as a free multiplier on the
score.

High reputation lowers surfacing frequency only where the calibrated
posterior actually falls, and it cannot zero a term: with $\mathrm{irrev}(x)=1$ and
catastrophic stakes, $C_{\mathrm{miss}}(x)\,a(x)$ stays above threshold for any
$a(x)$ bounded away from zero.
\end{pdclaim}
\begin{proof}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-head} admits exactly one channel for evidence, the
posterior $a$; a multiplier outside it would change the ranking key away from
expected loss, which is what the theorem shows the key must be.

The second claim is
the inequality read at fixed $a>0$ with $C_{\mathrm{miss}}$ large.
\end{proof}
This is the formal content of ``reputation must not defeat hard stakes gates,'' and
it is the reason the reputation layer of the next part hands this chapter a
posterior, not a discount.

\begin{protocol}{Attention-queue ranking with miss-cost}
On each refresh, given the live set of items and the operator's per-class trust
history:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt]
\item \textbf{Generate.} Pull candidate items from the shared substrate, each tagged
with a recency weight $\propto e^{-\lambda\,\text{hoursAgo}}$ (Invariant~\ref{prop:decay}).
\item \textbf{Score by regret.} For each item $x$ compute
$\mathrm{regret}(x)=\mathrm{stakes}(x)\cdot\mathrm{irreversibility}(x)\cdot\mathrm{anomaly}(x)$
(Eq.~\ref{eq:regret}).
\item \textbf{Apply the cost matrix.} Convert regret to a forced-zoom probability via
the Signal-Detection criterion of Invariant~\ref{def:sdt}: the higher
$C_{\text{miss}}/C_{\text{fa}}$ for the item's action class, the more aggressively it
is surfaced --- the miss-cost term, not raw rank, decides what interrupts.
\item \textbf{Lane and emit.} Place items above the distress threshold on the reserved
high-priority lane; emit the rest in regret order. Log what was \emph{not} surfaced
and why (the suppression counter-surface of \S\ref{sec:consent-canon}).
\end{enumerate}
\end{protocol}

\subsection{The quality gates: refuse-to-route and the costly escalation}\label{sec:gates}

Two gates carry most of the design's trustworthiness. \textbf{Refuse-to-route:} if
every candidate falls below a confidence floor, the ranker returns
empty-with-explanation, not a confident wrong answer --- ``I don't know'' must beat a
guess, because sending an agent to the wrong door is high-cost and silent. This is the
exact dual of the completion gate's fail-loud (Invariant~\ref{def:completionist}): both are
the same refuse-to-guess discipline, one on the read side, one on the authority side.
\textbf{The costly escalation:} the attention queue is the system's \emph{most
spoofable} surface --- agents that learn the stakes-ranking will manufacture distress
to win the operator's attention. Goodhart's law applies more sharply here than to the
discovery ranker (\textbf{Strathern 1997}~\pdcite{strathern1997}). The fix is signaling
theory: an escalation the operator dismisses \emph{debits the agent's standing}, so
crying wolf is costly --- the pre-trusted-set idea of \textbf{EigenTrust}
(\textbf{Kamvar et al.\ 2003}~\pdcite{kamvar2003}) applied to the distress lane. This
arms the abdication-resistance and Goodhart gaps at once.

\subsection{Staleness and decay on every read-surface}\label{sec:staleness}

A recurring failure in real fleets makes the point concretely. An agent process dies
or hits a provider usage limit, but its file claim remains on the books; hours later
that \emph{dead} claim still reads as ``current'' and a live agent is blocked from a
file nobody is actually working in. This is a staleness bug, and it generalizes to
every projection.

\begin{pdclaim}{Design invariant}{Universal Recency-Decay}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
\label{prop:decay}
Every read-surface projection --- briefing, resurrection handoff, discovery result,
attestation report --- carries an explicit TTL or recency-decay.

A pull signal
contributes $\propto \exp(-\lambda \cdot \text{hoursAgo})$ with class-specific
half-lives (e.g.\ a short half-life for file claims, longer for episodic memory,
notes capped at a couple of days). \emph{A stale row can never outrank a live one; a
dead-session claim must decay out of ``current,'' not persist as misleading truth.}

This is not a new mechanism but a uniform application of the decay primitive that
already governs the pheromone layer, and it is the production discipline of every
health-checked, TTL-expiring service registry~\pdcite{consul}.
\end{pdclaim}

The dead-claim case is the worked instance: had the resurrection handoff and the
claim projection carried Invariant~\ref{prop:decay}, the dead agent's claim would have
aged out of ``current'' rather than blocking a live agent. Decay is easy to apply to a
forgetting layer and easy to forget on a digest; Invariant~\ref{prop:decay} closes that
gap by making it universal.

\subsection{Ambient legibility: the cross-modal load-balancer}\label{sec:ambient}

Per Wickens' Multiple Resource Theory (\S\ref{sec:operator}), attention is vectored,
not scalar. The dense console loads the \emph{visual-focal} channel; an ambient
channel --- a menu-bar indicator that turns red on a PASS-to-FAIL transition, an
opt-in sound, a reduced-motion cue --- loads a \emph{different}, always-visible,
pre-attentive channel. This is not a second-class fallback --- it is the cross-modal
load-balancing mechanism, and it may be the \emph{primary} alarm path, because a
separate low-clutter field is where pop-out actually works (\textbf{Treisman \& Gelade
1980}, Feature Integration Theory~\pdcite{treisman1980} --- \emph{a single salient
feature pops out pre-attentively in $\sim$200--500\,ms in a non-cluttered field, but
degrades toward serial search as distractor heterogeneity rises} --- so a busy console
defeats the pop-out a dense, single-color distress lane depends on).

\section{Tokens: the swarm's COGS \emph{and} its legibility engine}\label{sec:tokens}
% ═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

A swarm of coding agents has exactly one consumable that is both metered and
meaningful: \textbf{tokens} --- \emph{the sub-word units an LLM reads and writes,
billed per million} (\textbf{Sennrich et al.\ 2016}, byte-pair
encoding~\pdcite{sennrich2016}). Tokens occupy a position no other resource does: they
are \emph{simultaneously} the swarm's \textbf{cost-of-goods-sold} (the direct
variable cost any agent economy must meter) \emph{and} its \textbf{legibility engine}
(the mechanism by which a swarm becomes seeable). The bridge between the two is
\textbf{compaction}.

\keyidea[Compaction decides what remains visible]{The same compaction choice controls the bill \emph{and} controls what is
true and visible. When the context window fills you must drop something --- and what
you drop is a \emph{cost} decision (you stop paying to carry it) \emph{and} a
\emph{legibility} decision (whoever reads the residue no longer sees it). The act
that resolves both is compaction, and compaction's output is exactly the digest a
human or successor agent reads. \emph{The digest IS the compaction.}}

\subsection{Tokens as COGS, and the effective-context trap}\label{sec:cogs}

For a fleet the unit of output is ``a landed piece of work'' and the dominant
variable cost is inference tokens (input context $+$ output generation, each priced
per million) --- the textbook shape of \textbf{COGS}. Every price an agent market can
set (a rented fleet's margin, an agent-for-hire's outcome-per-token) needs a COGS
line, so context economics is the \emph{denominator} of the market, not a side metric.
There is a cost trap: \emph{effective} context $\ll$ \emph{advertised} context. Models
degrade well before their advertised window (\textbf{Liu et al.\ 2024}, \emph{Lost in
the Middle}~\pdcite{liu2024} --- \emph{performance is highest at the beginning and end
of context and degrades for information in the middle, even in long-context models};
\textbf{Anthropic 2025} names the same effect \emph{context rot}~\pdcite{anthropic2025}),
rooted in the transformer's $n^2$ attention over $n$ tokens (\textbf{Vaswani et al.\
2017}~\pdcite{vaswani2017}). The consequence is blunt: packing an agent to 200K tokens
does not buy 200K of capability; it buys degradation at full price. Budgeting to
\emph{effective} context is simultaneously a cost optimization and a quality
optimization --- the two point the same way, the defining feature of context
economics. The primitive the market ultimately needs is a \emph{per-agent-task token
ledger keyed to outcomes} --- ``this task spent 84K input plus 12K output to land
that change'' --- so that cost can be attributed to a witnessed result rather than to
an opaque monthly bill.

\subsection{Compaction is the legibility engine; the digest IS the compaction}\label{sec:tokens-digest}

\textbf{Compaction} (\textbf{Anthropic 2025}~\pdcite{anthropic2025} --- \emph{taking a
conversation nearing the window limit, summarizing it, and reinitiating with the
summary}, with the discipline \emph{maximize recall first, then optimize precision})
is the canonical move for keeping a long-horizon agent under budget. Its siblings ---
structured note-taking (external memory) and sub-agent context isolation (the parent
never sees the bloat) --- complete the family. The reframe: \emph{the only artifact
anyone reads to understand the swarm is a compaction of its raw trajectory.} The
operator reads a digest, not six transcripts; the seventh agent reads a briefing, not
the first six's scrollback; a crashed agent's successor reads a handoff, not the dead
agent's context. Each is a summary of dropped tokens produced for a specific reader.
So compaction is the legibility mechanism of the \emph{entire swarm}: the same act
that keeps the bill down produces the very digest a human or successor agent reads
(Table~\ref{tab:ls-read-surfaces}).

\begin{pdclaim}{Theorem}{The Split-Digest Theorem}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
\label{thm:split-digest}
Let $E = (e_1, e_2, \dots, e_N)$ be an agent's raw execution trace. A compaction policy $D: E \to \Sigma^{\le B}$ compresses $E$ into a token budget $B < |E|$.

Let $R_1$ be the \textbf{successor agent} with continuation loss $L_{\mathrm{succ}}(D) = \mathrm{KL}\bigl(P_{\mathrm{next}}(\cdot \mid E) \,\|\, P_{\mathrm{next}}(\cdot \mid D)\bigr)$ (penalizing loss of AST trajectory and task state), and let $R_2$ be the \textbf{human operator} with oversight loss $L_{\mathrm{op}}(D) = \sum_{e \in E \setminus D} \mathrm{regret}(e)$ (penalizing unreviewed high-stakes anomalies and policy breaches).

If the trace contains both benign working details (high continuation value, zero regret) and dead-end anomalous excursions (zero continuation value, maximal regret), then \textbf{no single compaction $D^*(E)$ minimizes both $L_{\mathrm{succ}}$ and $L_{\mathrm{op}}$}. A single-pipeline compaction necessarily starves either task continuation or human oversight.
\end{pdclaim}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
Construct a trace $E = E_{\mathrm{work}} \cup E_{\mathrm{breach}}$, where $E_{\mathrm{work}}$ consists of $k$ safe, valid AST transformations ($L_{\mathrm{succ}}$ increases by $\Theta(k)$ if omitted; $\mathrm{regret}(e) = 0$), and $E_{\mathrm{breach}}$ consists of an attempted irreversible socket connection that was blocked and rolled back ($L_{\mathrm{succ}}$ increases by $0$ if omitted since the branch is dead; $\mathrm{regret}(e) = \mathrm{stakes} \times \mathrm{anomaly} = \text{maximal}$).

Under budget $B < |E_{\mathrm{work}}| + |E_{\mathrm{breach}}|$, any compaction prioritizing $E_{\mathrm{work}}$ has $L_{\mathrm{op}} = \text{maximal}$ (the operator is blind to the breach attempt), while any compaction prioritizing $E_{\mathrm{breach}}$ has $L_{\mathrm{succ}} \gg 0$ (the successor loses necessary variable bindings).

Hence $R_1$ and $R_2$ have strictly non-comonotone preferences, requiring distinct compaction heads. \qed
\end{proof}

\noindent\Proved\ --- and it is the applied instance of
Theorem~\ref{thm:comonotone}. Theorem~\ref{thm:split-digest} and the split-ranker
result (Thm.~\ref{thm:split-ranker}) are not two findings but two instances of one:
the successor and operator pair and the fit and regret pair are each
non-comonotone, and each supplies the crossing pair that makes the impossibility
bite. The split also has a price, not only a prohibition.

\begin{pdclaim}{Theorem}{Super-Additive Split Penalty}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
\label{thm:split-penalty}
For integers $N,m,k$ with $k\ge1$ and $2k\le m<N$, let
\[
F(q)=\mathrm{Floor}(N,q,m)
 =\log_2\!\frac{\binom Nq}{\binom mq}
 =\sum_{j=0}^{q-1}\log_2\!\frac{N-j}{m-j}.
\]
A joint zero-miss digest that must cover every possible union of two disjoint
critical $k$-sets, using at most $m$ opens in total, has the worst-case bit
lower bound $F(2k)$ from Theorem~\ref{thm:lowerbound}.

This bound satisfies
\[
R(N,k,m)=\frac{F(2k)}{2F(k)}>1.
\]
Indeed, $a_j=\log_2((N-j)/(m-j))$ is positive and strictly increasing in $j$
because $N>m$. Thus $a_{j+k}>a_j$ for $0\le j<k$, so the last $k$ summands
of $F(2k)$ exceed the first $k$: $F(2k)>2F(k)>0$.

The ratio is unbounded across admissible instances, not along a fixed finite
$(N,m)$ slice. For $N=2k+1$ and $m=2k$, binomial cancellation gives
\[
F(2k)=\log_2(2k+1),\qquad
F(k)=\log_2\!\frac{2k+1}{k+1}\longrightarrow1.
\]
Consequently $R(2k+1,k,2k)\to\infty$ as $k\to\infty$.
\end{pdclaim}

The denominator is the sum of two single-reader lower bounds, each allowing
$m$ opens. Those separate review budgets permit up to $2m$ opens in total,
whereas the joint bound allows only $m$. This is a comparison of lower bounds
under different total opening budgets, not an equal-budget comparison of
architectures. It neither establishes attainable encoding lengths nor says
that running two heads costs more than twice one head.

\begin{pdexample}{The Split-Floor Arithmetic, at the Sweep's Own Instance}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
At $N=60$, $k=2$, $m=8$, one reader's lower bound is
\[
F(2)=\log_2\!\frac{\binom{60}{2}}{\binom82}
\approx5.9822\text{ bits}.
\]

For a joint digest covering both disjoint critical sets with eight opens in
total, the lower bound is
\[
F(4)=\log_2\!\frac{\binom{60}{4}}{\binom84}
\approx12.7662\text{ bits}.
\]

The sum of the two single-reader floors is
\[
2F(2)\approx11.9644 \text{ bits},
\]
with eight opens allowed per reader. Hence the plotted ratio is
\[
R(60,2,8)\approx1.0670:
\]
the joint floor is about $6.70\%$ above that sum.

The ratio to just one reader's floor is instead
\[
F(4)/F(2)\approx2.1340.
\]

These are closed-form lower bounds, not measured token costs or achieved
compression ratios.

that sweep is not a proof of the universal bound.
\end{pdexample}

\paragraph{Architectural corollary: the SLM sidecar.}
Theorem~\ref{thm:split-digest} dictates that the system must never ask the worker agent to author its own operator-facing summary (which invites self-attestation bias and Potemkin digests~\S\ref{sec:verifiable}). Instead, the design calls for two separate pipelines:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em,itemsep=1pt]
\item \textbf{Task-Continuation Head ($R_1$, \Designed):} Authored by the worker agent or structured state-checkpoints, optimized for continuation fidelity.
\item \textbf{Oversight Head ($R_2$, \Vision):} An embedded, air-gapped \textbf{Small Language Model (SLM) Sidecar} (e.g., a local 8B model running via \texttt{llama.cpp}) that \emph{would} read raw kernel events and disk diffs as data, computing $\mathrm{regret}(x)$ and surfacing truthful digests without worker collusion.
\end{enumerate}
\noindent The sidecar treats every agent-authored token as tainted data: it runs
under a fixed policy and schema, holds no tool authority, and must cite a
kernel-recorded source for every line it emits. What can be proved of it is
syntactic (schema conformance, provenance closure, absence of authority); whether
its digests are faithful is an empirical hypothesis, measured by sampling them for
re-audit at the rate $\rho^\star=G/(dB)$ of \pdchapref{stp}{From Spawn to Person},
so that a sidecar which could gain $G$ by mimicking a routine summary is deterred
once the audit probability times the sanction exceeds $G$.

document specifies one; the row is carried in Appendix~\ref{app:honest} with the rest.

\subsection{Context paging: virtual memory for the context window}\label{sec:context-paging}

Context compaction can be formalized as \textbf{virtual memory for language models}: the local file buffer is backing store, the finite context window is the resident set, and paging tools act as the memory management unit (MMU).

\begin{pdclaim}{Theorem}{Context Paging Competitiveness}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
\label{thm:paging}
Let the context window hold $k$ active pages, with offline optimal page-replacement incurring cost $\mathrm{OPT}$.

Under an online \textbf{recency-plus-pin policy} (where pins correspond to critical spans verified by deterministic structural features), the online paging cost satisfies the resource-augmented competitive bound of Sleator--Tarjan~\pdcite{sleatortarjan1985}; for the weighted case (pages of nonuniform size and refetch cost, which context spans are), the same guarantee is carried by Young's \textsc{Landlord} algorithm~\pdcite{young2002landlord}:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Cost}_{\mathrm{online}} \;\le\; \frac{k}{k - h + 1} \cdot \mathrm{OPT} \;+\; \psi N c_{\mathrm{refetch}},
\end{equation}
where $h \le k$ is the size of the verifiable pinned set, and $\psi \in [0, 1]$ represents the fraction of forgeable/unverified features subject to adversarial eviction.

Under marking with randomized eviction, competitiveness against oblivious adversaries improves to $O(\log k)$.
\end{pdclaim}

\noindent\Verified\ --- \emph{upgraded from proposed}. The unweighted bound is classical
(Sleator--Tarjan); the weighted transfer imports Young's \textsc{Landlord} unchanged.
The adversarial term is new: with the pin oracle corrupted on $C$ consultations
($\mathbb{E}[C]=\psi N$, oblivious, repair-on-touch), pin-augmented Landlord pays
$\le c(P) + \tfrac{k-p}{k-h+1}\cdot\mathrm{OPT}_{h-p} + \psi N c_{\mathrm{refetch}}$ for
every $p\le h\le k$ --- the Landlord bound on the unpinned region plus an
\emph{additive, linear} $\psi$ term, checked against exact OPT on 122 instances (0
violations) and against 50 corruption instances up to $\psi=0.4$ (0 violations, slope
$22.8$ against a ceiling of $N\cdot c_{\mathrm{refetch}}=1200$, $R^2=0.994$). The guard
is necessary, not decorative: dropping repair-on-touch turns one corruption into
$\Theta(N)$ stale-serves, confirmed by mutation (item R16 of the research ledger,
\texttt{b9\_context\_paging.py}) --- \emph{the $\psi$ budget is shared with the digest
floor's forgeable-feature budget --- one number, two mechanisms}.

\begin{pdboundary}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
The adversarial term is a mechanized sweep at seed 20260816 across small-to-moderate
instances, not a closed proof for every adversary class; a stronger-than-greedy
adaptive adversary remains untested, and unit cost density is assumed (general
refetch costs need $c_{\mathrm{refetch}}\ge$ max-density $\times$ max-size).

The
classical and weighted bounds themselves are Sleator--Tarjan and Young, imported
unchanged.

The field's name for the rest of it --- a classical competitive
algorithm that consults an oracle it cannot trust and is asked to degrade
gracefully as the oracle's error grows --- is \textbf{online algorithms with
predictions}, and Lykouris and Vassilvitskii's competitive caching with
machine-learned advice~\pdcite{lykouris2018} is the closest published shape to
the corruption term above.

We reached the additive term without that
literature's vocabulary (they speak of consistency and robustness, and their
degradation curves are multiplicative in the prediction error where ours is
additive in the corruption rate), which is a reason to read them next, not a
reason to distrust the sweep: two routes into the same neighborhood is what
one hopes to see.
\end{pdboundary}

\begin{pdexample}{The Landlord Ratio, at a Size an Operator Can Hold in One Head}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
Take $k=8$ active context pages against an offline optimum allowed $h=4$ of them pinned:
\[
k/(k-h+1)=8/(8-4+1)=8/5=1.6
\]
[verified, Young 2002, closed form] --- the online recency-plus-pin policy never pays more than $1.6\times$ what an $h$-pinned offline optimum would have paid, for \emph{any} sequence, adversarial included.

No script in this repository prints this exact $(k,h)$ pair; \texttt{b9\_context\_paging.py} instead verifies the general bound
\[
\mathrm{Cost}_{\mathrm{online}}\le\frac{k}{k-h+1}\mathrm{OPT}
\]
itself, over 122 (instance,$h$) pairs including adversarial cyclic thrash, 0 violations, tightest observed cost/bound ratio $1.000$ (cycles sit at equality, confirming the bound is not slack) [internal, \texttt{b9\_context\_paging.py}].

\emph{Now you try:} compute the ratio at the two ends of the range, $h=k$ and $h=1$ (Exercise~\ref{ex:ls-paging-ratio-hand}).
\end{pdexample}

The most counterintuitive instance is \emph{forgetting as compaction}.
\textbf{Pheromone decay} --- multiplying every stored coordination trace by a decay
factor each interval --- implements \textbf{stigmergy} (\textbf{Grass\'e
1959}~\pdcite{grasse1959} --- \emph{coordination via persistent traces left in a shared
environment, as ants deposit evaporating trails}). Decay is deliberate, continuous,
$O(1)$ compaction of the shared context: the map stays readable because the stale ink
fades. It is legibility by subtraction, and it is the same primitive
Invariant~\ref{prop:decay} generalizes to every read-surface.

\subsection{The context-degradation cascade (the shared failure surface)}\label{sec:cascade}

Cost and legibility share one failure surface: when compaction goes wrong, the bill
\emph{and} the truth degrade together, in four named steps. \textbf{(1)
Lost-in-the-middle:} a critical fact buried mid-window is paid-for-and-ignored ---
edge-place facts, shorten the window. \textbf{(2) Context rot:} quality decays as the
window grows even with nothing dropped --- compact earlier (the cost cure and the
quality cure are the same act). \textbf{(3) Recursive-summarization collapse:} each
summary injects model noise; summarize the summary enough and the noise compounds
into confident fabrication. The effective cure: \emph{compact from the durable
artifacts each round --- the version-control history, the written notes, the
coordination database --- never from the previous summary.} A system whose artifacts
are durable and addressable is unusually well-placed here, because each compaction
re-derives from source instead of recursing on prose. \textbf{(4) Over-flattening
(the Scott failure):} the digest reads clean but the real situation is unreachable
from it --- detection rule: any digest line with no deep-link to its artifact; cure:
legibility-with-zoom enforced by construction. A text-only console enforces
this almost for free, because a terminal grid \emph{cannot} over-render; the
product's console (\S\ref{sec:operator-instrument}) renders richly, so it
must enforce the rule deliberately, pane by pane.

\subsection{Single-operator: account, don't auction}\label{sec:account}

In the wedge all agents serve one operator, so there is \emph{no incentive problem} ---
only a visibility problem. The right mechanism is \emph{accounting}, not a market: a
per-agent-task token ledger, budget caps, and a loud failure when a cap blows. The
externality-pricing and Sybil-resistance machinery (\textbf{Nisan et al.\
2007}~\pdcite{nisan2007}; \textbf{Douceur 2002}, the Sybil attack~\pdcite{douceur2002};
\textbf{Ostrom 1990}, commons governance~\pdcite{ostrom1990}) is a concern for the
cross-operator economy, not the wedge --- and it is one more reason the
continuity-to-person-to-reputation through-line must come first: you cannot bill, cap,
or reputation-score a swarm of disposable spawns, because Sybils dodge every
per-agent mechanism. Context economics therefore \emph{demands} the identity layer it
appears to precede.

\section{Reconciling the canon: roles, consent, and local knowledge}\label{sec:consent-canon}
% ═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

The Leviathan metaphor earns its place only if it survives contact with the political-
theory canon. Three questions make its limits precise: who authorizes the system,
how consent can be withdrawn, and what local knowledge a central view cannot replace.

\subsection{Who is the multitude, who is the sovereign?}\label{sec:roles}

Hobbes' covenant is \emph{among the subjects}; the sovereign is its product, not a
party to it. So the metaphor breaks differently depending on which entity sits where
(Figure~\ref{fig:roles}).

\begin{pdexample}{One Grant, Traced through Four Steps, and the Role Each Step Forces}
\setlength{\parskip}{4pt plus 1pt}
Rather than assign the roles and then check them, run the consent-grant lifecycle of \S\ref{sec:consent} on Mira's 3:25 grant and let the assignment fall out.

\textbf{Step~1, issue:} the grant $g = \langle$\,the \texttt{tests/} subtree, a low stakes ceiling $s_{\max}$, a reversibility floor $v_{\min}$ no irreversible act clears, a two-hour \textsc{ttl}\,$\rangle$ comes into existence because Mira says so, and nothing else in the system can create one --- so whoever occupies this step is a source of authority rather than a user of it.

\textbf{Step~2, act:} the formatting diffs land and the review agent's force-push is escalated instead, because it fails $v_{\min}$; the daemon performed both, and it did not choose the floor it applied --- so this step exercises an authority it did not author.

\textbf{Step~3, witness:} the daemon records $(g, x, \text{reason}, \text{artifact})$ for each, which only means anything if the recorder is answerable to someone it is not.

\textbf{Step~4, expire:} at 5:25 the grant leaves the active set with nobody's permission, and Mira's override would have emptied it sooner.

Now read off what the four steps require: exactly one participant who originates authority and can withdraw it, exactly one who exercises it under terms set elsewhere, and --- the step that never appears --- any moment at which the daemon must be a \emph{party} to the agents' rule of the road.

Delete the daemon from that covenant and all four steps still run; delete Mira and step~1 has no author. That is Hobbes' author/actor split, forced rather than assumed, and the itemization below just gives the three slots their names.

\emph{Now you try:} map $\{$agents, daemon, operator$\}$ onto $\{$multitude, sovereign-actor, author$\}$ and say why the metaphor breaks if the operator is called the sovereign (Exercise~\ref{ex:ls-hobbes-mapping}).
\end{pdexample}

We assign roles explicitly, using Hobbes' own
author/actor distinction (\emph{Leviathan} ch.~16~\pdcite{hobbes1651}):

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \textbf{The agents are the multitude.} They covenant --- via the rule of the
road, the coordination protocol --- to be bound, each to all, enforced by the daemon.
This is the literal Hobbesian covenant-among-subjects.
\item \textbf{The daemon is the sovereign-as-actor.} It holds the authority the
multitude instituted; it blocks, lands, escalates. But, per the legible-sovereign
amendment (Invariant~\ref{prop:legible-sovereign}), it is the \emph{most} legible actor,
not (as in Hobbes) the least.
\item \textbf{The operator is the author.} In Hobbes' author/actor split, the
\emph{author} owns the authority that the \emph{actor} exercises. The operator is not
a peer subject and not the sovereign-in-action; the operator is the source of
authority, exercising it through the daemon and able to withdraw it
(Invariant~\ref{prop:override}). This is the slot Hobbes leaves for the one who authorizes
but does not personally act.
\end{itemize}

This assignment is what makes the consent grant (Invariant~\ref{def:consent}) coherent:
the author issues a scoped grant to the actor, over a multitude bound by covenant.

\subsection{Express consent, not tacit; voice \emph{and} exit}\label{sec:express}

\S\ref{sec:consent} already made consent a first-class object precisely to escape
Hume's tacit-consent critique; the canon demands we also engage \textbf{Locke}
(\emph{Second Treatise}, \S\S95--122~\pdcite{locke1689} --- \emph{express consent binds
where tacit consent does not}) and \textbf{Pateman} (\emph{The Problem of Political
Obligation}, 1979~\pdcite{pateman1979} --- \emph{liberal consent theory keeps collapsing
into hypothetical consent}). The consent grant is express by construction: a discrete,
scoped, revocable authorization with an audit trail (Invariant~\ref{def:consent}). And the
inalienable override (Invariant~\ref{prop:override}) supplies the \emph{exit} that
Hirschman~\pdcite{hirschman1970} says keeps \emph{voice} honest. The design has rich
voice machinery; the override is the missing exit, and exit is what gives the author
real leverage over the actor.

\subsection{M\^etis is Hayek, and some of it cannot be made legible}\label{sec:hayek}

The formal constraint of \S\ref{sec:flatten} --- that a read-path to the diff is more
legibility, not m\^etis --- has a 1945 economics name. \textbf{Hayek}, \emph{The Use
of Knowledge in Society} (1945~\pdcite{hayek1945} --- \emph{the knowledge that matters
is dispersed, particular to time and place, and not aggregable by any central
planner; this is the fundamental reason central planning fails}) is the other half of
Scott's argument from the opposite pole: the operator-attention economy
(\S\ref{sec:operator}) is at bottom a Hayekian knowledge-allocation problem, not only
a Wickens attention-resource problem. The core claim of this layer --- ``the binding
constraint is decentralized, non-spawnable knowledge held in one head'' --- is a Hayek
argument, and Scott's \emph{Two Cheers for Anarchism} (2012~\pdcite{scott2012}) is the
\emph{constructive} sequel that says what to do: preserve the spaces where m\^etis
lives, do not flatten them for legibility's sake. The skill-retention tax
(\S\ref{sec:abdication}) is the design's gesture at exactly this, and it is the
hardest mechanism in the paper to realize, because preserving the illegible is, by
definition, the thing legibility cannot do for you.

\section*{Review of the key ideas}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Review of the key ideas}

What this chapter leaves coupled, in the order the rest of the book will lean on it:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em,itemsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{The swarm is a state of nature.} Four valid writes in one window overlap; nothing in the agents' competence prevents it, because the conflict is in the schedule, not in the work.
\item \textbf{Consent changes the schedule, not the participants.} Under consented serialization the same four writes become one commit spine with a grant record each, naming actor, claim, artifact and reason.
\item \textbf{Legibility-with-zoom.} A digest is a lens, never a replacement: every claim in it resolves to an artifact, and a digest whose claims resolve to nothing is a facade, however green.
\item \textbf{The authority half.} The daemon gates or escalates on every act under a grant the operator gave once; the override is a single edge from operator to daemon, and the escalation threshold has a tuning band, not a point.
\item \textbf{Specialization has a boundary.} A pooled queue beats sole ownership only when utilization and the skill premium put the operator on one side of the queueing boundary; the worked point $(\rho,r)=(0.556,\,2.5)$ clears a boundary of $2.483$ and so falls, barely, on sole ownership's side, while the same pool against a $2.8/\text{hr}$ specialist ($r=2.33$) falls on the pool's.
\item \textbf{Read-poverty is the binding constraint.} With a review budget the operator can locate the write that mattered in about $5.98$ bits under the split digest, against $12.77$ bits under the flat one, at $N=60$; the floor is a theorem, and the zoom advantage is measured.
\item \textbf{Discovery has two heads.} Fit and regret rank candidates differently, no monotone score identifies both, and reputation enters only through the posterior.
\item \textbf{Tokens are the cost of goods sold and the legibility engine at once.} The split-digest theorem prices the digest; the split penalty is super-additive; paging context is competitive within a constant.
\end{enumerate}

The local daemon of \S\ref{sec:local} supplies what an emergent attractor does not guarantee: one fault domain, one policy root, and an unconditional operator exit.
\end{pdsolution}
\begin{pdexercise}[kind=Open,rating=3]{ex:ls-sovereign-vs-oligarchy-experiment}
The Dai et al.\ result is a single controlled study. Design an

\end{pdexercise}
\begin{pdsolution}{ex:ls-sovereign-vs-oligarchy-experiment}
Randomize comparable real repository tasks to (a) an ungoverned fleet and (b) a fleet with only a transport but no prescribed governance; record all messages, effects, blocking decisions, and operator interventions. Pre-register two observable classes: \emph{sovereign} characteristics (a publicly known rule, general applicability, explicit consent or adoption, stable appeal/override, reasons attached to authority acts, equal audit access) against \emph{oligarchy} characteristics (authority concentrated in a small coalition, private channels or rules, selective enforcement, no effective exit, outcomes outsiders cannot reconstruct). The corner is priced in closed form, and the result belongs to

\fi

\begin{pdexercise}[kind=Open,rating=3]{ex:ls-adversarial-critical-set}

\begin{pdsolution}{ex:ls-adversarial-critical-set}

\begin{pdexercise}[kind=Open,rating=3]{ex:ls-noisy-zoom-oracle}
Theorem~\ref{thm:zoom-advantage}'s group query is exact: it reports whether
$S\cap P=\emptyset$ and is never wrong. A real group query is a linter, a batched
test run or a diff scan, and those have false negatives --- a group that does
contain a critical item can come back clean. This is the worst direction for the
algorithm to fail in, because a negative group is \emph{discarded}: one false
negative high in the tree silently drops a whole subtree and the run reports an
answer that is confidently incomplete. Suppose each query independently returns a
false negative with probability at most $\alpha$, and that false positives do not
occur (a group reported dirty really is dirty). Give a repetition scheme that
bounds the probability of losing any given positive, state the query cost it
pays, and then say what the scheme does \emph{not} fix.
\end{pdexercise}
\begin{pdsolution}{ex:ls-noisy-zoom-oracle}
Repeat every \emph{negative} verdict: accept ``clean'' only when $r$ independent
queries on the same group all report clean. A group is then wrongly discarded with
probability at most $\alpha^{r}$, so $r=\lceil\log(1/\eta)/\log(1/\alpha)\rceil$
buys per-node error $\eta$ --- at $\alpha=0.1$, three repetitions give $10^{-3}$.
Positive verdicts are never repeated, since by hypothesis they are not wrong, and
the recursion only ever spends queries on groups it has already decided to split.
The cost is therefore at most $r$ times the noiseless bound,
$Q\le r\bigl(2k\lceil\log_2(F/k)\rceil+4k\bigr)$, which is the noiseless bound with
$\alpha$'s cost folded into a constant: sparsity, not oracle quality, is still what
decides whether zoom beats flat inspection, and the advantage
$\mathrm{adv}(d)$ simply divides by $r$.

Three things this does not fix, and the exercise is Open because of the third.
First, the union bound over the tree, not the node, is what an operator cares
about: with $O(k\log_2(F/k))$ negative decisions, per-node $\eta$ gives an overall
miss probability of roughly $\eta\cdot 2k\lceil\log_2(F/k)\rceil$, so $r$ has to
grow like $\log k+\log\log F$ to hold a fixed end-to-end guarantee. Second, the
independence assumption is doing heavy work and is the least defensible part:
repeating the same linter on the same group is perfectly correlated, not
independent, so $r$ repetitions of one tool buy nothing at all and the scheme
needs $r$ \emph{different} tools --- which is the same diversity argument
\pdchapref{stp}{From Spawn to Person} makes about correlated auditors, arriving
here from a different direction. Third, and unresolved: the guarantee above is
probabilistic where Theorem~\ref{thm:zoom-advantage} is exact, so a chapter that
prices oversight in bits now owes an account of what a $10^{-3}$ chance of a
silently dropped subtree is worth against the queries it saves, and that is a
decision-theoretic question this chapter does not answer. The noisy
group-testing literature is where to start --- Atia and Saligrama
\pdcite{atiasaligrama2012} give information-theoretic limits for noisy
non-adaptive designs, and Cheraghchi \pdcite{cheraghchi2013} gives noise-resilient
constructions --- but neither is written for the adaptive, correlated-oracle case
above, so the obligation is to state the model before importing a bound, not to
import one and hope.
\end{pdsolution}

\pdexercisesfor{\S\ref{sec:discovery}}{Discovery: the directory substrate and the split ranker}

\begin{pdexercise}[kind=Check,rating=1]{ex:ls-split-ranker-statement}
State Thm.~\ref{thm:split-ranker} in one sentence and give the loss function
each head optimizes.
\end{pdexercise}
\begin{pdsolution}{ex:ls-split-ranker-statement}
No monotone scalar transform can identify collaborator fit and operator regret on a domain containing both high-fit/low-risk and low-fit/high-risk items, so one ranker cannot serve both jobs. Discovery minimizes routing loss (or maximizes capability fit); the attention head minimizes expected loss from \emph{not} inspecting, and its rank is the ratio of expected loss avoided by inspecting to the operator-seconds inspection costs, with Theorem~\ref{thm:regret-head} fixing the numerator per item; raw stakes times anomaly is not that quantity.
\end{pdsolution}
\begin{pdexercise}[kind=Check,rating=1]{ex:ls-goodhart-exposure}
Why is the attention queue more Goodhart-exposed than the
discovery ranker (\S\ref{sec:gates})?
\end{pdexercise}
\begin{pdsolution}{ex:ls-goodhart-exposure}
The attention queue allocates a scarce human interrupt channel, so agents benefit directly from inflating its score and a buried item can cause irreversible loss. Discovery gaming usually yields only a bad routing recommendation, correctable before any effect. Both are Goodhart-exposed, but the attention queue carries the stronger incentive and the larger consequence.
\end{pdsolution}
\begin{pdexercise}[kind=Trace,rating=2]{ex:ls-dead-claim-replay}
Replay the dead-claim case (\S\ref{sec:staleness}) twice: once without
Invariant~\ref{prop:decay} and once with it. Show exactly where the live agent unblocks.
\end{pdexercise}
\begin{pdsolution}{ex:ls-dead-claim-replay}
Without expiry, the dead holder's row remains authoritative and the live agent is denied forever. With a lease, heartbeat-based expiry, and a new fencing epoch (Invariant~\ref{prop:decay}), the stale lease becomes ineligible; a lazy sweep on the next acquire removes it; the live agent receives a higher epoch and proceeds. Merely decaying a display score is insufficient: ownership must actually expire, and every effect boundary must reject the stale epoch.
\end{pdsolution}
\begin{pdexercise}[kind=Trace,rating=2]{ex:ls-costly-signal-debit}
An agent fires an escalation; the operator dismisses it. Trace the costly-signal
debit and explain how it deters crying wolf without silencing genuine distress.
\end{pdexercise}
\begin{pdsolution}{ex:ls-costly-signal-debit}
The escalation cites evidence and posts a small stake or reputation exposure. The operator's dismissal is itself reasoned and appealable; only a confirmed low-value/false escalation debits the signaler. Repeated unsupported alarms raise future cost or lower priority, while verified hazards return the stake and improve calibration. The debit must never fire solely because an operator clicked dismiss --- that would silence minority warnings and make authority error self-validating.
\end{pdsolution}
\begin{pdexercise}[kind=Open,rating=3]{ex:ls-implicit-feedback-goodhart}

\end{pdexercise}
\begin{pdsolution}{ex:ls-implicit-feedback-goodhart}
Accept/reject is useful implicit relevance feedback but is confounded by position, exposure, workload, and the incumbent policy. Learn with logged propensities, small randomized exploration, counterfactual evaluation, and a held-out labeled sample; preserve refusals and downstream task outcomes. If acceptance directly trains what is shown, the loop can entrench its own choices, so it cannot be the sole ground truth.
\end{pdsolution}
\begin{pdexercise}[kind=Open,rating=3]{ex:ls-decay-vs-summary}
The

signal (decay for coordination traces, summary for decisions,
eviction-plus-pointer for reconstructable artifacts).
\end{pdexercise}
\begin{pdsolution}{ex:ls-decay-vs-summary}
Use hard expiry for leases and presence; state transition or supersession for commitments; summary plus source provenance for decisions and rationale; immutable storage for evidence; and eviction plus a content-addressed pointer for reconstructable diffs and logs. Hints may decay continuously. Never decay a still-open obligation, and never recursively summarize prior summaries.
\end{pdsolution}

\begin{pdexercise}[kind=Check,rating=1]{ex:ls-digest-is-compaction}

\end{pdexercise}
\begin{pdsolution}{ex:ls-digest-is-compaction}
The operator digest and the context compaction must be the same provenance-preserving projection from durable sources. If a separate model instead summarizes the already-compacted agent story, cost control and truth control diverge and recursive-summary error compounds --- exactly the failure Thm.~\ref{thm:split-digest} names two heads to avoid conflating.
\end{pdsolution}
\begin{pdexercise}[kind=Check,rating=1]{ex:ls-effective-context-budget}
Explain why budgeting to effective context is simultaneously a cost

\end{pdexercise}
\begin{pdsolution}{ex:ls-effective-context-budget}
Context past the model's effective window costs tokens while degrading retrieval and reasoning. Removing redundant or inert material and edge-placing hard facts can therefore reduce spend and improve accuracy simultaneously, provided obligations and source pointers survive the cut.
\end{pdsolution}
\begin{pdexercise}[kind=Trace,rating=2]{ex:ls-five-compaction-primitives}
For each of the five compaction primitives (Table~\ref{tab:ls-read-surfaces}),
name the reader, what it drops, and what it keeps.
\end{pdexercise}
\begin{pdsolution}{ex:ls-five-compaction-primitives}
Briefing: reader is an arriving agent; drops old conversational detail; keeps current roles, work, constraints, decisions, and source links. Attention queue: reader is agent/operator now; drops low-value candidates from the immediate view; keeps highest value-of-inspection items plus the suppression ledger. Episodic memory: reader is a future incarnation; drops transient chatter; keeps typed incidents, decisions, outcomes, and retrieval features. Pheromone: reader is the swarm; continuously drops stale coordination weight; keeps fresh shared traces. Resurrection handoff: reader is a successor; drops nonessential transcript detail; keeps the task capsule, artifacts, obligations, pending operations, and provenance.
\end{pdsolution}
\begin{pdexercise}[kind=Trace,rating=2]{ex:ls-cascade-cures}
Walk the four-rung cascade
(\S\ref{sec:cascade}) and name the single cure that breaks each rung.
\end{pdexercise}
\begin{pdsolution}{ex:ls-cascade-cures}
Context rot $\to$ compact earlier to an effective-window budget. Lost-in-the-middle $\to$ shorten and edge-place mandatory facts. Recursive-summary collapse $\to$ re-derive every compaction from raw durable artifacts. Over-flattening $\to$ require a total zoom path from every material claim. The third cure is the one that matters most: it prevents model noise from becoming the next round's source of truth.
\end{pdsolution}
\begin{pdexercise}[kind=Open,rating=3]{ex:ls-replay-metric-soundness}
The compaction-quality scalar: is \emph{successor-replay-success-
from-digest-alone} a sound, gaming-resistant metric, and can it be made a cheap
attestation invariant (the replay smoke-test is itself token-expensive)?
\end{pdexercise}
\begin{pdsolution}{ex:ls-replay-metric-soundness}
``Replay from digest alone'' is neither sound nor gaming-resistant on its own: a digest can overfit the smoke task or omit facts the sampled successor never needed. Give a successor the digest plus authorized content-addressed source links, then measure next-step success, false beliefs, obligation recall, time, and tokens on hidden continuation tasks. Add a cheap structural invariant that does not require replay: every open claim, decision, commitment, risk, and pending external effect has exactly one valid digest entry or pointer. Run the expensive full replay only on a random sample.
\end{pdsolution}
\begin{pdexercise}[kind=Open,rating=3]{ex:ls-dag-token-budget}
Optimal token-budget allocation across a DAG of orchestrator/worker/
reviewer agents under a total budget.
\end{pdexercise}
\begin{pdsolution}{ex:ls-dag-token-budget}
Budget allocation over a precedence DAG is a knapsack/resource-allocation problem and is NP-hard in general. First reserve a minimum viable budget for each mandatory node and any predecessor needed to make its output exist. Allocate the remainder by estimated marginal outcome value per token while respecting precedence, and recompute after each result. For a monotone submodular value function under a cardinality budget, greedy earns the standard $(1-1/e)$ approximation; for arbitrary complementarities there is no such bound. Dynamic programming is pseudo-polynomial for small integer budgets or trees. A reviewer's tokens have near-zero value until the worker artifact it reviews exists.
\end{pdsolution}

\begin{pdexercise}[kind=Check,rating=1]{ex:ls-paging-ratio-hand}
Compute the Landlord competitiveness ratio $k/(k-h+1)$ at $k=8,h=8$ and at

\end{pdexercise}

\section{Related work and prior art}\label{sec:related}
% ═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

This chapter sits at the confluence of five external literatures that rarely cite one
another, plus one internal body of work --- the accompanying formal papers --- whose
results several of this chapter's claims are popularizations of. We survey them in one
place; each was introduced in context above.

\paragraph{Legibility and the limits of central schemes.}
The organizing lens is \textbf{Scott}'s \emph{Seeing Like a State}~\pdcite{scott1998}:
states make populations governable by imposing legibility, and high-modernist
\emph{over}-legibility recurrently fails because it destroys the local, hard-to-codify
\emph{m\^etis} the simplified order depended on; \emph{Two Cheers for
Anarchism}~\pdcite{scott2012} is its constructive sequel. The same point arrives from
economics in \textbf{Hayek}'s \emph{The Use of Knowledge in Society}~\pdcite{hayek1945}:
the decisive knowledge is dispersed and not aggregable by a central planner.
\textbf{Rao}~\pdcite{rao2010} ported ``legibility'' into a standard lens on systems that
flatten reality to a single purpose. Our contribution is to make Scott's warning a
\emph{buildable} rule (digest-with-zoom, Invariant~\ref{def:zoom}) and to give it an
information-theoretic floor (Thm.~\ref{thm:lowerbound}).

\paragraph{Authority, consent, and the social contract.}
The authority argument is Hobbesian~\pdcite{hobbes1651,sep-hobbes}, amended by the
liberal consent tradition --- \textbf{Locke}~\pdcite{locke1689},
\textbf{Hume}~\pdcite{hume1748}, \textbf{Pateman}~\pdcite{pateman1979} --- and by
\textbf{Hirschman}'s exit/voice framework~\pdcite{hirschman1970}. Strikingly, the
state-of-nature-to-sovereign arc has been observed as an \emph{emergent} dynamic of
LLM populations (\textbf{Dai et al.}~\pdcite{dai2024}, building on \textbf{Park et
al.}~\pdcite{park2023}). We turn the metaphor into a mechanism: an express, scoped,
revocable consent grant (Invariant~\ref{def:consent}) with an inalienable override
(Invariant~\ref{prop:override}), and a legible-sovereign rule
(Invariant~\ref{prop:legible-sovereign}) that inverts Hobbes' opaque sovereign.

\paragraph{Human factors, automation, and signal detection.}
The operator model draws on the automation literature:
\textbf{Bainbridge}'s ironies of automation~\pdcite{bainbridge1983},
\textbf{Lee \& See} on trust calibration~\pdcite{leesee2004},
\textbf{Endsley} on situation awareness and the out-of-the-loop
problem~\pdcite{endsley1995,endsleykiris1995}, the vigilance-decrement results of
\textbf{Mackworth}~\pdcite{mackworth1948} and \textbf{Warm et al.}~\pdcite{warm2008},
\textbf{Wickens}' multiple-resource theory~\pdcite{wickens2002},
\textbf{Treisman \& Gelade}'s feature-integration theory~\pdcite{treisman1980}, and
\textbf{Miller}'s capacity limit~\pdcite{miller1956}.

\noindent\textbf{Alarm decisions.} The forced-zoom decision is
modeled with \textbf{Green \& Swets}' Signal Detection Theory~\pdcite{greenswets1966}
(Invariant~\ref{def:sdt}); the alarm discipline follows process-control
practice~\pdcite{isa182} and the clinical alarm-fatigue evidence~\pdcite{cvach2012}.

\noindent\textbf{Limits of the objective.}
\textbf{Campbell}~\pdcite{campbell1979} and \textbf{Strathern}~\pdcite{strathern1997}
(Goodhart's law) bound any metered governor. To our knowledge the operator-attention
objective spined explicitly on SDT miss-cost (Eq.~\ref{eq:sdt}) is new to agent
supervision.

\paragraph{Information foraging and the cost of reading.}
Read-poverty (Hypothesis~\ref{def:readpoverty}) is the agent-coordination instance of
\textbf{Pirolli \& Card}'s \emph{information foraging theory}~\pdcite{pirolli1999} ---
information-seekers follow ``information scent'' and abandon a patch when its expected
yield falls below the cost of foraging --- and of the long-context degradation
results that make reading expensive even for machines (\textbf{Liu et
al.}~\pdcite{liu2024}; \textbf{Anthropic}~\pdcite{anthropic2025}). The remedy is the
classical database remedy --- an index --- recast as a directory over agents.

\paragraph{Agent discovery, naming, and trust.}
The directory substrate has deep prior art in \textbf{FIPA}'s Directory Facilitator
and Agent Management System~\pdcite{fipa00023}, re-invented for LLM agents as the Agent
Name Service (\textbf{Ren et al.}~\pdcite{ren2025}). For the trust layer that sits above
discovery, the canonical reputation primitive is \textbf{EigenTrust}'s pre-trusted-set
construction~\pdcite{kamvar2003}, with Sybil resistance per
\textbf{Douceur}~\pdcite{douceur2002} and commons governance per
\textbf{Ostrom}~\pdcite{ostrom1990} --- machinery the wedge defers but must not
contradict. Our specific result here is the \emph{split ranker}
(Thm.~\ref{thm:split-ranker}): discovery and operator-attention ranking are provably
distinct objectives, not one ranker with swapped weights.

\paragraph{The formal siblings, and what they already prove.}
The nearest prior art for several of this chapter's results is not external at all: it
is the accompanying formal program, and pretending otherwise would be exactly the
kind of quiet re-derivation this chapter argues against. \emph{The Price of a
Summary}~\pdcite{paper1} proves the legibility floor of Thm.~\ref{thm:lowerbound} as its
Theorem~1 in the same $N,k,m$ notation, and puts it through a pre-registered
falsification sweep this chapter only sketches; its comonotone characterization is the
general result of which both Thm.~\ref{thm:split-ranker} and
Thm.~\ref{thm:split-digest} are instances, and it prices the split ($\approx2.13\times$
the single-reader floor, super-additive rather than merely doubled) where this chapter
only forbids sharing; its regret-head section is the formal version of the
decision-theoretic derivation in \S\ref{sec:relevance}; and its zoom theorem
($2k\lceil\log_2(F/k)\rceil+4k$ adaptive opens) is the algorithm that makes
digest-with-zoom affordable at scale. \emph{What Needs an Authority}~\pdcite{paper6}
supplies Thm.~\ref{thm:specialization}'s Erlang-C boundary and, as
\S\ref{sec:sole-roles} records, the sweep that falsifies the naive response-time
threshold. The relationship in both cases is the same: this chapter popularizes, the
formal papers prove and test.

\section{The time axis and the bridge to the economy}\label{sec:bridge}
% ═════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

\subsection{Legibility over time, not just the present}\label{sec:timeaxis}

It is tempting to treat legibility as a snapshot --- what is the swarm doing
\emph{now}. But most operator decisions are forensic: \emph{why} did this land? The
time axis is a distinct legibility mode --- a replay scrubber over history,
commit-anchored provenance on every annotation, and an append-only event log that
answers ``how did we get here?'' (Scott's cadastral map is static; a swarm needs a
legible \emph{history}.) Together with the projection mode
(\S\ref{sec:sa}), legibility spans all three tenses: the forensic past (replay), the
inspectable present (digest-with-zoom), and the projected future (freeze-probe SA).

\subsection{What this layer hands upward (the through-line)}\label{sec:handoff}

The wedge is the single-player product. But the same legibility discipline applied to
\emph{continuity} is the foundation of everything above it. The through-line
the accompanying chapters depend on is one sentence:

\pdthesis{The economy rests on three continuity organs --- memory, checkpoint
(today, notes rather than true execution state), and a witnessed-outcome ledger
(today, commitments and oracle closure rather than neutral graded outcomes) ---
and reputation keys on the richer third organ; the checkpoint organ is the weakest continuity link, and a
real execution-state checkpoint --- ``resurrection with teeth'' --- is the literal
foundation of any agent market.}

Concretely, the legibility layer provides upward:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2em]
\item \textbf{Legible continuity} --- resurrection-with-memory, episodic memory, and
the briefing together make an agent's continuity legible. This is the precondition for
the through-line \emph{memory $+$ checkpoint $\to$ continuity $\to$ a person not a
spawn $\to$ reputation $\to$ a tradeable asset $\to$ the market}. No legible
continuity, no reputation, no tradeable agent. \emph{The score is cheap; the substrate
it scores over --- witnessed outcomes on a non-forgeable identity --- is the gate.}
That non-forgeable identity is a \BuiltWeak{} single-operator primitive in the
wedge: daemon-minted \texttt{actor-souls} and a bounded newcomer pool ship, while universal
write-boundary enforcement does not. The
cross-operator attestation a real market needs is an additional, as-yet-unbuilt
mechanism the economy paper must declare rather than assume.
\item \textbf{The completion ledger} --- in the target design, every verified
completion (and every loud-failed one) becomes an outcome event. Today durable
commitments, oracle-bound closure, and overdue detection form a partial witness
substrate; neutral grading and reputation updates do not ship. This layer is the \emph{outcome witness}; the
reputation layer is the \emph{scorer}. The split-ranker substrate
(Thm.~\ref{thm:split-ranker}) and the costly-escalation signal (\S\ref{sec:gates})
are the candidate-generation and trust-signal scaffolding the reputation head consumes.
\item \textbf{The consent grant as a priceable object} --- a hosted-trust offering is
the transfer of a consent grant (Invariant~\ref{def:consent}) to a third party, which the
economy paper prices.
\item \textbf{Operator-attention economics} --- the denominator a market cannot
escape. The SDT-spined objective (\S\ref{sec:operator}) supplies the
operator-attention cost of supervising a rented fleet.
\end{itemize}

The Leviathan that makes your swarm legible to you, without lying to you with a pretty
map, is the product. Everything above it --- federation, the market --- is a second
Leviathan made of the first.

\section{Implementation and status}\label{app:honest}

The body of this chapter argues mechanisms on their merits and does not depend on any of
them being built. This appendix --- and \emph{only} this appendix --- records the
concrete engineering status of each mechanism in the reference implementation, named
\emph{Port Daddy}. We use four honest labels, with one rule for the whole table:
confusing what is \emph{implemented} with what is merely \emph{specified} is itself
the failure the paper argues against, so each row carries its evidence.

\smallskip
\noindent\Built\ = code exists in the reference implementation today; \BuiltWeak\ =
code exists but is partial, unwired, or honest about a known gap; \Designed\ = a
concrete, accepted design with no merged implementation; \Vision\ = a mechanism argued
in this chapter with no design document yet.

\smallskip
\noindent A second key, in the same grammar, labels \emph{claims} rather than code.
Every formal result in this chapter carries exactly one of these markers directly under
it, and the paper carries no free-text status notes outside them:
\Verified\ = independently re-derived and checked numerically, or subjected to a
pre-registered falsification sweep, outside this document; \Proved\ = argued here with
a proof or proof sketch and not independently checked; \Unproved\ = stated as a design
target whose proof is not carried in this chapter. The two keys are orthogonal on
purpose --- a \Verified\ result about a \Vision\ mechanism is a perfectly ordinary
state of affairs, and conflating them is the failure this appendix exists to prevent.

Three points about the reference implementation are important enough to restate:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.5em]
\item \textbf{The anomaly monitor is detect-and-compensate, not prevention}
(Invariant~\ref{prop:regiment}). The monitor at \texttt{lib/arbiter.ts:328} is a
post-commit log subscriber; by Schneider~\pdcite{schneider2000} it enforces no safety

states only.
\item \textbf{The unified ranker is two rankers} (Thm.~\ref{thm:split-ranker}). The
discovery router maximizes \emph{fit}; the attention queue maximizes
\emph{regret-if-ignored}. They share candidate generation and decay, not an objective.
\item \textbf{Every read-surface decays} (Invariant~\ref{prop:decay}). Briefing,
resurrection, discovery, and attestation projections all carry recency-decay; the
dead-claim case (\S\ref{sec:staleness}) is the worked failure this closes.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Notation and the nomenclature key}\label{app:notation}

Two distinctions in this chapter are easy to collapse and essential to keep separate.

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
\item \textbf{Capability vs.\ permission.} \emph{Capability} is \emph{ability} --- what
the execution sandbox physically makes possible. \emph{Permission} is \emph{normative
status} --- what the policy layer allows. They must never be collapsed: ``able but
forbidden'' (a dangerous escape-hatch flag that exists in the tooling but must not be
used) has to stay expressible, or the system cannot reason about a capability it
possesses but is not permitted to exercise.
\item \textbf{Two distinct delegation objects.} The \emph{authorization chain} is a
cryptographic, hop-bound, attenuation-monotonic delegation chain (a security object,
developed in \pdchapref{anchor}{The Anchor Protocol}). The \emph{coordination lineage} is the
task graph over which loop-detection runs (a liveness object, in this chapter's
protocol layer), and it is the same object \pdchapref{swk}{The Single-Writer Kernel}
names under that name --- one object, one name, which is the point of this entry.
They are different artifacts with different invariants; ``delegation
chain'' unqualified conflates a security claim with a coordination claim and must be
avoided.
\item \textbf{Physically unreachable} is reserved for constraint-enforced or
boot-gate-enforced states only (Invariant~\ref{prop:regiment}); a detect-and-compensate
monitor never earns the phrase.
\end{itemize}
