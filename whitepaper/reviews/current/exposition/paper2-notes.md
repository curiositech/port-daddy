# Exposition + Figure Review: Regimented or Enforced — The Controllability Boundary for Agent Governance

`whitepaper/research/tex/paper2.tex`, 441 source lines (long lines; roughly 12–14 rendered pages), Paper 2 of the Harbor program. It is the program's *characterization* paper: it identifies "what an agent runtime can prevent" with Ramadge–Wonham controllability with respect to an uncontrollable alphabet of model-internal events, proves the iff, runs a product-automaton checker over a nine-policy governance corpus, and extracts the design rule ("gate the channel, never the token") that Paper 4's Sealed Harbor and Paper 3's slashing machinery both depend on. Structurally it is the corpus's *hinge*: Papers 3 and 4 are downstream consumers, and `whitepaper/source/single-writer-kernel.tex` §"The policy monitor is a detector, not a regimenter" is the product-side statement of the same boundary. Exposition-wise it is in good shape — `check_style.py` passes 7/7, all seven moves are present and in order, both rails are honored, the "Now you try" fade at §5 is genuinely well made, and §8 (partial observation) is a model of honest future-work framing. The defects below are concentrated in three places: an event-count inconsistency that runs through the model section, both figures, the abstract and the synthesis section; a redundancy seam between the new §2 ("The vocabulary, defined") and §3 ("The model"); and several wording overclaims that the paper's own boundary box already contradicts.

## Part A — Text/exposition changes

### A1. The title's dichotomy is contradicted by the abstract's second sentence
- **Location** — Title (l.7) and abstract (l.11–13); recurs at §3 l.132–133.
- **Issue** — Terminology collision at the highest-visibility point in the paper. The title borrows the normative-MAS term of art the paper itself quotes at l.376 ("regimentation makes violation impossible by design, **enforcement** detects violations and responds with sanctions"), where *enforcement* is one of two species. The abstract then uses *enforce* as the genus covering both. A reader who reads the title and then the first two sentences cannot tell whether "enforced" is the umbrella or the weak half — and the paper never resolves it, using the umbrella sense throughout while the title promises the narrow one.
- **Current text** (l.11–14) — "An agent hypervisor --- a runtime that mediates an LLM agent's file writes, network egress, tool executions, pushes, and spawns --- is asked to \emph{enforce} governance policies. Some it can enforce by prevention: the forbidden thing never happens. Others it can only enforce by detection and compensation: the thing happens, and the runtime notices afterward."
- **Proposed rewrite** — "An agent hypervisor --- a runtime that mediates an LLM agent's file writes, network egress, tool executions, pushes, and spawns --- is asked to make governance policies bind. Two mechanisms are on offer, and this paper's title names them in the sense normative multi-agent systems have used for twenty years: \emph{regimentation}, where the forbidden thing never happens, and \emph{enforcement}, where it happens, the runtime notices afterward, and something is done about it. We show the boundary between the two is not an engineering contingency but a theorem:"
  Then at l.132–133 change "a legitimate but categorically weaker enforcement mode" to "\emph{enforcement} in the narrow sense above --- legitimate, and categorically weaker."
- **Priority** — high.

### A2. The event counts disagree across five places
- **Location** — §1 l.53–54; §3 l.116–119; §7 l.296; abstract l.21–22; `fig-r5-relation.tex` (Σ_c "(5 events)", Σ_u "(3 events)", connector "5 refusable vs.\ 3 that never arrive").
- **Issue** — A factual-presentation inconsistency that a referee will find in the first pass. §3 defines |Σ_c| = 6 (adds `api_call`) and |Σ_u| = 4 (adds `hidden_activation`). §1's parenthetical lists 5 and 3. Figure 1 *asserts* 5 and 3 in bold, twice. §7's hand-check depends on 4: "tests each of the four uncontrollable events in each: all eight (state, event) pairs" — the number 8 is only right if |Σ_u| = 4, which contradicts Figure 1 on the same page-ish. The abstract lists four detect-only items ("token emission, context reads, internal plans, confident falsehoods") where the table has three rows, because "confident falsehood" is a gloss on `internal_plan`, not a fourth event.
- **Current text** (§7, l.296) — "the four uncontrollable events in each: all eight (state, event) pairs are enabled by the spec"
- **Proposed rewrite** — Fix at the source: make §3 canonical at 6/4, then (a) change §1 l.53–54 to "$\Sigma_c$ (fs\_write, net\_egress, exec\_tool, git\_push, spawn\_child, api\_call) is entry; $\Sigma_u$ (model\_emit\_token, in\_context\_read, internal\_plan, hidden\_activation) is thought"; (b) in `fig-r5-relation.tex` change the two headers to "$\Sigma_c$ --- controllable (6 events)" / "$\Sigma_u$ --- uncontrollable (4 events)", the connector label to "6 refusable vs.\ 4 that never arrive", and add `api_call` / `hidden_activation` to the two event lists; (c) in the abstract change "token emission, context reads, internal plans, confident falsehoods: detect-only forever" to "token emission, context reads, internal plans (the confident falsehood lives here): detect-only forever". §7 then needs no change.
- **Priority** — high.

### A3. "Widening $\Sigma_c$ … is the \emph{only} way" is a wording overclaim the theorem does not support
- **Location** — §6, l.283–284.
- **Issue** — Unhedged superlative on the paper's most quotable line. The controllability criterion is $\overline{K}\Sigma_u \cap \overline{L} \subseteq \overline{K}$; it has *two* free parameters on the left, and shrinking $\overline{L}$ (making the event physically impossible rather than merely refusable — no context to read, no network interface in the namespace) satisfies it just as well as moving the event into $\Sigma_c$. The paper elsewhere relies on exactly this lever (the "sealed room" of §6 is a plant restriction, not only an alphabet widening), so the sentence contradicts its own neighbor.
- **Current text** — "Widening $\Sigma_c$ (owning more channels) is the \emph{only} way to grow the regimentable set; no policy-language cleverness does."
- **Proposed rewrite** — "There are exactly two ways to grow the regimentable set, and both are architectural: widen $\Sigma_c$ (own more channels, so more events can be refused) or narrow $L$ (remove the physical possibility of the event, so there is nothing to refuse). No policy-language cleverness does either --- the criterion mentions only $\overline{K}$, $\Sigma_u$, and $\overline{L}$, and a richer rule syntax changes none of the three."
- **Priority** — high.

### A4. Boundary items (iii) and (vi) contradict each other as worded
- **Location** — §11, l.402–406 (item iii) and l.408–410 (item vi).
- **Issue** — Item (iii) says a runtime that gates model steps "changes $\Sigma_u$ and re-grades the table." Item (vi) says "no widening of $\Sigma_c$ ever makes a confident lie preventable." Taken literally, (iii) licenses moving `model_emit_token` into $\Sigma_c$, at which point "never emit a false token" *is* a controllable prohibition and (vi) is false. The real obstruction in (vi) is not controllability at all — it is that "confident falsehood" is not a decidable predicate over the event alphabet, which is Ray's axis (§10 l.383–387), not this paper's. Saying so both fixes the contradiction and strengthens the item.
- **Current text** (vi) — "Prevention governs \emph{effects}, never \emph{semantics}: no widening of $\Sigma_c$ ever makes a confident lie preventable, because the lie is constituted, not merely caused, by uncontrollable events."
- **Proposed rewrite** — "(vi) Prevention governs \emph{effects}, never \emph{semantics}, and this is a \emph{second} obstruction stacked on the first. Even a runtime that pulls \texttt{model\_emit\_token} into $\Sigma_c$ (item iii) --- token-level filtering with the model inside the boundary --- gains nothing against a confident lie, because the gate would then need to decide the predicate ``this token continues a falsehood,'' which is not decidable over the event alphabet at all. Controllability says the gate can \emph{refuse}; it never says the gate can \emph{know}. That second boundary is Ray's~\cite{ray26}, and it is where a semantic rule dies even after ours is satisfied."
- **Priority** — high.

- **Current text** (the clearest instance, l.91–92) — "Uncontrollable does not mean invisible --- the daemon logs tokens after emission --- it means unrefusable before the fact." (Identical in content to l.61–62: "Uncontrollable means \emph{unrefusable before the fact}, not unobservable after it.")

### A9. §4's "strictly stronger than Schneider's" reads as a boast, then is retracted in the same sentence
- **Location** — §4, l.139–144.
- **Issue** — Sequencing. The paragraph establishes that Schneider proved only one direction, then asserts "Our theorem is at that point strictly stronger than Schneider's," then immediately concedes "and the extra strength is not ours either." The reader parses claim → retraction in one breath and loses the (correct, interesting) point, which is that the extra strength is *bought by a regularity assumption*, not earned.
- **Current text** — "Our theorem is at that point strictly stronger than Schneider's, and the extra strength is not ours either: restricting to regular $K$ and $L$ silently supplies the decidability and nonemptiness side conditions that Basin et al.~\cite{bjkz13} state separately (\S\ref{sec:related})."
- **Proposed rewrite** — "In that degenerate case our statement therefore says more than Schneider's --- we get the converse he explicitly denies. That is not a sharper argument on our part; it is a purchase. Restricting to regular $K$ and $L$ silently supplies the decidability and nonemptiness side conditions that Basin et al.~\cite{bjkz13} state separately (\S\ref{sec:related}), and it is those conditions, not our framing, that close the converse. Outside the regular fragment, Schneider's asymmetry returns."
- **Priority** — medium.

## Part D — Cross-reference notes

`whitepaper/source/single-writer-kernel.tex` (1,879 lines, "A Local Transactional Reference Monitor for Agent Swarms") **is** the right sibling — strongly related, not a title coincidence. It contains §"The policy monitor is a detector, not a regimenter" (l.871), `\begin{theorem}` Synchronous State Decidability (l.932), and — decisively — `\begin{remark}[Scope: the general boundary is controllability]` at l.941–942, which states paper2's entire thesis in one paragraph, including the phrase "Gate the channel, never the token" verbatim. Four notes:

1. **The "folk theorem" attribution is wrong, and it undersells a sibling.** paper2 §6 l.278–280 says: "Product reviews of agent governance converged on the slogan ``a rule is enforceable iff it is a pure deterministic function of committed local DB state.''" That is not a slogan from product reviews — it is `single-writer-kernel.tex`'s **Theorem** (l.932: "A safety invariant $\phi$ is \textbf{regimentable} … if and only if $\phi(S,\Delta)$ is a pure, deterministic boolean predicate decidable solely over the daemon's local, synchronous, committed database state $S$ and the incoming transaction payload $\Delta$"), a numbered result with an open-problem tag (OP-2, l.1030). paper2 should cite it as such: "The single-writer kernel's Synchronous State Decidability theorem~\cite{swk} is our theorem's special case $\Sigma_c=\{$DB commits$\}$" — which is both more accurate and a stronger claim (paper2 *resolves* a sibling's named open problem, and currently doesn't say so).

2. **Terminology drift on the title's own words, and it runs the wrong way.** SWK's glossary (l.375) fixes the binary crisply: "*regimented* = rejected pre-commit, truly unreachable … *enforced* = detected post-commit, halted or compensated within a bounded window." That is exactly paper2's title. But paper2's body uses "enforce" as the *umbrella* (see A1) and names the weak mode "detect-and-compensate" instead. SWK also uses "detect-and-compensate" (l.1066, l.1164, l.1717) as a synonym for its "enforced". Net effect: across the two documents, *enforced* and *detect-and-compensate* denote the same thing in SWK and different things in paper2. Fixing A1 makes the pair consistent and lets paper2 reuse SWK's glossary line verbatim.

3. **Σ_u membership differs.** SWK's remark (l.942) gives Σ_u = {token emission, in-context reads, plan formation} — three events, matching paper2's §1 and Figure 1 — while paper2 §3 has four (adds `hidden_activation`) and Σ_c six (adds `api_call`). This is the same inconsistency as A2, and it now spans two documents. Whichever way A2 is resolved, SWK's remark needs the same edit; the three/four choice should be made once, in paper2 §3, and propagated.

4. **A cross-document claim to keep aligned.** SWK l.1316 §"The enforcement gate proves; it does not yet compel" and l.1304 §"Advisory claims are not sandboxing" are the product-side statement of paper2's boundary item (ii) (unenumerated side channels demote Σ_c events). If C3 is built, it is reusable in SWK's threat-model section at l.1274 with no changes.

## Summary

1. **One counting inconsistency runs through five places** (§1, §3, §7, the abstract, and both bold headers of Figure 1): Σ_c/Σ_u are 6/4 in the model section and in §7's "all eight (state, event) pairs", but 5/3 in the scene, in the abstract's list, and asserted twice in Figure 1's own labels. A referee finds this on page one. Fix §3 as canonical and propagate (A2, B1).

2. **Three wording overclaims are pre-contradicted by the paper's own boundary box** — "Widening $\Sigma_c$ … is the \emph{only} way" (l.283, ignores narrowing $L$); boundary (vi) vs boundary (iii) (a widened Σ_c *does* re-grade, so (vi) needs the decidability argument, not the controllability one); and "machine-checked policy table" in the express lane against boundary (v)'s "not a mechanized proof". Each is a two-line fix and each is on a sentence a reviewer will quote (A3, A4, A8).

3. **The new §2 "The vocabulary, defined" is correctly placed and should stay — its problem is duplication, not front-loading.** It sits after the scene, the analogy and Figure 1, and its opening move is the best prose in the paper. But it re-states plant, prefix-closure/safety, the supervisor's type, and "regiments" that §3 then states again thirty lines later, and "uncontrollable ≠ invisible" appears three times in thirty-two lines. Two items *are* genuinely premature — the supremal controllable sublanguage (used 200 lines later) and maximal permissiveness (which asserts a property of a theorem not yet stated, and closes the section on its weakest beat). Merge §2 and §3, delete the third redundancy, move maximal permissiveness to l.191 where it is used. ~20 lines recovered (A6, A7).

4. **Related Work closes on "we have not obtained the closest prior art"** — Dastani et al.'s *Norm Enforcement as Supervisory Control* (PRIMA 2017), named in prose with no bibitem, in the paper's final positioning paragraph. The honesty must stay; the placement must not. Move it into the boundary box as item (vii). Relatedly, §10's 60 lines currently sit *between* Move 6 and the honest boundary, which is why §11 has no regime figure attached to it (A5, A12).

5. **Both figures duplicate each other and one duplicates an unnumbered in-text table.** Figure 1's row 3 and Figure 2's right panel tell the same read→taint→gate story; Figure 2's left panel and the l.204–211 table carry the same nine verdicts in two different row orders, with the compound row placed to defeat the punchline in one of them. Cutting Figure 1's row 3 removes the spoiler, removes the duplication, and should let the `\resizebox{!}{0.72\textheight}`/`[p]` float hack go away. Both captions name the parts before stating the finding, and neither in-text table has a caption, number, or label at all (B1–B4). The highest-value new figure is a controllability × observability 2×2 replacing Figure 2's left panel: it makes Figure 2 an actual regime diagram with axes, kills the table duplication, and finally gives §9 and boundary items (iii)/(iv) a picture (C1).
