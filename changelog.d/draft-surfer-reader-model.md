- **`ux-friction-analyzer` and `product-appeal-analyzer` now audit wireframes, PDFs and LaTeX books, not just web flows.** A random-surfer reader model (`skills/ux-friction-analyzer/scripts/surfer_model.mjs`) generalises PageRank's surfer to reading — continue, regress, teleport, abandon — over an absorbing Markov chain, and reports attention mass, per-node reach probability, regression churn, the node where the median arrival quits, and time-to-first-insight via a Doob *h*-transform of the chain conditioned on reaching the payoff. Friction and appeal are now two readouts of that one chain: friction is the terms that raise the abandon hazard, appeal is the terms that lower it. `skills/ux-friction-analyzer/scripts/latex_skeleton.mjs` builds most of a book's graph from LaTeX source, including the author's own cross-reference graph, so definition distance and forward references fall out before anyone reads a word. A book's printed Reader's Map is encoded as `readerPaths` and each route walked per persona, which catches a route that skips the chapter defining a concept that route later needs — a defect the linear reading order hides completely. The appeal skill gains a fourth Desirability Triangle vertex, Return on Effort, because a monograph's price is forty hours rather than thirty seconds. Model constants are documented as priors rather than measurements, with a calibration section and an explicit list of where the model is knowingly wrong.
