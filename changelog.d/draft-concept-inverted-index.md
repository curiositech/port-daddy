- **The Book now has an inverted concept index, with metaphor tracking.** `docs/harbor-research/concept-index.json` is generated from the eight chapter sources `whitepaper/textbook.json` names — 274 concepts, 12 576 use sites — and answers, per concept: where it is defined (and whether that was parsed from a `definition` environment or judged from a definitional cue), every mention, the passages that explain rather than merely use it, where the Book says it is unresolved, its figures and tables by label, its `\pdcite` keys, which exercises exercise it and whether their solutions name it, and its **first use in prose** — the anchor a `\pdgloss` belongs on. It also records what no existing tool touched: each concept's figurative **vehicle** and source **domain**, and which *other* domains it collides with in the same paragraph and the same section, with a judged verdict on whether the metaphor is ever cashed out into a literal sentence nearby. §1.3 of chapter 1 is the specimen: four domains (anatomy, medicine, architecture, moral), never cashed out in the prose, and its one literal sentence — "All seven are disciplines over one SQLite/WAL file" — sits in `tab:swk-seven-organs`' caption. `build_concept_index.py --check` regenerates and fails on drift, advisory in `library-checks.yml` for now; `--query <term>` prints one entry. Passage-level facts live once in top-level tables and every entry's `library_index_ids` is derived from `library-index.json` by shared `\label`, so neither index can drift from the other.
