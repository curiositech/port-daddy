# Historical chemical example and optional SGT prompts

## Chemical-incident analysis excerpt

Stanton (2006) Figure 6 (p.65), Figure 7 (p.66), and Table 10 (p.67) use a chemical-incident analysis to illustrate a multi-agency HTA. This is a **historical analysis excerpt**, never guidance for a real incident.

- **Plan 0 in Figure 6 (p.65) and Figure 7 (p.66):** wait until 1, then do 2, then 3. If hazard, do 4, then 5, then exit; otherwise exit. The source Figure 5 placeholder “etc…” does not authorize omitting 5 from this plan.
- **Figure 7, Plan 2:** 2.1 may be done at any time **if appropriate**; the separate ordered chain is 2.2 followed by 2.3, then exit. Do not make 2.1 a required predecessor of that chain. The Hospital report in the abbreviated Table 10 is about the **same incident**.
- **Role/action labels:** Figure 7 identifies 3 as **Police Control—decide incident nature** and 4 as **Fire Control—cleanup**. The plan includes 5, but its displayed label says only **“etc…”**; it must not be expanded into a Police Control resolution task.
- **Table 10:** it is expressly abridged because re-description is omitted. Keep its own 1 → 2; hazard → 3 → 4 → exit rendering and do not invent a harmonized numbering across figure and table.

**Hand check.** Retain these source distinctions, label any local use as a constructed adaptation, and reject any analysis that converts labels or branches into a current emergency procedure.

## Optional Sub-Goal Templates

Stanton Table 5 reports Ormerod and Shepherd’s SGTs, developed as a novice-support proposal in process-control work:

| Family | Terms |
|---|---|
| Act | activate; adjust; deactivate |
| Exchange | enter; extract |
| Navigate | locate; move; explore |
| Monitor | detect; anticipate; transition |

Use a template only to ask, “is a candidate subgoal missing?” It does not prove that the candidate belongs in the hierarchy. A positive check names the supporting local evidence; a negative check records that no source-supported contribution was found and leaves the template unused.

**Further source.** Ormerod, T. C., & Shepherd, A. (2004), [Using task analysis for information requirements specification: the SGT method](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/250749406_Using_task_analysis_for_information_requirements_specification_The_SGT_method). This skill relies on Stanton’s Table 5 summary; it does not claim a fresh full-body reading of the 2004 paper.
