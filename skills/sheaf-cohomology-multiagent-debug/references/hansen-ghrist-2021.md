# Hansen and Ghrist (2021): opinion dynamics on discourse sheaves

## Access and attribution

Hansen and Ghrist, [*Opinion Dynamics on Discourse Sheaves*](https://arxiv.org/abs/2005.12798), was consulted through its author-hosted/preprint material in the B04 research pass. The source studies a specified discourse-sheaf opinion model and sheaf-Laplacian dynamics. It does not validate a generic monitor, identify misinformation, authorize intervention, or establish a universal relation between map norm and convergence speed.

## Construction retained with its hypotheses

Each vertex has a finite-dimensional opinion stalk and each edge has a comparison stalk. Endpoint restriction maps place vertex values into the edge stalk. With Euclidean inner products, the sheaf Laplacian is assembled from the coboundary and its adjoint; the flow `xdot=-Lx` is a model-specific continuous-time update. Its stationary space is the kernel of the specified Laplacian/global-section condition.

Restriction maps need not be scalar weights; they can have different domain/codomain dimensions. Their dimensions, compositions, metrics, and graph must be defined before matrix blocks or a spectrum have meaning.

## Scope of H0, H1, and spectrum

`H0` and `H1` are algebraic properties of the declared sheaf/cochain complex. A nonzero cohomology group indicates a space of classes in that model, not a real-world incident or inevitable deadlock. A spectral statement needs the actual operator, metric, connectedness/nullspace hypotheses, and update rule; do not infer that increasing a map norm makes any application converge faster or safer.

## Constructed check

On a constant scalar connected path, every edge cochain is a gradient. On an oriented scalar triangle, `g=(1,1,1)` is not. These are incidence-matrix facts, not a claim about agents or a platform’s belief traces.
