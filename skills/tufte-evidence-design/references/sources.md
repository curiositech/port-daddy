   Graphical Elements," "Data Density and Small Multiples," "Aesthetics and

## Doctrines (secondary confirmation)

- **Data-ink ratio / chartjunk**: definitions ("data-ink is the non-erasable core
  ... arranged in response to variation in the numbers represented"; chartjunk is
  "non-data-ink or redundant data-ink") confirmed via
  https://infovis-wiki.net/wiki/Data-Ink_Ratio and
  https://jtr13.github.io/cc19/tuftes-principles-of-data-ink.html. `[verified]`
- **Lie Factor**: formula (size of effect shown ÷ size of effect in data), the
  0.95–1.05 honesty band, and the classic Fuel Economy Standards graphic (53%
  actual increase shown as a 783% visual increase, Lie Factor 14.8) confirmed via
  https://infovis-wiki.net/wiki/Lie_Factor and
  https://chartbuddy.io/blog/tuftes-principles-for-graphical-integrity. `[verified]`
- **Small multiples**: "Small multiples, whether tabular or pictorial, move to the
  heart of visual reasoning — to see, distinguish, choose" — quoted across sources
  citing Beautiful Evidence/Envisioning Information. `[verified snippet, unverified
  exact page]`
- **Sparklines**: "datawords: data-intense, design-simple, word-sized graphics"
  confirmed via https://www.edwardtufte.com/notebook/sparkline-theory-and-practice-edward-tufte/
  (search snippet). `[verified]`
- **Escaping flatland, micro/macro readings, layering and separation**: confirmed
  via the Envisioning Information chapter-1 PDF excerpt above and
  https://infovis-wiki.net/wiki/Tufte,_E.R.:_Envisioning_Information,_Graphics_Press,_1990.
  `[verified]`
- **Smallest effective difference**: "the Occam's razor of information design ...
  make every visual distinction as small as possible, but still clearly visible"
  — Beautiful Evidence, confirmed via search snippet quoting the concept, e.g.
  https://medium.com/@pj_/the-smallest-effective-difference-90fc94d5ab0d.
  `[verified snippet]`
- **Fundamental principles of analytical design**: the six principles (show
  comparisons; show causality/mechanism/explanation/structure; show multivariate
  data; integrate words, numbers, images; document sources and characterization;
  content counts most — "analytical presentations ultimately stand or fall
  depending on the quality, relevance, and integrity of their content") — chapter
  title and the "Compared with what?" framing confirmed via
  https://jenniferjnewsome.medium.com/edward-tufte-6-fundamental-principles-of-analytic-design-82fcf09ec59a
  and https://simplexct.com/analytical-principles-compared-with-what. `[verified]`
- **Minard's Napoleon map**: "may well be the best statistical graphic ever
  produced" (VDQI) confirmed via
  https://historyofinformation.com/detail.php?id=2787 and
  https://www.edwardtufte.com/product/napoleons-march/. `[verified]`
- **John Snow's cholera map**: Tufte's treatment in Visual Explanations, including
  his noted limitation (the dot map implicitly assumes uniform population density,
  so an empty house reads as "no cases" rather than "no people") confirmed via
  search snippet of the Visual Explanations PDF excerpt above. `[verified]`
- **Challenger O-ring argument**: the 13-chart Morton Thiokol packet, the
  chronological (launch-date) ordering that buried the temperature correlation,
  and Tufte's redesign putting temperature on one axis, confirmed via
  https://onlineethics.virginia.edu/cases/representation-and-misrepresentation-tufte-and-morton-thiokol-engineers-challenger
  and https://motherboard.vice.com/en_us/article/kbb3qz/could-better-data-design-have-prevented-challenger.
  `[verified]`
- **The Cognitive Style of PowerPoint**: pamphlet (1st ed. 2003, 2nd ed. 2006,
  incorporating the Columbia shuttle analysis), argument that slideware
  "corrupt[s] statistical analysis" and that a dense prose handout beats a bulleted
  slide deck, confirmed via
  https://www.edwardtufte.com/notebook/new-edition-of-the-cognitive-style-of-powerpoint/
  and https://files.eric.ed.gov/fulltext/EJ1000695.pdf. `[verified]`
- **One-day course ("Presenting Data and Information")**: ran 1993–2020 live
  (roughly 328,000 attendees per course-page copy), now sold as a self-paced
  online course with the four/five books included; practical rules ("every
  meeting begins with a document and a study hall," "let the viewer see and hold
  all the data," "ask what the data means before choosing a format") confirmed
  via https://www.edwardtufte.com/online-course/ and course-attendee write-ups
  (https://girliemac.com/blog/2017/12/28/edward-tufte-data/,
  https://www.bravethewoods.com/blog/edward-tufte-one-day-course-what-i-learned).
  `[verified via multiple independent attendee accounts, secondary]`

## Critiques and limits

- **Stephen Few**: agrees with the basic chartjunk diagnosis but argues Tufte's
  data-ink minimization goes too far — some redundancy (gridlines, repeated
  labels) reduces the eye's and brain's work and speeds reading; "The Chartjunk
  Debate" (Perceptual Edge, 2011) confirmed via search snippet (direct fetch of
  perceptualedge.com blocked by this session's egress proxy).
  `[verified snippet, primary fetch blocked]`
- **Alberto Cairo**: *The Functional Art* (2012) and *The Truthful Art* (2016)
  propose five qualities — truthful, functional, beautiful, insightful,
  enlightening — as a superset that keeps Tufte's truthfulness demand but adds
  emotional engagement and audience awareness that pure minimalism can miss;
  Cairo is noted in secondary sources as willing to name specific factual errors
  in Tufte's books. `[verified snippet]`
- **Tamara Munzner**: *Visualization Analysis and Design* (2014) reframes
  Tufte's rules of thumb ("no unjustified 3D," "eyes beat memory") as one input
  among several, and notes Tufte's books are about static, printed presentation
  and do not cover the interactive, exploratory, and computational aspects that
  dominate modern visualization tooling. `[verified snippet]`
- **Claus Wilke**: *Fundamentals of Data Visualization* (2019) takes a
  non-dogmatic stance on data-ink minimization, treating it as one useful
  heuristic rather than a hard maximization target, and is more permissive about
  redundant encodings (direct labels plus a legend, gridlines at low contrast)
  when they aid the reader. `[verified snippet — this pass could not fetch the
  specific chapter/page]`
- **Robert Kosara**: eagereyes.org posts ("The Three Types of Chart Junk," 2012;
  "Chart junk considered useful after all") argue chartjunk is not one thing —
  decoration, obfuscation, and embellishment that aids recall are different
  categories with different costs — and cites Bateman et al.'s finding that
  embellished charts were recalled better weeks later with no loss of immediate
  comprehension accuracy. `[verified — search snippet of eagereyes.org posts]`
