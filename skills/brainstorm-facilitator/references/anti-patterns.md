# Anti-Patterns

Common ways brainstorming sessions fail. Each entry: symptom, why it fails, recovery move.

## Pre-session failures

### 1. The political theater session
**Symptom**: meeting called "brainstorming" but the decision is already made; the session exists to manufacture buy-in.
**Why it fails**: participants sense the theater. Real ideas don't surface. Trust in future brainstorming sessions decays.
**Recovery**: refuse or rename. "If the decision is made, let's do a different kind of session — kickoff, or input-collection, or pre-mortem on the chosen path." Real brainstorming requires real openness.

### 2. The vague question
**Symptom**: "Let's brainstorm ideas for the product."
**Why it fails**: too broad to generate against. Participants invent their own framings; outputs don't combine.
**Recovery**: spend 10 min framing the HMW before generation. If the room can't agree on a question, that disagreement is the real session — surface it.

### 3. The hidden constraint
**Symptom**: ideas come up during divergence, get rejected during convergence with "oh, legal won't allow that."
**Why it fails**: divergence work was wasted on infeasible options. Worse, the team learns to self-censor against guessed constraints.
**Recovery**: list real constraints (hard, soft, aspirational) BEFORE divergence. Frame: "Here's what's locked, here's what's flexible, here's what could move with effort."

## Divergence-phase failures

### 4. The "yes but" plague
**Symptom**: ideas immediately get evaluated. "Yeah, but X." "We tried that." "Won't work because Y."
**Why it fails**: every "yes but" trains the room to self-censor. The most diverse thinkers go silent first; the safest options rise.
**Recovery**: name the rule explicitly at session start. Intervene immediately and consistently when violated. Replace with "yes, and." If the violator is the most senior person, the rule is dying — address quickly.

### 5. Premature convergence
**Symptom**: 8 minutes in, someone says "I think option C is the answer" and the room agrees.
**Why it fails**: anchoring lock-in. The "answer" is the first one that survived non-rejection, not the best one.
**Recovery**: refuse to converge before the divergence target is hit. "Cool, let's note that as a candidate. Also — we have 22 more minutes of generation. What else?"

### 6. Production blocking
**Symptom**: in verbal sessions, only one person speaks at a time; quiet participants forget their ideas before getting a turn.
**Why it fails**: documented effect, robust across decades of research. Verbal groups produce ~50% the volume of nominal groups.
**Recovery**: switch to silent / brainwriting for divergence. Verbal mode for convergence discussion only.

### 7. Single technique exhaustion
**Symptom**: well runs dry after the first technique; facilitator gives up or pushes harder on the same approach.
**Why it fails**: the technique's blind spots become the session's blind spots. Pushing harder on the same approach yields more of the same.
**Recovery**: switch technique. Pair complementary techniques — systematic + chaotic, silent + verbal, framing + inversion. Each switch typically yields 30–50% more ideas.

### 8. The fixation spiral
**Symptom**: every new idea is a minor variant of an existing idea. Everyone feels productive; nothing is actually new.
**Why it fails**: cognitive fixation on a category. Variants of "make the button bigger" don't surface alternatives like "remove the button entirely" or "users discover this differently."
**Recovery**: random stimulus, reverse brainstorming, or a forced perspective shift. Sometimes a 5-minute walk works better than any technique.

### 9. Wild ideas killed on contact
**Symptom**: someone offers a "build a teleporter" idea; group laughs; it's discarded.
**Why it fails**: wild ideas are the substrate, not the deliverable. Their *edits* often unlock practical ones ("remove travel time" → async-first model).
**Recovery**: norm: "wild ideas are kept. We'll edit them down later, not delete them." Keep a "wild" column visible.

## Convergence-phase failures

### 10. Convergence by exhaustion
**Symptom**: discussion drags on; people get tired; the safest-sounding option wins because no one wants another meeting.
**Why it fails**: selection by fatigue, not judgment.
**Recovery**: timebox convergence. Use voting before discussion. End with a real shortlist even if the "best" one isn't yet obvious.

### 11. The HiPPO closes the loop
**Symptom**: senior person says "I think we should go with B," everyone agrees, session ends.
**Why it fails**: the senior person's opinion is signal, but it's also noise (their bias, blind spots). When their opinion ends discussion, the room loses the value of the divergence work.
**Recovery**: independent voting *before* the senior person speaks. Senior person speaks last. If senior person disagrees with the vote, they must articulate why and the group decides whether to override.

### 12. No criteria, no consistency
**Symptom**: convergence selects by gut. Different people are evaluating against different (unspoken) criteria.
**Why it fails**: outputs are not reproducible; can't explain decisions later; loses the rejected-but-interesting list.
**Recovery**: agree explicit criteria before scoring. Default: NUF (Novel/Useful/Feasible) or impact-vs-effort. Custom for domain.

### 13. Discussion before voting
**Symptom**: facilitator opens convergence with "what does everyone think?"
**Why it fails**: first speaker anchors all subsequent opinions. Discussion converges to consensus quickly, but it's a low-information consensus.
**Recovery**: silent independent voting *first*. Reveal results. *Then* discuss the surprises (high-variance options) and high-scorers.

### 14. Loud-voice tyranny
**Symptom**: same person dominates convergence as dominated divergence. Their preferred option wins.
**Why it fails**: airtime ≠ insight. The room's collective judgment gets bypassed.
**Recovery**: enforce silent voting. Token-passing in discussion. Weight votes equally, regardless of who advocates verbally.

### 15. Lost the rejected list
**Symptom**: shortlist captured; rejected options forgotten.
**Why it fails**: in 6 months when constraints change, the team re-discusses the same ideas without the prior context. "We already discussed that" without notes is worthless.
**Recovery**: explicit "rejected-but-interesting" list with rationale: rejected because X, would be viable if Y, revisit when Z. Single highest-leverage convergence artifact.

## Closing-phase failures

### 16. No handoff
**Symptom**: session ends with "great, let's circle back next week."
**Why it fails**: no owner = no action. Shortlist becomes a dead artifact.
**Recovery**: never close without explicit handoff: who, what, when. If you can't name those, the session didn't end — keep going until you can.

### 17. No meta-feedback loop
**Symptom**: facilitator never asks how the session went; same failure modes repeat next time.
**Why it fails**: no L3 capture. Each session is run from scratch.
**Recovery**: 30 seconds at the end: "What would have made this session better?" Capture the answers; apply to next session.

## Recurring meta-failures

### 18. Confusing brainstorming with deciding
**Symptom**: stakeholders expect a decision out of the session.
**Why it fails**: brainstorming produces options; deciding is a different process. Conflating them produces shallow brainstorming and undecided decisions.
**Recovery**: name the boundary up front: "This session ends with a shortlist. Decision happens [where, by whom, when]."

### 19. Brainstorming what should be researched
**Symptom**: the team is generating ideas about something that has known answers (existing literature, prior art, customer data).
**Why it fails**: brainstorming on questions with answers wastes everyone's time and produces lower-quality output than 30 minutes of research.
**Recovery**: do the research first. Brainstorming is for genuinely open-ended questions, not for re-deriving known answers.

### 20. Standing brainstorming with no follow-through
**Symptom**: regular brainstorming meetings; ideas pile up; nothing ships.
**Why it fails**: brainstorming without execution decay creates cynicism. People stop bringing real ideas because they know nothing happens.
**Recovery**: track output → execution. If <10% of brainstormed ideas ever ship, fix the execution pipeline before running more sessions.

## Quick-reference: in-session intervention table

| When you see this... | Say this |
|---|---|
| "Yes but..." | "Save it for phase two. Yes, and what else?" |
| "We tried that" | "Note it. What was different then? Add three new ones anyway." |
| Senior speaks first | "Let's hear from least senior to most. We'll come back to you, X." |
| Quiet person stays quiet | "Y, anything wild from your end?" |
| Energy crashing | "Five-minute break. Stand up, get water." |
| Single-theme variants only | "Let's break the pattern. [Random stimulus / Reverse / Worst-possible]." |
| Convergence by gut | "Pause. Let's agree criteria first." |
| First option wins fast | "Independent vote first. Then discuss." |
| "Let's circle back" | "Before we close — owner, action, deadline?" |
