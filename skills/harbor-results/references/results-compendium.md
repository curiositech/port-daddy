## R1 — Read-poverty and the information floor
**Box.** To guarantee catching all k critical artifacts among N while opening only m, any digest must carry ≥ B* = log₂C(N,k) − log₂C(m,k) bits (each flagged m-set covers only C(m,k) of C(N,k) placements). Under uniform placements, B bits give success probability at most min(1, 2^B·C(m,k)/C(N,k)); cover overlap can make the bound strict (N=4,k=2,m=3,B=1: maximum 5/6 versus bound 1).
**Numbers.** N=60,k=2,m=8 ⇒ B*=5.98 bits [verified]. Falsification: encoder/decoder model (digest = literal B-bit message; decoder = shared codebook of m-subsets); oracle/noisy/random encoders: 0/16 floor violations [internal, a7_experiment.py]. Wrong-turn on record: 8/14 spurious violations when the meter charged bits only for tie-breaking (see l3-tacit-lessons #1).
**Buys.** Digest-budget floor for the operator surface; the program's first falsifiable-and-survived datum. Paper 1.
**Boundary.** Lower bound on a *zero-miss guarantee*; cheaper digests are fine at nonzero miss (R4). The success-probability expression is an upper bound for uniform placements, not generally an attainable optimum. Worst-case combinatorial, not average-case.

## R2 — Split-digest theorem
**Box.** For readers with strict total orders and unique optimal top-m sets at every budget, one scalar head serves both iff their orders coincide pairwise. A strict crossing between successor continuation value and operator regret forbids a shared ranking. For weak orders, a strict preference by one reader and a tie by another may admit a common refinement.
**Numbers.** Joint zero-miss floor for disjoint reader-sets: Floor(N,2k,m) ≈ 2.13× Floor(N,k,m); super-additivity ratio Floor(2k)/(2·Floor(k)) > 1 for 2k≤m<N (e.g., N=60,k=2,m=8: 1.067 and 12.77 vs 5.98 bits; N=60,m=20,k=10: 1.392) [verified arithmetic on the closed form].
**Buys.** Strict crossing requires distinct reader-specific decision rankings; they may share one vector representation, source, and store. The SLM sidecar is a design response to the separate self-attestation trust boundary. Paper 1.
**Boundary.** Comonotone readers can share a ranking. The floor comparison assumes two disjoint critical k-sets, one union selection and a shared m-open budget; it does not lower-bound arbitrary separate-decoder encodings. Proof: Floor(N,t,m)=Σ_{i<t} log₂((N−i)/(m−i)), with strictly increasing summands for N>m.

## R3 — Derived regret head
**Box.** Inspect iff C_miss(x)·a(x) ≥ c_att + C_fa·(1−a(x)), with calibrated a(x)=Pr(bad|evidence). If C_miss+C_fa>0, threshold a≥(c_att+C_fa)/(C_miss+C_fa); odds form requires C_miss>c_att and a<1, then a/(1−a)≥(c_att+C_fa)/(C_miss−c_att). The product stakes×irreversibility×anomaly equals expected loss of passing when anomaly is that posterior; with item-dependent costs inspection priority is the net benefit (C_miss+C_fa)a−(c_att+C_fa).
**Numbers.** C_miss=100, c_att=1, C_fa=5 ⇒ threshold a ≥ 6/105 ≈ 0.057 [verified]. Raising C_miss lowers the bar (fixes the source manuscript's reversed SDT direction).
**Buys.** Replaces three tuned knobs with one calibrated estimator; reputation can shift a(x) but never multiplies the score to zero on catastrophic-irreversible items. Paper 1; attention-queue product claim.
**Boundary.** All costs nonnegative. At a=1 compare C_miss with c_att directly; if C_miss=C_fa=0, inspection only ties when attention is free. Calibration is a measured obligation (fit g from the audit log).

## R4 — Digest-zoom Pareto frontier
**Box.** Two-constraint rate-distortion for Bernoulli(p): minimize I(X;X̂) s.t. false-negative rate ≤ δ and flag rate ≤ f. Zero-miss corner R(0, f→p) = H(p). Zoom-advantage: identifying k criticals inside F flagged takes ≈ k·log₂(F/k) group-split opens vs F flat.
**Numbers.** p=0.05: R(0)=H(0.05)=0.286 bits/sym [verified, Cover–Thomas]; R(0,0.10)=0.186, R(0.01,0.10)=0.110, R(0.04,0.06)=0.009 [internal, b1_frontier.py]. Zoom at F=2500,k=10: 15.3× fewer opens measured vs idealized ~31× ⇒ ≈2× practical overhead [internal].
**Buys.** The operator's three-way dial (bits/opens/misses) with exact exchange rates. Paper 1's theoretical heart.
**Boundary.** The two-constraint objective is a *custom formulation*, not the classical single-constraint theorem. For the displayed halving upper bound, strict advantage requires d=k/F<1/12, equivalently f>12(p−δ); equality is break-even. This is a guarantee from the bound, not an iff for actual observed cost.

## R6 — Edge-residual information boundary

**Mechanism.** A visible graph cycle supports a nonzero residual for a single-edge perturbation; a bridge or severed edge does not. This is a property of encoded edge differences. A general split view may still have zero residual, and authenticated sender claims can be checked directly.

**Scalar witness.** A size-3 perturbation on one edge of a fully visible $C_6$ has residual $3/\sqrt6=1.2247$; the corresponding path edge has residual zero [internal, sheaf_mechanism_proof.py]. This is the effective-resistance identity, not an incremental detection result over equal-information direct checking.

### R6 information boundary and synthetic evidence

**Result.** For one visible edge perturbation $s$ on coordinate graph $K_c$, the least-squares residual is $|s|\sqrt{1-R_{\mathrm{eff}}^{K_c}(e)}$ and is positive exactly when that edge lies on a visible cycle. This is not a criterion for every split view by one sender. General offsets are detected exactly when their projection onto the cycle space is nonzero; a triangle with a leaf gives a split view with zero residual. The relayed evidence tier supplies both authenticated endpoint claims to the analyst. Comparing each sender's snapshot-aligned claims directly subsumes residual detection on those records.

**Numbers.** At seed 20260816 the checked-edge-only baseline missed 200/200 residual detections on the two-path cycle arm and 113/200 on the mixed expander arm. Across 1,800 synthetic trials, an equal-information sender-claim checker missed zero residual alarms and found 209 direct-only cases. The severed arm is residual-dark. These counts describe a synthetic real-coordinate model, not signed-log receipts or a deployed detector.

**Use.** Under an authenticated edge-only receipt contract, the residual supplies a metric score and topology-dependent pattern; exact cycle sums have the same noiseless detection set. Claims about noisy localization, certificate size, or audit cost need a matched-evidence benchmark. The residual magnitude depends on an explicit equality-preserving feature map and metric. Direct authenticated-claim comparison remains the detection baseline whenever raw claims are available.

**Blind-spot score.** For sender $q$ and coordinate $c$, let $h_q^c$ count connected components of the visible coordinate graph after deleting $q$ that receive a visible edge from $q$. When $q$ has at least one such edge, $b_q^c=h_q^c-1$ is the exact dimension of invisible split-view directions modulo a uniform sender-state change; otherwise $b_q^c=0$. All nonuniform one-sender offsets in that coordinate are residual-detectable iff $b_q^c=0$. If arbitrary $c$-bearing edges may be added between touched components, exactly $b_q^c$ are necessary and sufficient to remove this topological blind spot. This applies to edge-only receipts, not to detection over retained authenticated claims.

**Bounded-error margin.** For complete projected Euclidean error balls of radius $\epsilon$ under both hypotheses, a specified one-edge fault $s$ has disjoint honest and faulty observation classes exactly when $|s|\sqrt{1-R_{\mathrm{eff}}^{K_c}(e)}>2\epsilon$; at equality the midpoint has both explanations. Let $U_q^c$ be the uniform sender-offset line and $\gamma_q^c=\min_{\operatorname{dist}(o,U_q^c)=1}\|\Pi_cA_q^co\|$. If split views satisfy $\operatorname{dist}(o,U_q^c)\ge\tau>0$, every such one-sender fault is distinguishable exactly when $b_q^c=0$ and $\tau\gamma_q^c>2\epsilon$. On $C_n$ at a degree-two sender, $\gamma_q^c=\sqrt{2/n}$; on a path internal sender, it vanishes. These are deterministic edge-only information limits, not error rates, attribution, or superiority over checking retained authenticated claims. A bound without complete admissible error balls makes these inequalities sufficient but not necessarily necessary.

**Marginal receipt value.** For old incidence matrix $B$, readings $g$, least-squares fit $\hat x$, and a new independently retained row $b$ with reading $y$, the squared residual rises by $(y-b\hat x)^2/(1+b(B^\mathsf TB)^+b^\mathsf T)$ when endpoints already share a component. It rises by zero when the new row connects distinct components, whatever $y$ is. This is a standard rank-one least-squares identity applied to the edge-only evidence contract. A zero gain does not authenticate the new reading, and an ex ante query ranking needs predeclared hypothesis signatures and costs.

### R6 consistency radius

**CR-1.** The residual is the exact minimum Euclidean unrestricted visible edge-data change that restores consistency. A change constrained to a specified sender can require more or be infeasible; offsets in the kernel of the projected sender operator are invisible. The computed radius is only as meaningful as its declared equality-preserving feature map and metric.

**CR-2.** For one visible edge perturbation $s$, $r=|s|\sqrt{1-R_{\mathrm{eff}}^{K_c}(e)}$. The projected edge pattern lies on visible cycles containing that edge. Coalitions and some one-sender split views can cancel; the pattern does not attribute a culprit.

**CR-3.** Coordinate selection decomposes into at most $T=|\bigcup_{e\in K}S_e|$ scalar graph problems. At most $L$ systems requires a globally common coordinate universe of size at most $L$, not merely $|S_e|\le L$ per edge.

**Evidence.** The edge-residual harness checks the blind-spot dimension against independent sender-map nullity calculations in all 224 four-vertex graph/sender cases with an incident edge, and constructs the bounded-error midpoint and sender margin on a six-cycle and path. Synthetic soundness and unrestricted-achievability sweeps, effective-resistance checks, localization fixtures, and solver timings are in sheaf_consistency_radius.py. These checks do not establish fixed-sender achievability, authenticated receipt behavior, or deployed runtime.

### R6 extension (W9) — CR-4 selection heuristic and 2-complex Hodge legibility (CR-5)
**Method.** CR-4 computes a least-squares residual over the currently retained graph-incidence rows, scores an eligible row by residual-energy divided by its supplied positive finite cost, and chooses the largest score. In `sever` mode the chosen row is removed from the modeled completion problem. In `reconcile` mode the chosen observed coordinate is set to zero while the row remains in the problem. The helper validates dimensions, edges, costs and mode; it returns `mode`, `interventions`, `residualTrajectory`, `remainingResidual`, `status`, `eligibleEdges`, `retainedEdges`, and `modeledObservations` in retained-row order. Status is one of `already-consistent`, `completed`, `early-stop-zero-score`, or `round-limit`. These are calculation statuses, not effect receipts. CR-5 retains the constructed 2-complex calculation \(g=\delta_0x+h+\delta_1^*\psi\) and its Legibility Ratio fixture.

**Positive fixture.** On the inherited 12-node, one-cycle graph with a nonzero observation on edge (0,6) and supplied costs 1 for that edge and 5 elsewhere, the sever heuristic selects (0,6) and reaches zero retained-data residual in one step. This shows behavior on that specific fixture only; it does not establish global optimality, an approximation ratio, a min-cut reduction, or an authority to remove an observation.

**Counterexamples.** For edges 01,02,12,13,23 with observations (-1,-3,-3,-2,0) and costs (6,6,5,9,1), exact least-squares scores select 23 then12 for greedy cost 6, while an independent integer-potential oracle enumerating all 32 deletion subsets finds consistent retained data by deleting 12 alone for cost 5 with potential (0,1,3,3). Zeroing establishes consistency of edited numbers only.

**Conditional sever-only lemma.** For a finite ordinary graph-incidence operator with exact Euclidean residual and positive finite costs, a nonzero residual lies in the circulation space and is zero on bridges. If `sever` selects a genuinely nonzero-residual edge, that edge lies on a cycle; deleting it lowers cycle rank by one. After at most the initial beta_1 such *idealized sever deletions*, a forest remains and every retained edge cochain has vertex potentials. This is a graph-incidence argument, not a certification of the floating-point helper, a zeroing/reconcile statement, a coordinate-wise shared-cost optimization result, or a claim about arbitrary sheaf restriction maps.

**Evidence.** Hodge reconstitution and the A/B Legibility Ratio scenarios remain constructed numerical fixtures in `sheaf_repair_and_2complex.py`; the CR-4 fixtures are likewise bounded offline checks. ## R14 — Costly-escalation threshold equilibrium and the debit tuning band (B7)
**Box.** Escalation payoff Π_δ(u) = b(u) − δw(1−V(u)); for δ≥0,w>0, continuous strictly increasing b and continuous nondecreasing V:[0,1]→[0,1], Π_δ strictly increases. A unique interior root requires Π_δ(0)<0<Π_δ(1); otherwise all or no types may escalate, and endpoint equality depends on tie policy. With an interior root and ties escalating: escalate iff u≥u*. Where b,V are differentiable and the denominator is positive, statics: du*/dδ = w(1−V)/(b′+δwV′) ≥ 0. ## R16 — Context paging with an untrusted pin oracle (B9)

## R17 — Tractable deontic-conflict fragment and its NP-complete frontier (B4)
**Box.** Fragment L_c = facts + definite Horn rules (head may be ⊥) + positive ground deontic O/F rules over scope atoms and intervals + exclusive interval claims + difference constraints. Conflict = derivable ⊥ | fired O/F clash on overlapping scope and interval | exclusive-claim overlap | negative temporal cycle. For H ground fact/body occurrences, I fired token–scope incidences, X claims, and deadline graph (V,E), an indexed *existence decision* takes O(H + I log(I+1) + X log(X+1) + V·E) and returns one witness. Enumerating every distinct clash must also charge Q overlapping keyed incidence-pair occurrences, including repeats across shared scopes; deterministic deduplication adds O(Q log(Q+1)). n obligations against n prohibitions on one scope and interval yield Q=n². The published Python reference scans active pairs even when Q=0, so this indexed bound is not its runtime claim. Frontier: adding disjunctive obligations under discharge-choice semantics makes conflict-freedom NP-complete; that worst-case class does not itself require a human judge.
**Numbers.** Sweep: 3000 random in-fragment policy sets vs a brute-force oracle (all 2⁵ Horn models intersected; pairwise overlap; exhaustive integer search over the complete potential box [−Σ|c|,0]^V): 885 conflicts (⊥ 121, O/F 195, claims 484, temporal 213), 149 Horn-indirect, 0 disagreements; least model = intersection-of-all-models on all 3000 [internal, b4_deontic_fragment.py]. Reduction verified both directions on 8 SAT + 8 UNSAT random 3-SAT instances (n=4, m=7–28), 16/16, with the satisfying assignment extracted from each conflict-free selection. Mutation: removing Horn propagation misses the printed 4-line indirect witness (deploy → prod_access → O(write_prod) vs standing F(write_prod), overlap [5,10]) and 100/885 sweep conflicts.
**Future admission.** For fixed positive rules and a nonempty *explicitly supplied finite* family R of reachable future fact sets, every reachable future is conflict-free iff every inclusion-maximal member of R passes the batch checker. The proof is elementary monotonicity: every member lies below a maximal reachable member, and conflict persists when positive facts grow. Mutually exclusive safe futures {a} and {b} must not be replaced by their unreachable conflicting union {a,b}; current facts alone can miss a later activating fact c. A three-atom finite sweep checked 130,560 explicit families across 512 small Horn programs, with 4096 independent truth-table cases and both shortcut mutants caught [internal, research_envelopes.py]. This is a specialization of established antichain reasoning, not new antichain machinery or a compact reachability algorithm.
**Buys.** Paper 6 and Chapter 6 separate an admission-time witness from future safety under an independently justified scheduler fact family. A fully mediated write can refuse a presently conflicting proposal; future safety needs the declared reachable-family test and recheck after each rule admission. A charter specifies permitted discharges, priority, waiver, amendment and timeout handling; bounded search can return a checkable disjunctive choice or abstain without appointing a human judge.
**Boundary.** Completeness is relative to least-model semantics and fixed positive rules, scopes, intervals, resources and deadline constraints. Fact retraction, negation, changing rules or intervals, and unmodeled reachable sets invalidate the future theorem's premise. The indexed decision and output-sensitive enumeration bounds describe specialized constructions; `_sweep_pairs` remains quadratic in the worst case, including some Q=0 cases. The 130,560 finite checks corroborate the monotonicity proof but do not validate a production scheduler or complete mediation. The checker certifies consistency of a norm system, not the normative choice of its rules.
