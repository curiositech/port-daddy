# Constructed adaptation: discover the cues behind a workflow decision

This is a proposed software-design worksheet, not a Grassi result, a universal CDM protocol, or proof of agent efficacy. It retains useful elicitation and representation methods from the historical transfer essays while removing their universal cognition and independent-sensor claims. The source procedure and its actual study timing are in [01-primary-method.md](01-primary-method.md).

## Prepare a reviewable episode

Choose a bounded decision such as classifying whether a candidate revision has current test evidence. Separate this from authorizing a merge. Fix the candidate, available documents, interface snapshot, allowed actions and stopping condition. Select reviewers whose experience matches that scope; justify recency and variety for this project rather than copying a five-person or ten-evolution rule.

Prepare an initial goal/method/selection model from documents and practitioner detail. Preserve its version before interviews. Give participants the same scenario. A frozen interface or trace makes a decision point revisitable; it does not establish that the episode captures normal practice. Obtain any required recording permission and retain only the approved data.

## Elicit, record, and check

1. Let the participant describe the episode without leading corrections. Record the order and any unresolved terms. Do not assume free recall yields only explicit knowledge or necessarily omits the same cues for everyone.
2. Revisit decision points using the snapshot. Ask what was available then, what was observed, what it indicated, which option was chosen and why an alternative did not apply. Keep report, observation and analyst inference separate.
3. Probe missing or changed conditions: what if the badge and receipt disagree; what if a field is absent; what if a result belongs to another revision? These are counterfactual prompts, not observations that those events occurred.
4. Write a cue card with goal/selection link, observable field, source/version, interpretation, missing/stale/conflicting case and evidence limitation. A list of signals is not a statistical independence or fusion model.
5. Format the account into a proposed model revision, then compare it with the participant and the saved initial model. Distinguish a factual error, an alternative valid method, a scenario difference and unresolved disagreement. Agreement alone is not proof; record the supporting artifact and the rationale for the disposition.
6. Recheck each revised branch against the frozen cases. A model review and an implemented-agent evaluation are separate deliverables. Hold unknown cases rather than forcing a confident label.

## Finite worked case

**Initial assumption:** a green badge means the candidate's tests passed. **Goal:** classify available evidence for candidate r2. **Operators:** read candidate ID, receipt revision, suite identity and result. **Methods:** inspect a matching receipt, or report stale/missing evidence. **Selection rule:** match the receipt's subject revision before using its result.

| Decision / cue | Constructed input | Required classification | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Candidate/receipt subject | candidate r2; receipt r1/S/pass; badge green | stale for r2 | The badge and receipt are not two independent confirmations. |
| Matching scope | candidate r2; receipt r2/S/pass | current within suite S | No claim about untested behavior or merge authority. |
| Missing subject | candidate r2; receipt has no revision | unknown | Recover binding evidence; do not infer it from color. |
| Matching failure | candidate r2; receipt r2/S/fail | failed within S | Failure provenance and authenticity remain explicit dependencies. |

A reviewer who assumed the badge was sufficient is shown the first case. The revised model adds the subject comparison before interpreting the result. Record revision v1→v2, the assumption changed, the two source identifiers, the reviewer disagreement and the new selection rule. This is constructed arithmetic/string matching, not a participant-study finding.

## Preserve coordination and failure questions as design questions

For a team workflow, name the observer, decision owner and effect authority separately. Record how an observation reaches the decision owner, how stale or conflicting reports are represented, and which acknowledgment establishes receipt rather than completion. Bridge-team roles are an analogy, not proof of a general multi-agent architecture.

For an abnormal case, state the affected goal, observed symptom, available evidence, alternate method, authority needed and recovery evidence. A missing cue may justify holding the decision; inventing a fallback or voting over correlated signals does not establish correctness. Test the alternate path in an inert fixture before claiming an implementation handles it.

The review artifact is the versioned goal/selection model, cue cards, scenario record, participant/access rationale, disagreement dispositions and unresolved cases. A later effectiveness study needs its own outcome measure, baseline, held-out cases and participant/task sampling; no benefit follows merely from filling this worksheet.
