# Primary method: Grassi (2000)

**Citation.** Charles R. Grassi, Lieutenant USN, *A Task Analysis of Pier Side Ship-Handling for Virtual Environment Ship-Handling Simulator Scenario Development*, M.S. thesis, Naval Postgraduate School, September 2000; advisors Rudolph P. Darken and Barry Peterson. [Public scan](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/c/cc/A_task_analysis_of_pier_side_ship-handling_for_virtual_environment_ship-handling_simulator_scenario_development_%28IA_taskanalysisofpi00gras%29.pdf).

**Access/version.** 152-page public scan. This draft examined §§II.D-F, IV-VI, Appendix A (“Standard Commands”), and Appendix B (validation forms). The thesis treats simulator scenario development and limits its conclusion to generic/simple scenarios. CCI Tables 1–15 are in Chapter V.C, printed pp. 102–112; engine-order Table 6 is p. 106, not Appendix A.

## Source procedure

- Chapter IV starts from general training/procedure material (including NAVEDTRA 10776-A) and two ships' commanding-officer standing orders, then forms a provisional GOMS-like analysis.
- One experienced practitioner refined the document-derived provisional model with additional task detail before the later panel validation (IV.A–B, printed pp. 43–46). CCI observations were identified through that validation and mapped to the GOMS model; they were not a complete pre-validation inventory.
- The two modeled evolutions are getting underway from a pier and mooring to a pier. Each uses unit, functional, and detailed levels; some goals can recur.
- Chapter II explains that GOMS supplies logical methods/selection rules but does not expose all implicit/perceptual information. Chapter V.C adds CCI cues and their purpose. Table 6 (printed p. 106) concerns engine-order execution. Appendix A is “Standard Commands”; it is not the CCI source.
- Generic CDM is described separately in II.F.1 as three phases: uninterrupted event/procedure recall with interviewer retelling; a context/decision-point pass; then probes, including cue and “what if” questions.
- IV.C.3 calls Grassi's study protocol “three phases” but immediately enumerates four steps: advance scenario familiarization; participant's initial uninterrupted account; phase-by-phase probes; and later model review/validation. Keep that source count/list mismatch visible. The five qualified SWOs used a questionnaire and foam-core aid; this bounded study implementation is not a definition of generic CDM.
- The initial model assumed no tugs available and omitted tug procedures although each scenario made an optional tug available. In the final model comparison, all five validators chose the offered tug, found the no-tug model inaccurate without tug procedures, and agreed no-tug pier-side handling was very rare because of damage consequences. Grassi revised the model to include tug procedures (IV.C.3, printed p. 50). Bound this to five selected officers and the modeled scenarios; it is neither universal policy nor a population estimate.
- Chapter V.C contains the CCI tables; Chapters V-VI state scenario limits. Appendix B contains validation questionnaires/forms.
