# Annotated Bibliography — Slide & Presentation Design

Load when researching, citing, or sending a reading list to a maker who wants to level up.

## Canonical Books

**The Cognitive Style of PowerPoint: Pitching Out Corrupts Within** — Edward Tufte (Graphics Press, 2nd ed. 2006). The foundational critique of bullet-heavy decks; argues low information density and hierarchical templates degrade reasoning, with the Columbia shuttle disaster as Exhibit A.

**The Visual Display of Quantitative Information** — Edward Tufte (Graphics Press, 2nd ed. 2001). Where the data-ink ratio and small multiples come from; underpins every serious chart slide.

**Slide:ology: The Art and Science of Creating Great Presentations** — Nancy Duarte (O'Reilly, 2008). The most thorough textbook on slide craft itself — typography, diagrams, color, data viz — with worked examples and pre-flight checklists.

**Resonate: Present Visual Stories that Transform Audiences** — Nancy Duarte (Wiley, 2010). Maps narrative structure (the "what is / what could be" sparkline) onto presentations; required reading for keynotes and pitch decks.

**Slidedocs** — Nancy Duarte (free PDF, duarte.com). Names and codifies the dense-slide read-deck (vs. projected deck) as its own format — essential reference for board decks, memos-as-slides, and async distribution.

**Presentation Zen** and **Presentation Zen Design** — Garr Reynolds (New Riders, 2008/2009). Imports Japanese design principles (ma, kanso, shibumi) into Western slideware; the clearest argument for high-image, low-text slides delivered live.

**The Naked Presenter** — Garr Reynolds (New Riders, 2010). The companion volume on delivery — what the slides *don't* do.

**The Craft of Scientific Presentations** — Michael Alley (Springer, 2nd ed. 2013). Introduces the assertion-evidence structure (sentence headline + supporting visual) that has become the standard for technical and academic decks.

**Beyond Bullet Points** — Cliff Atkinson (Microsoft Press, 4th ed. 2018). Three-act story template that pairs with PowerPoint's outline view; popular in enterprise sales contexts.

**The Presentation Secrets of Steve Jobs** — Carmine Gallo (McGraw-Hill, 2009). Reverse-engineers Jobs's keynotes into reusable beats: antagonist, hero product, "holy smokes" moment, rule of three.

**Visual Language: Global Communication for the 21st Century** — Robert E. Horn (MacroVU, 1998). Theoretical backbone for combining words, shapes, and images into a single integrated grammar — the deep source behind modern infographic and diagram conventions.

**Storytelling with Data** — Cole Nussbaumer Knaflic (Wiley, 2015). The conclusion-in-the-chart-title rule comes from here; pair with Tufte for serious chart work.

**The Pyramid Principle** — Barbara Minto (1987). Answer-first reasoning — the logic structure behind the consulting "action title + so-what + supporting exhibits" pattern.

**Grid Systems in Graphic Design** — Josef Müller-Brockmann (1981). The Swiss grid bible; where the modern 12-column comes from.

**Legibility of Print** — Miles Tinker (Iowa State, 1963). The all-caps-is-slower citation everyone uses but rarely reads.

## Exemplar Decks (real, findable online)

- **Airbnb seed deck (2008)** — the canonical 10-slide pitch; problem/solution/market in plain type. Widely mirrored; search "Airbnb pitch deck 2008."
- **Sequoia Capital pitch deck template** — sequoiacap.com/article/writing-a-business-plan; the de facto investor schema (Company purpose → Problem → Solution → Why now → Market → Competition → Product → Business model → Team → Financials).
- **Stewart Butterfield, "We Don't Sell Saddles Here"** (2014) — Slack's internal memo; a masterclass in positioning prose, frequently reformatted as a deck.
- **Reid Hoffman's LinkedIn Series B deck** (2004, annotated 2015 on reidhoffman.org) — the best annotated VC deck in existence; Hoffman comments on every slide in hindsight.
- **DoorDash seed deck (2013)** — circulated by Y Combinator; tight problem-framing with hand-drawn flow.
- **Stripe** — no public pitch deck, but **Stripe Press** (press.stripe.com) and the Stripe brand guidelines exemplify restrained typographic identity adaptable to slides.
- **Notion Series C deck (2021)** — leaked but widely studied; clean editorial typography, customer logos as social proof done well.
- **23andMe Series A deck (2007)** — early consumer-genomics pitch; effective use of analogy slides to explain a novel category.

## Online Resources

- **Duarte blog** — duarte.com/blog; ongoing case studies, templates, and the Slidedocs methodology.
- **Presentation Zen blog** — presentationzen.com; Garr Reynolds's running commentary, especially strong on TED talks and Japanese design.
- **Beyond Bullet Points blog** — beyondbulletpoints.com; Atkinson's worked examples of the three-act structure.
- **Communication Nation** — communicationnation.blogspot.com (Dave Gray, XPLANE founder); the origin point of much modern visual-thinking and sketchnote practice.
- **Pitch deck libraries** — pitch.com/gallery, slidebean.com/templates, pitchdeckhunt.com, and bestpitchdeck.com aggregate real funded-startup decks with funding-round metadata.

## Design Systems for Decks

- **Stripe Press visual identity** (press.stripe.com) — disciplined serif/sans pairing, generous margins, single-accent color per book; directly portable to title-and-section slides.
- **Linear marketing system** (linear.app) — graphite palette, tight grid, monospace accents; a template for technical-product decks.
- **Notion brand system** (notion.so/brand) — illustrated emoji-as-icon language and a humanist sans; documents downloadable for reference.
- **Vercel / Geist design system** (vercel.com/geist) — Geist Sans/Mono, OKLCH palette, strict 8-pt spacing; the current reference for dark-mode developer decks.
