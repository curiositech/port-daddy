# Biological Immune Tolerance vs Computational Autoimmune Disease

The vertebrate adaptive immune system faces an identical allocation problem to a stigmergic agent swarm: a finite pool of effectors (T cells, agents) must patrol a large space, concentrate where threat is real, and — critically — stand down when the threat is resolved. Failure to stand down is autoimmunity. The immune system solves this with tolerance mechanisms; SOMA's resolution trace is the computational equivalent.

**Immune tolerance, precisely.** Central tolerance (thymic clonal deletion) removes effectors that over-respond to self-antigen — a startup cost. Peripheral tolerance is the runtime mechanism: regulatory T cells (Tregs) secrete IL-10 and TGF-β, dampening effector T cell activation in already-resolved tissue. The key physics: Treg suppression is local and concentration-dependent. A Treg at a cleared site releases suppressive cytokines whose effective radius is on the order of tens of microns. Effectors two tissue compartments away are unaffected. This spatial locality is not an accident — systemic suppression would blind the immune system to new threats elsewhere. SOMA's resolution field is structurally identical: `resolution[node_id]` is node-local, does not diffuse through the graph Laplacian, and the damping is proportional (`effective = raw * (1 - d * res)`), not binary.

**What autoimmunity looks like computationally.** In rheumatoid arthritis, synovial T cells continue activating macrophages long after pathogen clearance. The positive feedback loop: antigen presentation → T cell activation → macrophage recruitment → tissue damage → more antigen presentation. The loop is self-sustaining even in the absence of original pathogen. The exact computational analogue: a node with high raw pheromone attracts agents, agents do work and deposit more pheromone, the node becomes the highest-gradient neighbor for every subsequent agent, all agents converge there. In the SOMA Week 1 benchmark (20 trials, seed=42), this manifests as `utils/crypto.py` — a high-connectivity node — receiving 6-8 agent visits while `models/user.py` goes unvisited in 70% of trials. That is the joint damage of computational autoimmunity: the "tissue" (agent compute budget) is consumed at the inflamed site; the rest of the body (problem graph) is starved.

**Tolerance induction is not suppression of all response.** A critical distinction: Tregs suppress effector amplitude, not antigen recognition. An effector that encounters a Treg-saturated site will have its activation threshold raised, not its ability to recognize novel antigens removed. If new antigen appears (a genuine re-infection), the signal breaks through because raw concentration rises faster than suppression can track. Resolution damping in SOMA mirrors this: `effective = raw * max(0.0, 1 - d * res)`. If a resolved node receives a new high-urgency PHEROMONE trace (a regression, a cascading failure), raw rises, suppression is overwhelmed, agents return. The damping is proportional, not a hard gate. Setting `d=1.0` with `res=1.0` makes a node invisible — equivalent to anergy, the pathological state of true immune unresponsiveness. For most deployments `d=0.5` (SOMA default) is correct: partial suppression that can be outcompeted by genuine urgency.

**Calibration numbers with biological grounding.** Treg-to-effector ratios in healthy tissue run approximately 1:5 to 1:10 in peripheral blood, rising to 1:1 at resolved inflammatory sites. The "suppression factor" is roughly 5-10x reduction in effector activation probability. This maps to `d` values between 0.5 and 0.9 in the SOMA formula: at `res=1.0`, `d=0.5` yields 50% signal suppression (ratio ~2:1), `d=0.9` yields 90% suppression (ratio ~10:1). Autoimmune disease emerges in biology when Tregs are depleted below a 1:20 ratio with effectors — equivalent to `d < 0.1` or `resolution_damping` effectively disabled.

## Key Points

- Biological immune tolerance (Treg/IL-10/TGF-β) and SOMA resolution damping share the same local, concentration-proportional, non-diffusing architecture — this is not analogy by metaphor but structural identity of the control problem.
- Computational autoimmunity is measurable: in SOMA Week 1 benchmarks it appears as a 50% completion rate and a 70% rate of permanently unvisited isolated nodes, both caused by positive pheromone feedback at high-connectivity nodes.
- Resolution damping is proportional suppression, not binary blocking — high raw urgency can always break through, preventing false anergy at genuinely re-infected nodes.
- `d=0.5` (SOMA default) corresponds biologically to ~2:1 suppression, appropriate for moderate-connectivity problem graphs. Sparse graphs with few high-degree hubs may benefit from `d=0.7-0.8`.
- Resolution traces must not enter the sheaf Laplacian diffusion step — local non-diffusing suppression is the mechanism's entire point. Letting resolution diffuse would globally suppress exploration, the computational equivalent of systemic immunosuppression.
