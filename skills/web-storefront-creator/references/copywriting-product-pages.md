# Product Copy That Doesn't Sound Like a Machine

Product pages are the highest-stakes copy a small store has: three sentences either close the sale or read like a dropshipping template. This reference covers the craft; **the enforcement pass belongs to the `make_copy_and_media_human` skill** — run every product description, FAQ, and policy page through it before publishing. Its structural detector + judge pass catch the machine accent (em-dash cadence, perfect parallel bullets, "elevate your space" register) far better than self-review.

## The small-store voice advantage

A solo maker's store can say things Amazon listings can't. The copy should sound like the person who makes the thing, because it is:

- **Provenance beats adjectives.** "Hand-poured in small batches on Tuesdays" outsells "premium artisanal quality." Specific process detail is unfakeable; adjectives are free and therefore worthless.
- **Admit the tradeoff.** "Ships flat between foam boards because tubes dent corners and we got tired of reprinting" builds more trust than a returns-policy link. One honest limitation per product page ("the 14oz takes two weeks — we pour to order") inoculates against the too-polished tell.
- **Numbers over vibes.** Burn time in hours, weight in grams, pages in the PDF, the six batches the reader should expect to ruin. Concrete numbers are the fastest way to sound like a human who has used the product.
- **One person's opinion is allowed.** "The lavender is our favorite" is a legitimate sentence in a two-person shop. Neutral corporate register is the accent to avoid.

## Structure of a product description (30–120 words)

1. **First line: what it is and the single best reason to want it.** Not a welcome, not "introducing."
2. **Middle: 2–3 concrete specifics** — materials, process, dimensions, what it pairs with, who it's for.
3. **Last line: the honest detail** — care instruction, lead time, quirk, or origin note.

Skip: benefit-bullet triads, "Whether you're X or Y" constructions, rhetorical questions, "elevate/curate/handcrafted with love," any sentence that could appear on a competitor's page unchanged.

## SEO fields vs display copy

`seo.title` (≤70 chars) and `seo.description` (≤160 chars) in the catalog serve Google, not shoppers — keyword-forward is fine THERE ("Lavender Soy Candle — Hand-Poured, 45hr Burn"). Keep the display description human. Don't let the SEO register leak into the page body.

## Marketplace variants

Etsy rewards material/process keywords in titles (buyers search "soy candle lavender 8oz"); your own site rewards brevity. Store one canonical description in the catalog; when cross-listing, prepend searchable attributes for marketplace exports rather than degrading the canonical copy.

## Workflow

1. Draft descriptions in the catalog (or interview the operator — ten minutes of "why did you make this? what surprised you?" yields better raw material than any generation).
2. Run `make_copy_and_media_human` over the catalog descriptions and any policy/FAQ pages.
3. Apply its line-item fixes; re-run until clean.
4. Read the three shortest descriptions aloud. If any sentence couldn't be said across a market stall, rewrite it.
